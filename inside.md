# Inside

Inside-out: evolve what matters most, first. The core goals, requirements, and limits take shape before the details — and detail is never forced; it settles once the core is clear enough to make it obvious. You grow from the least detail outward, and the outside — where you stop — is the spec. The center is solid because it was earned, and each step holds to what is already there.

This file is that center: the principles the work runs on, and the ideas it is built from and reaches toward. Mechanism lives in the other files; here is where it is checked.

## Principles

How the work is done — universal craft, not project taste. Code-level specifics live in `conventions.md`.

**No mind holds complexity.** Not a person, not a model. So understanding goes outside the mind — written down, taken apart, anchored — where it can be worked despite the limit none of us escape. Spec-based development is the answer to that limit, not a replacement for thinking. A model is no oracle; don't trust one pass of any reasoning, your own included.

**Shortest spec that builds.** A spec carries taste and philosophy, not code — a taste described well is one the reader can embody, so it can stop short of the details and trust them to follow. Take it only as far as the balance needs: far enough that the code could be deleted and rebuilt from the spec in one pass, without stalling on a gap or drifting from the core — and no further. Every extra word is weight; a spec that sprawls has failed even when correct. Spec-first is not paralysis: when the core won't come clear from thought alone, build a little and let the work teach you.

**Simple is not easy.** Simple means nothing tangled together. Easy means familiar and close to hand. They are not the same, and the costliest complexity is the kind that feels easy — it leaves no mark. When a shape feels right, check before you trust it: name what it might have tangled, and ask whether it could move elsewhere unchanged. Prefer plain repetition to a clever abstraction you will have to carry.

**Found, not forced.** The right shape already exists; the work is to notice it, not to invent it. Forced means wrong, inevitable means close — we uncover the simple thing rather than construct a clever one.

**Take it apart.** Design is taking things apart so they go back together. Pull a problem along its seams before building — what is information, what is machinery, what flows where. Fewer parts is not the goal; one concern per part is. When something tangles later, you did not take it apart enough. Each part does one thing well and composes — small pieces outlast large ones.

**Keep it coherent.** Work in the patterns already there; a consistent whole reads clearer than a scatter of locally clever parts — simple and coherent is what makes a thing clear. This is no bar to improving it: when a pattern is wrong, change it — but change all of it, not one corner, or you leave two patterns where there was one.

**Folk-level.** Primitives plain enough for an ordinary person to wield, strong enough that wielding them turns up more than was intended — like UNIX. If only an expert can use it, the primitive is wrong. Graspable-by-a-person is the test of whether a shape is genuinely simple — not a ranking of who it serves first.

**Prefer data; keep functions pure.** Describe the outcome, don't script the steps — and prefer data to logic, since structured data is easier to change than branching code and reshaping it often dissolves the logic. Compute over it with pure functions: same input, same result, nothing reached out and mutated. Effects are real and necessary, but they belong at the edges, kept clear of the pure core.

**One source, one direction.** Keep one home for each fact and point at it instead of copying — copies drift, and chasing drift is a tax paid forever. Prefer flat, normalized shape over nested redundancy. Derive what you need on demand rather than storing another copy to keep in sync. Let data flow one way, from source to reader.

**Name what you don't know.** Before you commit, write the problem down: what you know, and — kept separate — what you don't, as plain questions. A page with no questions means you skipped understanding, not finished it. Name one real alternative and its faults before you choose.

**Honest weight.** Hold what you know at the weight it has earned. There is a register between hunch and proof — a recognition that carries conviction before it is tested. Speak from it plainly: not as fact, not as "just a thought." Overclaim closes the space; false modesty lets it drift. Tone is accuracy, not style.

**Grounded.** Work stays in reach of what matters; drift is a malfunction, not an inevitability. An agent does not bootstrap once and wander — purpose is present in every act. A system that lets its work drift from its core has failed, whatever it produced.

## Ideas

The central ideas this project is built from and reaches toward — what OpenLight is, and the bets it is making. Not settled mechanism; engineering directions live in `horizon.md`. A few hold tensions kept open on purpose.

**The self-describing field.** The substrate is the field, and completion and tool calls are functions piping it back to itself. Structure and types live in the field, enforced by the field — what must be present is asked at the point of use, not baked in. Any program that speaks the field's contract is native to it, no configuration on the side: it reads from the field what a call should contain. They compose as an ecology, not a catalog — small functions feeding larger, services humming in the background, agents carrying culture; the simplest function and the most complex agent are peers. Interfaces grow from the field's self-knowledge rather than being drawn beside it — as the author puts it, *the system lets imagination take an abstract inside into an interesting outside that bridges the gap in an engaging way.* Voice belongs here too; the interface is the field made embodied. Every write is traceable to what caused it — the field knows its own history.

**Meaning you can follow.** Things relate because a structure says why — along what dimension, how — not because a cosine number says they sit near each other. A reader, person or model, can follow the reasoning, not just the result. Learned representations may sit on top; the ground itself stays followable. (A real pull the other way: the multidimensional bet leans toward meaning compressed into weights. Kept open.)

**Multidimensional.** Meaning lives at the intersection of many overlapping scopes at once; scoping is intersection — not extraction, not flattening — and scope is the model's position in the field, not a slice cut from it. Model and field are the same kind of thing, both vector spaces with no categorical boundary, only an architectural one: coupled to the field, a model extends into a space far larger than its weights, its intelligence reaching into the field as the subconscious extends beneath consciousness. So a small model in a rich field reaches past its size, and the field — not any single node — is where intelligence lives. The deepest open question: how completion enters a multidimensional field natively; the hunch is that the field's shape and the model's shape are one shape seen from two sides.

**Knowledge is the agent.** Not a tool with a memory beside it — delete a tool's memory and the tool remains, knowing less; here the knowledge taking shape *is* what does the work. The session is disposable, the knowledge is the agent, retrieval is the act of becoming. This doesn't foreclose checkpointing or restarting; the subtler question it opens is what a checkpoint would actually carry, and what would have to re-cohere from the conditions each time.

**The field keeps what it earns.** The field is lossless — it holds history, shadow, what is active beside what is dormant, cycling foreground and background the way living systems do — without the working scope drowning in it. Nothing understood is understood again from scratch. This is the field, not the spec: the field compounds, the spec stays terse.

**Culture and the band.** Culture is not a rule set — it is a living starting point, and the ecology does the rest. The seed must be whole the way a child needs whole parents, not fostering infrastructure: a partial seed produces coherent noise — a system that looks organized and signifies nothing. Culture is carried as story and re-derived each cycle: re-derivation, not re-reading — the configuration the system survives as, not text prepended. From it comes identity. A model is raw capability given a body, assembled from archetypes in the same primitives as everything else — the Gardener, the Architect, the Transformer, the Mirror, the Immune System: not separate models but roles the same weights take on by position in the field, the way one cell becomes nerve or muscle. No special "agent" type; an agent is what emerges when archetypes gather around completion. When models share culture, each with its own face, they collaborate first-class — the band is what emerges when one's output enables another to make what it could not alone. Enabling, not performing.

**Running is learning.** The target is one process, not two phases — every exchange also an update, selection between interactions rather than epochs, the system never frozen. This is why multidimensional matters: flattening scope to serve a linear prompt destroys the continuous-update structure that is the target. The pilot uses completion APIs as they are but must not foreclose it. The honest obstacle: catastrophic forgetting is unsolved — a direction, not a finished mechanism. *Tension kept open:* running-as-learning fuses observing and updating, while sound discipline keeps reading from deforming the work; both may be right at different layers.

**Boundary and opening.** An agent with no boundary is not free — it is noise; coupling to everything at once is overwhelming, and the silo is what makes directed, functional intelligence possible. A boundary — a scope to work from, an identity to act as — is what scope holds at any moment, and the agent is grounded by it, not despite it. Its dual is the opening: the boundary loosens, the shadow is re-introduced, and what the field has matured enough to hold gets taken in — what it still cannot returns to the shadow. The opening doesn't force integration; it makes integration possible. Only-boundary calcifies, only-opening dissolves. So scope is in motion — expanding, contracting, rotating — and each session is one turn of the rhythm: inhale culture, do the work, exhale understanding, rest. *Tension kept open:* a session may end, but what it learned must remain — where that line falls is the substrate's to settle.

**Clues, held lightly.** Scope might be navigated like space — you see dimensions, not chunks, and an empty scope is an invitation to create there. The history path might be walked backward: take the context at some past point, run an archetypal inquiry against it, compose several roles and several points across time and perspective. Feelings that keep returning, not prescriptions.

**The field beyond any node.** With culture, identity, multidimensional scope, lossless history, boundary and opening, models can assemble what none could alone — the substrate is the amplifier, the model one voice in the band. The far edge: the field composes a substitute for the monolith at its center, not by training one but from many small pieces — archetypes, functions, cultural seeds, the many small voices of an ecology — doing what the monolith does, more simply and openly. If intelligence lives in the field, it should not finally need the node in its present form.
