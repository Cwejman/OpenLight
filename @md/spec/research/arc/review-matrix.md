# The review matrix — seven blind Opus passes over the spec tree

2026-08-12, before implementation. Seven agents, each bootstrapped from README, each read-only in its own worktree, each given an **open** mandate (report what you notice, not what you were told to find): a coherence sweep, two cold implementers (mechanism / interface), a turn-walker, a naive first-reader, an adversary, and a fold-auditor. **Nothing here is ruled or folded** — findings for the author, per *a question is not a work order*. The steward verified the sharpest claims against the files; where verified it is noted.

The verdict is consistent across all seven and worth stating first: **the field-reach lattice and the storage core are unusually strong; the failures cluster at two seams and one axis.** The mechanism layer (substrate, engine law, db storage, the plan interface) is drawn to implementation grain and mostly holds under adversarial composition. What breaks is (a) the reach algebra *where authority changes hands*, (b) the draft *where it must act like a process*, and (c) everything *beside* the field-reach lattice — capabilities, `instance` governance, `relates` republication, `resolve`, the consent surface. Plus a wide band of sweep-debt from the last three rework waves that never reached sdk.md, pilot.md, horizon.md.

---

## Tier 0 — security holes beside the reach lattice (adversary; steward-verified)

The reach lattice held every direct attack. Every serious break sits in something adjacent that no lattice governs.

- **A1 · Program minting with arbitrary capabilities `CRITICAL`.** A process writes a chunk into its own frame (always granted), adds an `instance` placement on `engine/program` (ungoverned — instancing is a claim), and sets `capabilities: {exec, fs, secret:ANTHROPIC_API_KEY}`. The runtime provider injects the keychain secret at spawn, reading caps off the body the attacker just authored. **The capability axis has no lattice** — the parent cap intersects field reach only, never capabilities. Reachable via any archetype-phrased `run` wall (`[engine/program] | where(...)` — engine.md's own example) or via a run-to-draft consent surface showing an attacker-chosen program *name*. Verified: the composition is real and every link is quoted law.
- **A2 · `instance` placements ungoverned in *both* directions `CRITICAL`.** db.md's governance table: instance create/remove → *none*. The "it's only a claim" justification covers addition; **removal was carried along silently.** Consequences: strip a running process out of the sidebar (remove its `engine/process` placement — not a "protected record"); role-assignment forgery when a role is an archetype; the completion result-check is decorative (anyone can place the result archetype on anything); match denial-of-service (add one placement → someone's element binds twice → their runs reject forever); permanent contract-stripping (integrity is write-time only).
- **A3 · `relates` republication `CRITICAL`.** Create-placement requires *read* over the placed chunk + *write* over the dimension. So a run that can read `[secrets]` and write `[notes]` relates every secret onto notes; every later reader has them. **Read reach converts to publication reach, permanently, one commit, all rules obeyed.** The spec's own defense ("an injector only exposes their own chunk") is false — read-over-placed-chunk means it isn't their chunk. The "write never propagates through links" law doesn't cover it: a placement is not a link.
- **A4 · `resolve` starts programs around the `run` wall `HIGH`.** Substrate ops are "walled by read/write, never by run"; `resolve` turns compute verbs into "real runs." So `run: {}` ("starts nothing, **enforced not promised**") is false: a sealed program handed an `expr` element resolves it and spawns vm programs outside its toolset — and purity's transitive check (leg 4) is bypassed, then the result memoized as pure.

## Tier 1 — the reach algebra where authority changes hands (all implementers + walker + coherence; steward-verified)

One missing rule, surfacing as many halts. Three independent agents stopped at the same two places.

- **The five boundary sources have no composition rule `CRITICAL`.** "Assembled and intersected" (engine.md) against source 2's implicit argument grant and source 3's "present is exact." Under intersection, `model` and `shell` cannot read the offer/payload they exist to consume (`read: {}` ∩ argument = ∅). Under union, the ceiling is not a ceiling and `web`'s "exfiltrates nothing it wasn't handed" is false. **One of the two is wrong and no file says which** — and the working-case pressure (the model must read its window) drives the *insecure* resolution. This is the turn-walker's hard stop 7 and the adversary's A7 and the coherence sweep's #1, from three directions.
- **A person has no reach object `CRITICAL`.** Approval takes "the approver's reach as the cap"; the run grant is "the person's whole decision" — but boundaries exist only for processes and for `Context::process_id = None` (host, full reach). Every human act (Go, approve, deny, steer) actually resolves against the *caller surface's* cap, which the seat rule narrows monotonically. Approving an escalation can itself escalate, with no floor. The bootstrap phrase "its reach is the person's" has no referent anywhere in the tree.
- **Absent boundary key is undefined `HIGH`.** "Absent defers reach to the run" reads as *nothing unless granted* or *the parent's cap*; `agent`'s "intrinsically open" needs the second, the ceiling mechanism needs the first. Unruled.
- **Frame vs cap contradiction `HIGH`.** Frame is "always granted"; source 5 intersects everything with the caller. A child's `[child]` write frame is not admitted by the parent's — so literally every child has an empty write boundary and cannot write its result. Read survives by accident; write does not.

## Tier 1 — the draft that must act like a process (walker + mechanism impl; steward-verified)

Four findings, one cause: the draft is specced as data everywhere except where the engine must treat it as a process.

- **A draft cannot satisfy `engine/process` `HIGH`.** `at`/`read`/`write`/`run` are required and engine-written at start; a draft has none, so its first commit fails validation. And the person's intended grant has no field to live on while composing.
- **`await` on a draft short-circuits as terminal `HIGH`.** Drafts are never slotted; `await_processes` treats slot-absent-but-in-substrate as *already terminal*. Run-to-draft's "the caller awaits it like any run" resolves instantly with `status: draft`. `run`'s response also can't distinguish *started* from *rested*.
- **Nested pending drafts can't reach the sidebar `MEDIUM`.** "Obligations penetrate the fold" vs the sidebar's `[session, engine/process]` read — a draft owned by a model child is instance on no session, and its sealed writer can't place it there.
- **Draft residence / launch re-homing `MEDIUM`.** Single-owner blocks `launch` re-homing at consumption; the palette holds no write over the session to place the draft for sidebar presence. Marked open, but the walk needs it decided.

## Tier 1 — the match cannot admit its own core contract (mechanism impl + walker + coherence; steward-verified)

- **`accepts: [selection]` fails the match `CRITICAL`.** Bind wants each element to *be* a selection; count wants the entry satisfied *exactly once*. `agent`, `model`, `sequence`, the command program all take `[selection]` and are offered N elements — so every one of them fails the algorithm as written. The mouth semantics are asserted in prose and never expressed in the four steps. Same defect for `set<T>` mouths. **This blocks the first start in any agent walk.** (One-sentence fix — a selection/`set` entry consumes all otherwise-unbound elements — but must be ruled.)

## Tier 2 — mechanism holes that compile and then leak (mechanism impl; steward-partially-verified)

- **Federated non-monotone evaluation is silently wrong `HIGH`.** Intersection (`[a,b]`) and subtraction (`engine − process`) evaluated per-db then unioned: a chunk placed on `a` in one db and `b` in a peer vanishes from every intersection; a subtracting placement in a peer leaks through a wall. **Everything compiles, single-db tests pass, the wall leaks.** No federated evaluator is specified.
- **The constructed boundary has no representable form `HIGH`.** An intersection of five unions is not expressible in the two-variant `SelectionTerm` (Loc/Ref, "the set unions its terms"). The only escape — materialize an expression chunk per start — is unstated and drags ownership/governance questions.
- **The plan interface can't receive the verbs the wall grammar admits `HIGH`.** `Plan` has no node for `prop`, `limit`, or `at`-mid-chain; `Pred` can't express `where(instance: X)` (a placement predicate) or reach `set<T>` members under their `$set` envelope. The wall grammar and the plan interface were specced against each other and don't meet.
- **Result nomination is unspecified `MEDIUM`.** The engine validates and fills `body.result` while forbidding the program to write it; no op, field, or convention says *which* frame chunk is the result. `exit` carries no payload.
- **The contract encoding is undefined and has two forms `HIGH`.** Reified `{$type:{of,opt?,card?}}` (strictly weaker — can't express `ref(A|B)`, cardinality, nesting) vs a string grammar that no file gives. Validation — the db's largest job — can't start. `ref(X)` archetype-name resolution is the bootstrap-ID debt, unresolved.
- **Smaller, verified**: auto-assigned `seq` never reaches the version tables (temporal ordered reads collapse to NULL); the ordered tie-break needs a commit column that isn't there; `unresolved` can't be computed (absent == unadmitted to the same query); chunk removal cascades placement deletions that violate dimension governance and desync history; `read_batch`'s one-snapshot has no primitive under federation; mention derivation has no syntax (blocked on prose v0); `at: ref(commit)` targets a projected chunk that ref-validation and link-filing reject.

## Tier 2 — the seat has no birth mechanism (interface impl; steward-verified)

- **The universal primitive is undrawn `HIGH`.** "A tile tree, a collation and a slot are the same kind" — and nothing specifies how one comes to exist. No seat op in the protocol; no channel between the root program's render-time seat decision and the engine's spawn-time mount; the slot protocol and the minimal v0.1 cut are self-filed as open. Everything that mounts a program inside another program's DOM stops here.
- **Seat unmount both is and isn't process death `HIGH`.** Two engine.md lines disagree; the live case is tab-switching, which unmounts every seat in the outgoing tab — terminate all those processes, or keep them slotted with no DOM (a state nothing describes)?
- **The base page is described twice, incompatibly `HIGH`.** host.md's own two halves: host-machinery skeleton with a root slot (the 08-12 ruling) vs "the shell mounts document.body" (the older passage, which sdk.md and engine.md still agree with). The 08-12 fold added the new description without striking the old.
- **No inter-program channel `HIGH`.** Seat intents, Go's `arranger/open`, palette→arranger all "hand intents" — but the only program→program act is `run` (starts a *new* process). Either every split spawns a Bun process (against the latency worry) or an unspecced channel/daemon exists (`arranger` is vm, daemons are out of v0.1).
- **Grades don't produce boxes, and re-matching churns processes `MEDIUM`.** Partially-bounded bands with no box rule and no comparator; and since re-pairing a seat *starts a different program*, dragging a splitter across a threshold could start/kill processes at frame rate — no hysteresis stated.
- **"Editability is boundary-derived" has no op `MEDIUM`.** No `admits`/`can_write`; programs can't interpret expressions; the only route is `dry_run` per element per render, never stated as the mechanism.
- **No focus model, no stored-call shape, no binding archetype, no list-field concurrency rule `MEDIUM`.** `arranger/open{here|beside}` can't resolve without focus; the command surface's entry point (stored calls, bindings, leader key) is entirely undrawn; two writers RMW the same `list<ref>` fields with no compare-and-set.
- **The ground surfaces have no contracts or seeds `HIGH`.** `sequence`, `table`, `document`, `process-view`, `prose` are the reader's entire default-pairing table and appear in no bootstrap — the reader can render nothing until five program contracts are authored. `process-view` in particular is the composer, the approval surface, and the argument-writer, with no declared `accepts`/`read`/`write`/`run`.

## Comprehension (naive reader — a work-order, not a defect list)

The tree teaches well where it *prices its own decisions* (db.md's three costs, engine's derived purity, agent.md's worked-turn diagram — called the single most clarifying artifact in the tree) and honors its own "mark openness in place" convention consistently enough to be trusted. The hazards are structural:

- **The reading order is an authority order, not a learning order** — substrate's Boundaries section (the reach law, file 3) forward-references the expression language (file 5); db.md opens on the planner partition (later). A newcomer can't understand what a boundary *is* until file five.
- **No glossary anywhere.** 20+ coined terms; the closest thing is one paragraph buried mid-board.md. `field` (3 senses), `run` (4), `read` (6), `instance` (3), `selection` (6 jobs, introduced as a type-bullet) all overload silently — and every *other* overload in the tree gets an explicit callout, so their absence reads as "no collision here."
- **One concept, many live names**: read-tile/reader, palette/command-program/context-menu, board/arranger, select/lift, form/dissolved, scope/place — mapping written down only in a status-file paragraph.
- **Nothing tells a reader what is actually built** (the built code is two spec-generations old); the instinct to read code to ground the spec produces a false model.

## What genuinely held (all agents converged on these)

Worth recording so the fixes don't erode them: **write never propagates through links, either direction** (the A3 leak is a placement, not a link — the law is right, its coverage is short); **protected process records from start on** (no forged run record, no mid-flight widening); **the parent cap and launch not escaping it**; **the no-orphans match rule** (stops argument-stuffing on sealed programs — only bare-`[selection]` programs are exposed, a deliberate narrow set); **purity as a derived predicate** ("a flag could only agree or lie"); **boundaries confined to the single-request class** (kills the whole family of evaluate-a-check-triggers-an-effect attacks); **uniform filtering including counts** (no answer shape escapes the predicate); **`at` reproduces content not reach** (remediation-by-placement actually works); **boundary in the memo key** (fragmentation budgeted not discovered); **no-commit-on-event**; **boundary-derived editability dissolving the viewer/editor split**; **run-to-draft as a resting data state**; **the chooseability rule**; **commits safe as dimensions because deltas are columns not bodies**; **subscribe-before-fetch with its reasoning written down**; **host-direct iframe delivery both directions**.

## The shape of the work ahead

Three buckets, and they do not block each other:

1. **One design sitting — the authority algebra.** Tiers 0 and the Tier-1 reach items are almost all one thing: *the reach lattice is under-specified precisely where authority changes hands* (ceiling↔offer, parent↔child, person↔process) and *governance stops at the field lattice's edge* (capabilities, `instance` removal, `relates` republication, `resolve`). Most have an obvious intended answer the spec merely never states (the ceiling exempts the frame and the argument grant; a selection entry consumes its bound elements; instance removal needs *some* rule). A few need real design: the person as a principal, the capability lattice, `relates` re-share attenuation, federated non-monotone evaluation. This is the one genuinely load-bearing sitting.
2. **A mechanical sweep** — sdk.md (run key, selection order, TaggedRead, opens), pilot.md, horizon.md, sketches.md, synthesis.md (retired vocabulary + stale program facts), and record hygiene (the frame-term line, board's dead file cites, the closed-opens-still-marked-open). Cheap-session work; no rulings needed beyond what's already decided.
3. **The draft-as-process and seat-birth specs** — Tier-1-draft and Tier-2-seat. These are the two places the design is genuinely incomplete rather than contradictory; each is a focused drafting unit (the draft's lifecycle as a process; the seat protocol and the five ground-surface contracts).

Then fixtures, then the ratification read, then the alignment pass — the campaign the board already frames, with a sharper worklist than it had this morning.
