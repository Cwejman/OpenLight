## Preliminary: the given ground and the derived layer

Everything below descends from five givens (from the two files, taken as fact, not re-argued) and seven facts about the reader's lived situation (derived from the givens, and the actual entry point of this derivation).

**Givens**
- **G1** Knowledge compounds by interconnection; what stays isolated is re-derived, and that cost surfaces later, elsewhere.
- **G2** Compounding systems share one shape: small self-standing units, one medium, one join.
- **G3** Every head is bounded — human or model session.
- **G4** Readers arrive with differing goals and want differing endpoints.
- **G5** The code is not cracked. A complete, law-grade tree can still fail, and does, when written in the order things are *defined* rather than the order they are *understood*.

**The reader's situation (F — the entry angle)**
- **F1 Bound.** Working memory is small. A unit must be graspable with what the reader already holds plus what it states — nothing else.
- **F2 Blank arrival.** The reader may hold nothing (a fresh session literally holds nothing; a tired person effectively holds little). The structure cannot *assume* a prior reading; it can only *declare* what it stands on.
- **F3 Goal.** The reader arrives aimed. The goal is a filter: every unit is on the path or is noise. Noise is not neutral — it is cost.
- **F4 Cost precedes value.** Attention is spent before its return is known. So reading is not one commitment but a chain of continue/stop decisions, one per unit, each taken under-informed.
- **F5 First-met weight.** The first material forms the frame all later material is read into; correcting a frame costs more than forming one. Attrition is also front-loaded. The entry is the only unit every reader reads, read with the least shared context.
- **F6 Return.** The reader who comes back is a different reader: holds more, wants less, and needs to know what changed. A structure readable only once fails most of its uses.
- **F7 The writer is bounded too.** Whoever writes or maintains a unit cannot hold the whole. Therefore the rules must be *checkable inside one unit*, or they will not be checked.

## 1. The values

*That which has nothing beneath it. Three.*

**V1 — Truth.**
Nothing is presented as more settled, more complete, or more certain than it is. Includes truth about the structure's own state: an unfinished thought is published as unfinished, not smoothed.

**V2 — Reader sovereignty.**
The reader's attention is theirs to spend and their route is theirs to choose. The structure serves the reader's goal, not the writer's order; it offers, it does not conscript.

**V3 — Compounding.**
Every unit must be standable-on: findable, questionable, and reusable as floor for work not anticipated when it was written.

### Why not fewer

- *Collapse V1 into V3?* Compounding needs a true floor, so truth is instrumentally implied — but only for load-bearing claims. Truth also demands marking an inconvenient open question that no one will ever stand on. V3 does not reach that. V1 stands.
- *Collapse V2 into V3?* A structure could compound while dictating one route (a textbook does). It would still be wrong toward the reader. V2 stands.
- *Collapse V3 into V2?* Serving the present reader's goal well does not make the result standable-on by the next one. Compounding is a claim about the *future* reader; sovereignty is about the present one. Distinct.

### Why not more

Candidates tested and rejected as *derived*, not foundational:
- *Why before how* — derived twice below (P1). A principle.
- *Engagement / pull* — derived from V2 + V3 (curiosity is the reader's own; a unit that repays earns the next click). Not irreducible.
- *Brevity / economy* — the visible face of V2 under F1/F4.
- *Wholeness of the record* — derived from V1 + F6 (the returning reader must see what changed).

**Note (open):** V2 as stated fuses two things — *don't waste the reader* and *don't dictate to the reader*. They are held as one because they issue from one stance: the reader, not the writer, is the authority over their own reading. Splitting them is defensible; it has not been necessary here.

## 2. The principles

*Each value made checkable. Eight. Each carries its trace.*

**P1 — Why before how, at every scale.**
*(V2 via F3/F4; V3 via durability — see below.)*
A unit states the situation it serves before the mechanism that serves it.
*Check:* a reader who reads only the opening holds a correct, coarse account of what this is for and can decide whether to continue.

> P1 has two independent derivations, which is why it is the strongest of the eight.
> **From sovereignty:** the continue/stop decision (F4) is uninformed unless purpose arrives first. Mechanism-first forces the reader to pay before knowing.
> **From compounding:** what transfers to unanticipated work is *purpose*; mechanism is local and perishable. Stability rises with abstraction; so the durable part must be delivered first, because it is the part most likely to be all the reader takes.

**P2 — Reading order is goal order; definition order is dependency order; the reference absorbs the conflict.**
*(V2 via F3; the direct answer to G5.)*
The two orders genuinely conflict. Prose follows the reader's order; each dependency is either dispatched in one line in place or cut to a reference.
*Check:* no unit requires a *forward* reference to be understood. Every backward dependency is named, and following it is optional to comprehension of this unit.

**P3 — Every unit stands alone and declares what it stands on.**
*(V3 + F1/F2/F6.)*
References are threads to pull, not prerequisites already pulled. What the unit assumes is stated, not implied.
*Check (cold-open):* a fresh head reading only this unit can say what it is about, what it assumes, and whether it serves their goal — or can name exactly which declared assumption they lack. Both are passes. Silent confusion is the failure.

**P4 — Status is written in place, and shown at the join.**
*(V1 + F4.)*
Every unit's epistemic register is legible before its body is read; and every reference displays the *destination's* register.
*Check:* the reader can tell, without arriving, whether the thing on the other end of a thread may be stood on.

> The second half is the non-obvious part and is forced: the reader spends attention *before* arrival (F4). Status held only at the destination informs a decision already made.

**P5 — One join, carrying its reason.**
*(V3 + G2.)*
Exactly one kind of connection between units — the reference. No second mechanism (no implicit ordering, no "read the whole tree first", no meaning smuggled into hierarchy). A reference states where it goes, what the reader gains, and the destination's status.
*Check:* remove all hierarchy and ordering; is the structure still navigable? If not, meaning is hiding in a second join.

**P6 — Shallow.**
*(V2 + F1.)*
Every level of nesting is a thing the bounded head must hold. Prefer many peers plus references over depth.
*Check:* entry to any unit in few hops. *(Open: the exact bound. ≤3 is a testable candidate, not evidence.)*

**P7 — Nothing is claimed beyond what is held.**
*(V1.)*
Each claim is grounded in place, referenced, or marked open. No third option. Rhetoric that outruns evidence is a defect, not a style.
*Check:* sample claims in a unit; classify each. An unclassifiable claim is a bug.

**P8 — Every stopping point pays.**
*(V2 + V3 via F4.)*
Two faces of one rule. Per unit: name what the reader can now do or decide that they could not before. If the answer is "read the next one", the unit is not a unit — merge or delete it. Per reading: any prefix must be true, useful, and non-misleading on its own.
*Check (stop test):* truncate at each unit boundary and each section boundary. Is what remains correct and usable?

## 3. The composition

### 3.1 The unit — the fold

**Definition.** A fold is *one question, its answer, and the why that makes the question worth asking*.

**Its size is derived, not chosen.** Two bounds meet:
- *Lower:* a fold must repay its own opening cost (P8). Anything smaller than one answered question does not.
- *Upper:* a fold must be holdable whole while being read (F1). Anything larger than one answered question is not.

**Boundary tests.** If you can name two questions, it is two folds. If you cannot name one without depending on unreferenced content elsewhere, it is undersized or its dependency is undeclared (P3).

**Anatomy** (each element forced by a principle, none decorative):

| Element | Forced by |
|---|---|
| Name — states the *question*, not the topic | F6 (lookup by question), P2 |
| Register — confidence and force | P4 |
| Why — the situation in which lacking this answer costs something | P1 |
| Answer — one sentence, whole, early | P1 second half, F6 |
| Body — what must hold, then how | P1, P2 |
| Threads out — references with promises and destination status | P5, P4 |
| Open — what this fold does not settle | V1, P7 |
| Close — the question the reader now holds; and, if a goal is complete, that it is | §5 |

**On the ring (why → what must hold → how → practice) and whether it is fractal — a derived partial answer.**
The **why → answer** pair *is* fractal: it is forced at every scale by P1 and P8, both of which apply wherever a reader can stop, and a reader can stop anywhere. The **full four-ring** is *not* forced. It is forced only where a fold is large enough to contain internal stopping points; below that size the ring becomes ceremony and violates P8. So: the pair is universal; the ring is a function of size. *(Marked proposed — derived, unproven.)*

### 3.2 The join — the reference

One mechanism. It carries three things, all required:
1. **Destination** — addressable, stable.
2. **Promise** — what the reader gains, stated in outcome terms ("you will be able to decide X"), never topic terms ("more about X"). Forced by F3/F4: the promise is what the goal is matched against, and a topic cannot be matched against a goal.
3. **Register of the destination** — P4.

**Reason set (proposed, not settled).** The reference's reason is what absorbs the order conflict (P2), so it must be typed by *relation to the reader's need*, not by subject matter. Minimal candidate set:
- **grounds** — why you may believe this
- **depends** — what this stands on; follow if unfamiliar
- **next** — where this leads if your goal continues
- **open** — where this is unsettled

Four reasons over one mechanism does not violate "one join": the mechanism is single; the reason is a field on it. *(Open: whether the set is exactly these four.)*

### 3.3 The whole

Not a tree. **A set of folds, one join, and one or more entries.**

A tree imposes one order and so violates P2/V2; a flat pile forfeits orientation and so violates V3. The resolution: the only privileged object is the **entry**. Where hierarchy exists it is a *reading aid over* the field, never the structure itself — a claim testable by P5's check.

Two whole-level artifacts are forced, and neither is a fold:
- **The router** (lives at the entry) — named readings, each with an audience, a goal, and a declared endpoint. Forced by G4 + F3.
- **The frontier** — an index of the opens. Forced by U2 below: a reader must be able to see what is undecided *without traversing*, or they cannot distinguish "not written" from "not decided" from "I failed to find it."

### 3.4 Knowing settled from open *before* reading

Two placements, both required (P4): on the fold's head, and on every reference pointing at it.

**Register, proposed as two axes rather than one list.** A flat list conflates two independent things:
- **Confidence:** settled · proposed · open
- **Force:** law (must be followed) · knowledge (may be used) · record (true as history, not as instruction)

A log entry is *settled × record*: entirely reliable as fact, binding on nothing. A steward's take is *proposed × law-shaped*. A flat vocabulary cannot say either. **Superseded** is a third thing again — not a confidence but a lifecycle state, and it must remain addressable (F6: the returning reader needs the trail, not a hole).

*(Marked proposed. The flat five — settled, proposed, open, record, superseded — is the incumbent and is workable; the two-axis form is a derivation, untested.)*

## 4. The entry

### 4.1 Why first-met material weighs most — three mechanisms

1. **Frame propagation.** The reader builds an interpretive frame from the first material and reads everything after *into* it. A wrong frame is not one error; it is a multiplier on every later unit. Correction costs more than formation.
2. **Decision density under maximum ignorance.** The continue/stop decision (F4) is taken most often and with least information at the start. Attrition is front-loaded. The entry is the only unit every reader reads.
3. **Cheapest possible installation.** At entry the reader holds least, so shared vocabulary and shared purpose can be installed once, cheaply, and drawn on everywhere after. Anywhere else, installation must be repeated or assumed.

### 4.2 The forces on the entry — four

- **From above: invite.** It must name a goal the reader recognises as *theirs*. Not "here is what this is" but "here is what you came for."
- **From below: everything in place.** Nothing beneath it may contradict it. The whole is foreshadowed correctly, never surprised into. This is the force that makes the entry the *last* thing written and the thing rewritten whenever the whole moves.
- **Route** *(derived from G4 + F3).* The entry is where the reader's goal meets the structure's. It must let the reader identify which reading is theirs — and, critically, **where that reading ends**. "Stop where your purpose is served" is not a courtesy; it is the only way one entry serves many goals without dictating (V2).
- **Smallest true whole** *(derived from P8 applied to the most likely stopping point).* A reader who reads *only* the entry must come away with a true, small version of the entire thing. The entry is not a preface to the content; it is the content at its coarsest true resolution.

### 4.3 Consequences

- The entry is why-first with the least slack of any fold: most readers, least shared context, highest frame cost.
- Routes are declared **at the entry only**. A fold that carries a route re-imposes one order on all its readers and so breaks P2. *(Open: whether local routes at large sub-entries are a legitimate exception or the first crack in the rule.)*
- The entry has no *depends* references — there is nothing before it. Every dependency it needs, it installs.

## 5. The emanation

### 5.1 The atom of a reading is a pull, not a page turn

A reading proceeds because the fold just read **created a question it did not answer**, and offered the thread. Engagement is not decoration on top of content; it is the structural fact that each fold ends holding an unanswered question the reader now owns.

**Method consequence:** a fold closes by naming what it opened, not by summarising what it said. A summary returns the reader to where they were; a named opening moves them.

### 5.2 How the goal steers

The goal is the selection function over threads. Each reference's promise is matched against it. This is why the promise must be in outcome terms — a topic label cannot be matched against a goal, only guessed at.

Goals also **refine as the reader learns**. A reader who arrived wanting X often discovers they wanted Y. This forbids fixed routes as the primary mechanism: routes are suggestions laid over a uniform join (P5), so re-aiming costs nothing.

### 5.3 The human/model asymmetry — and why one structure still serves both

Both are bounded (G3) and both arrive blank (F2). The difference is *who takes the continue/stop decision*:
- For a human, the decision is taken by the reader, and the lever is **engagement** — the pull of the named question.
- For a model, the decision is taken by whatever selects its context, and the lever is **selectability** — the promise and the status on the reference, read without reading the destination.

These are two consumers of **one artifact**: the promise-and-status-bearing reference. Engagement is human-only; selectability is its machine-side counterpart; the same join delivers both. This is why the thesis's claim — one structure for people and agents alike — survives the asymmetry rather than being defeated by it.

*(Open: whether optimal fold size differs. A model can hold more at once but has no incremental attrition; a human has both bounds. Plausibly the human bound is the binding one and serves both, but this is unevidenced.)*

### 5.4 Where a reading rightly ends

**Correctly:** when the goal is served, or when no remaining thread's promise matches it. For this to be *recognisable*, two things are needed:
- nameable at entry (the router declares each reading's endpoint), and
- recognisable in place (a fold that completes a goal says so: "if you came for X, you have it").

**Incorrectly:** when the reader is exhausted, lost, or no longer believes the next unit will pay. Each of these is a defect in the structure, not in the reader. This is the load-bearing move: *ending is a designed outcome, not an accident*, and an unplanned ending is a bug with a named cause (§6).

### 5.5 The returning reader

F6: a returning reader is a different reader. The second visit is a **lookup**, not a reading. Three requirements follow:

1. **Addressable and question-titled** — lookup works by question, so the name must be the question.
2. **Answer near the top** — re-reading must cost one glance, not a traverse.
3. **Change legible** — status, supersession trail, and what has moved since. Without this the returner stands on stale floor while believing it fresh, which is the most expensive failure available: it is silent, and it propagates.

**The traverse/lookup tension, and its resolution.** Traverse wants why-first; lookup wants answer-first. Both are non-negotiable. Resolution: **at small scale the why is a sentence, not a section, and the answer follows immediately.** This is also the constraint that stops P1 from degenerating into preamble bloat — the most common way why-first fails in practice.

*(Open: whether pure-lookup folds — a definition, a table of constants — may legitimately be answer-first with the why reduced to a clause. This is the sharpest open question against "every fold why-first at every scale.")*

### 5.6 What compounds into what they do next

Durability is ordered: **values > principles > mechanisms > methods.** What compounds is what holds still. Therefore a reading must deliver the durable layer first and the perishable layer last — the same ordering P1 demands for the opposite reason. What the reader carries out should be re-emittable: statable to a third person from memory. If it is not statable, it will not travel, and if it does not travel it does not compound (G1).

## 6. Failure modes and the acceptance tests

### 6.1 Fatigue — reading costs more than it pays

| # | Cause | Caught by |
|---|---|---|
| **Fa1** | **Definition order surfaced as reading order.** The reader holds unmotivated machinery. *(The named defect in G5.)* | T2, T1 |
| **Fa2** | **Unpaid units.** Folds that exist only to set up other folds. | T3 |
| **Fa3** | **Depth.** Too many levels, or too many named things needed to parse one sentence. | T9, T8 |
| **Fa4** | **No landing.** Uniform density, no marked stopping points; the reader cannot rest, so they leave. | T3 |
| **Fa5** | **Lingo.** Undeclared vocabulary; the reader is excluded by terms they were never given. | T8 |
| **Fa6** | **Uncompounded repetition.** The same claim re-said in several places; the reader cannot tell what is new, so must read everything. | T6 |

### 6.2 Unanswerable question

| # | Cause | Caught by |
|---|---|---|
| **U1** | **Answer exists, is unfindable.** No fold titled by that question; no reference promises it. | T5, T9 |
| **U2** | **Answer does not exist and is not marked open.** The reader searches forever, unable to tell "not written" from "not decided" from "I missed it." **Absence must be represented.** An open fold is a first-class unit and the honest output of an unfinished thought. | T5 (+ the frontier) |
| **U3** | **Two answers, no ruling.** Contradictory folds, neither superseded. | T6 |
| **U4** | **Answer found, currency unknown.** The reader cannot tell if it still stands. | T7 |

### 6.3 Understanding fails to land

| # | Cause | Caught by |
|---|---|---|
| **L1** | **Missing why.** The reader has the mechanism and cannot apply it to a case not in the text. No transfer. | T4 |
| **L2** | **Missing frame.** Dropped into a level with no prior, assumptions unstated. | T1 |
| **L3** | **Abstraction with no instance.** A general claim with nothing felt or particular under it. | T11 |
| **L4** | **Overclaim.** The reader believed something firmer than it was, hit the contradiction, and now discounts the whole. *(Damage is not local — it costs the reader's trust in everything else.)* | T7 |
| **L5** | **Answers a question the reader did not have.** Correct, complete, and irrelevant. | T2 |

### 6.4 The suite

Split by F7 — the writer's head is bounded, so tests a writer can run alone are separated from tests requiring the whole.

**Unit-local (a writer runs these on one fold, alone):**
- **T1 Cold-open.** Fresh head, this fold only. Can they say what it is for, what it settles, what it assumes? Naming a missing declared assumption is a pass; silent confusion is a fail. → P3, L2, Fa1
- **T2 Ten-percent.** Read only the opening. Is what is held true, coarse, and enough to decide whether to continue? → P1, Fa1, L5
- **T7 Status audit.** Head declares register; every claim is grounded / referenced / open; every outgoing reference shows destination status. → P4, P7, L4, U4
- **T8 Vocabulary audit** (mechanical). Every term is defined in-fold, defined at the entry, or referenced at first use. → Fa5, Fa3
- **T11 Instance.** At least one concrete case or worked instance. → L3
- **T12 Payment.** Name what the reader can now do that they could not. If the answer is "read the next one", merge or delete. → P8, Fa2

**Whole-local (run at review):**
- **T3 Stop test.** Truncate a route at every unit and section boundary. True? useful? non-misleading? → P8, Fa2, Fa4
- **T4 Transfer.** After a route, decide a case not covered in the text, correctly. *The real test of landing.* → L1
- **T4b Teach-back.** Restate the takeaway to a third person from memory. *The test of compounding* — "simple to state, simple to teach." → §5.6
- **T5 Question inventory.** Collect real reader questions; for each, name the fold and the path from entry. A miss becomes either a fold or an **open** fold. → U1, U2
- **T6 Single-answer audit.** Each question answered in exactly one settled place; duplicates are references; contradictions are ruled or demoted. → U3, Fa6
- **T9 Hop test.** Entry → any fold within N. → Fa3, U1
- **T10 Return test.** A prior reader finds a specific answer in one hop and can see what changed since. → F6, U4

**Verdict-level (the arc's own finish line, from the source):** a senior reader finishes with no unanswerable questions; a fresh session bootstraps and can be trusted; the author reads without fatigue and stays engaged. These are the acceptance tests of the *whole*; T1–T12 are the ones that make passing them reachable rather than lucky.

## 7. The method — writing one fold

Built on the one proven method finding: **prose regenerated from a tightly compressed proposition inventory converges on the inventory's own wording.** A tight brief *is* the prose; the writer has no freedom left. Therefore compression level is a control knob, and variation must be sought in the *entry angle*, not in the wording — four blind variants at one compression level lost nothing and found nothing.

1. **Name the question.** One sentence, interrogative, in the reader's words. Two questions → two folds. *(Sizes the fold before anything is written.)*
2. **Name the reader and the goal.** If more than one goal is served, either serve the common core and branch by reference, or split. Do not average two readers into one that does not exist.
3. **State the answer in one sentence.** If you cannot, the fold is not ready — **write an open fold instead.** Open folds are cheap, honest, and required (U2). Do not write around a hole.
4. **Write the why.** The situation in which lacking this answer costs something. Concrete, felt, particular — the felt experience beats the abstract claim. Hard constraint: *the why must be shorter than the answer it motivates* (this is what keeps P1 from becoming preamble, §5.5).
5. **List the load-bearing claims loosely** — as claims, not sentences. Mark each: grounded here / referenced / open. Loose deliberately: a tight inventory writes the prose for you and it will be the inventory's prose.
6. **Choose the entry angle explicitly, and vary it across folds.** This is where the variation must go.
7. **Write the body in the reader's order.** Where the reader's order conflicts with dependency order, dispatch the dependency in one line and cut a reference (P2). Never reorder the reader to suit the definitions.
8. **Name the threads out.** Promise in outcome terms; destination status attached.
9. **Close with what it opened** — the question the reader now holds. If the fold completes a declared goal, say so.
10. **Run the unit-local tests** (T1, T2, T7, T8, T11, T12). Cut whatever fails T12.
11. **Register it.** Set confidence and force; if it supersedes something, mark the superseded thing *in place*, do not delete it (F6).

**Two whole-level method rules:**
- **Write the entry last; rewrite it whenever the whole moves.** It is the smallest true whole (§4.2) and it goes stale silently.
- **Author corrections are data.** Record wording corrections as rules rather than as fixes. Three are already earned: *honesty beats rhetoric*; *the felt experience beats the abstract claim*; *lingo kills grace*.

## 8. Open — marked, not asserted

1. **The hop bound and the fold-size bound.** No evidence for any specific N. ≤3 hops is a proposal.
2. **Fractality of the ring.** Derived here: the why→answer pair is fractal, the full four-ring is size-dependent (§3.1). Derived, unproven.
3. **The reference reason set.** grounds / depends / next / open — proposed, untested; the set may be smaller or larger.
4. **Register as two axes (confidence × force) versus the flat list.** Derived improvement, untested against practice.
5. **Whether pure-lookup folds may be answer-first.** The sharpest challenge to "every fold why-first at every scale" (§5.5). Genuinely undecided.
6. **Whether routes may be declared anywhere or only at entries** (§4.3). A local route at a large sub-entry is either a legitimate exception or the first crack in P2.
7. **How branching routes end per audience.** Whether one core with branches, or several full lines, and how a branch declares its endpoint.
8. **Whether human and model readers want the same fold size** (§5.3). The join serves both; the size may not.
9. **Whether V2 should split** into economy and non-dictation (§1).
10. **The instruction set's reliability.** §7 has not been run by a second head. Until a fresh writer produces a passing fold from it unaided, the method is a hypothesis, not an instruction set — which is precisely the source's own standing open item.
11. **Whether the acceptance suite is complete or merely sufficient.** It was derived backwards from three named failures; a fourth failure family may exist that none of T1–T12 catches.
