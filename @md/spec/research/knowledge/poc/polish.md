# The polish

## 1. Why the arc is polished now

The knowledge arc, [everything its entry connects](../README.md), was written a decision at a time, and much of it grew by addition rather than being laid by the gradient. This plan brings it under [the code](../code.md) before it moves to [its own repository](repository.md), and it is written for a session that starts with nothing but the files.

The sign is the face, a brief's heading and first paragraph, which [the practice](practice.md#3-the-surface-is-declared-by-position) now flags past 400 characters. A brief that grows by addition lengthens its one paragraph until the face is the whole brief and nothing in it can be skipped; on 2026-09-14, 60 faces across the arc ran past the flag. The confidence lines grew the same way, into histories of each ask.

*Measured with the surface's check on 2026-09-14; that briefs and their confidence lines grew by addition was seen in the briefs themselves.*

## 2. What a session reads first

A session starting the polish reads the arc's entry, the code whole, [the practice](practice.md) and [the skill](skill.md), then this plan. The code says what a brief is, the practice how one stands in markdown, and the skill how a session works on the files.

It works from the arc's folder, `@md/spec/research/knowledge` in OpenLight's repository, and runs the surface's check there: `bun poc/surface/surface.ts . --check`. The check warns of mounts that miss, links whose file or heading no longer exists, and images that break the practice, and it lists apart every face past the flag.

*Seen, 2026-09-14: the check was run on the arc that day, and a link to a missing heading is among the warnings it gives.*

## 4. The method, one file at a time

Each file is one round of [the code's making](../code.md#5-the-making), and nothing in it is written before the whole file is held.

**The outline.** The file is read whole, with its line of the check. Then an outline, a few lines per brief and not prose: what the brief gives, the sentence or two its face will be, what goes beneath it, and where one brief is really two. The outline is where a fault is cheapest.

**The writing.** The file is written again whole from the outline. A brief that held is moved and never retyped. Nothing the file claims is lost: every claim keeps its grade and every decision its date, and a refusal found inside a brief moves to its level's run of refusals, [the surface's](surface/refused.md) or a section of refusals where the level has one. Plain words, and no term used before it is given.

**The fresh head.** A subagent holds only the code, the practice, the draft, and for a mounted file the brief that mounts it, since that is where the file's own brief is read. It writes nothing, and hands back what it did not understand, what came before its ground, and what it would cut. The file is written again from that, and the rounds end when a round changes only words.

**The cascade.** A change to the practice, or to a brief other files stand on, can move the ground under files already done. The session reads those files for the change before going on, and brings this plan in line if its own ground moved.

**The close.** The check is run for the whole arc, since a renumbered heading breaks every link written to it. The file is committed on its own, on the branch in use, its message saying what moved and what was kept with a reason, and its line in [the record](#8-where-the-work-stands) is written.

*Reasoned, the code's making applied to one file; whether a file of twenty briefs fits one session's rounds is not yet known.*

## 6. What the author decides before the work begins

Four decisions are the author's, and the lean of the session that wrote this plan stands beside each. The first touches every file, so a session asks for all four before it writes anything, and acts on a lean only once the author has agreed.

**The confidence line.** The lean is that a line keeps its grade, its date and its ground in a phrase, and that the path of asks goes to the commit messages, since git is the history the practice names.

**The ideas.** They are the author's positions, the names kept as coined. The lean is to break the faces past the flag and split a paragraph holding several points, keeping the author's wording and never restructuring an idea. The other choice is to leave them as they are.

**The flag.** The ratified code holds seven faces past 400, the longest 605. So either those faces want the author's reading, or the flag is too tight for a brief that opens a large understanding. The lean is to keep 400, since a face past it is read and may be kept with a reason, and to let the code's seven stand as the author's judgment.

**The model.** Whether Opus or Fable does this better is not known from anything measured. The lean is a trial before the order begins: both draft the widgets, set aside and not committed, and a fresh head reads the two drafts against the code. The chosen model then takes the order from the practice, and the widgets are written again in their own place.

*Proposed, 2026-09-14, awaiting the author.*
