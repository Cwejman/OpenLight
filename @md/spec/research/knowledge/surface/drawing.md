# What the prose cannot say

Prose says what a brief means. It cannot say what a brief is: how large, what rhythm its paragraphs have, where it sits in the holarchy, how much hangs beneath it. Those are facts of shape, and shape is seen rather than read.

So the surface draws them, and the code says why it must. A reader judges what a part will cost from its look before spending any reading on it, and [the labs failed that judgment more than any other](../study/findings.md#1-heaviness-is-seen-before-it-is-read). A rule never fixed it and a picture did.

## 1. One drawing, at the fidelity its room affords

There is one drawing and not a family of them. Its subject runs from the whole body down to the paragraphs of a single brief, dividing as the holarchy divides, so the rhythm of one brief's paragraphs is the same drawing at its finest depth.

What changes is fidelity, and the room it is given sets it. A wide strip carries levels and names; a small mark beside a title carries a brief's paragraphs and nothing more. It never shows more than its size can hold legibly, which is the gradient turned on the drawing: the biggest understanding the room can carry, and no more.

Every mark in it is computed from the substrate and none is invented. That is what makes a drawing legitimate ink under [the design language](../../../design.md#why-flat--the-newspaper-precedent), which spends ink only on what the field already holds, and it is also the only reason a reader can trust the picture enough to act on it.

*Reasoned, 2026-09-12. The fault it answers is seen: the first cut built six separate figures, each asking for estate the reading wanted, which the author named as one of the two reasons it was hard to use.*

## 2. A drawing earns its place where its prose is not

Beside a brief whose prose is fully in view, a drawing of that brief says what the reader can already see, and it costs attention the reading wanted. Beside a title whose prose runs past the fold, the same drawing says what cannot be seen yet, and it is the judgment the code asks for.

So a drawing is not placed and then justified. It appears where what it describes is not visible, and it withdraws where the thing itself has arrived.

What hangs beneath a brief is never visible from that brief's prose. So that drawing always earns its place, and it is what a reader sees of a level before pressing into it.

*Reasoned, the author's, 2026-09-12.*

## 3. The cursor follows the reading

Every drawing shows where the reader stands without the reader placing it, and a drawing that cannot show that is drawing the wrong thing. Each also fits whole at its own size, since a drawing that must itself be scrolled has stopped being one.

Pointing at anything that names a brief lights that brief in every drawing at once. One brief is one thing seen from several distances, and a drawing where the lighting does not follow has left the reading.

*Seen as a fault: the first cut's hovers did not all sync, and the author named it twice in one sitting.*

## 4. What is open

Which projections the drawing takes. A rectangle dividing into its parts, a radial with the root at the centre, a wide strip above the panes carrying the path: each was tried once and none was judged. Whether more than one is wanted, and where each sits, waits on a use that asks for it.

*Open, 2026-09-12.*
