# What was tried and refused

## 1. A pane holding one brief, with its siblings in a picker

The retired program gave a pane to a single brief and put the rest of its level in a list to choose from.

That makes a level a choice between peers, where [a level is read through](README.md#2-a-level-to-a-pane).

## 2. Panes scrolling at graded speeds

The one in front running freely and each behind it slower, so the row would stay in step.

The row is not meant to be in step. [Each pane is a reading of its own](panes.md#2-a-pane-scrolls-its-own-level-and-nothing-else) and keeps its own position, and the author's verdict on the coupling was that one small scroll moved three panes at once.

## 3. A rightmost pane that only presents doors

A preview pane showing faces and no prose.

The level beneath is opened as prose directly, and [the drawing beside a brief](drawings.md#2-a-briefs-level-beside-its-heading) already says what that level holds for a fraction of the room.

## 4. Two scroll axes in one region

The retired program put a frame with its own horizontal and vertical scrolling inside the page, which the author judged critically hard to use.

Sideways is [a scrub](moving.md#1-sideways-is-a-scrub-never-a-second-scroll) instead.

## 5. Six separate figures

The retired program built the gauge as six drawings, each asking for room the reading wanted. With the frame above, it was one of the two faults the author named at the close.

Several figures are not the fault; six that shared nothing were. [Each figure now fills a gap the reading leaves](drawings.md#6-each-earns-its-place-and-ink-is-what-limits-it) and they draw one material and behave alike, so a reader learns a family rather than six strangers.

## 6. A rectangle dividing into rectangles

Tried for the body whole, and the shape a treemap gives.

Refused on the geometry [the plate's own brief](drawings.md#3-the-body-as-droplets-on-a-plate) sets out: its cells degenerate into slivers, where a droplet stays compact. Not refused for room, which neither figure buys.

## 7. Rigid rings with the text outside

The retired program's radial: bands of even thickness, names and weights set beside the figure rather than in it, and an empty centre.

The author judged it failed, and [droplets](drawings.md#3-the-body-as-droplets-on-a-plate) replace it rather than rings or bars.

## 8. A record of the work kept outside the tree

The retired program read a harness's transcripts directly and drew them, which made the surface a monitor of one tool rather than a reader of a substrate.

The surface is [handed a directory and reads only that](README.md#11-what-it-is-handed), so whatever wants to be read writes itself into the substrate first.

## 9. An overlay a reader has to move into

The retired program's card was a place to act: it could be pinned and its rooms pressed from inside.

Reaching it is the problem. On a crowded figure the pointer cannot get from a cell to an overlay without crossing other cells, and crossing them retargets it. Every fix for that is apparatus, a forgiving corridor or a hold delay or a fixed place away from where the reader is looking, and each costs more than it returns. [An overlay that only tells](moving.md#3-the-rest-of-a-brief-is-told-on-asking) needs none of them.
