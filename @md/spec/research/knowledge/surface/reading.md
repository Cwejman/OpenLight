# How a body is read

Wherever you are reading, you are at a path in the holarchy. No brief is orphaned, since each one is a part of the understanding above it, and that is what a surface can be built on: there is always somewhere you are, and always a way you came.

So the reading is laid out the way the path is. Each level of the path takes a pane, and the panes stand side by side, left to right. Going deeper adds a pane on the right and takes nothing away, which is what keeps where you came from present while you read.

## 1. A pane is a level, not a brief

A pane holds one level of the holarchy: its briefs, in the order the code lays them, as prose read straight down. That order is a reading and not a menu, since each brief at a level stands on the ones before it, so the pane gives the level as it was composed.

Pressing a brief opens what is nested beneath it in the pane to the right. You do not enter the brief, because you have just read it. You enter the level under it.

So a brief's prose has one home on screen, the pane of its own level, and is shown nowhere else. That is the code's one home met in the geometry rather than in the writing.

*Reasoned, the author's, 2026-09-12. The model refused beside it gave a pane to a single brief and its siblings to a picker, which turns a level into a choice between peers and hides the order the code composed.*

## 2. Only a door can be pressed

A brief with nothing beneath it has no level to open, so it cannot be pressed. The difference shows before a reader tries it, and a level read straight down then says where its depth lies.

Each pane lights the brief whose level stands to its right. That is the path made visible in place rather than only in a strip above, and it is what keeps the association with where you came from, which no depth is worth losing.

*Reasoned, 2026-09-12.*

## 3. A pane is short, and scrolls on one axis

A pane scrolls when its content exceeds it and on one axis only, which is [the design language's rule](../../../design.md#scroll-and-seams) and not this surface's own. Two axes in one region are two regions pretending to be one.

Panes running at graded speeds, one freely and each behind it slower, is not needed. It was solving a pane that held a whole file, and a pane that holds a level is short because a brief is brief.

A level too big to read is the exception, and the surface does not hide it. Lab ten's piece carries seventy-two briefs at its second level by [the study's own count](../study/measured.md#1-the-study-against-the-gradient-2026-09-12), and that pane is punishing because the piece is broken there.

*Reasoned, 2026-09-12; the count is measured, and carried from the study without being checked here.*

## 4. Where only one pane fits

On a narrow screen the panes are a stack and one is seen at a time. The path is then the only thing holding placement, so it is drawn rather than implied.

*Preferred, the author's, 2026-09-12.*

## 5. What is open

A link is not a path move, and that is the sharpest thing unsettled. Following one lands you at a brief whose own path from the root differs from the path you stand in. Rebuild the panes to the target's path and where you came from is lost. Keep them and the path lies about where you are. Neither can simply be chosen.

How many panes hold before the leftmost falls away, and whether the root stays present, is not settled either. What a reader sees of a brief before pressing it is not open but answered, in [what the prose cannot say](drawing.md).

*Open, 2026-09-12.*
