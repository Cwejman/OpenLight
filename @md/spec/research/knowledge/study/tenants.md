# Tenants beyond prose

Knowledge is the substrate's first tenant, and the code's sixth section says the shape reaches further. This file holds the cases as they occur, between the study and the project's vision: each a domain named as it would look as entries in the substrate, held open and marked so. Begun 2026-09-11.

## 1. Sound

A digital audio workstation as the substrate would hold it. A patch, an instrument's configuration, is a brief: whole and readable alone, and a part of a track, which is a part of an arrangement, which is a part of a piece. The temporal buffers, MIDI and audio, are the raw beneath, one link away from the patch that plays them, never restated inside it. The gradient runs from the piece to the note: a listener or a collaborator enters at the piece and descends as far as their need takes them, and stops whole at any level. Every edit is a step, the holon viewed by its change, so a session at the workstation leaves a record as a writing session does, and a track can be branched on its own without the whole piece carrying the conflict. Entries are hospitable here as anywhere: by instrument, by section, by the last change, by the session that made it. And people make a track together the way they make knowledge together: each lays down a group or a bus of their own, a holon that is theirs and a part of the piece, branched on its own and connected back, the piece runnable from anyone's copy and shared as a substrate rather than as a bounced file. The bounce still exists, for public listening: it is the debrief of the piece, the surface handed to whoever only wants to hear it, with the whole beneath for whoever wants to build on it. That one distinction is the heart of the code manifested in sound: the walls come down, the glue goes, and building on another's work becomes first class. How two patches drive each other rather than merely nest is answered in [resonance](resonance.md), which asks the question of synthesis and brings back what mutual driving costs: a port and a delay. What the code asks of prose, a face that says what it gives and confidence written beside a claim, has its sound-shaped forms to be found: a patch that says what it sounds like before it is opened, a take marked as kept or superseded.

Reasoned, the author's, from a thought in passing; nothing built, and the first tenant after knowledge only because it was the one that came to mind.

## 2. Image

The visual holds the same way, and it brings the other half of the interface with it. A node-based environment such as TouchDesigner already works as a holarchy of operators: each node whole, readable alone, and a part of a network, with the signal flowing through as the buffers flow under a patch. Its artfulness is the point: composition by wiring, seen live. As entries in the substrate, a node is a brief and a network a holon, every parameter change a step, and the visualisation modules become the drawing components the practice's visuals section hypothesises, composed from what the text says rather than laid by hand. That is where the associative interface comes from: prose and the visual in one medium, a brief beside the shape it describes, the shape beside the sound it plays, and an entry to any of them from any other, so a reader moves by association rather than by a file tree.

Reasoned, the author's, the same night as sound; nothing built.

## 3. The social, as a product across tenants

The third case is not a domain but what happens between them. Sharing music is the example: today it is a feature of a social platform, which holds the people, the channel and the music in one wall. Over the substrate it is a product across tenants, the author's term, a cross-tenant product: the music is an entry in the sound tenant, the people are entries of their own, and the sharing is a connection between them, with no platform in the middle. If the substrate-native connection is itself the service, hosting as a business in the author's naming, then the host earns by carrying connections and everyone views their own emanation of the same sources: the same people, the same common source, but each their own channel, composed from what they connect to rather than from what a platform chooses to show. Broadcasting is the radio of this social medium: one source sent across unified receivers, each receiver composing it into their own channel. And because every broadcast is an entry, people broadcast off each other as artists do, a DJ session over another's tracks, a podcast over another's talk, a mix of mixes, each a connection to what it stands on, so the whole of it is a substrate a listener navigates rather than a feed they are fed. The streaming services are the case in point. Imagine one of them taking the personal side of the artists themselves, what an artist would share in such a medium, the sessions, the takes, the mixes off each other, the talk around the work. It would be the strongest gain such a service ever offered, and it is exactly the gain that cannot be monopolised: a cut of the size the streamers take today would not survive it, because the value is in the connections between artists and listeners, and a host that takes too much of what it only carries is walked around. The gain is too strong to own, and that is the stance in one example. What such a service becomes instead, in the author's naming, is a digital commodity provider as a service: it holds the royalty, the rights and the accounting that an artist cannot carry alone, and exposes the work for integration, so that anyone's channel, session or mix can connect to it over the substrate and the royalty flows with the connection. The service earns on holding what must be held and gives what compounds. A job market holds the same way, an exchange as a market hub: the offers and the people are entries, the matching is the connection, and the market is whoever hosts the connecting rather than whoever owns the listing.

Reasoned, the author's, the same night; the names are the author's and kept as coined; nothing built.

## 4. The live edge

Every case above has an edge where the world comes in: a stream, a sensor, a market feed, a message from another host. The substrate holds those too, as connections to external sources kept live rather than as data copied in. Compute runs on demand of the event: an external event arrives carrying its data, and the substrate progresses by it, a program matched to what the event gives runs, and what it produces lands back as entries with the event as their ground. Nothing polls and nothing is scheduled; the world advances the substrate, and the record shows what advanced it and when. This is how the drawing stays live, how a shared channel updates, and how a piece of sound or image responds while it is played.

Reasoned, the author's, the same night; nothing built.
