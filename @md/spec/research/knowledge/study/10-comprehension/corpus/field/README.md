# Who else is on this ground

One agent was sent to find whoever argues the same thesis, and whoever argues hardest against it; two more mapped the products and the research lineage that surround it. This folder holds what they found, and the shape of the finding is itself useful: the position is unoccupied, the neighbours are commercially crowded and shallow, and the best objection is not the obvious one.

## 1. Nobody holds the whole thesis

Every neighbour holds two or three claims and explicitly rejects or ignores the rest. The combination appears nowhere as a single position. The loneliest claim — that text is not the right substrate — is actively *losing* an argument in public, because the most-read 2025–26 writing on extending models argues the opposite: markdown, bash and natural language beat typed tool schemas, and the extension-mechanism pendulum has swung away from structure twice.

Two claims have a crowded lane and a shallow one. Several companies say "sessions don't compound," and almost all of them solve it by summarising transcripts into a store you retrieve from — keeping the transcript and bolting on a memory layer. **None makes context an addressable, reproducible location.** [`neighbours.md`](neighbours.md) holds the claim-by-claim map.

## 2. The best objection is not "structure is hard"

It is Dynamicland's inverse principle — that agency is maximised by minimising what the computer knows — and the objection that a richer typed substrate is another opacity rather than a cure for one. The strongest form of it turns on who the binding constraint is: programs, or the people who have to hold the thing in their heads. [`opposition.md`](opposition.md) holds it at full strength, in its own words, with the empirical and economic layers beneath it and the one answer the evidence supports.

## 3. Plain files won, for a reason nobody predicted

Across the whole personal-knowledge product category, the single most consequential 2026 fact is that **plain Markdown became a moat because coding agents can read it** — and the one product that moved *away* from files, four years late, landed into a market that had just re-valued the thing it gave up. The second is that MCP or a command-line interface is the universal 2026 feature, which means the AI layer is being *externalised* rather than built in. The third is that the venture-funded middle has evacuated personal knowledge for meetings and agents. [`tools-for-thought.md`](tools-for-thought.md) holds the census.

## 4. The research lineage is thin, honest and mostly self-funded

Three independent structure-editor and codebase-as-database ventures converged on the same funding answer within a year: consulting. The one explicit commercial post-mortem names the structured editor itself as the thing models invalidated. And from the research side, the field's most reflective practitioner names the missing piece as **theory rather than engineering** — "What Subtext needed was a Theory of Change." [`substrate-research.md`](substrate-research.md) holds the census and the post-mortems, which are unusually candid.

## 5. The timing reading

Heating hard on the commercial edge, flat at the foundations, and the two are not connected. "Context layer" as a company category did not exist before spring 2025 and now names at least four accelerator companies; "agent harness" became a common noun in 2026. Meanwhile the custodians of the underlying 30-to-60-year-old claims — Smalltalk, OpenDoc, Xanadu, the semantic web — are moving at research pace, largely without AI.

One window may be closing: the extension-mechanism pendulum has swung away from structure twice, from plugins to instructions and from typed tool registries to markdown skill folders. If that wins a third time, the argument for a typed medium gets harder to make each year, not easier.
