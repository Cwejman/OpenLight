# The profession that carried these practices

The structured-authoring tradition has an institutional layer — societies, standards bodies, conferences, a vendor market — and its state is a fact about how much weight the tradition can still bear. One agent checked it at source and found a structural shock that is widely under-weighted.

## 1. The Society for Technical Communication is gone

STC **ceased operations on 29 January 2025** and filed Chapter 7 bankruptcy (case 1:25-bk-11577, E.D. Va.). Membership had fallen from roughly 25,000 at its early-2000s peak to about 4,500. There is no STC Summit in 2025 or 2026; any live summit page is stale. Anyone citing "STC's current position" is citing a defunct organisation.

The surviving venues are CIDM's ConVEx, DITA Europe with DITA-OT Day, and tekom. The US Bureau of Labor Statistics projects **1% growth** for technical writers 2024–34 and explicitly names generative AI as a demand limiter.

## 2. tekom's position is regulatory, not architectural

tekom's "Report 2025" is an activity report, not research — no sample, no adoption data. **No current tekom or tcworld industry survey with structured-authoring adoption figures could be found.** Its published position papers concern the AI Act and the Machinery Regulation. The tcworld 2026 conference lists nine subject areas and **DITA and iiRDS are not among them**; AI and "intelligent delivery" are.

## 3. iiRDS is still being pump-primed in year nine

Version 1.3 shipped 2025-11-03 with a handover-documentation profile integrating VDI 2770 and an optional JSON-LD serialisation. The consortium has **27 members**, overwhelmingly German technical-communication service providers and tool vendors, plus five industrial adopters. The tell: the consortium still **funds pilot projects at up to €5,000 net each**, with applications open to 2026-09-30 and a mandatory conference presentation. A standard subsidising €5,000 pilots in its ninth year is not one the market is pulling.

## 4. The revival is rhetorical and it is happening at the conferences

DITA Europe 2026's second day is roughly half AI sessions — agentic support on a structured foundation, agentic AI and MCP, "Why DITA in 2026: The AI Age," DITA and enterprise AI — against **one** session on the specification itself. The trade press front page is dominated by documentation-agent series and a "content triforce" pitch arguing DITA plus iiRDS plus microcontent is the backbone of a trustworthy AI pipeline.

That pitch has, so far as the sweep could establish, **zero supporting measurement**. The one arXiv result on structured output requirements cuts the other way: JSON, XML, LaTeX and Markdown output formats all *substantially degrade* reasoning and writing performance across open-weight models, with the dominant cost entering at the prompt.

## 5. The market is unreadable

Grade everything in this section opinion dressed as data.

2025 component-content-management-system market-size estimates range from **$2.14B to $4.6B** across report mills — a greater-than-2× spread means none are usable, and there is no Gartner Magic Quadrant for the category. Capital activity is quiet: MadCap has been private-equity-owned since 2022 and acquired IXIASOFT and Xyleme; Heretto's last round was $17M in December 2022; Paligo's was $29M in April 2023. **No 2025–26 funding or consolidation events were found.**

## 6. The reading

DITA is stable but stalled, not revived: the specification is six years late and has one committer, the tooling ships only a preview, the professional body that carried it in North America is bankrupt, and the conference taxonomy no longer names it. The toolkit and the vendors are alive. The standard is not advancing.
