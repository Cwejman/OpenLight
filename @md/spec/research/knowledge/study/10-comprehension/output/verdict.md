# What the evidence did to each claim

This is the answer to the question the piece asks: taking the five claims in [what is being weighed](claims.md) one at a time, what did the material do to them. Three come out unvalidated rather than refuted, one is barely touched, and one survives narrowly. The last section holds what the evidence supports that the claims did not have to argue for, because a weighing that only prosecutes is not a weighing.

## 1. The gradient is unvalidated, and the one direct result lands on half of it

Nothing measures the ordering claim. Nobody has varied a document set's arrangement while holding its content constant, so the central proposition — that laying the biggest understanding first and nesting the rest beneath gives more understanding for less effort — has never been raced against an alternative. That absence is the first thing to hold, and it is in [the missing studies](unknown.md) §2.

The one place the evidence speaks directly is the advance-organizer literature, and how much of the gradient it reaches has to be argued rather than assumed. The principle it tests is Ausubel's: open with something more abstract than the body, so that it subsumes what follows. Organizers do help, at a small effect — but the analysis of 112 studies found the pattern inside that effect running against the theory, with lower effects for abstract and for written-only organizers and higher effects for organizers that did not subsume. *Measured; the finding is the meta-analysts' own, and it concerns which organizers worked rather than whether they worked at all.* The figures are in [what a reader is measured to get](reading/README.md) §3.

Two halves of the gradient meet that finding differently. The nesting is subsumption-shaped by construction: the understanding at the top is the one everything beneath stands under, which is the relation the theory describes. But the opening is not, because the code instructs the opposite of an abstraction — say what the brief gives, in the reader's terms, giving the understanding in small, never a map of the parts to come. So the result is a warning about one way the gradient can be written badly, and the code already instructs against that way. It is not a verdict on the shape, and this piece treated it as one until a reader who held only the code and the draft said the equation had never been argued.

One result supports putting the governing understanding at the top: relations high in a text's structure influenced comprehension far more than the same relations low in it. *Measured once, in 1975.*

Against that, three results converge on depth from three directions, and they are worth stating as one finding because separately each looks oblique. At a fixed number of items, adding a third level of hierarchy cost readers time and orientation, in all three depth experiments. Routing a model reader through one index helps and a second level gives no benefit and sometimes hurts. And the one direct design prescription says not to invest in layering depth at all. Different populations, different methods, no study designed against another — but nothing in this material points the other way, and the gradient's answer, that its levels are checkpoints rather than layers, is the untested rule in [the missing studies](unknown.md) §5. *Measured in each case; the convergence is this piece's reading.*

### 1.1 The heaviest thing said against it, and what answering it costs

Against a claim with no measurement of its own, the heaviest thing in either direction is the one direct design prescription the sweep produced: invest in correctness, completeness, currency, findability, worked examples and a stated reading procedure — and do not invest in format, in layering depth, or in stripping guidance for expert readers. *Reasoned by one agent from the professional-reading studies; not measured.* It is stated in [how professionals actually read](reading/professionals.md) §6.

Three of its items are not in contention. Correctness, completeness and currency are properties of content that the code does not speak to, and worked examples are something it neither asks for nor forbids. Findability is the interesting one, because a gradient is a findability device — an ordered tree with an entry is precisely how a reader is supposed to reach the part they need — so on that item the prescription and the code want the same thing by different names.

One item contradicts the code directly, and it is the one to carry: do not invest in layering depth. The code's answer would be that its levels are checkpoints rather than layers, which the prescription's author never considered — and that answer is exactly the untested rule in [the missing studies](unknown.md) §5. So the contradiction stands. It is one agent's synthesis of studies that never varied arrangement, which is why it is not decisive; it is also the only direct prescription anyone in this material produced, which is why it is not dismissible.

## 2. The checkpoint is untested, and the nesting and the link share one measured argument

The claim has three parts and the evidence treats them differently.

That a reader who stops at any level has gained something and lost nothing has no experimental test anywhere in this material. The nearest evidence is the segmenting effect, supported across 56 investigations — and its authors identify the mechanism as letting the reader control the pace, not as what stopping leaves the reader holding. So the measured benefit of breaking a text into parts is not the benefit the claim asserts. The nearest working instance is Wikipedia's summary style, a twenty-year deployment of readable-alone-and-nested at seven million articles whose own rule states the same condition, and which has no measurement behind it either. *Untested; the segmenting figures are in [what the effects actually say](reading/effects.md) §1.*

The nesting has one real argument in its favour, and it is measured. Network link structures produce far more disorientation than hierarchical ones and hurt low-knowledge readers most, which is the nearest thing in this literature to an argument for a holarchy over a graph. *Measured once, 54 participants, one domain; in [what the effects actually say](reading/effects.md) §2.*

The link is the same finding seen from the other side, and it is where the cost sits. Hypertext links measurably impair reading, and the readers hurt worst are those with the least working memory and the least prior knowledge — so links cost, and the hierarchy is what makes them affordable. Whether a link should announce what following it gives has no test at all, and sits in [the missing studies](unknown.md) §5.

## 3. One home per fact has lost its usual justification, and confidence marks have no test

One home per fact is routinely justified by the redundancy effect, and the agent that read that literature returned a flat verdict against the justification: the effect is about two channels competing in the same moment, not about a fact stated once and restated elsewhere weeks later. The rule's real ground is drift — one home means one place to change — and nothing in this material measures drift at all. Aviation, meanwhile, has a long-standing safety convention that does the opposite on purpose, duplicating critical items across checklists.

Writing a claim's confidence into the text has no direct experimental test. The nearest literature concerns attribution and source reliability rather than an author's own settledness label, and the agent that named it did not verify it.

Both rules survive as preferences with reasons; neither survives as a finding. *Untested, both; the evidence and its bounds are in [the missing studies](unknown.md) §5.*

## 4. The making is barely touched

Review as such works at a defensible base rate; how you review returns nulls; the fresh-head practice is measured exactly once, at a small gain; and whether writing a thing whole survives a corpus larger than one rewrite can hold is addressed by nothing in the sweep. Of the five claims this is the least examined, and the sweep is the wrong instrument for it — it was sent to read a literature, and the making is a practice.

[What is known about the making](making.md) holds all four answers and the absence. *One measurement at a small effect, one clean absence, and review verdicts carried at one remove.*

## 5. The medium claim survives narrowly, in the form the code actually states

This is the claim the material engages hardest, and the adversarial lane — the agent sent to argue against the project's own thesis — returned the best summary available: typed, self-describing structure is the right substrate for a narrow set of properties, and only where the types are declared or deterministically observed rather than inferred.

That lane was testing a stronger form than [what is being weighed](claims.md) §5 states — that text as a medium must be superseded — and refuted it outright: prose won the payload slot. The lane's own summary of why is that the systems which won typed the edges around a prose or code body rather than typing the body itself — a generalisation over the cases in [the substrate question](medium/README.md) and [where structure pays](structure/README.md) rather than a measured finding, and this piece carries it as the lane stated it. *Reasoned by one agent over its own survey.* The code does not make that stronger claim; it holds prose as the floor knowledge lives on and asks for the shape to be carried past prose into the rest of what a computer holds. So the refutation lands beside the claim rather than on it, and what remains is the narrow half, which the code can have.

What supports it is precise. Machine-facing descriptions pay under intervention, repeatedly. Enforcement before generation cannot live in a prompt. Provenance is a fact about the world rather than a property of the reader. Navigation pays, and explanation does not. *Measured, in the agent-facing lane, which is also the least peer-reviewed territory in the sweep.*

What bounds it is equally precise. The gate — never type what must be inferred — overrides every reason to type a thing. Two objections stand unanswered: that a type rots silently while text rots loudly, and that the structure premium may be a tax on weak models that is falling each year. And the combination the claim wants has been built nowhere, while the market spent 2026 re-valuing plain files because agents can read them. The evidence is in [where structure pays](structure/README.md) and [the substrate question](medium/README.md).

## 6. What the evidence supports that the claims did not have to argue for

The piece would be dishonest if it only prosecuted, so this is the defence, gathered from where it was found. One asymmetry has to be admitted first: the five claims are what gets prosecuted, and most of what follows defends rules of the code that are not among them. A reader weighing the verdict should know the wins and the losses are not scored over the same list.

Five things, and then one the market decided. Narrative is the largest and most robust effect in the whole text-design literature, and the code asks for a story wherever the understanding is a chain of causes — and this is the piece's strongest support for the code, resting on a paper nobody in the sweep opened. Hierarchy is what makes links affordable, which is section 2's measured argument. Signalling — saying where the ground shifts, in the reader's terms — is the cheapest supported intervention in the literature, and larger for readers who know less. People prefer navigating to searching, which is what a browsable order is for. Prose is the right medium for the payload, on the adversarial lane's own verdict. And the strongest measured document-design result in the whole material is a writing one: a stripped, task-focused manual cut learning time by 40% and let learners do two and a half times as much, in [where better writing stops helping](ceiling.md) §6 — with the meta-analysts' own reading, that plain cutting of verbiage accounts for nearly the whole effect, sitting squarely against the code's line that simple is not terse. Beside them, plain files in a repository, which is the medium this code is practised in, is what the market converged on for reasons nobody predicted.

None of that validates the arrangement. It says the code's materials — stories, hierarchy, signals, prose, files — are the ones with evidence behind them, and that what remains unproven is the shape they are put in. *Signalling, hierarchy and the navigation preference measured where they are stated; narrative's size carried from an unopened lead; prose-as-payload and plain-files reasoned and observed. The gathering is this piece's own.*

What every figure above inherits — the sweep's conditions, whose marks these are, and what was never checked — is in [what this rests on](ground.md).
