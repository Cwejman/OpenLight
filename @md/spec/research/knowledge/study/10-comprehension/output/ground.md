# What this rests on

Every figure in this piece inherits the conditions of the sweep that produced it, and those conditions are unusual enough to change how the numbers should be read. They are stated here once and pointed to from the entry rather than repeated in every brief.

## 1. What the sweep was, and the words it left behind

On 2026-08-26, forty-five research agents each worked one pass over an assigned territory, briefed to hunt contradictions rather than confirmations, and each returned one report. That is why this piece says forty-five agents and forty-five reports interchangeably.

Four words from that arrangement are used throughout and are worth having. A **lane** is one territory worked by one or more agents — the agent-facing lane, the adversarial lane. The **register** is the sweep's own list of the runs it commissioned; eighteen agents appear in it, and the other twenty-seven are sub-agents those eighteen spawned, whose briefs were not recorded. The **coverage chart** is a document the project already held before the sweep, surveying eleven territories in one pass, whose every source was a lead nobody had opened; a figure that reached the sweep through the chart rather than through an agent's own read of a paper is therefore weaker, and is marked that way. And **the raw** is the forty-five reports themselves, which sit beneath the material.

Because most agents inherit a parent's brief and framing, and at least two are revisions of or reactions to a sibling's output, the reports are not forty-five independent observations. Where this piece says a finding recurs across the sweep, read that as convergence across a partly shared lineage.

## 2. Two vocabularies of confidence, and how they appear

The agents graded their own claims in a vocabulary about deployment: shipped-and-adopted, shipped-niche, announced, paper-only, opinion. A claim graded paper-only is a real published result with no deployment behind it; a claim graded opinion is an assertion, whoever made it.

This piece marks claims in the code's vocabulary — measured, seen, reasoned, preferred — which is about how a claim was arrived at rather than how far it shipped. The two do not translate, so the italic line closing a section carries this piece's mark and, where it changes what the figure is worth, the source's grade beside it; the words are glossed in the entry. A claim with no mark is one this piece did not grade, which means the reader should treat it as the material left it rather than as settled — and where an unmarked claim is load-bearing, that is a fault in this piece rather than a licence.

## 3. Whose this is, and what it did and did not check

This piece was written by a session working under the code, holding the code, the practice beneath it and the material, and nothing of the project's other documents — not an independent reader, and not the code's author either. A reader grading a verdict that comes out three-fifths unvalidated should know that the hand holding the scales was working inside the thing being weighed.

The agents read their sources at source: primary papers, repositories, filings, specifications. The session that wrote this piece did not. It read the material, no raw report, and verified no figure against a primary document.

So every mark here is a borrowed mark, twice over — from the corpus, which took it from the reports, which took it from the literature. Treat a figure as worth what its agent's own verification note says it is worth, and no more.

## 4. The three biases

The first is a search budget. Each session had a quota of web searches, and most agents report finding the quota already spent when their work began, so they gathered by fetching addresses directly instead — repositories, package registries, preprint servers, regulatory filings. That over-samples anything with a public repository, a changelog or a machine-readable endpoint, and under-samples blog posts, conference talks, trade press, vendor whitepapers, non-English material and paywalled academic venues. Several agents say plainly that their absences in those areas are unproven rather than established.

The second is paywalls, and the consequence is systematic: effect sizes and sample sizes for older, better-designed studies are often exactly what could not be verified, while repository star counts and release dates are verified to the day. This material is therefore sharper about the health of software than about the strength of science.

The third is that almost nothing in the agent-facing lane is peer-reviewed — the agent that worked it says venue-grade peer review is absent across the board. That covers the context-file nulls, the representation study, the file-finding result and the tool-description interventions, which is to say it covers the most load-bearing conclusions in [where structure pays](structure/README.md). Those are the cleanest experiments in the sweep and the least reviewed, and both halves are true at once.

## 5. What the sweep was sent to answer, and what this piece asks

The sweep was commissioned against two documents the project already held: a ladder of knowledge-structure rules, and the coverage chart. It was not commissioned against the five claims in [what is being weighed](claims.md), which are drawn from the code as it stood sixteen days after the sweep ran.

So the verdicts in this piece are answers to a related question rather than to exactly the one it asks, and the fit varies by claim: the reading and substrate territories were entered deliberately and answer well, while the making was barely entered at all.

## 6. The fabrication that was caught

One agent caught an automated summariser inventing verbatim quotations and sample sizes in two separate places, and re-verified roughly half its own findings by extracting text directly from primary documents. A second agent flagged one table of its own numbers as having come through a page summariser rather than its own read of the source.

Where an agent marked something unverified, the material marks it too, and so does this.

## 7. Where the detail is

This piece is a debrief: a stretch of work ends, its conclusions go to the surface and the detail stays beneath for whoever needs it. The conclusions are here; the detail is [the material](../corpus/README.md), which holds each finding once, in one place, with the confidence its report gave it, at roughly ninety thousand tokens.

Each of its briefs links the raw reports it rests on, and the raw holds three things deliberately not reproduced anywhere above — the leads each agent chose as the highest-value things to read next, the negative findings in full, and the exact quotations.
