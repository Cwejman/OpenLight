# The missing studies

Some absences are worth more than the findings around them, because they say which arguments cannot be settled by citing anything and must be settled by preference or by running the experiment. Four bear on the position this piece weighs, the fifth holds four rules that have no test at all, the sixth turns that on this piece, and the last says what the sweep never entered.

## 1. The crux experiment does not exist

There is no head-to-head of model-assisted, human-declared structure against a well-written prose document, on the same corpus, with the same frontier reader.

Every result that favours declared structure beat a model-extracted or an unstructured baseline. None was raced against good prose. That is the comparison the medium claim turns on, and it is the one nobody ran. *Named by the agent that argued hardest against the claim, as its own most important negative.*

## 2. Nobody has varied organisation while holding content constant

No independent, non-vendor study varies a document set's arrangement — typed page kinds, one topic per page, heading depth, cross-link density — holding the content fixed, and measures answer quality.

The nearest proxies do something else. Carrying a document's heading hierarchy into its retrieval chunks helps retrieval, which is a claim about chunks rather than about how to organise a corpus. And the study that comes closest to showing that restructuring changes what a model can answer re-presented the same data as readable cards instead of a database schema, and nearly doubled answer quality — which is presentation, not partition.

So the results on structure and retrieval say what they say, and they do not say how to organise a body of knowledge. This is the absence that leaves the gradient unvalidated. *Absence named by the agent that surveyed the retrieval territory, in its own words.*

## 3. Nobody has watched a professional use a document over months

No study anywhere in the sweep measures how a professional's use of a document changes over repeated exposure. The comprehension literature is immediate effects on a captive reader, usually a student, on a passage read once, and the professional literature does not close that gap so much as fail to address it.

That matters because the outcome this kind of project cares about has a name in the literature and no measurement. Deciding a case the text does not cover maps onto what psychologists call the situation model — having reconstructed what the text is about, as against having understood its surface — and that has only ever been operationalised as inference questions and sorting tasks on short passages. The construct exists, the payoff of effortful reading is located in it, and nobody has measured it for the population or the materials that matter.

## 4. Nothing bounds how large a body of knowledge can get

No study in this literature identifies an optimal document or section length, and the depth-and-breadth experiments cannot generalise past their own fixed item counts. Nothing in the sweep addresses whether writing a thing whole survives a corpus larger than one rewrite can hold, which is the practical limit on the making.

A related absence sits beside it rather than under it. Nobody has tested whether letting a reader skip a level substitutes for writing two versions of the prose for two kinds of reader. The evidence on writing for readers who already know the subject, in [what the effects actually say](reading/effects.md) §3, says under-explaining does not help your best readers — but the skip-instead-of-fork alternative is simply untested.

## 5. Four of this code's rules are unevaluated rather than supported

Four things the code holds have no direct experimental test, and nothing in the comprehension literatures addresses them. This is their one home; the verdict brief points here.

**One home per fact.** The rule is routinely justified by the redundancy effect, and the agent that read that literature returned a flat verdict against the justification: what the effect measures is identical content delivered in two channels at the same moment, in three experiments with 25 technical apprentices, degrading learning inside one working-memory episode. That is not a claim about a fact stated once and restated in another document read weeks later. Two things push the other way — the more elaborated, partly restating texts are the ones that won for low-knowledge readers, and distributed restatement is the mechanism behind spacing, one of the most robust effects in learning science. Aviation, meanwhile, has a thirty-five-year-old safety convention that duplicates critical items across checklists on purpose. The rule's real ground is drift, and nothing here measures that either.

**Writing a claim's confidence into the text.** The nearest literature is on multiple-document comprehension and sourcing, where readers who attend to a claim's source build better situation models — but it concerns attribution and source reliability rather than an author's own settledness label, and the agent that named it did not verify it. *Unverified lead, not evidence.*

**Treating a reader who stops early as having been paid.** Nothing measures it. The nearest thing is the segmenting effect, whose measured mechanism is letting the reader control the pace rather than what stopping leaves them holding.

**Writing a link so it says what following it gives.** A plausible extrapolation from the signalling literature, and not an established result.

Hold all four as preferences with reasons, not as findings. *Absence established by one agent's read of the primary literatures; absence of evidence in paywalled venues is weaker than absence elsewhere.*

## 6. This piece is written under all four

It marks its claims, it is built as levels a reader can stop at, its links say what following them gives, and the facts it repeats have been driven toward one home each over several rounds, imperfectly. So the piece cannot be neutral about the four rules above, and a reader should discount its form as evidence for its own rules accordingly.

The honest position is that they are cheap, and that cheap untested rules are worth holding where the alternative is also untested — which is where this field mostly is. *Preferred, by this piece, and stated as a preference.*

## 7. What the sweep never entered

Named as out of scope and not covered: design systems as a source of truth, localisation and what a reader in another language does to an English corpus, API documentation as the derived-documentation case, teaching materials written for learners of the mechanics, the economics of who pays for documentation, and whether Markdown in a repository is the end form.

The full list of what the sweep was asked and what came back is in [the material's method file](../corpus/method.md).
