# How professionals actually read

Almost all of the comprehension evidence was measured on students reading a short passage once, so the question that matters is whether it transfers to someone reading a document as part of their work. Two agents were sent to find out. Expertise turns out not to protect anyone, the reversal effect everyone cites has no professional evidence, finding rather than understanding is where the failures happen, preference is not comprehension, and the lane closed with the only design prescription in the sweep.

## 1. Expertise raises the whole curve and shields nothing

The best-designed professional-reader experiment in the sweep put 105 practising lawyers on contracts written in two styles — ordinary prose and legal register — and compared them against a fresh analysis of data from 108 laypeople who had read the same materials. Both style and legal training moved recall and comprehension on their own. Neither modified the other: the test for an interaction returned p = 0.360 for recall and p = 0.638 for comprehension, so training bought no protection against the harder style. In a second experiment, 102 lawyers rated the simplified contracts equally enforceable and higher on quality, style and likelihood of a client signing. The results held regardless of years in practice. *Measured, preregistered, peer-reviewed.*

Its companion study gives the mechanism from a corpus of about ten million words: contracts are disproportionately loaded with rare jargon, clauses nested inside other clauses, and passives, and those features reduce recall and comprehension "even for experienced readers."

Two independent populations agree. The validation survey behind the standard taxonomy of API-documentation problems found no statistically significant difference in how novices and experienced developers experienced them. And the best-instrumented study of developer time — 78 professionals, seven real projects, 3,148 working hours, monitored across all applications — found comprehension time falling steadily with experience, from about two-thirds of the day for the least experienced to under half for the most. Experts read faster and are blocked by the same content defects as everyone else.

## 2. The reversal everyone cites has no professional evidence

The expertise-reversal effect — that assistance which helps novices harms experts — is supported by a strong meta-analysis: 60 experimental studies, 176 effect sizes, 5,924 participants, no publication bias detected.

Its moderator table is where it breaks for this purpose. Educational status has five levels and there is no category for practising professionals, because there were no such studies to code. The nearest proxy is vocational training, whose estimate for high-knowledge learners is indistinguishable from zero with an interval wide enough to contain almost any effect, from three studies. And within every education stratum the harm to high-knowledge learners is non-significant, which suggests much of the effect is a comparison across education levels rather than across expertise within one. *Measured; the extension to professionals is unevidenced, and the authors' own illustrative "large expertise difference" compares trainee hairdressers with psychology students.*

## 3. Retrieval, not comprehension, is the failure mode

This is the one place medicine and aviation converge independently, and it is the most consequential finding in the folder.

A think-aloud study with 13 healthcare professionals preparing consultations from a paper guideline found the guideline did not support them in finding the recommendation that applied to their patient; the failure was retrieval and navigation, not comprehension. A NASA analysis of eleven aircraft quick-reference handbooks found the lines that guide a pilot through a procedure hard to follow across pages and when nested, and checklists chaining jumps for no evident reason — with the load-bearing usability questions still tagged as needing further research thirty-five years on.

Two professions that share nothing, the same failure. It is the strongest reason to treat findability as a first-class property rather than as a consequence of good writing — and [what is known about finding](finding.md) is where that runs into the thinnest evidence in this material. *Measured; small-N and observational in both cases.*

One observational result points the same way from the other side. Across 61 general practitioners and 12,880 decisions, vague recommendations were followed in 36% of cases against 67% for clear ones. It is confounded — specificity, controversy and required behaviour change are entangled attributes of the same recommendations — but it is large, and it is real professionals making real decisions.

## 4. Preference is not comprehension

The only randomised format trial in practising clinicians put 181 physicians across four countries on a layered against a standard narrative guideline format. Preference for the layered format was overwhelming, 72% against 16%. Correct understanding moved from 58% to 72%, p = 0.06, and appropriate clinical action from 92% to 98%, p = 0.10 — a near-miss at this sample size rather than a null, so the honest reading is that the comprehension gain is unproven in either direction while the preference is not in doubt.

Grade any format claim resting on practitioner preference accordingly, including the claims in this piece that rest on what readers say they want. *Measured, randomised, underpowered for the outcome that matters.*

## 5. The artifact does not carry the effect on its own

The surgical safety checklist is the cleanest natural experiment available on whether a well-designed procedural document changes outcomes at scale, and it deflates. The famous before-and-after across eight hospitals halved mortality. A later study across 101 hospitals and more than 200,000 patients returned a null on both mortality and complications. The one randomised trial, across two hospitals, found complications falling substantially and mortality falling in one hospital and not overall.

The pattern is coherent: where the checklist arrives as an engaged programme it works, and where it is mandated by policy across a system it does not. Designing the artifact is necessary and radically insufficient. *Measured; one randomised trial, two observational.*

## 6. The one design prescription the lane produced

The agent that read this literature end to end closed with a prescription, and it is the only direct one anywhere in the sweep. Invest in correctness, completeness, currency, findability, worked examples that show the normal case, and a stated procedure for how the document is meant to be read for a given purpose. Do not invest in format, in layering depth, or in stripping guidance for expert readers.

*Reasoned by one agent from the studies in sections 1 to 5; not itself measured, and it is one head's synthesis rather than a finding.* Held at that weight it is still the single thing in this material that argues hardest against arrangement being the lever, which is why it is stated here rather than left in the raw.
