# What the cold reads found

The edition, the practice and the two programs' rooms were rewritten through the evening of 2026-09-11 and nobody who had not written them had read any of them. Four fresh sessions were sent at them at the close, each holding only what it was asked to judge. This is what they returned, newest first, and what was done about it.

A record: it amends the state, it is folded, and it retires when it is served. Nothing here is the author's verdict, which is still owed.

## 1. The author's asks on the edition, not folded

Kept 2026-09-12 from the asks of 2026-09-11, when that record was retired to git. Not a cold read but the author's own, and like the entry beneath it, **proposed and not done.**

**Effort and ease are one measure, and the edition does not say so.** A win is understanding against the effort it cost in [the gradient](../../code.md#31-the-gradient) and in the first two sections, while [the prose principle](../../code.md#41-the-prose-of-a-brief) names its measure ease and says the gradient set it. The author left open during the rewrite whether a win is counted against least reading or easiest reading, and holds that effort and ease are the same thing; the edition uses both words and never joins them.

**The order principle may not be explicit enough to keep an agent from the mistakes it names.** The author accepted [4.2](../../code.md#42-the-order-of-a-holarchy) as it stood, with that doubt said aloud.

## 2. The edition, read alone against its own conditions

A session holding only `code.md`, with no practice and no context, asked to judge it by the conditions it states and to answer three questions. It found sixteen faults. **Nothing has been folded; the edition is the author's and this is proposed, not done.**

**Where it got lost.** In 3.3, at the sentence about a plan carrying its place in the order, which was written into the edition that same evening. It had been holding four things by then, went back to the top of the paragraph twice, and still could not say whether a plan carries both a status and a grade or status instead of one. Second and third: 4.2's sentence about a folder's entry ordering its files, which it could not parse at all and gave up on, and 4.2's own closing confidence line, which it reconstructed rather than understood. All three are in the two most procedural sections, which is where a writer actually returns.

**Could it write under the code after one reading**, which the edition's first paragraph claims. The shape, yes. The mechanics, no, and its reasons are specific: the notation for a confidence line is never stated, only shown on four principles; nothing separates *seen* from *measured*, and *preferred* is unexplained in a list of evidential grades; a folder's entry file is never named, which is the most concrete thing a writer needs on the first day; and there is not one example in the document, so every rule must be applied from its abstract statement alone. Its judgment: the claim is currently overstated and by the code's own rule should carry a grade.

**The single change it would make.** Split 3.3 into rooms and give the confidence rule the four or five paragraphs it actually contains, one each for the grades and what they mean, for borrowed grades, for status on directive briefs, for placement in the order, and for settled meaning settled for now. It is where the document is heaviest, where the reader got lost, where the one-point-per-paragraph rule is most visibly broken, and where four other sections' closing lines point.

**The heaviest faults beneath that.** *Ground* carries four meanings, one of them the title of 3.3. The four grades are used throughout and defined nowhere. Confidence is written on four paragraphs out of forty, in a document whose second condition is that confidence is written. Section 1 opens by promising an ontology and a topology it never gives, in two words its own 4.1 forbids, and follows with an apology in the position 4.1 reserves for what the section gives. The rule against opening with a map of the parts is broken by the two section openings immediately preceding it, so the document cannot be obeyed and imitated at once. *A model infers, it does not comprehend* has two homes, and so do the grade list and the rule that a link is an offer. 4.3 is a second brief about links and belongs beneath the first. *Brief* is a noun and an adjective in the same sentences, *win* is defined twice, and *harness* is used four sections before it is given. Two links are empty. Three claims are asserted flat in the body and retracted at the foot. One instruction hedges where a writer needs a decision. And three lines carry plain breakage.

## 3. The reader's room, read three times

A session holding the code, the practice and the room, three times over, each reading the fold of the last. The first two refused most of what they read; the third said another round would still change understanding and named what to move. **All three are folded**, in the commits of that night.

The first found the room's load-bearing words used and never given, room and spread and rail among them, so its reader finished unable to say whether two of them were one object; three confidence marks in a hundred paragraphs, so a program its own entry calls unusable read as settled documentation; and an entry that handed over a directory of its parts while listing last the briefs it told the reader to read first.

The second found the vocabulary lifted but the order still wrong, three briefs standing on words a fourth gave later; a brief that had become a bin for five senses of one word; the program described twice, once in the briefs and once in the plan; and a confidence line citing a rule of the code that the code does not contain.

The third found *region* defined once and used in a second, contradictory sense in the two briefs that most needed it; the figures unnamed, miscounted and, in two cases, given as proper names they had never been given; and the room's largest true fact, that it is critically hard to use, nowhere on the entry's surface as prose.
