## Instruction

Then write, honoring this request from the author of the project these files belong to:

"Bring me into contact with the existing research and other people's work in a way that engages me — meaning that compounds as I read. Not a scorecard of my project's claims; the project is only the shore I depart from."

Six rules extend the code for this piece:

- **The document is folds all the way down.** A heading starts a fold; the text between it and its first subheading is that fold's surface — what it is for and what it concludes. Detail drops into subsections beneath, where a reader who has what they need skips past and one who wants more reads on. The same order law holds at every level: the folds under one parent stand in reading order, each standing on the surfaces before it — inside a section, its subsections hold the way the sections hold in the whole piece. A nested fold may be skipped; when a later part depends on something the reader may have skipped, link to that heading, so the skipping reader knows where their ground is.

- **The centre carries the whole.** The piece must pull the reader onward, and the pull comes from meaning itself: every part the reader meets matters to what they already hold, so understanding compounds and nothing reads as overhead. What makes that possible: knowledge is a web, and importance in it is reach — a few understandings connect to everything (change one and every other finding changes its meaning), while most touch only their own subject. Reach decides place in the folds: the farthest-reaching understandings are the centre and stand highest — the piece's opening and the surfaces of its top folds — stated plainly and completely, so they carry the comprehension of everything beneath; the less something reaches beyond its own subject, the deeper it folds, until the edges hold the detail only its own subject needs. Placed so, each part takes meaning from what the reader already holds and gives meaning back — and stopped anywhere, at any depth, the reader leaves holding the most that could be held in that much reading. 

- **Bullets are folds without an address.** A bullet list is a fine way to fold small things — but a bullet longer than a sentence or two must work like a fold: it opens with its point in bold, then its body, so the reader decides from the bold alone whether to read on. And a bullet cannot be linked to — so anything a link may need to reach gets a heading, not a bullet.

- **Dead-simple language.** International English that a non-native speaker reads without friction: only words everyday speech already uses, short sentences, one idea per sentence. If a sentence needs a word you would not say out loud to a colleague from another field, use a plainer word or split the idea into two sentences. No academic, literary, or clever vocabulary — ever, even where it would be shorter. The test: a reader from outside this field of work follows every sentence on first read. Simple does not mean brief — explain everything fully; simple is only about the words and the sentences.

- **Only what you know.** Your knowledge of these subjects ends where the raw ends — write as if that is honest, because it is. Say what you know plainly and concisely; never stretch thin knowledge into a full section. Where your knowledge runs out and a source holds more, the link is the fold you did not write: place the source's own web address in the text (a real URL from the raw where one exists), with the sentence around it saying what is behind the link. The piece reads whole without any of these links — a link is an offer, never a missing piece — and it ships complete with doors left unopened.

## Constraints

- **No story.** The pull onward is significance, not suspense: a part earns its place by what it changes about what the reader already holds, never by what it promises next. No walk, no scenes, no characters carrying the reader from one thing to the next. People and their works are content — name them, quote them where the raw carries their words — but they do not carry the structure.

- Write from the raw alone: fetch nothing from the web, and read nothing beyond the files named in Bootstrap.

- One piece, a single markdown file, saved as `output.md` beside this prompt file. The whole arrangement lives inside this one document: the centre, the folds, the depths — everything the rules above ask for is done with its headings alone, and that limits the depth you can fold to. If the depth is not enough, say explicitly where you are compromising to comply.

- Markdown convention: blank lines between list items.

- Do not commit anything to git.
