# What a model reads

The code says writing for people and building context for models are one problem. Read this and you will know where the evidence agrees, where the model's wall matches the person's, what helps a model and what does not, and how the harness has moved since the claims about it in the code and the material behind it were written. The grades are the agents'; nothing was checked again.

## 1. The same wall

A model hits the code's wall in the code's way: it must hold what matters and reach for the rest, and it gets worse not when its context fills but when its context fills with the wrong thing. What it needs held apart from the rest, its rules, is lost when the context is summarised, and a bigger window does not change either fact. Paper-only, replicated, in the paragraphs below.

A model's context degrades long before it fills, and what degrades it is composition, not length. The context-rot study by the vector-database company Chroma, across eighteen models, hid a fact (the needle) in a long body of text (the haystack) and found that a single distracting passage lowers performance, that degradation is faster when the needle resembles the question less, and, against intuition, that shuffled haystacks beat logically coherent ones in every model; on a memory benchmark a 300-token focused prompt beat the same content inside 113,000 tokens for every model. NoLiMa (ICML 2025): with literal word matching removed, eleven of thirteen models fall below half their short-context score at 32,000 tokens. Paper-only, replicated in spirit. The coherent-haystack result is the least cited and cuts against "give it a well-written document": a model is not helped by a long document being well organised. It is helped by being handed less.

Compaction silently deletes the rules. [Chen 2026], on governance decay, 1,323 episodes across seven model families: zero policy violations with the full context, about 30% after the context was summarised, 59% in the worst family, and an attack that biases the summariser into dropping policies "defeats every evaluated model"; pinning the constraints outside the lossy summary restored zero. Paper-only, single author, deterministic benchmark. This is the sharpest case that compression by inference over text is unsafe, and that the fix is to mark some content as not compressible, which is a type.

A larger window does not dissolve this. One-million-token context went general in March 2026 and the "just paste it in" option gets cheaper each year, but the studies above measure degradation from structure, not from running out of room. Reasoned, by the context-engineering agent.

## 2. What helps a model

What helps a model is the navigational half of the code, and the evidence for it stops one level down. An index read before the content, a tool found rather than held, a heading chain carried with each piece, a pointer to the right file, a description that says what a tool does: each moved a measured outcome. A second level of pointers did not. Paper-only and shipped, in the paragraphs below.

Progressive disclosure (loading a name and description first and the body only on demand) works once. [He et al. 2026], the only controlled test of the pattern, across three harnesses and against a conventional retriever: as the corpus grows, one level of routing loses less than reading the corpus directly and eventually beats it; a second routing level gives no benefit and sometimes reduces accuracy. Paper-only. Addressability pays at depth one; depth two is speculative. The code's holarchy nests without limit for a person; for a model, the evidence so far stops one level down.

Making tools navigable rather than present is the largest measured win, and the famous number for it is a hypothetical. The mechanism, a model exploring a filesystem of capabilities on demand instead of holding every schema, is supported by two studies: one found 6.2 points more hit rate at one ninth of the tokens against dumping the full context, the other 52 to 57% token savings from representing tool schemas differently, with the gain saturating on strong models and small catalogues. The 98.7% figure is an illustration, [as the numbers brief says](numbers.md#3-the-newest-folklore-is-vendor-arithmetic). Claude Code, one of the two most-used coding harnesses, now defers tool schemas by default. Shipped for the pattern; paper-only for the size.

Carrying the heading chain with each piece helps retrieval. [Yang 2026], 1,600 queries over a production markdown knowledge base: splitting on headers, merging by meaning, and prefixing each piece with the hierarchy of headings above it, with no extra model calls, raised the rank of the right answer among the first five results (MRR@5) from 0.374 to 0.463 overall and 0.828 to 0.925 on answerable queries; in the comparison without prefixes, agreement between the two people judging relevance (kappa) collapsed from 0.45 to 0.04, because stripping the chain strips the disambiguation the judges needed too. Paper-only, single author. This is the crux for the code: what helps is carrying hierarchy into the piece, not splitting the document.

Presentation helps and partition does not. Choosing chunk boundaries by meaning rather than by sentence costs more than it gains, replicated three times through 2026, with sentence chunking matching it up to about 5,000 tokens; while re-presenting the same data about a scientific workflow's provenance as human-readable structured cards, instead of letting the model query the database schema, nearly doubled answer quality. Paper-only, both. Where the boundaries fall matters little; what each piece carries matters much.

Guidance helps by getting the agent to the right file, not by improving what it writes there. [Shepard and Albrecht 2026], on the standard benchmark of real repository issues (SWE-bench Verified): probe-and-refine tuning of repository guidance gave 33.0% resolved against 25.5% unguided, with file coverage up 14.5 points and patch quality flat. Paper-only. The Mintlify benchmark, 2,400 trials across twenty documentation sites and two models: plain markdown was worse than HTML because agents invented file paths, and a link to an index file cut dead URLs by about 90% with accuracy flat in the high nineties for every format; vendor-run, open-sourced, and its metric is 404s rather than a flattering accuracy claim. Shipped-niche. Machine-facing descriptions move success: augmenting poor tool descriptions in the protocol by which tools are offered to a model (MCP) gave 5.85 points more task success at 67% more steps, and standard-compliant descriptions raise selection among competing tools by the amount [the numbers brief](numbers.md#3-the-newest-folklore-is-vendor-arithmetic) corrects. Paper-only, experimental.

A routing file the agent reads before content is the pattern the field converged on, empirically. Skills folders, agent instruction files, resource lists: the knowledge layer flipped from retrieving chunks into a prompt to reading an index first. Shipped, widely. That is the code's contract, arrived at by the industry without the code.

## 3. What does not help a model

What does not help a model is prose written to explain, and a second channel of instruction typed over the first. The context file at the root of a repository, the summary of a file's meaning, the installable skill, and the label definitions moved into a schema each cost tokens and none moved success, on the only controlled measurements there are. Paper-only, in the paragraphs below.

Repository context files (a file such as `AGENTS.md` at the root of a repository, read by a coding agent before it starts) do not move success and cost a fifth more. [Gloaguen et al. 2026], from ETH Zürich, on SWE-bench tasks with generated context files and on fresh issues from repositories with developer-committed ones, across models and agents: context files "do not generally improve task success rates, while increasing inference cost by over 20% on average," and the most-recommended content, the repository overview, was unhelpful; agents followed the instructions. [Khatri 2026], 288 runs on two agents, bounds any effect at ten to fifteen points and finds failures are implementation skill, not missing repository knowledge; a manipulation probe found the real file "never converts a near-miss to a pass." Paper-only, two designs agreeing. Meanwhile the artifact is the most adopted convention in the field, sixty thousand projects by its foundation's count and 845,000 files by filename on GitHub, adopted on no measurement; one study of 124 pull requests found lower runtime and fewer output tokens with such files, which can be squared with the cost finding if the file adds input tokens and removes wasted output, and nobody has done the squaring.

Prose written for a model carries almost none of what the source carries. [Sam-Bodden 2026], pre-registered, with the right files given so that only the representation varied: natural-language summaries answered 4 of 45 behavioural questions where the source answered 27, and a frontier model's summaries scored exactly as poorly as a 3-billion-parameter model's, so the gap belongs to the representation; rendering a file's remainder as signatures resolved no more issues than deleting it, N of 70, p of 0.75; compressed context matched whole files at a third of the tokens. Paper-only.

A noise floor sits under every small effect in this brief. The same paper measured that temperature-zero inference flips about 9% of per-instance outcomes between byte-identical runs, which sits under every effect of a few points reported on the benchmark, this debrief's included. Measured.

Skills (a folder of instructions and files an agent loads for one kind of task) lower success and raise cost, and go stale in silence. WebDev-Skills-Bench (August 2026), 31 public skills across a thousand tasks and four models with length-matched controls: injecting the target skill reduced success within two tries (Pass@2) by 1.3 to 4.2%, raised tokens 72 to 394%, and helped in 17 to 36% of pairs; Repo2Skill-Evo: every one of 105 release transitions invalidated part of the skill set, and frontier agents repair 30 to 70% of it. Paper-only. Adoption is real, 53,000 specifications on one marketplace; efficacy is not established.

Typing the instructions creates a second channel that overrides the first. Placing label definitions in a schema underperformed the system prompt by 11 to 13 points; when prompt and schema conflicted, accuracy fell 5 to 45 points. Forcing a model's output to match a grammar as it generates (constrained decoding) degrades reasoning, worsening with strictness. Format sensitivity is a capability tax that shrinks: a weak model varied 40% on a code task by template alone, a strong one was robust; a compact structured format's accuracy edge over JSON is 0.8 points inside a 2.8-point interval, null. Paper-only. A study that passed a message through six models in turn found strong relays lossless in every format and an injected error persisting to the last hop in every format, and gave the one-line summary: "Structure buys a faithful, error-localizing channel — not an error-correcting code."

## 4. Where person and model agree

They converge on navigation and diverge on explanation. Both benefit from where things live, how to run tests, and which conventions cannot be inferred; only the person benefits from what the code means. Reasoned, by the docs-for-agents agent, from the studies in the two sections above.

The convergence depends on the model, and there is no shared measure of readability. Reasoning-tuned models show significant alignment with human difficulty patterns on obfuscated code; instruction-tuned and coder-tuned models show near-zero correlation. Token perplexity does not track human judgments of understandability. A code-health metric calibrated for human comprehension predicts semantic preservation under model refactoring across 5,000 files: human-friendly is model-friendly, on that evidence. Paper-only, all three.

The objection that structure reduces human agency is real and mislocated. It applies to structure as an authoring tax, where the person paying is not the person benefiting; it does not apply to structure as the decomposition of an opaque process into inspectable units. Decomposing a spreadsheet agent's execution into auditable typed actions improved users' comprehension of the task and their error detection, in a study of sixteen; and protocols that make an agent write down what it is doing improve safety without costing performance, with the gain growing as the monitor gets stronger. Paper-only, small. Structure costs agency when imposed at authoring time and buys it when offered at decision time.

## 5. How the harness moved

The harness moved in the year before the sweep, and the movement splits the claims about it. The material behind the code said there was no branch to take and nowhere to step back to; that is false in the two most-used harnesses. The code says a context runs through a closed function over a linear transcript, that the state it stood on is never versioned, and that no answer can be re-derived; the first and third stand, and the second is now partly false, since memory and files are versioned and only the transcript is not. The protocol got more typed as it got stateless, and memory became files with versions. Shipped and shipped-absent, in the sections below.

### 5.1 The protocol got more typed

The protocol shed its conversation and hardened its types. MCP's revision of 2026-07-28 removed protocol-level sessions and the initial handshake and became stateless; at the same time discovery became mandatory, every result carries a required type, every list and read carries a required time-to-live and cache scope, and tool schemas were widened to the full JSON Schema keyword set. Shipped, with the main SDKs near half a billion downloads a month. The protocol moved down a layer and got more typed on the way; its resources, read-only and addressable, are the primitive the plan describes, and the ecosystem converged on tools-only servers instead.

### 5.2 Branching shipped

Branching and stepping back shipped in the two most-used coding harnesses. Claude Code checkpoints every prompt, keeps a hundred file snapshots, and offers branch, fork and rewind; Codex has fork and an ephemeral side thread, and a fork shares its parent's prompt cache, so branching is cheaper than re-running. Shipped. Compaction in Claude Code is no longer pure inference over text: a published table governs what is re-injected from disk, the project file, memory, the plan, path-scoped rules, skill bodies under a token cap, recently modified files, and only the conversational middle is summarised. Shipped.

### 5.3 What remains absent

What remains absent is a query surface, shared structure and reproducibility. Nothing lets a session ask what is in its context or address a past state by identity; a branch is a copy of a transcript, not a shared store, and the on-disk format is documented as internal and changing; no mainstream product can say "this completion came from exactly this context, re-derivable," and where replay exists in papers it is deterministic only because the model's responses were recorded. Shipped-absent. An August 2026 paper on what it calls execution edits states the unsolved problem: existing systems fork "without deriving what each edit must preserve," so an unsafe edit can run a tool twice, discard a result the task still needs, or conflict with a call that began before the edit. Nobody has safe branching in production. Reproducibility is being traded away for context economy, on purpose: server-side compaction and background consolidation make the recorded context non-reconstructible from the transcript.

### 5.4 Memory became files with versions

Memory was commoditised from above, as files with versions. Anthropic shipped managed agents in April 2026, memory stores three weeks later, background consolidation in May, and a versioned memory API in July: a store is a workspace-scoped collection of documents addressed by path, every change creates an immutable version attributed to the session, versions survive the memory's deletion, and access is enforced at the filesystem. Google's memory bank shipped revisions and access conditions. Shipped. The labs bet on files, not graphs, and the vendors that had bet on graphs moved within six weeks of each other into the one thing a lab will not build, memory shared across harnesses.

The memory benchmarks collapsed as evidence, and poisoning is the unsolved hole. A graph system's leaderboard lead disappears when the amount retrieved per query is controlled; lexical search over the raw log matches structured memory; serving cost was never measured. 1.2% poisoned entries drop accuracy from 0.85 to 0.30, a write-time screen rejected none of 360 poisoned memories, and provenance-weighted retrieval is indistinguishable from no defence. Paper-only. A writable store plus prompt injection is a durable attack surface that later sessions read as trusted.

### 5.5 The crawler file publishes to nobody

The file `llms.txt`, proposed as an index a site publishes for crawlers, publishes to nobody. Across 900 monitored domains over seven months, 1,227 requests for the file and zero from any frontier crawler; Google states it has no effect; adoption is 10% of 300,000 domains with no link to citation. Shipped-niche, refuted as a retrieval channel. It is alive only as the index an agent fetches on demand, the use measured in [the second section](#2-what-helps-a-model). Agents are now most of documentation traffic on one platform, 66% in July 2026 with 84% using explicit machine routes, self-reported and unaudited.

## 7. What could not be established

The agents could not establish any measure of MCP resource adoption; whether typed context beats prose on frontier models, the only controlled studies being on older models and showing the effect shrinking; any independent replication of a vendor context-management number; funding or production-deployment counts for any memory vendor; or whether background consolidation improves downstream accuracy, since neither lab publishes a number.
