# What a screen driven from data costs

The code's fifth section says what draws a screen should declare what it takes and gives in the same medium as the data, and the plan's sketch goes further: which component can draw a thing would be a query over those declarations. Read this and you will know what happened to everyone who tried: the note-taking tools that typed their objects, the editors that stored code as structure, five companies that drove their interfaces from the server, the form libraries that render from a schema, the low-code market, and the protocol that just won for agent interfaces. The pattern matches [the medium's](medium.md), and it is more specific about what a contract must carry: a capability the client can name, a version stamped on every declaration, and an escape hatch that is a tier rather than a shame. The grades are the agents'; nothing was checked again.

## 1. Files won, for a reason nobody predicted

Plain files became a moat because agents can read them, the venture-funded middle of the note-taking market left for meetings and agents, the nearest running frame for the plan is a research lab's plugin runtime, and the editors that stored code as structure did not survive the model. Shipped, stated and prototype, in the sections below.

### 1.1 Files won

Plain files became a moat because agents can read them. Obsidian added a database view over its markdown files in May 2025, with all data staying in the files, went free for commercial use, ships releases every few weeks, and offers a command-line interface as its agent surface with no assistant in the app. Logseq, whose differentiator was plain text plus an outliner, spent four years rewriting onto SQLite, with nodes unifying pages and blocks, typed properties and tags as classes, and shipped its 2.0 in July 2026 after a thirteen-month release hole, into a market that had just re-valued the thing it gave up. Shipped, both, from the changelogs; that the community read the move as too late, with long-time users leaving because agents could no longer read the files, is the opinion of one launch thread. This is the single most adverse fact in the sweep for a "structure not files" thesis, and the lesson is not that structure is wrong but that if the structure is not files it must project to files as a first-class two-way surface. Reasoned, by the tools-for-thought agent.

### 1.2 The middle evacuated

The venture-funded middle evacuated note-taking for meetings and agents. Tana split into a maintenance outliner and a new agentic meeting product; Notion killed its mail client for "agent-based email workflows"; Mem is "your AI chief of staff"; Coda is "Superhuman Docs"; Kosmik sunset in May 2026 and Dendron is dead. What remains as a pure tool for thought is bootstrapped and small, and every one of them shipped an MCP server, so the AI layer is being externalised rather than built in. Shipped, from the changelogs. No empirical study establishes that linked note-taking improves any outcome.

### 1.3 The nearest running frame is Patchwork

The nearest running frame for the plan is Patchwork. The research lab Ink & Switch's malleable-software runtime defines tools as plugins declaring a type, an id, the datatypes they support, and a load function; rendering is a contract of an element and a cleanup; composition is by embedding a view with a document and a tool id; documents can pin the tool that renders them. A skill file teaches coding agents to write Patchwork tools, so a model is the on-ramp to end-user programming. Research prototype, unusually mature, with no known user outside the lab. It encodes hard-won constraints: lazy loading, a stable public id, light DOM, mandatory cleanup.

The lab's other lessons are three. Do not standardise the contract before you have users of it: the Block Protocol, an open standard for typed data-driven blocks, is paused at version 0.4. Branching, not merging, is the durable win: every local-first system converges on branches while the ones that bought adoption gave up merging. And composability beats generation: "coding agents without a composable architecture give you zero-to-one silo apps." Stated by the lab and its alumni.

### 1.4 The structured editors did not survive the model

The editors that stored code as structure did not survive the model. Dark Inc ran out of money in June 2025, and its founder's post-mortem names the cause: "our online structured editor didn't make sense when the LLM is generating the code, and it's a separate place to how people are coding using LLMs and agents"; the editor had been half the codebase and "too fancy for our own good." Stated, first-hand. Unison, which stores code in a database, shipped its 1.0 after eight years and in February 2026 opened a consulting group to reach cash-flow positive; Subtext's author calls twenty years of work "a series of overambitious failed experiments." Stated. Hazel is the one alive, vigorously, on typed holes, unfinished places in a program whose expected type is known and can be given to a model. Measured from repositories. Three independent ventures converged on consulting or insolvency within a year, and the one explicit commercial post-mortem names the structured editor as the thing the model invalidated.

## 2. Driving the screen from the server ends in negotiation

Five companies hit the same wall and invented the same fix, and it is not a query. Airbnb, Plaid, DoorDash, Lyft and Shopify each converged on capability negotiation: the client tells the server which component types it can render and the server refuses to send anything else. Nobody solved shipping a new component to an old client; Plaid's spec says adding a new pane kind is a major version "which require[s] a client SDK upgrade," so the promised benefit of shipping interface without a release holds only within the vocabulary already shipped, plus indefinite maintenance of a frozen graph per stranded client generation. Shipped and adopted, all five. Dispatch everywhere is a pinned key, a section type, a facet id, a one-of, never a bid over the data; a renderer registry is universal and a renderer query is shipped nowhere at scale. Reasoned, by the agent, from the five.

The escape hatch is a named architectural tier, and the boundary erodes. Lyft's semantic components exist "as an escape hatch from server-driven land," because it is hard to model animation and fully client-side interaction from the server, and "the dividing line between semantic and declarative can be hazy." DoorDash has an untyped custom struct, and "one pitfall we kept running into was gating the usage of custom"; the hatch shrank when they matched styling types to the design system's typed vocabulary. Stated by the teams. Two shipped systems reached opposite conclusions on fallbacks: DoorDash built generic fallback views per category and "have not found this to be useful at all," since a new component is usually a new design; Shopify renders a client-side default layout for an unknown section type. The reconciling read is that a fallback serves a variation and fails a genuinely new component, where omission beats degradation. Reasoned, by the agent.

Uber's Screenflow came closest to the plan and is a shutdown. A restricted subset of TypeScript with components as the only top-level definitions, compiled to an intermediate form, with definition files carrying types and default values so native clients could generate matching protocols. Its versioning answer is the correct one and it costs a compiler, a monorepo and a build matrix: every screen specified a version, and continuous integration recompiled every changed screen against every previously shipped runtime, because "the older runtime could fetch a recently updated screen" and a call to a non-existent API would crash. After about two years, at least sixteen flows in production; the department closed in July 2020. Stated, first-hand account of the abandonment.

Two traps from the same reports are silent. A serialiser that rejects any value it was not compiled to know, fed a component vocabulary that grows, crashes on the first new value, which is why DoorDash left SwiftProtobuf; and recursive component trees are "close to impossible" to fetch through a query language that selects fields, which is why Airbnb kept its sections flat. Stated. The strongest existing implementation of a mount as a call the host resolves is Shopify's Remote DOM, a controller mapping remote components to native ones across a message channel with hosts in JavaScript, Kotlin and Swift, and it is alive. Shipped.

## 3. Rendering from a schema runs out at the last fifth

Rendering a screen from a schema runs out at the last fifth, by the admission of the people who maintain the libraries that do it. The one place a component's contract, what it accepts and what may sit inside it, can be queried at runtime is a content editor, not a component framework; the dominant framework removed its runtime contract; and every product that lets users define their own types keeps the set of views closed. Stated and measured, in the paragraphs below.

The maintainer of the most sophisticated schema-driven form library wrote down, in 2026, that the abstraction leaks. JSON Forms resolves a renderer by taking the highest-ranked tester over the schema node and its UI node, with a negative rank meaning not applicable; it is the closest thing in the form world to capability negotiation. Its maintainer's issue of May 2026: "logic leaks into the 'UI' layer," "customization is coarse-grained," changing a label computation means overriding every renderer, and the proposed next version routes testers through a presentation model instead of the schema. Stated, shipped-niche. Its larger rival, react-jsonschema-form, looks widgets up in a hardcoded two-level table by type and name and throws on an unknown combination; its most-requested change, collapsing the schema and UI schema into one document, has been open since 2017. Shipped and adopted. One library's answer to the last fifth is pass a component, the other's is write a tester and outrank us, and both are admissions that the declarative layer ran out.

A component's accepted-types contract is queryable at runtime in exactly one mainstream system, and it is a content editor. WordPress's block registry declares attributes with types, and containment contracts, parent, ancestor and allowed blocks, in a file; registers them into a runtime store; and exposes "returns an array of block types that the set of blocks received as argument can be transformed into" and a ranked predicate search returning "the highest-priority transform where the predicate function returns a truthy value." Shipped at scale. Two page builders let a component express, as a query, which children it accepts or which components may fill a slot. Shipped-niche. The dominant component model moved the other way: React 19 removed prop types and silently ignores them, so its contracts exist only in types erased at build; and the one format that could have become the cross-ecosystem answer, the custom elements manifest, has been frozen since May 2024 and is read only by documentation viewers. Measured from the specifications and changelogs.

Every product that lets users define types closes the set of views, and the closure is one level deeper than views. Notion's new views API discriminates configuration by a type of exactly ten values; Airtable's field types are a 33-member enum and its view types six; Tana's supertag carries fields and a content template and builds a title from an expression over fields, the closest shipped thing to the plan's template over a call's arguments, computing a string rather than a subtree; Anytype's protocol has relation formats as a closed enum of fifteen, object layouts of 28, and view types of six, while templates, types and relations genuinely are objects in the same store. Shipped, read from the protocols. No one lets a third party register a renderer that bids to render a type, and no one lets you define a new property type either; extensibility stops at instances of a fixed vocabulary in both dimensions. View configuration everywhere is a union keyed by a closed tag, which is precisely the shape a declaration-and-call model replaces. Reasoned, by the agent.

## 4. The protocol that won is opaque

Interactive interfaces for agents consolidated inside MCP, and the unit is a sandboxed iframe with no props schema. The MCP Apps extension reached stable in January 2026 and absorbed both prior contenders, OpenAI's Apps SDK and MCP-UI, by their authors' own account; a server declares a resource at a `ui://` address with an HTML profile, a tool links to it, the host renders it in a mandatory sandbox and talks to it over JSON-RPC, and security is declarative by content-security policy rather than by type. Twelve million downloads a month, eleven hosts including ChatGPT, Claude, VS Code and Microsoft 365. Shipped and adopted. The typed component tree lost at the protocol layer and is thriving one layer up, where OpenUI Lang parses a compact streaming language into a typed element tree validated against a schema generated from your own component library, at 414,000 downloads a month six months after its first publish. Shipped-niche, steep ramp, one vendor's governance. The split is stable because the two solve different problems: the iframe gives cross-host portability without design agreement, the typed tree gives design-system fidelity but needs host and server to share a vocabulary.

The implication runs the plan's way. A project whose interface is already typed data can emit into an iframe bundle or into a host's own components without rewriting; the commoditised asset is the hand-written widget, the durable one is the typed model plus a small renderer per target. Reasoned, by two agents independently. Ship the typed layer and treat the winning protocol as one adapter.

## 5. What the low-code market says about derivation

The market bifurcated, with one real casualty class. Airtable's mark fell from 11.7 billion dollars to about four on secondaries and it sold for 1.28 billion in cash on about 480 million of recurring revenue; Pegasystems told the SEC that "unprecedented changes in the AI market caused clients to delay their purchasing decisions"; Appian grew 19% and deleted "low-code" from its own description. Lovable, the largest of the tools that generate an application from a prompt, went from 100 million to 500 million in annualised revenue in eleven months, company-claimed and unaudited. Measured from filings and reporting, with the revenue claims stated. What is dying is the proprietary visual runtime sold to prosumers and agencies; what is fine is governed process automation over regulated data; and the buyers' stall is paralysis, not substitution. Reasoned, by the agent.

Retool's reversal is the datum that matters. Retool, the incumbent builder of internal tools, abandoned its proprietary visual abstraction in June 2026 for React and TypeScript, saying the abstraction "prevented LLMs from working fluently," and repositioned as the governance layer that imports apps built elsewhere and wraps them in permissions and audit; its own survey of 307 executives found 93% concerned about generated tools in production and 8% with strong central governance. Shipped, vendor-sponsored survey. Read precisely, it is not a verdict against derivation but against closed, non-inspectable derivation: every survivor's remaining moat is typed contracts, data access rules, permission inheritance and schema, and every AI-native winner's admitted weakness is maintenance. Deriving an interface from typed contracts is a winning bet in 2026 only if the derivation emits inspectable artefacts in a standard language rather than a proprietary interpreted format; betting on a closed declarative runtime is betting on the thing Retool just spent a rewrite escaping. Reasoned, by the agent.

The open-source internal-tool builders are alive and repositioning around agents, and the claim that they are fine until the first interaction beyond create, read, update and delete and then drop to custom code is a widely repeated practitioner claim the agent did not verify against a citable source. Measured for the activity; unverified for the ceiling.
