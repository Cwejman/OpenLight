This is the single hardest piece of evidence that AI is denting low-code demand. ([SEC 8-K Ex-99.1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1013857/000101385726000049/q22026_ex-991.htm))

### Pivots

- **Retool abandoned its proprietary low-code abstraction.** On 17 June 2026 it relaunched on **React + TypeScript**, stating that generating code "is now the fastest way to build custom software" and that its old abstractions were a liability that "prevented LLMs from working fluently." It now *imports* apps vibe-coded in Replit, Lovable and v0 and wraps them in permissions/audit — repositioning as the governance and deployment layer rather than the builder. *Shipped.* ([Retool, 17 Jun 2026](https://retool.com/blog/retool-launches-react-ai-app-builder)) Supporting posture: "Enterprise AppGen: the future of low-code" (25 Feb 2026), "vibe coding risks" (3 Mar 2026), and Retool-vs-Claude-Code (31 Mar 2026): *"You think you're 80% done because the thing works on your machine. In reality, you're about 20% done."*
- **Webflow** bought AI content startup **Vidoso** (4 people, $3.7M raised) on 12 Mar 2026, after Intellimize (2024); CEO Linda Tong now frames Webflow as "an **agentic marketing platform**," not a website builder. ([TechCrunch, 12 Mar 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/12/webflow-buys-ai-content-generation-platform-vidoso-to-bolster-its-marketing-suite/))
- **OutSystems**: every 2026 press release is agentic — "Open Agentic Systems Platform" (2 Jun 2026), "Agentic Systems Engineering" with Mentor + Enterprise Context Graph (31 Mar 2026), AWS agentic partnership. It still brands itself "Powering AI Development Innovation with Low-Code." Tellingly, 2026 releases cite **G2** rankings, not Gartner.
- **Mendix** (Siemens): blog output is entirely agentic orchestration + industrial AI, folded into Siemens' manufacturing stack.
- **Glide** rebranded to **GlideOS**, published "AI engineering is the next wave after no-code" (28 Jul 2026), and added MCP so ChatGPT/Claude/Cursor drive Glide.
- **Bubble** shipped an AI Agent (16 Oct 2025), Claude Sonnet 4.6 on launch day (17 Feb 2026), agent-built native mobile (4 Aug 2026).

### Counter-evidence: the market relocated

- **Lovable**: $100M ARR (Jul 2025) → $200M (Nov 2025) → $400M (Feb 2026) → **$500M (Jun 2026)**, ~146 employees, 1M new projects/week; **$400M raise at $13.3B, 12 Aug 2026**.
- **Replit**: $150M ARR at $3B (Sep 2025) → **$9B valuation (11 Mar 2026)**; Visa investment (28 May 2026).
- **Base44 under Wix**: Q2 2026 revenue $563.1M (+15%), ARR $1.963B; Base44 gross margin going from **near-zero to ~60%** in 2H after Wix shipped its own LLM ("Base 1"). Wix's own filing calls Base44 "**the no-code application platform**."

Sum: Lovable + Replit alone are ~$650M+ of ARR that did not exist in 2024 — roughly Airtable's entire ARR, created in 24 months.

## Verdict

"AI codegen is killing low-code" is **too strong; bifurcation fits the evidence better, with one real casualty class.** What is dying is the *proprietary visual runtime for generic app-building sold to prosumers and agencies* — Airtable's mark down 65%+ and sold for 2.7× ARR, Wix's agency channel slowdown, Bubble's defensive reorganisation. What is fine is *governed process automation over regulated enterprise data*: Appian +19%, Pega Cloud ACV +22%. Pega's ACV stall shows AI is causing **purchase-decision paralysis**, not substitution — buyers are waiting, not leaving.

The structurally important datum for a derived-UI bet is Retool's June 2026 reversal. Retool concluded the *abstraction* was the liability — because LLMs can't manipulate it — and moved to React/TypeScript while keeping the schema, permissions and connection layer as the durable moat. Read carefully, that is not a verdict against derivation; it is a verdict against **closed, non-inspectable** derivation. Every survivor's remaining defensibility is precisely typed contracts: data access rules, permission inheritance, governance. Every AI-native winner's admitted weakness is maintenance (TechCrunch's Lovable piece, 9 Jun 2026, flags exactly this) and unit economics.

So: **deriving UI from typed data contracts is a winning bet in 2026 — provided the derivation emits inspectable, standard-language artefacts rather than a proprietary interpreted format.** The contract is the asset; the renderer must not be a walled garden. Betting on a closed declarative runtime is betting on the one thing Retool just spent a platform rewrite escaping.
