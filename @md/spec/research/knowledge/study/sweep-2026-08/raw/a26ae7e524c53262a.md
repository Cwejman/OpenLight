## 1. Diátaxis, at full strength and its critics

**Its claim, in its own words.** Not "four boxes" — two axes that it says *define* the territory. From `diataxis.fr/foundations/` (retrieved 2026-08-26): action/cognition (knowing how vs. knowing that) crossed with acquisition/application (at study vs. at work). Four modes follow, each with its own *voice*: reference states, explanation discusses, how-to guides, tutorial teaches. The site cites **no research, no empirical study, and no other theory** — the argument is entirely self-contained. **Grade: opinion.**

Its best practitioner testimony is about voice, not filing. On HN 2026-08-01 (`news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49138188`), a team lead handing a large legacy codebase to a client: *"It was so clear what you were saying and what 'voice' you were writing in… It made it so much easier to be coherent and clear about everything."* Its fairest self-description of value is diagnostic: Diátaxis *"certainly helps reveal the problem… It brings it into focus and demands that it be addressed."*

**Critically for your weighing: Diátaxis does not actually argue for splitting into typed files.** Verbatim, from the page discussed below: *"Diátaxis is not a scheme into which documentation must be placed - four boxes… this does not mean that there must be simply four divisions of documentation in the hierarchy."* And: *"This will tend towards a clear, explicit, structural division into the four categories - but that is a typical outcome of the good practice, not its end."* The file-per-type reading is the adopters' invention. If you were treating Diátaxis as the strongest outside argument for splitting, it is weaker than advertised — its author disclaims the mandate.

**Failure reports are documented, not merely theorised — and the best one is exactly your question.** Kayce Basques (Google, Chrome/Pigweed technical writer), `news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42340740`, 2024-12-06: *"We tried adopting Diataxis on pigweed.dev as a literal blueprint and it resulted in too much fragmentation. E.g. the explanations for a Pigweed module were on one page, but the tutorial was on another. Users and teammates found it annoying to have to jump back-and-forth so much."* The mechanism matters more than the verdict: *"If you only link to explanations from the tutorial, some (most?) users won't click those links, and therefore may never get exposed to the theoretical foundations."* They retreated to **sections within a page** rather than page-per-type. The original mandate is on record as SEED-0102, "Consistent Module Documentation," `pigweed.dev/seed/0102.html`, 2023-02-10. **Grade: shipped, named organisation, no metrics reported.**

Basques's second criticism is unanswered: *"How do homepages and READMEs fit into the map?… I'm not convinced that you can boil down index pages to just a mix of explanations, tutorials, references, and guides."* For a knowledge base that must land a bounded reader, the entry point is load-bearing and Diátaxis does not model it.

Two smaller documented failure modes: a navigation tax — *"it tends to turn 1-click docs into 2-click docs"* (HN 2026-08-01, on burying API reference under a "Reference" section) — and enforcement drift, a 20-year teacher reporting docs rejected at a sprint for "mixing modalities" (HN 2024-12-05).

**Has it moved since 2024? Only by subtraction — and this is the finding.** In August 2026 Procida **deleted `diataxis.fr/complex-hierarchies/`**, the only page addressing documentation with more than one organising axis. It 404s today; archived at `web.archive.org/web/20260625055713/https://diataxis.fr/complex-hierarchies/`. His reason, HN 2026-08-02: *"Ugh, I don't like that page and I have actually deleted it… There is a real problem there, and that page doesn't do a good enough job of dealing with it. I have something cooking that is much, much better."* **Nothing has replaced it** — I checked the live navigation on 2026-08-26. The deleted page's own answer to type-vs-audience collision had been *"Let documentation be complex if necessary"* — a permission, not a method. Otherwise 2026 commits are typos, translations, and an Atom feed. Repo issue #170, "Agentic skill" (2026-08-15), is open and unanswered.

**Adoption is real but unverifiable.** Procida's figure is *"several hundred projects"*; the adoption page was **removed in 2023** because he could not keep up (repo issue #117). The only enumerable list is Divio's ~25-entry, self-nominated, explicitly "incomplete" page, including four unverifiable "(internal)" claims — Bosch, Ericsson, Tesla, Zalando. Largest genuine sustained adoption is Canonical (`ubuntu.com/blog/diataxis-a-new-foundation-for-canonical-documentation`, 2021-12-15; now Ubuntu Server, MAAS, Multipass, Juju, Charmed K8s/Kubeflow/MLflow). **Five years on, `canonical.com/documentation` reports no metrics of any kind.** Sequin's "We fixed our documentation with the Diátaxis framework" (2024-12-06) reports **zero numbers**. **Grade: widely-adopted as practice; opinion as assessment.** OpenAlex (250M+ works) returns Byzantine liturgical manuscripts for "Diataxis" — no scholarly footprint. And `diataxis.fr/quality/` explicitly rejects measuring its central claim: deep quality *"can only be enquired into, interrogated."*

**Divio.** `docs.divio.com/documentation-system/` is still live, still headed *"The Grand Unified Theory of Documentation – David Laing,"* still fronting Procida's PyCon Australia 2017 talk. It is diagram-first and prescriptive; Diátaxis is prose-first and axis-derived. Procida on the difference (HN 2026-08-01): *"Same fundamental ideas, but I got a lot of things wrong in that earlier version."* Several practitioners still prefer Divio: *"when it comes to diagram, I still refer to the Divio version as Diataxis version description is too abstract."* Note also that the taxonomy predates both: Django's docs got Tutorials/Topic guides/Reference/How-to in commit "Massive reorganization of the docs," **2008-08-23**; Kaplan-Moss named three of them in prose at `jacobian.org/2009/nov/10/what-to-write/`, 2009-11-10.

## 2. The 2025–2026 AI turn

**Tooling changed; authoring did not.** I found no survey, and no organisation reporting that it restructured documentation for LLMs and measured an outcome.

**The retrieval finding, now verified directly (arxiv.org/abs/2608.00824, submitted 2026-08-01).** Yang Yang, *"Structure-Aware Semantic Chunking with Title-Chain Prefixes: A 1600-Query Evaluation and the Measurement Trap in Text-Transform Ablations,"* cs.SE, 8 pages, replication package doi 10.5281/zenodo.21744653. Verbatim: header-split + semantic merge + title-chain prefixing, *"zero additional LLM calls: the title chain reuses the document's own header hierarchy instead of a generated summary. On a 1600-query stratified evaluation over a production Markdown knowledge base, the pipeline improves MRR@5 from 0.374 to 0.463 (+23.8%) on the full set and from 0.828 to 0.925 (+11.7%) on the answerable subset (n=563), with dual-annotator Cohen's kappa 0.45."* Its methodological sting: in a prefix on/off ablation *"dual-annotator agreement collapsed from kappa 0.45 to 0.04 under identical prompts — stripping the title-chain context strips the disambiguation signal annotators need to agree on relevance."* **Grade: paper-only**, single author, one production KB, not peer-reviewed. **This is the crux evidence for your question and it favours carrying hierarchy *into* the chunk, not splitting the document.**

Adjacent, also verified: `arxiv.org/abs/2608.11022` (2026-08-11, accepted eScience2026) — the same provenance data re-presented as structured cards *"nearly double[s] answer quality compared with schema-based querying."* Presentation, not partition.

**Docs linting:** Vale's only 2025–26 release is v3.18.0 (2025-08-20), no LLM features. No study links linting to any outcome. **Grade: shipped-niche; opinion as assessment.** A WTD Portland 2026 talk on an AI doc auditor reports the shape: *"great at syntax errors, terrible at 'is this explanation clear?'"*

**The conversation moved past content types.** Write the Docs Portland 2026 (May 3–5) had **no Diátaxis talk**; the IA talks were about *user progress* stages and *decision support under time pressure*. Meanwhile `7act.org` — Ferri-Benedetti's Seven-Action Model, a needs-based rival to Diátaxis — went agent-native (every page as HTML + `index.md` + `model.json` + `llms.txt`, plus `AGENTS.md`), while Diátaxis added an RSS feed. Its author's own honesty: *"this one isn't backed by extensive research or factorial analysis."*

## 3. DITA / topic-based authoring

Calcifying, not dying: DITA 2.0 has been in beta since 2021-06-14 and Beta 03 (2026-07-02) closes a 4.7-year gap, with the DITA-2.0 branch effectively a one-person project (`github.com/oasis-tcs/dita/releases`, retrieved 2026-08-26). The toolchain is alive — DITA-OT 4.4.1 shipped 2026-08-21 — but the *standard* is not advancing, and I found no peer-reviewed evidence for its core reuse/conditional-profiling ROI claims in OpenAlex, Crossref or arXiv. AI has not revived it: the 2026 retrieval evidence above rewards Markdown heading hierarchy, which is free, over XML semantics, which is not.
