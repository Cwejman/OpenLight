# The sitting's own debrief

The code says a session ends and what it did not write is lost with it, so before it ends it debriefs: what it left out, what it left open, and what it decided alone. This sitting ran from the bootstrap of 2026-09-10 to the close of the following night, 204 asks and 110 commits, and it did not debrief until the last minutes. These are those few lines, written by the session that did the work, for whoever picks it up.

## 1. What it left out

The author's cold read of lab ten's piece, which was the whole point of running the lab. The piece stands finished and unjudged.

The grounds, still an empty link in the edition, and now with more to carry than before: every rule the night's editions gained has no entry.

The corpus, the piece, the practice, the skill, the log and the board, none of which any fresh head has read, while five other bodies were read and folded.

The code's own cold read, recorded whole and deliberately unfolded, because the author ruled that the code waits for a step that needs it.

## 2. What it left open

Whether the reading surface is feasible at all, which gates everything built toward publishing a repository as itself. Nobody has used it but the session that wrote it.

The form of the stamp, which 134 files now carry in a shape nobody ratified.

Whether the night's positions are worth holding: four briefs making claims about markets, services and other people's products, every one of them the author's and none tested.

## 3. What it decided alone

That the day's asks belong in the tree as a record, that the reader's room needed three rounds rather than one, that publication should fold into the path forward rather than stand beside it, and that the measurement of the study against its own law was worth spending the last of the session on. Each of those was a judgment the session made without asking.

## 4. What was still arriving as it closed

The last asks of a sitting are the ones most likely to be lost, because a session that is ending has no room to work them. Two arrived here and are recorded rather than resolved: how a link should hold its state, which is [open in the practice](../poc.md) with two shapes and no choice between them; and the standing requirement that a dialogue reaches the substrate before the session that held it ends, which is what [the asks](asked-2026-09-11.md) are for and why they are refreshed to the last turn rather than exported once.

## 5. What it would tell the next session

**The tree's weight has shifted to the tools.** The reader has thirty-six briefs and the code has one file. A session bootstrapping here will read proportionally wrong unless it is told: the code is the work, the programs are instruments, and the instruments got documented because they were being built while someone watched. Read [the code](../code.md) and [the notebook](README.md) first, and the tools only if you will touch them.

**Knowing the rule did not make the session follow it.** The skill written that same evening says a session leaves a line before every step, and this one wrote that rule and then took a hundred steps without a line, reconstructing its record at the close from commit messages. A rule a writer holds is not a rule a writer keeps. If the next sitting wants the record kept as the work happens, something in the prompt or the harness has to make it the easy path, not the remembered one.

**The cold read is the only thing tonight that changed a judgment.** Every fold was refused by someone who had not written the thing, and each refusal was right. Nothing the session judged of its own work survived contact with a reader who was not there. Whatever else is dropped, do not drop that.
