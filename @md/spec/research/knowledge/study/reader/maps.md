# How it maps

A reader who can see only the room they are in is reading a file again. So beside [the spreads](reads.md) stand six figures, each drawing the same whole at a different distance, so that where you stand is never the only thing you can see. Each can be closed when the reading wants the space, and what is closed is remembered.

*Seen. That there are too many of them for the room they have, and that each asks for space the reading wants, is the second fault named in [the path forward](forward.md#1-use-before-anything-more).*

## 1. The gauge

What stopping at each level costs: the prose owned at each depth beneath where you stand, and the running total.

It is the code's promise made checkable, since if the biggest understanding really does come first, the top is short and each level below it heavier. On the piece this was built against it shows that promise failing: the piece stops being short at its second level, where seventy-two rooms carry half its words. It was the first figure built and it is the one the program is for. *Measured, on that one piece.*

## 2. The descent

Where [the rail](reads.md) names the descent in a single line, this gives it a row a step: the name of each room you passed through, what it holds in its own prose against what waits beneath it, and the rooms you did not enter as weighted marks beside the one you did. Rooms you have been in before are marked among them, so the way down and the way you came read in one figure. *Seen.*

## 3. The radial

A radial drawing of the room you stand in: each arc a room, its angle the text beneath it, its shade the depth, its **hue** the region it belongs to. A region is one of the branches directly beneath the root, and six hues are assigned to them in fixed order and never cycled; a seventh region takes no hue rather than repeating one. *Measured by a palette validator, which passed the six on lightness, chroma, contrast and colour-blind separation in both themes, with one pair in the band that is legal only where something else also distinguishes them, which here is the name and weight written into every arc. Not seen by any colour-blind reader.*

An arc says its name whole, or only its weight, or nothing, because a cut-off word is worse than silence.

The hub carries four small things: what is being read here against what waits in the rooms; that room's own paragraphs as its texture; a ring that fills as the prose is read; and a needle that swings to whatever the pointer is on. Standing on a leaf the figure roots at the room you are in rather than vanishing.

Links are drawn as chords across it, so a room's relations show as well as its place, though a chord says only that a relation exists and not what it is. *Seen; and short of what was asked, which is in [the path forward](forward.md#3-the-figures).*

## 4. The division

**The division** draws the whole as the holarchy divides it: each room a tile of its parent, weighted by what it holds, its own prose a darker band inside it, labelled where the tile is large enough. Division is what a holarchy is, so the picture and the thing agree, and a tile is something a pointer can find where a line is not.

It divides only while a tile can still be seen. Below that **floor** it stops and marks itself as holding more, and the room it could not draw is reached by pointing instead, which [how it marks](marks.md#1-the-hover-and-the-card) describes. *Reasoned: below the floor a tile is too small to see, so the only honest thing left to do with that depth is hand it to the pointer.*

## 5. The files

The filesystem alone, indented, opening and closing, each file carrying its region's hue and its weight. The abstract division and the explicit tree stand beside each other rather than one instead of the other, because a reader who knows a repository wants to see it as it is. *Preferred; it was asked for after the division alone proved not to give it.*

## 6. The page

A strip lying along the page you are reading, position running left to right and the band's height saying what each part is, with the viewport a window along it. Click anywhere on it to go there. *Seen.*
