# How it marks

A reader who meets the same room in the reading, in the gauge and in the drawing should meet one thing, not three. So everything that names a room carries the same identity, and pointing at any of them lights all of them at once, everywhere, with no exception. A view where the lighting does not follow is a view that has left the reading.

*Seen, and incomplete: not every hover syncs today.*

## 1. The hover, and the card

A pointer on anything that names a room lights that room in every view, swings the drawing's needle to it, and opens a card: its face, what it holds here and in its rooms, its paragraphs and how many run heavy, what it points at and what points at it, and whether it has been read or changed since.

The card is summoned rather than placed, so it is glass rather than a box: the ground shows through it, blurred, its edges dissolved, with no shadow and no drawn border. A click pins it, and pinned it can be acted in, offering its rooms, what points at it, the way in and the step out. *Seen.*

Where a room lies deeper than [the division's floor](draws.md#4-division-where-a-picture-must-agree-with-the-thing) it has no tile of its own, so the room that holds it is marked instead. The lighting always resolves to something a reader can see. *Reasoned, and built after the floor left deep rooms unanswerable.*

## 2. What has been read

What a reader has seen is kept in their browser, so the drawing dims as it is read and lights warm where a room changed since it was last seen. A bar says how much of the whole stands read, changed and unread.

A room is named for the file it lives in and the words of its heading rather than its number, so renumbering a document, which the practice's numbered headings make common, does not make everything look unread.

What is owed beyond this is subscription. Read and unread in one browser is memory, and it tells nobody anything. A reader following a region should be able to choose the grain at which they hear that it changed, from a whole branch down to a single room, and be told at that grain.

*Seen for what is kept; the subscription is reasoned and not built.*

## 3. Heaviness, seen before it is read

Every room carries its texture wherever it is named, warm where a paragraph runs past five hundred characters, so a reader can judge what a room will cost before spending anything on it.

*Reasoned. The code says comprehension follows the confidence a reader's first glance leaves them with, and the labs found this the judgment they failed most often. The five hundred is the practice's open question and not a settled measure. What is measured is only this: the piece this was built against carries eighty-four paragraphs past it.*

## 4. Ground that moved after you did

A room stands on what it links to, and git knows when each file last changed. Where something a room points at was committed after the room itself, the room is marked: its ground moved and it was not written again.

What the mark measures is the distance between two states, the target as it stood when the room was last written and the target as it stands now. Resolving a link to its earlier state is not built, so the mark says the distance exists without being able to show it. How it should be resolved, by pinning a link to a commit or by keeping a stamped copy of the ground inside the brief that stands on it, is [open in the practice](../../poc.md#4-links-and-citations).

It is a suspicion and not a verdict, since a pointer often survives its target changing. It is also the cheapest honest signal a reader or a harness has, costing nothing but a read of the log. *Seen, on this room: four of its briefs carried the mark on the night it was written, which is what a room that revises its own ground should expect.*

## 5. Colour carries depth into the prose

The hues that mark a region in [the drawing](draws.md#5-regions-carry-hue) run into the prose itself, and depth is shaded within them, so a reader moving between the figure and the text meets one colour system rather than two.

The cues stay subtle. Colour in prose is a whisper about where you are, never a second voice competing with the words.

*Preferred, the author's; the hues are validated but their use in prose is not built.*

## 6. A pointer at a room is never a dead end

A room named in the prose, in a door, or in what points at a room always leads somewhere and always carries what following it gives. A backlink that names a room and leaves it stranded, with no way into the interconnection it belongs to, is the fault this rule exists to prevent.

*Seen, as a fault, on the piece this was built against.*
