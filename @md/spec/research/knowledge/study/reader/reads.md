# How it reads

A reader descending a holarchy loses their place if each step replaces the last. So every step of a descent takes its own column, laid left to right, and one such column is a **spread**. The room you are in is the rightmost; the rooms you came through stand beside it, still readable, still carrying their own instruments.

That is the whole of what the spreads are for: to keep where you came from present while you go deeper, so that placement is never traded for depth.

*Reasoned, and built in a first shape the author judged critically hard to use. What follows is what it is to be; [the order](forward.md) says how far the build has reached and what comes next.*

## 1. Three spreads divide the screen

Up to three spreads stand side by side and divide the viewport between them. They take the whole width, as a newspaper takes its page, and there is no window scrolling sideways within a frame.

Beyond three, the leftmost falls away, so the root stays present until the fourth step down. Below the width where three can hold prose at its measure, the spreads reduce in number before they reduce in measure, because prose set too narrow costs more than a lost column.

## 2. A spread presents doors, not a wall

A spread shows one room and nothing beneath it: its face, its weight, its own paragraphs as a texture, and its prose. The rooms beneath follow as doors, each a name, a weight and one line of what it gives.

Nothing beyond a room's own prose is loaded. A brief with twenty rooms is twenty ways on rather than a wall to scroll, and the rightmost spread is a door presenter rather than a turbo-scrolled file.

Unloaded entries are general, not a property of file edges. Wherever the reading stops, what lies beyond it is a door.

## 3. Descent is topological, not by file

A file is not a spread. A document nests inside itself, and a reader wants to descend those headings across spreads exactly as they descend folders. So a spread is a room at whatever grain the room happens to be, and a descent may cross a file's edge without the reader noticing a boundary they did not care about.

## 4. The room ahead

A chosen room becomes a spread. The rooms not yet chosen contend for the same next column, which asks for its own mechanic rather than a click into the dark.

That column belongs to whichever room the pointer rests on, wherever the pointer is: a door in a spread, a step of the descent, or any name in [the drawing](draws.md). The room opens there as the spread it would become, and a click commits it.

So descent is committal and remembered, and looking ahead is free. Hovering never enters the record of where you have been and never marks a room read.

## 5. Scrolling

The spreads are one reading rather than three, but they are not one distance. The spread you are in runs freely; each behind it moves more slowly, since it holds less prose per step of the holarchy. A shorter spread reaches its end and waits.

The head of each spread holds at its top while its prose runs beneath it. A scroll never jumps back, and the position of a spread is kept when you leave it and restored when you return, so a level you come back to returns you to the room you were in there rather than to its closed doors.

## 6. Moving sideways changes depth

Moving along the row of spreads changes which level is in focus, choosing among what stands at the level rather than entering a room. Entering is downward; the row is lateral.

## 7. Every name is a way

Everything that names a room is a way to it: a door, a step of the descent, a name in the drawing, a link in the prose, a name under what points at a room.

Navigation goes through the browser's own history, so back and forward work and every room has an address that can be shared. Links in the prose resolve to their targets and are followed inside the program; a link that leaves the substrate says so. Every room says what points at it, and a room its parent's entry introduces is named as introduced rather than listed as though a stranger pointed at it.
