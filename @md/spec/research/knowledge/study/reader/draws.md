# How it draws

A holarchy is geometry before it is text: depth, weight, and relations across it. So [the gauge](gauges.md) is drawn rather than tabulated, and this brief is what the drawing owes.

The drawing is computed, never generated. A shape that may be invented will be, and a reader who cannot trust the picture cannot use it.

*Reasoned, and built in a first shape that the author judged short of what was asked. The specific shortfalls are in [the order](forward.md).*

## 1. Nodes, edges and text in one figure

The radial form is the working shape: each arc a room, its angle the text beneath it, its shade the depth, its hue the region it belongs to.

What it owes is text among the shapes rather than beside them. Names and weights sit in the figure, and the levels read as organic bands rather than rigid rings, each node sitting further out the more it holds. Drawn that way the figure holds its shape at a smaller size, which is what buys the estate back for the reading.

## 2. A part's estate is proportional to its text

A room's form should take room in proportion to what its text needs, not in proportion to a fixed slot. Where a part is fine-grained its form yields; where it carries a great deal its form takes the space to say so.

An arc says its name whole, or only its weight, or nothing, because a cut-off word is worse than silence. Half the name is never drawn.

## 3. The hub is estate, not decoration

The centre carries what is being read here against what waits beneath, that room's own paragraphs as texture, how far the prose has been read, and a needle that swings to whatever the pointer is on.

It is the largest uninterrupted area in the figure and it is currently the least used. Standing on a leaf, the figure roots at the room you are in rather than vanishing.

## 4. Division, where a picture must agree with the thing

Beside the radial the whole divides as the holarchy divides it: each room a tile of its parent, weighted by what it holds, its own prose a darker band inside it, labelled where the tile is large enough.

Division is what a holarchy is, so the picture and the thing agree, and a tile is something a pointer can find where a line is not. It divides only while a tile can still be seen; below that floor it stops, marks itself as holding more, and hands that depth to the pointer.

## 5. Regions carry hue

A region is one of the branches directly beneath the root. Six hues are assigned in fixed order and never cycled; a seventh region takes no hue rather than repeating one.

The same hues run into the prose and into every other grade, so that a room's region is one fact wherever it appears.

*Measured by a palette validator, which passed the six on lightness, chroma, contrast and colour-blind separation in both themes, with one pair in the band that is legal only where something else also distinguishes them, which here is the name and weight written into every arc. Not seen by any colour-blind reader.*

## 6. Relations are drawn, not merely counted

Links are chords across the figure, so a room's relations show as well as its place. A chord that says only that a relation exists is the cheapest form of this and not the finished one.

## 7. The trail, projected sideways

The same slice projected right to left is the second form the drawing takes: the left edge is now, and rightward runs the trail of records, one column per round or sitting, so a piece's history reads as a landscape the eye crosses toward the past.

Its material is one state per round, which the snapshots already keep.

*Reasoned, the author's; nothing of this form is built.*

## 8. What a model may choose

A small local model has one job here and it is not drawing. It chooses: which projection, centred where, at what depth, and a caption for a group of events.

Its output is a declaration over a fixed vocabulary of drawing components, so coherence belongs to the components and is never asked of the model. The task is regular on both sides, records and headings in, declarations and captions out, and the labs already produce the pairs to tune on.

*Reasoned; nothing of this is built, and it is the last thing the program needs.*
