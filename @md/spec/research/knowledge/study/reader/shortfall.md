# Where it is not what was asked for

Written at the close of the sitting that built it, against the record of what was asked during that sitting. The gap is not a list of bugs. It is the things said once, understood, and not yet in the thing, which is exactly what a long sitting loses and what [the asks, read as substrate](transcript.py) now make checkable.

## 1. The spreads are less than the mechanic asked for

They were asked to scroll at their own rates, the room you are in freely and each one behind it more slowly, since a room behind holds less prose per step of the holarchy. They scroll by the same distance instead, which is synchrony without the grading.

They were asked to let a reader change depth by moving along the stack, choosing among what stands at a chosen level. Moving along the stack does nothing; depth changes only by entering a room.

And each descent was to be remembered for its stack, so returning to a level returns to the room you were in there. It is not remembered. Returning to a level shows its doors again, closed.

## 2. The raw is not openable

A brief should be openable as the markdown it actually is, expanding in place and staying in step with the reading, divided from the prose around it by rhythm alone and taking a surface of its own only where the pointer asks. Nothing of this is built. What a reader sees is always the rendering.

## 3. The figure is short of what was drawn for it

It was asked to merge with the texture, each arc carrying the lines of its own prose rather than a flat fill, and to hold nodes and edges with text among them as an earlier drawing did. It carries names, weights, ticks at the hub and chords for links, and that is all: the arcs are flat, and the chords are not a drawing of relations so much as a mark that relations exist.

## 4. There is still a server

The parsing belongs in the page, so that one program reads a granted directory locally and a manifest when published, web-native and substrate-native at once. It runs a small server instead, because a page cannot read a folder on its own without being given one. Until that changes, nothing here can be published as it stands.

## 5. Two programs where there should be one

[The surface](../surface/README.md) watches a session being made; this reads what was made. They were understood as one thing in the same sitting that built them separately, and they remain separate: a substrate read here cannot see the work that produced it, and the work seen there cannot be read as substrate.

## 6. Nothing is subscribed to

Notification was to be a subscription to a level of summary, so that a reader following a region hears what changed at the grain they chose. What exists is read and unread in one browser, which is memory rather than subscription, and it tells nobody anything.

## 7. Narrow, mobile, and one more grade

A stack navigation for narrow screens was raised and not built; at narrow widths the maps simply fall above the spreads. A third bounding between the whole and the page, the file you are inside rather than the section, was named as a one-line change and not made. And a small local model was to choose the projection and caption a group of events, which is the only part of the drawing that wants a model at all; there is none.

## 8. It is hard to use

Stated by the author at the close, and it outranks everything above: scrolling and the overall usability are critically low. The parts are there and using them is work. What is known of the causes, none of it measured: the one scroll moves three spreads at once so a small gesture changes more than a reader means; the room ahead opens on any hover, so the pointer crossing a list churns a whole column; the spreads are narrow because three share the width, which sets the prose against its own measure; the maps are many and each one asks for the estate the reading wants; and nothing is tuned, since every part was built once and never returned to.

None of this is a detail to be swept up after the features. A reading surface that is tiring to use fails at the only thing it is for, which is the code's own argument turned on the tool: what looks like too much is harder to understand, and effort begins before the reading does. The next sitting on this should spend itself on use rather than on more.

## 9. What this file is for

The code says a brief carries its confidence and that nothing is settled that has not been said. A built thing is no different: the distance between what was asked and what stands is knowledge about the thing, and keeping it beside the thing is the only way the next sitting starts on ground instead of in a pile. Seen, in this sitting: most of what is listed here was said clearly, acknowledged, and would have been lost.
