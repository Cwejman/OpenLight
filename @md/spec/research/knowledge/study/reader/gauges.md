# How it gauges

A number of tokens tells a reader nothing. Thirty-five thousand is neither large nor small until you know where the words sit, and that is the question this program exists to answer: what would stopping here cost, and what waits beneath.

So beside [the reading](reads.md) stands one instrument, and it answers that at every depth. It is not a panel of figures. It is one gauge seen at several fidelities, and the fidelities compose.

*Reasoned. The first cut built six separate figures that each asked for estate the reading wanted, which is the second fault the author named.*

## 1. One instrument, in grades

The grades run from the whole down to the prose itself, and the prose is the finest grade rather than a thing beside the instrument. A page strip along what you are reading, a division of the whole into its rooms, a drawing of where you stand, a bare weight per level: these are one thing at four distances.

A reader moves between grades rather than opening and closing widgets. What is in focus is drawn in full; what has been let go recedes to a shape, and then to a mark.

## 2. Estate follows attention

Visual estate is not fixed per figure. As one area is let go of and another invited in, the first asks for less room and the second takes it, so the whole always fits and the detail is always where the eye is.

This is the code's own gradient turned on the instrument: the biggest understanding at a glance, the next on approach, the text only on arrival.

## 3. The cursor moves with the reader

Every grade carries a cursor that follows where the reading is, without the reader placing it. A grade that cannot show the cursor's position is showing the wrong thing.

Each grade also fits whole at its own size. A gauge that must itself be scrolled has stopped being a gauge.

## 4. What stopping costs

At the coarsest grade the instrument states the promise the code makes and lets a reader check it: the prose owned at each depth beneath where they stand, against the running total.

If the biggest understanding really does come first, the top is short and each level below it heavier. Where that fails the instrument shows it failing, which is the one thing a total can never do.

*Measured, on the piece this was built against: it stops being short at its second level, where seventy-two rooms carry half its words.*

## 5. Never a list

A list of the rooms beneath is homogeneous and the holarchy is not. It says what is there and nothing of which inner room it belongs to, or how much it weighs against its neighbours.

Where a hierarchy has to be shown explicitly, as a reader who knows a repository will want, it is shown as the tree it is, with each entry carrying its weight and its region, and not as a flat run of names.
