# Evidence: a bullet list is a brief whose nested briefs have no headings

*Recorded 2026-09-02, the day of lab 07's run. Grounds the breakdown rule the lab was cut to test. Strength: **measured** — the counts and the depth measurement below were taken from the two outputs by script, not estimated. What the measurement does not reach is marked at the end.*

## The phenomenon

A bullet list never arrives alone. It needs a paragraph before it, or the items land without context — so under the code a list always comes as one surface plus its items. That is a brief with nested briefs, wearing a costume the writer does not recognise.

Lab 06's output is the specimen. The shape appears in 10 of its 23 sections, at `##` and `###` level alike, and bullets appear nowhere else in the document: never mid-section, never followed by prose that builds on them. The writer was not being careless. It was following principle 5, which permits a bold-led bullet, and it never saw that the permission had built a level of structure.

The cost is that the level was never judged. Because the shape was not recognised as briefs, principle 1 was never applied to its contents: the wins sat in the items, undivided, all arriving at one depth in one serving. **The failure was one of recognition, not of taste** — the writer could not see the structure it had written, and so could not check its content against the laws.

## The measurement

Read the two together. The document grew 43%, and every word of that growth sits below the line where a reader can stop; the level a reader actually meets shrank by nearly half. Measured across the whole document at `###` and above, the reduction is only 8% — but 13 of the 23 sections had no bullets to break down and carry ~1,700 words verbatim, so that figure measures dilution rather than the effect.

## What it establishes

**The test for a bullet is the win, not the length.** The code's current test is size — past a few sentences it is a brief. Size was a proxy. The real test is whether the item carries a gain of its own: an item with a win is a brief and needs a heading, because a win needs an address and room for its detail. An item with no win inside it stays a bullet. Lab 06 obeyed the length rule and still broke the win rule 25 times, which is what a proxy failing looks like.

**Recognition is the rule's job.** A writer who is told to look for the shape finds it. The 07 writer, given nothing but the observation, found groupings the flat lists had hidden — three of §1.2's five items turned out to be the whole received case for shallow structure, all three refuting it, a sentence that exists nowhere in the source.

**The gradient is real and it is payable.** A reader stopping at the same depth reads 46% less and holds more.

## What it does not establish

- **Whether a writer told up front produces the same gain natively.** Lab 07 was a rework of an existing document, not a fresh write. The fresh-write arm has not run.

- **Whether readers prefer the result.** No cold read of the 07 output has happened. Everything above is structural measurement, not reception.

- **The size of the cost to law 2.** The rework put some facts in two homes — the surface names a finding, the brief below evidences the same finding — and the folklore figures (1x/10x/100x, 2.4x, 7±2, Dunbar's 150) genuinely appear twice, because naming the figure *is* the win. The writer reported this; nobody has measured how far it spreads.

- **Whether the groupings are true.** Grouping asserts relationships the flat list did not. Those readings are the 07 writer's, not the corpus's, and they are now structural rather than arguable.

## Drift the rework introduced

Two counts in the new surfaces are wrong, and both came from the same slip: hoisting wins into a surface tempts the writer to count, and the count was taken from the old bullet structure rather than from the facts. §1.1 opens "Five figures" where the five former bullets name seven. §1.2 opens "Five genuine studies" where the five former bullets hold at least six studies, one of which — Luhmann's archive — is not a study. Neither claim exists in lab 06.

This is a real hazard of the rule, and it belongs with the rule: **a surface that summarises its briefs makes new assertions, and new assertions need checking against the briefs below them.**
