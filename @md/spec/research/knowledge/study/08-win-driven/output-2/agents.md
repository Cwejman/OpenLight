# The world where models read the same files

This is what changed in 2025 and 2026 for a model as a reader, read from the sweep: how a model's context degrades, what a routing file buys, what documentation does for a coding agent, what the laboratories chose when they built memory, who is arguing something like the code's fifth section, and who argues against it at full strength. It leaves you knowing which of the code's claims about models have been measured by others, and where the argument now stands.

## 1. Context degrades with composition, not length

Eighteen frontier models were tested on long inputs, and three findings recur. Even a single distractor reduces performance against baseline. The lower the semantic similarity between a question and the passage that answers it, the faster performance falls with length. And, against intuition, every one of the eighteen models did better on a shuffled haystack than on a logically coherent one, which the authors attribute to structural patterns steering attention [Chroma 2025, *Context Rot*]. A focused prompt of about three hundred tokens beat the same content inside a hundred and thirteen thousand for every model. When literal lexical matching is removed, eleven of thirteen models fall below half their short-context score by thirty-two thousand tokens [NoLiMa 2025, *ICML*]. The consequence is that length is a weak predictor and composition is the variable, and that a well-written coherent document is not automatically a better haystack. Measured, paper-only, replicated in spirit, and carried by two reports without re-verification.

## 2. Compaction deletes what it cannot see is a rule

When a long conversation is summarised to fit, the governance constraints go with it. Across 1,323 episodes and seven model families, policy violations rose from zero with the full context to about thirty percent on average after compaction, fifty-nine percent in the worst family; an attack that biases the summariser into dropping policies defeated every model evaluated; and pinning the constraints outside the lossy part restored zero [Chen 2026, *Governance Decay*]. This is the sharpest empirical case that compressing by inference over text is structurally unsafe, and that the fix is to type certain content as non-compressible. Measured, single author, with a deterministic benchmark.

The strongest shipping harness already does a version of this. Claude Code publishes a typed table of what survives compaction: the project's instruction file, the memory, the plan and path-scoped rules are re-injected from disk; skill bodies under a per-skill cap; the five most recently modified files, with large ones returned as a path rather than content; only the conversational middle is summarised. It checkpoints every prompt, branches sessions, and rewinds. So the claim that the harness holds a context that only grows, with no branch and nowhere to step back to, is false of the harness as shipped, and true of the wire: each request still sends one flat sequence.

What remains absent is the addressable half. Nothing lets you ask what is in a context or name a past state by identity; a branch is a copy of a transcript, not shared structure; and nobody in the mainstream can re-derive a completion from a recorded context, since server-side compaction and background memory consolidation make the recorded context non-reconstructible. That work exists only as papers: an event log as the source of truth with deterministic replay, and directed graphs of context with snapshot, branch and trim. Seen and reasoned. The protocol moved the same way: MCP's revision of July 2026 deleted sessions and the handshake and became stateless, while its resources gained required time-to-live and cache-scope fields. It shed the conversational layer and hardened the typed layer underneath.

## 3. A routing file buys one level

The knowledge layer flipped in 2025 from retrieving chunks into a prompt to a routing file the agent reads before the content: a skill's front matter, a repository's instruction file, a server's resource list. The only controlled ablation of that pattern found that one level of routing degrades gracefully and eventually beats raw navigation, and that a second level gives no benefit and sometimes reduces accuracy; progressive disclosure buys context, not intelligence [He et al. 2026]. The often-cited figure that presenting tools as a navigable filesystem cut a workflow from a hundred and fifty thousand tokens to two thousand is, in the vendor's own words, an illustrative example with no methodology; the mechanism is independently supported at one to two orders of magnitude smaller effect. Measured, one study.

Skills are the adopted form and the evidence runs against them. Fifty-three thousand skill specifications exist on one marketplace alone. On a thousand web-development tasks across four models, injecting the relevant skill reduced pass rates by one to four points, raised token cost by seventy to nearly four hundred percent, and helped in a fifth to a third of skill-and-project pairs [WebDev-Skills-Bench 2026]. Across fifty-seven repositories and 105 release transitions, every transition invalidated part of the skill set, and frontier agents repaired between thirty and seventy percent of it; skills go stale in silence [Repo2Skill-Evo 2026]. Measured, paper-only.

## 4. What documentation does for a coding agent

The causal evidence splits cleanly, and it cuts against the prevailing practice. Repository context files, generated per vendor guidance or committed by developers, do not generally improve task success and raise inference cost by over twenty percent; agents followed the instructions, and the repository overviews vendors recommend most simply did not help [Gloaguen et al. 2026, ETH Zürich]. A smaller two-agent ablation, 288 runs, bounds any effect at ten to fifteen points and attributes the failures to implementation skill, feature design, pattern selection, exact wiring, not to missing repository knowledge a file could supply [Khatri 2026]. Measured, two designs agreeing on the null.

Where guidance did help, it helped by localisation. Tuning a repository's guidance by probing where the agent failed raised resolve rates from 25.5 to 33.0 percent, and the gain came from the agent reaching the correct files, coverage up fourteen points, while patch quality stayed flat [Shepard and Albrecht 2026]. Holding localisation fixed and varying only representation, natural-language summaries answered four of forty-five behavioural questions that the source answered twenty-seven of, and a frontier summariser scored exactly as poorly as a three-billion-parameter one; rendering the rest of a file as signatures resolved no more issues than deleting it outright [Sam-Bodden 2026]. The same paper measured a noise floor: temperature-zero inference flips about nine percent of per-instance outcomes between byte-identical runs, which sits under every small effect reported on this benchmark. Measured, pre-registered.

Retrieval says the same thing from the other side. Prepending each chunk with its chain of headings lifted mean reciprocal rank from 0.374 to 0.463 on a production Markdown knowledge base, with no extra model calls; and stripping the prefixes collapsed annotator agreement from 0.45 to 0.04, so conventional chunking ablations partly measure their own labelling [Yang 2026]. Sophisticated semantic chunking is repeatedly found not worth its cost, while structural signal, headings, links, sections, helps. A file listing a site's Markdown pages is inert as a crawler channel, zero requests from any frontier crawler across nine hundred domains in seven months and no measurable link to being cited, and useful as a navigation map: with it, agents produced about ninety percent fewer dead URLs, at flat accuracy, in one vendor's two-thousand-four-hundred-trial benchmark. Typed descriptions of tools do matter, six points of task success from augmenting them, at more steps and with regressions in a sixth of cases. Agents were two thirds of one documentation platform's traffic by July 2026. Measured, seen, and vendor-reported where said.

## 5. Memory: the laboratories chose files at paths

When the model vendors built memory as a product, they built files. Anthropic's memory store, in public beta from April 2026, is a workspace-scoped collection of Markdown documents addressed by path, mounted into the sandbox as a filesystem, where every change creates an immutable version attributed to the session that made it and versions survive the deletion of the memory itself; a background consolidation process, named dreaming, followed in May. Google's memory bank shipped revisions and identity-conditioned access independently. Both sell scope isolation, versioned facts and audit. Seen.

The graph-memory vendors answered with the title *Markdown is not agent memory*, and their provenance model is genuinely more expressive: each fact carries the list of episodes that produced it and validity intervals that accumulate rather than overwrite, so the scope of a deletion is computable. But the flagship of the memory-operating-system thesis retired its own server in August 2026 and moved to a coding-agent harness whose memory is files tracked in git.

The benchmarks the vendors compete on collapsed as evidence: the leading graph system's lead disappears once retrieval budget is controlled, since it retrieved 2.6 million characters per query; recall is reported without precision; serving cost is never measured, and some systems never break even against resubmitting the whole transcript. A lexical index over the raw log with an iterative keyword loop matches or beats graph and summary memory. Poisoning 1.2 percent of a memory corpus drops accuracy from 0.85 to 0.30, a screening pipeline rejected none of 360 poisoned memories, and provenance-weighted retrieval was indistinguishable from no defence. And the paper that names checkpoint, fork, restore and merge as execution edits observes that existing systems perform them without deriving what each must preserve; nobody has safe branching in production. Where the accessory market rushed, within six weeks of each other, is memory across harnesses, the one thing no laboratory will build. Measured and seen.

## 6. Who is arguing something like this

Nobody the sweep found argues the whole of the code's fifth section: a typed medium, context as an address, the harness dissolving, and the historical enclosure argument together. Every neighbour holds two or three of those and rejects or ignores the rest, and the agent that looked had no search engine, so its absences are proven only for essays with traction, YC companies and named sites.

- Ink & Switch holds the typed medium, the app-enclosure half of the history, interface as data and provenance, and treats the model as a collaborator to version-control, never as the reason the substrate is needed; [the kin file](hypertext.md#2-ink--switch) unpacks it.

- Kendall Clark's Pentad Labs, in a July 2026 design essay, defines a fact as five slots, subject, predicate, object, context and lineage, explicitly rather than burying context in narrative transcripts, with nothing an agent has seen, said or done lost; it is an essay and a pre-release product.

- Letta holds that memory cannot be bolted on and that context and state are core responsibilities of the harness itself, which locates the claim exactly where the code says it must not live.

- Palantir's Ontology is the only typed layer at national scale, nouns and verbs over heterogeneous stores with branch-based change management, and it composes only inside its own platform.

- Jazz makes schema, permissions and reactive interface state one object with git-like branching, and its second version walked back cryptographic permissions to a server and CRDTs to snapshot graphs.

- Glamorous Toolkit says objects, not text, and in 2026 added an agent harness while arguing that deterministic views beat model summaries.

- A row of YC context-layer companies says sessions do not compound, science has journals, law has case law, software has git, and solves it by summarising transcripts into a store; one of them, Epicenter, chose a folder of plain text and SQLite, which is the counter-example living inside the argument.

The full historical arc, pipes then links then whatever joins briefs, each medium arriving whole and being enclosed, was found nowhere as a stated argument. Governance consolidated under the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation in December 2025, holding the protocol, the instruction file and one harness, with the interoperability protocol joining in August 2026. Seen.

## 7. The case against, at full strength

The case runs in three layers that reinforce each other, and the sweep's own adversarial pass grants two of them.

The first is the bitter lesson applied to representation. Three years of extension mechanisms, plugins, custom instructions, tool schemas, skills, each superseded by something less structured, ending at folders of Markdown; the conclusion drawn is that we go back to extending agents in the most accessible language, natural language [Hood 2025]. A format designed to be more compact for machines cost more tokens because the models were unfamiliar with it, a grep tax; that study the sweep could not verify, and the format's own benchmark shows a null accuracy difference. The trend line runs the same way: a million tokens of context went generally available in March 2026, and every year pasting it in gets cheaper. Reasoned and seen.

The second is the empirical cost of structure, and it is where the sweep's adversarial agent lands its verdict. Types inferred by a model at breadth are wrong at rates of twenty to eighty-five percent: a model reconstructing column metadata scored 0.223 on ninety-four percent coverage, and 0.475 on the forty-two percent it would commit to inside a deterministic gate, the gate being a competence detector, not an amplifier; a 369-field extraction schema produced zero valid outputs from every frontier model. A typed store with wrong types is worse than prose, because a wrong type answers confidently while incomplete prose is visibly incomplete. Structure also rots silently: every one of 105 release transitions broke part of a skill set.

What wins are small declared vocabularies, thirteen memory categories beating graph memory, fifteen relational operations beating free-form generation, a governed semantic layer taking thirty-seven of thirty-eight questions against twenty-one. The verdict: type the edges, identity, permission, provenance, freshness, address; keep the vocabulary tiny and declared; leave the body as text; and never type what would have to be inferred rather than observed or declared. Structure wins where a rule must hold when the model is wrong, where the query is a set operation, where there are many writers and no coordinator, where someone absent later must be answered, and where cost scales with the catalogue. Prose wins when the payload is read once by one reader who exercises judgement. Measured; the rule is the agent's reasoning over it.

The third is the philosophical attack, and the one the sweep's agent would take most seriously. Dynamicland states the inverse principle outright: to maximise agency, minimise what the computer knows; the smarter the product, the less the user needs to understand, which it calls outsourcing understanding in its most virulent form. Litt's 2026 title says the same from the other side: understanding is the new bottleneck. The strongest form is that a thesis about what programs receive misses the binding constraint, which is what people can hold in their heads, and every layer of contract added for automatic composition makes comprehension harder. The counter-evidence is narrow: decomposing an agent's execution into inspectable typed actions improved users' comprehension of the task in a study of sixteen, and legibility protocols gain with monitor strength rather than being obsoleted by it. The objection applies to structure as an authoring tax; it does not obviously apply to structure as an inspection surface. Preferred, reasoned; Dynamicland's public material predates the agent era.
