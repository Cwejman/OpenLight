# Other people's work, from this shore

This is where the code's project meets the research and the systems built before it. It gives you the people and works nearest to what you are doing, what each found when they tried it, and which of their findings hold under measurement. The project is the shore only: you leave from it, and nothing here scores its claims.

Everything here comes from one web sweep of 2026-08-26, [forty-five reports written by research agents](../../../../sweep-2026-08/README.md), kept raw so any compression of them can be checked. I read the reports whole and opened no source. So every mark in these files is the agent's, carried by me. Measured means an agent read a figure from a primary text. Seen means an agent fetched a repository, a page or a changelog and saw its state. Reasoned means an argument, the source's or the agent's. Preferred means someone's opinion. Two agents caught automated summarisers inventing quotations and sample sizes, so a figure here is worth what its agent's own check made it worth, and where an agent flagged a figure as unverified or as folklore, I say so. Each brief ends by pointing to the report it came from, which holds the addresses and the detail.

## 1. What the three lineages agree on

The project stands where three bodies of work meet that seldom read one another. The first is the science of how a reader takes in a text. The second is the people who built media meant to carry understanding in their structure: hypertext, design rationale, documentation systems, the semantic web, typed stores. The third is the world of 2025 and 2026, where models read the same files people do. Each has walked part of this road, and each, separately, arrived at the same finding. What helps is getting a reader to the right piece at the right level. What fails is asking the writer to put the meaning into the structure.

The story of the second lineage has been told four times with the same shape. A group builds a medium to hold what it knows, and gives it structure so that the structure itself carries the understanding: typed links, argument slots, a vocabulary of kinds. The first months are good. Then the writing slows, because every sentence now costs a decision about its kind. The ones who know most write least, because their knowing does not come in kinds. The structure the readers needed turns out not to be the one the writers could give. A year later the store is half-filled and stale, and a newcomer asks a colleague instead. That is the story the hypertext lab told in 1999 [Shipman and Marshall 1999], the design-rationale people told of their capture tools [Karsenty 1996], the semantic web told of its ontologies, and the decision-record people are telling now: half of all decision records on GitHub were written once and never touched again [Buchgeher et al. 2023]. The chain is the sweep's reading across four reports; each link in it is seen or measured, the joining of them is reasoned.

What lived in each lineage has one shape. The body stayed prose. What got typed was the edge: the address, who wrote it, when, who may read it, what it points to. And what paid for the reader was reach. Carrying the heading hierarchy into each retrieved chunk lifted retrieval by a quarter on a production knowledge base [Yang 2026]. Guidance to a coding agent helped only by getting it to the right files, and not at all by explaining the code [Shepard and Albrecht 2026]. Physicians failed on a guideline not because they could not read it but because they could not find the recommendation that applied [Kilsdonk et al. 2016]. And in the reading laboratory, what sat at the top of a text's structure was what got understood, while the same relations low in the structure barely registered [Meyer 1975]. Measured, each in its own field.

So, read from this shore, the code's two laws stand on different ground. The first law, the reader's order and the whole brief, has a science under it, thin but real, with a fault line running through it: position, narrative and signalling held; size numbers and the abstract opening did not. The second law, one home and written confidence, has almost no reading science under it and is instead where the systems lineage keeps its graveyards and its survivors. The rest of this entry walks the lineages, the reader first and the graveyards after, and each section opens a file that unpacks it.

## 2. How a reader takes in a text

The comprehension science says six things that replicated. What sits at the top of a text's structure is what gets understood. A story is understood and remembered better than the same content as statements, by the largest margin in the whole literature, across more than thirty thousand participants [Mar et al. 2021]. Signal words, segmenting and asking the reader to explain each carry a small, replicated effect. And helping the expert costs the expert much less than the folklore says: the harm from assistance to high-knowledge readers is not significant inside any education level, and no study of practising professionals exists at all [Tetzlaff et al. 2025]. Measured, on students.

Three things fell. The advance organizer works a little, about a fifth of a standard deviation, and not for Ausubel's reason: the abstract, subsuming opening is the part the data failed to support. No number exists for the size or depth of a unit; every depth-and-breadth result is a fixed-item optimum, and the winning breadths were eight, sixteen and thirty-two. And the redundancy effect is about the same content in two channels at the same moment, not about a fact said in two documents. Measured, with corrections from a citation audit.

Professionals reading the documents of their work tell the sharper story. Syntax taxes lawyers as much as laypeople [Martínez, Mollica and Gibson 2023]. Preference is not comprehension: physicians preferred a layered guideline format four to one and understood it no better [Brandt et al. 2017]. And the failure mode, in medicine and in the cockpit, is reaching the right item, not reading it. [`reading.md`](reading.md) unpacks the effects that held, the ones that fell, the professional studies, and what hypertext and information-architecture research measured about links and finding.

## 3. The nearest kin: prose joined by links

The people closest to the code are the ones who built media of prose and links, and the sharpest thing they wrote is twenty-seven years old: formality imposes a cost at the moment of capture that writers route around, and nothing since refutes it [Shipman and Marshall 1999]. Ink & Switch is the living centre of the line. Its Patchwork is a running system in which documents, tools and code sit in one versioned store and tools compose in ways their authors did not plan; its Cambria proved that schema lenses work, discovered that translating on write fails, and stopped in 2020 with no successor. Seen.

The tools-for-thought market settled the question of files in 2026, for a reason nobody predicted: plain Markdown became a moat because coding agents can read it. Obsidian added a database view over its files and a command line. Logseq took the files away, four years late, and its own community read that as the wrong move at the wrong time. The venture-funded middle left note-taking for meetings and agents. Seen.

Design rationale and decision records are where this lineage measured itself. A scribed rationale document answered forty-one percent of the questions designers actually asked of it [Karsenty 1996]. Fifteen years after Nygard's post, one study has looked for an effect of decision records on quality or productivity and found at most a modest one, and sixty-three percent of records are born already accepted, skipping the deliberation they exist to hold [De Santana et al. 2026]. And the heavyweight proposal processes, Rust's RFCs and Kubernetes' KEPs, have no exit path: hundreds of open proposals with a median age of two and a half years, while the lightweight processes beside them clear. [`hypertext.md`](hypertext.md) unpacks the lab, the market, Luhmann's slips as the archive describes them, and the decision-record evidence.

## 4. The world where models read the same files

For a model as reader, 2025 and 2026 measured what people only argued about. Context degrades with composition, not length: a single distractor hurts, and across eighteen models a shuffled haystack was easier to search than a coherent one [Chroma 2025]. Compaction silently drops rules the model cannot see are rules, from zero violations to about thirty percent, and pinning the rules outside the lossy part restores zero [Chen 2026]. A routing file the agent reads before the content buys one level of savings, and a second level buys nothing [He et al. 2026]. Measured, paper-only, each replicated in spirit.

Documentation for a coding agent splits cleanly. Prose overviews in a repository's context file do not improve task success and cost twenty percent more [Gloaguen et al. 2026]. Guidance that gets the agent to the right files does help, by localisation alone [Shepard and Albrecht 2026]. Natural-language summaries answer almost none of the questions the source answers, four of forty-five against twenty-seven, and the gap belongs to the representation, not the summariser [Sam-Bodden 2026]. Measured. The labs' memory products chose Markdown files at paths with immutable, attributed versions, and the graph-memory vendors answered with the title "Markdown is not agent memory". Seen.

Nobody found argues the whole of the code's fifth section. The nearest are Ink & Switch, Kendall Clark's Pentad Labs, Letta, Palantir's Ontology and Jazz, each holding two or three of its claims and rejecting the rest. The strongest opposition is not that structure is hard. It is Dynamicland's inversion, to maximise agency, minimise what the computer knows, and the bitter lesson applied to representation: three years of extension mechanisms each less structured than the last. [`agents.md`](agents.md) unpacks the measurements, the memory products, the neighbours and the case against.

## 5. The documentation profession, and whether insight travels at all

The profession's frameworks rest on less than their adoption suggests. Diátaxis cites nothing and does not ask for the four-file split its adopters made; the best failure report, from Google's Pigweed team, is fragmentation, and the retreat was to sections within a page. Minimalism holds the only causal evidence that a document's design changes what a reader can do, and its own meta-analysts suspect the active ingredient was cutting redundancy, not the theory. DITA's next version has been in beta since 2021 with one committer, the society that carried it in North America went bankrupt in 2025, and no empirical study of its reuse savings exists. DORA's documentation multiplier went from two point four times, to negative, to no effect, to dropped as a named capability. Seen and measured.

Under all of it sits the organisational science, which is the most honest ground in the sweep. Knowledge fails to travel not because people will not share but because the receiver cannot absorb, the sender cannot explain, and the channel between them is painful [Szulanski 1996]. A document has no way to repair a misunderstanding in the moment, so the cheapest joint move is to go and ask someone [Clark and Brennan 1991]. Artifacts that generate something downstream get maintained; artifacts that only describe do not [Wohlrab et al. 2019]. And the surgical checklist chain, a before-and-after success, a hundred-hospital null, and a randomised win, says that the artifact does not carry the effect; the implementation does. [`documentation.md`](documentation.md) unpacks the frameworks, the research that measures what is wrong with documents and never what good ones buy, and the science of transfer.

## 6. Beyond prose: the typed lineage and its graveyard

The code's fifth section reasons about a unified medium in which programs, screens and models are all citizens. That road has been walked further than it looks, and the walkers left a clear record. Vocabularies outgrow their use by two orders of magnitude: twelve of schema.org's nine hundred and fifty-eight types reach ten million domains. Transitive hierarchies rot under many hands: millions of Wikidata classes are marked as both first-order and second-order classes. Validation beat reasoning; the OWL reasoners are dead and SHACL's downloads grew two and a half times in five months. Graphs built by models lose to plain retrieval at two hundred times the cost, and inferred types fail at breadth, while small declared vocabularies win. Measured and seen.

The exact idea of a typed, versioned knowledge store exists: TerminusDB, one maintainer, with its most structural migration unimplemented. Every production system that survived schema change did so by forbidding things, not translating them. Nobody merges structure; they take one whole side. Permission that is transitive turns one write into tens of thousands of index events, and the vendors are now abandoning depth as the wrong measure of cost. The structure editors were the first thing the language models made redundant, in their own founders' words. And interfaces as data converged everywhere on the same shape: a closed set of renderers, a pinned discriminator, and an escape hatch. [`typed.md`](typed.md) unpacks the semantic web, the stores, schema evolution, permission, the editors, server-driven and schema-driven interfaces, and where the low-code money went.

## 7. Numbers not to carry

This field runs on figures that trace to nothing. The one-to-ten-to-a-hundred cost ratio traces to an IBM training unit, not a study, and the authors of the hundred-times curve wrote themselves that five to one is nearer for small systems. The ten-to-one reading-to-writing ratio is Robert Martin's memory of watching an editor replay. Seven plus or minus two was called a pernicious coincidence by the man who wrote it. Nielsen's fifteen card-sorters were read off a graph. DORA's multiplier was walked back by DORA. [`folklore.md`](folklore.md) lists them with what the audit found, so that you can drop each one knowing why.

## 8. Open

The sweep is loudest about what nobody has measured, and these are the doors it left open. Each is a study that does not exist, as far as forty-five agents could find.

- No causal study of documentation exists in any design, no trial where a document was the manipulated variable and an engineering outcome was measured.

- No study reads a long technical document with professionals over months; the whole comprehension literature is short passages, one sitting, students.

- No head-to-head compares human-declared, model-assisted structure against a well-written prose document on the same corpus with the same frontier reader; every structure win was against extracted or unstructured baselines.

- No evidence exists on written status marks, on whether a link should name its outcome rather than its topic, or on whether skippability substitutes for prose forked by audience.

- No professional population has been tested for expertise reversal; the nearest proxy is three vocational studies with an interval spanning two and a half standard deviations.

- No system holds together typed contracts, branch versioning, one-hop permission and set-based navigation; branching an owner-scoped contract is where the literature is thinnest and every shipped system gives up.

- The three-term historical arc the fifth section leans on, pipes then links then whatever joins briefs, was found nowhere as a stated argument; that absence is the sweep's most confident one, and its search was weak on long-form media theory.
