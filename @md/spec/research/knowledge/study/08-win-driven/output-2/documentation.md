# The documentation profession, and whether insight travels at all

This is the profession that has written documentation for a living, read from the sweep: its frameworks and what they rest on, the research that measures documents, the survey everyone quotes and what it now says, and underneath all of it the organisational science of whether understanding moves between people at all. It leaves you knowing what a documentation structure can and cannot be expected to do, on the best evidence available.

## 1. Diátaxis does not ask for what its adopters did

Diátaxis derives four modes of documentation, tutorial, how-to, reference, explanation, from two axes, action against cognition and acquisition against application, each mode with its own voice. Its site cites no research, no study and no other theory; the argument is self-contained. Its author disclaims the file-per-type reading in so many words: it is not a scheme into which documentation must be placed, and a structural division into the four is a typical outcome of the practice, not its end. Its best testimony is about voice, that it made it clear which register one was writing in. Preferred.

The best failure report is exactly the question a knowledge base asks. Google's Pigweed team adopted Diátaxis as a literal blueprint and found too much fragmentation: the explanation for a module on one page and the tutorial on another, readers annoyed at jumping back and forth, and, the mechanism, that if you only link to explanations from the tutorial, most readers never click and never meet the theory. They retreated to sections within a page [Basques 2024]. Others report a navigation tax, one click becoming two, and enforcement drift, documents rejected for mixing modes. The framework's only page on documentation with more than one organising axis was deleted by its author in August 2026 as not good enough, with nothing replacing it. Its adoption is several hundred projects by the author's estimate, the adopter list was removed because he could not keep up, and its largest sustained adopter, Canonical, reports no metrics five years on. It has no scholarly footprint. The taxonomy itself predates it, in Django's 2008 documentation reorganisation. Seen.

## 2. Minimalism: the one causal result, and its misreading

Carroll's minimal manual is the only credible causal evidence that a document's design changes what a reader can do. In the first experiment, nineteen temporary office workers learning a word processor: forty percent less learning time and 2.7 times as many subtasks completed. In the second, thirty-two: fifty-eight percent more subtasks, twenty-nine percent less reading, and twenty percent fewer errors, the last not significant [Carroll et al. 1987, *Human-Computer Interaction*]. The subjects spent almost half their time recovering from errors. The meta-analysis pools thirteen effects and 288 people for d = 1.12, homogeneous, with the training-wheels studies excluded; it was an online conference submission, never peer-reviewed, and Carroll and van der Meij helped select the included studies. Its sub-analysis is the interesting part: the slash-the-verbiage component alone gives d = 0.89, near the whole, so the authors suspect the active ingredient was redundancy reduction rather than the task-orientation theory [Ginns, Hollender and Reimann 2006]. Measured, small, old, on word processors.

The theory's main critic replicated it. Van der Meij wrote in 1992 that it was not settled that a minimalist approach was most effective because critical experiments had hardly been conducted, ran them, found in favour in 1993, and by 2004 had moved on to instructional video. Six technical-communication journals from 2014 to 2019 contain no article on minimalism at all. The most repeated misreading is the one Carroll and van der Meij list first among ten misconceptions: minimalism means brevity; wantonly slashing text and leaving other design characteristics unchanged will not lead to a minimalist design [Carroll and van der Meij 1996]. Their contested tenet was guided exploration, not brevity [Williams and Farkas 1992]. And anyone invoking minimal instruction as settled walks into the most-cited paper in the neighbourhood, why minimal guidance during instruction does not work, 6,813 citations, aimed at discovery learning [Kirschner, Sweller and Clark 2006]. Measured and seen.

## 3. DITA is calcifying, and the profession's society is gone

The next version of the structured-authoring standard was promised for 2020 and is still in beta, with a third beta in July 2026; one report, correcting itself, dates the first beta to June 2021, a gap of nearly five years, and a sibling read the same release page as 2025. Every commit to its branch in the last twelve months is by one person. The toolkit is alive, releasing five days before the sweep, eighty-two percent by one maintainer. Three full-text searches of the scholarly indexes returned no empirical study of reuse savings; the thirty-to-forty-percent figures trace to vendor calculators and trade books citing each other. The Society for Technical Communication ceased operations in January 2025 and filed for liquidation, membership down from twenty-five thousand to about forty-five hundred; there is no summit in 2025 or 2026. The German professional body's 2026 conference lists nine subject areas and the standard is not among them, while a European conference's second day is half sessions on the standard in the age of AI, against one on the specification itself. The claim that structured content is better for machine retrieval has no study behind it; a widely echoed figure attributed to a vendor is absent from the page cited. What evidence exists rewards heading hierarchy and metadata, which Markdown supplies as well as XML. Seen; the calcification is the sweep's reading.

## 4. The research measures what is wrong with documents, never what good ones buy

The canonical software-documentation studies are large and descriptive. Eighty Microsoft developers naming obstacles to learning an API, fifty of seventy-four naming documentation [Robillard 2009]; more than four hundred and forty in surveys and interviews, no task outcome anywhere [Robillard and DeLine 2011]; 323 IBM professionals rating ten documentation problems, content beating presentation [Uddin and Robillard 2015]; 878 artifacts mined into a taxonomy [Aghajani et al. 2019]; 146 practitioners surveyed [Aghajani et al. 2020]; a ten-dimension quality framework presented as a vision [Treude, Middleton and Atapattu 2020]. None has an outcome variable.

The genuine exceptions where documentation was manipulated and performance measured are five: Carroll above; fifty-eight students with a platform of secure crypto examples, seventy-three percent more effective and sixty-six percent fewer possible vulnerabilities [Mindermann and Wagner 2018]; documentation strengthening the benefit of static typing on one task chosen because both were expected to matter [Endrikat et al. 2014]; pattern comments on already well-commented programs helping pattern-relevant maintenance [Prechelt et al. 2002]; and pushing buried API directives to the developer [Dekel and Herbsleb 2009]. No meta-analysis of documentation experiments exists. The one clean random-sample statistic in the area is that ninety-three percent of open-source respondents see incomplete or outdated documentation as a problem and sixty percent rarely or never contribute to it [GitHub 2017]. Seen and measured.

## 5. DORA's number went up, then down, then away

The survey everyone quotes said in 2021, from twelve hundred respondents and seven agree-or-disagree items, that teams with quality documentation were 2.4 times more likely to see better delivery and operational performance, with no interval, effect size or p-value published, from a snowball-plus-panel sample analysed by correlation-based structural equation modelling. In 2022, under surprises: documentation practices negatively impacted software delivery performance, at odds with previous reports. In 2023, nearly three thousand respondents: no effect on software delivery performance, the second year we see this behaviour, alongside a table of amplifiers, twelve-point-eight times for trunk-based development, that is a ratio of two unreported coefficients. In 2024: a quarter more AI adoption associated with seven and a half percent better documentation quality, and, in the methodology chapter, we didn't do longitudinal studies or a proper experiment.

In 2025, nearly five thousand respondents: documentation quality is no longer a named capability, its successors are healthy data ecosystems and AI-accessible internal data, the figures show ordinal bands with no numbers, and an endnote says that last year we spoke in terms of effects and this year we will speak in terms of comparisons. The much-cited percentage-lift table with a six-hundred-and-fifty-six-percent entry appears only on a capability page and a blog post, not in any report. A multivocal review found that of ninety-three papers citing the underlying metrics, none contained more information than the original. Measured, as a survey; the retreat is in DORA's own words.

## 6. Whether insight travels at all

Under the documentation literature sits the organisational one, and it is the most honest ground in the sweep, because it measured transfer directly and its motivating cost claim came with a signed confession.

The barriers to a best practice moving inside a firm were measured across 122 transfers in eight companies, and they are knowledge-related, not motivational: the recipient's lack of absorptive capacity carried the largest weight, causal ambiguity second, and an arduous relationship between source and recipient third, with motivation ranking low. The headline analysis used eighty-seven of 271 observations, and the author states that only the top three weights are stable [Szulanski 1996, *Strategic Management Journal*]. Absorption is a function of what the receiver already knows, not of how well the sender writes, a construct that replicated with heavy moderation, effects twice as large in low-technology industries [Cohen and Levinthal 1990; Stettler et al. 2025]. A client that has outsourced its technical staff has, by construction, destroyed its own capacity to absorb what the vendor writes. Measured, one strong study never independently replicated.

Two frameworks say why a document cannot close the gap alone. People minimise joint effort, not the sender's, so the cost of a perfect utterance often exceeds the cost of repairing an imperfect one together; a document is maximal in reviewability and revisability and zero in co-temporality and simultaneity, so grounding failures cannot be repaired in the moment and compound silently, and the least collaborative effort is usually to go and ask someone [Clark and Brennan 1991]. And boundaries differ in kind: syntactic, different words; semantic, different meanings; pragmatic, different interests, and the last requires both sides to transform their knowledge, not translate it. A client and a vendor differ in interests, so a glossary addresses the shallowest layer [Carlile 2004]. Reasoned, with laboratory support.

What structure does, and what documents do, was measured separately. On Windows Vista, 3,404 binaries, organisational metrics predicted failure-prone components at 86 percent precision and 84 percent recall, beating churn, complexity, dependencies, coverage and pre-release bugs [Nagappan, Murphy and Basili 2008]; the mirroring of organisation into architecture held in all five matched pairs of a study whose significance is a sign test at p = .03 [MacCormack, Baldwin and Rusnak 2012]. Among fifty-three practitioners in six automotive companies, motivation to maintain prescriptive artifacts, ones that generate code or other artifacts, was higher than for descriptive ones, and the guideline that followed is to produce locally relevant documentation as late as possible and only when actually needed [Wohlrab et al. 2019]. Among 411 Microsoft developers, lack of documentation was the strongest of twelve listed factors slowing a first check-in, and in the open question mentorship came first and documentation second, with developers without a mentor reporting significant lost time [Rastogi et al. 2015]. The transactive-memory effect on team outcomes is r = .44 overall and r = .77 with self-report against .38 with observers [Fausett et al. 2026]. Measured.

The cost claim under all of this is softer than its folklore. Boehm and Basili's own words in 2001: finding and fixing a problem after delivery is often a hundred times more expensive than during requirements and design, immediately qualified, we have added the word often, and for small non-critical systems the factor is more like five to one, with good architecture flattening it even for large ones [Boehm and Basili 2001]. A systematic review of fifty-seven requirements-quality publications found 47.5 percent of claimed impacts merely hypothesised and calls the evidence anecdotal or folkloric [Frattini et al. 2023]. And no causal study of documentation exists in any design; DORA itself hedges to we can speculate. The honest ceiling, in the sweep's words: a documentation structure can plausibly reduce syntactic and semantic friction; there is no evidence it closes a boundary of interest or substitutes for a mentor; and it decays unless something downstream consumes it. Measured; the ceiling is reasoned.

## 7. Handbooks at scale

Two artifacts are the existence proofs that writing everything down survives at scale, and neither carries an outcome. GitLab's handbook prints to over two thousand pages, is written first and communicated after, keeps chat for ninety days as a forcing function, and has split into a public and an internal handbook; the figure of six hundred thousand words appears in no GitLab source the sweep could reach, and its own style guide says to keep pages short because the amount of information is what new employees cite as hardest. Wikipedia's summary style makes a child article a complete article in its own right, and its size rule now runs on words, over eight thousand may need dividing, over fifteen thousand almost certainly, on readable prose only; its class counts, four million stubs against nine thousand featured articles, measure assessment coverage, not a designed pyramid. Seen.
