# The nearest kin: prose joined by links

These are the people and systems closest to the code: the ones who built media of prose and links to hold what a group knows, from the hypertext laboratories through tools for thought to design rationale and decision records. It gives you what each found when they tried it, who is still at it in 2026, and where this line measured itself and came up short.

## 1. Formality considered harmful

The sharpest critique of typed structure is twenty-seven years old and nothing since refutes it. Reflecting on a decade of building hypertext and argumentation systems, Shipman and Marshall named four reasons people route around formal structure: cognitive overhead, since every formal element costs extra decisions about chunking, naming, linking and labelling; tacit knowledge, since experts cannot reliably account for what they know and being made to introspect interrupts and changes the task; premature structure, since leaving things out means not having to characterise them; and situational structure, since different people and tasks want different structure from the same material [Shipman and Marshall 1999, *CSCW*, full text read by the agent]. The most telling observation inside it is McCall's: design students could not produce argumentation in the IBIS form even though videotapes of their natural discussions already followed that form. Producing structure after the fact is a different act from generating it.

Two of the paper's own systems were written off because the effort of formalising exceeded the service it bought, and its verdict on the design-rationale systems of the time was limited successes whose gains relied on social pressure, extensive training or continuing human facilitation. It is an experience essay, not a measurement, with 165 to 336 citations depending on the index. Its constructive answer was spatial hypertext, where structure stays implicit and emergent and is read off arrangement rather than declared [*Spatial hypertext*, CACM 1995]; Tinderbox still ships that after thirty years. Reasoned, grounded in system-building.

## 2. Ink & Switch

The lab is ten years old in 2026, with four research areas, local-first software, malleable software, programmable ink and universal version control, and it is the living centre of this line. Two things it made graduated to products, Automerge and the Muse app now called Allume. Geoffrey Litt, who wrote much of what follows, now works at Notion. Seen.

### 2.1 Patchwork

Patchwork began as version control for writers, lightweight branches, diff visualisation, chat-like history, and became a runtime for malleable software: a registry of datatypes, tools and actions over Automerge documents, where both user data and the software's code live in the same store and tools compose by embedding. A tool is a plugin module that declares which datatypes it supports, and a document or an embed can pin which tool renders it. The lab's own notebook reports the concrete result, that Patchwork made it relatively easy to compose tools in ways their authors did not expect, and one note gets nearest to the code's fifth section: the closer the loop, the less the distinction between what is written and what runs, the better it gets. A published agent skill teaches Claude Code and Cursor how to write Patchwork tools, so the model is the on-ramp to end-user programming. Notebook entries run to May 2026; the sweep could not establish that any Patchwork tool has a user outside the lab. Seen, a research prototype unusually mature.

### 2.2 Cambria, and what it actually taught

Cambria was the lab's answer to schema evolution in distributed systems: a lens, a small script of rename, convert, wrap, hoist and the like, that runs forward over data and backward over data, and over the schema at build time, so one specification yields both directions [Litt, van Hardenberg and Henry 2020]. Three things the essay says are worth more than the lenses. The team started by translating on write and abandoned it, because too much translation happened eagerly and a new schema arriving after a write broke it; they switched to tagging every write with the schema it was written under and translating on read, which is exactly what Avro has done for eighteen years. The headline operator in their own first figure, convert, is not a lens and guarantees no consistency. And the composition law you would need to make chained migrations safe, that applying two lenses one at a time equals applying them together, is one the lens literature had already shown to fail for map, flatten, merge and conditionals, for reasons that seem pragmatically unavoidable [Foster et al. 2007]. The lab lists Cambria as completed in 2020; the repository was last pushed in June 2024; there is no post-mortem. Jazz's second version is the only shipped descendant, treating a schema version as another branch dimension. Seen, and reasoned from the essay's own findings section.

### 2.3 Automerge, Keyhive, Subduction, Onomancy, Backstitch

Automerge 3.0 cut a Moby-Dick-sized document from 700 megabytes to 1.3 and one load from seventeen hours to nine seconds, and it remains about two hundred and fifty times smaller than Yjs by downloads. Around it the lab is building the pieces a versioned medium needs: Keyhive for local-first access control, with a key-agreement paper preprinted in July 2026; Subduction, a sync protocol for hash-linked data that its README marks not for production; Onomancy, human-meaningful names over self-certifying keys, promising that an account created offline already has a globally shareable name and never migrates; and Backstitch, real-time version control for Godot, the lab's first attempt to put universal version control in front of non-programmers. Seen.

### 2.4 The malleable-software essay

The June 2025 essay makes the enclosure argument the code's fifth section makes, from the application side: apps as avocado slicers, data shared among applications as what lets people compose tools, OpenDoc, Smalltalk and the filesystem as the alternatives that were enclosed [Litt, Horowitz, van Hardenberg and Matthews 2025]. On models it is cautious: code generation alone does not address the barriers to malleability, like bringing a talented sous chef to a food court. It does not make the medium-lineage half of the argument, and nobody the sweep found does. Preferred, as an essay; seen.

## 3. Andy Matuschak, and the second brain that was deleted

Matuschak, the most prominent tools-for-thought researcher of the last decade, argued in March 2026 that coding agents without a composable architecture give you zero-to-one silo apps; the two tyrannies are the application silo and programming as a specialisation, and the escape is agents plus declarative architectures like a code editor's facets [Matuschak 2026]. He then left on what he calls para-academic leave to build Pico, a conservatory for human attention. He is no longer building note tools. The era's best-read critique of linked notes is Joan Westenberg's *I deleted my second brain*, 598 points on Hacker News in June 2025 and now a dead link, itself a small data point on rot; the published counter-argument concedes the structural point, that the tool enables a contribution and does not make it. The sweep found no empirical study that linked note-taking improves any outcome. Preferred and seen.

## 4. Luhmann's slips as the archive describes them

The Niklas Luhmann archive counts about ninety thousand slips, roughly twenty-two thousand in the first collection from 1952 to 1960 and sixty-seven thousand in the second from 1961 to early 1997, of which about seventy-five thousand are content notes and the rest bibliography and index. That is forty-five years, not forty. The shape matters more than the count. The first collection has 108 divisions ranging from one slip to over four thousand; the second has eleven blocks of one to nine thousand slips with up to four sub-levels, one division holding 120 list-like subdivisions, and the archive adds that none of this is a strictly hierarchical or systematic relation. It is a counter-example to any shallow-structure rule, not evidence for one. Whether Ahrens's popular account is faithful to it, the sweep could not verify. Measured, from the archive's own description.

## 5. The tools-for-thought market in 2026: files won

Plain Markdown files became a moat in 2026 for a reason nobody predicted: coding agents can read them. Obsidian made its commercial licence optional in February 2025, shipped Bases in May, a database view stored entirely in the Markdown files' properties, and added a command line so an agent can do anything the app can without a model inside the app. It has no native assistant and the tightest release cadence in the category. Every product in the category shipped a command line or a protocol server in 2026, so the model layer is being externalised rather than built in. Seen.

Logseq is the cautionary real story. Its database rewrite, SQLite as the canonical store, nodes unifying pages and blocks, typed properties and tags as classes, shipped as 2.0.1 on 13 July 2026 after a thirteen-month hole in releases. It shipped into a market that had just revalued the thing it gave up: the top comment on its launch was too little, too late, and long-time users described leaving for Obsidian because plain text is non-negotiable and agents can no longer read the files. The file-based app was demoted to maintenance, and the two-way file mirror is still on a branch. Seen; the reading is the community's and the sweep's.

The venture-funded middle left note-taking. Tana raised fourteen million in February 2025 and in March 2026 split itself into a maintenance outliner and a new product for meetings and agents. Notion shipped agents, then custom agents, over a million built by May 2026, and killed its mail product for agent-based workflows. Mem is now a chief of staff, RemNote a study tool, Heptabase a tutor, Fabric a context warehouse, Coda a document surface inside a productivity suite. What remains as tools for thought is bootstrapped and small, Capacities, Craft, Anytype, Amplenote, Supernotes, Allume, or dead, Kosmik and Dendron. Roam is a functional zombie with a protocol server bolted on. The new entrants say the new thesis plainly: Hubble is an open-source note app for you and your agents. Seen.

## 6. Design rationale: the why that would not be captured

This is where the prose-and-links line measured itself, and the measurements are few and small.

### 6.1 gIBIS, QOC, and the capture bottleneck

The chain of representations runs from graphical IBIS, issues, positions and arguments as typed nodes [Conklin and Begeman 1988], through questions, options and criteria [MacLean et al. 1991], to rationale-management tools. Capture cost is richly described and never quantified. The one field evaluation had six experienced designers, external to a project, assess a past design with blueprints plus a professionally scribed QOC document. Questions about rationale were the most frequent kind they asked, and the document answered forty-one percent of them, thirty-one of seventy-six; the author's conclusion is that it is not because such documents are available that they will be consulted, even when they contain what designers need [Karsenty 1996, *CHI*, full text read by the agent]. The document existed only because two dedicated scribes transcribed four two-hour meetings, since the design chief did not believe designers would document rationale themselves.

The one controlled experiment gave seventeen subjects a retrospective rationale document for change tasks on two embedded systems and found significant gains in correctness and speed on one system and inconclusive results on the other, never replicated [Bratthall, Johansson and Regnell 2000]. Eighty-one practitioners say rationale matters and report barriers to capturing it [Tang et al. 2006]. Measured, small; the capture bottleneck is argued, not costed.

### 6.2 Decision records, fifteen years on

Nygard's 2011 post is an experience report, a format used on a few projects since August with positive feedback [Nygard 2011]. ThoughtWorks moved lightweight decision records to Adopt in November 2017 on no data and has not revisited them since. The first measurement came in 2023: from twenty-six million GitHub users, 921 repositories with 6,362 decision-record files. More than half the adopting repositories hold one to five records. About fifty-one percent of the files were committed once and never modified; 278 repositories had every record edited on a single day and then abandoned; forty-nine percent had a single author. Forty-two repositories, 4.6 percent, meet all three criteria of sustained use, twenty records, two authors, six months of editing. Adoption lagged Nygard's post by about five years [Buchgeher et al. 2023, *IEEE Access*]. Measured.

The first study to look for an effect reused that corpus with code-quality and productivity metrics and found predominantly small correlations, at most modest observable effects at scale, and that about sixty-three percent of records are opened directly with the status accepted, bypassing the deliberation they exist to capture [De Santana et al. 2026, *ICSA*]. The one in-situ trial is seven interviews at one consultancy over three months, positive on culture and silent on how many records were written [Ahmeti et al. 2024]. A template comparison with thirty-three undergraduates found Nygard's template beating MADR on comprehension, and its own related-work section states that no prior work shows decision records improve outcomes [Nogueira, Silva and Conte 2026]. The one place a positive, measured effect appears is decision records served to a language model: ten percent less development time on a single project, and rationale carried in records producing the end-to-end tests that the same instruction in a context file produced none of, a single-author, single-run preprint [Kitayama 2026]. And for generating records, a window of the three to five most recent matches full history [Gupta et al. 2026]. Measured, small; the preprint is a hypothesis.

### 6.3 Design documents and the six-pager

The canonical text on design documents is a 2020 blog post with no data and no sequel [Ubl 2020]. The only number Google has published about its own documentation is a footnote: when an internal wiki was retired, about ninety percent of its documents had no views or updates in the previous few months. The one controlled study of design-document format found none, as [the reading file](reading.md#33-preference-is-not-comprehension) says. The six-pager traces entirely to a 2017 shareholder letter that gives no measurement. A catalogue of over a hundred companies' processes is descriptive throughout; the sweep found no company reporting a measured result. Preferred, all of it.

## 7. Proposal processes: the heavy ones have no exit

The one consistent pattern across the open-source proposal processes shows in numbers the projects do not publish. Rust has 4,003 proposal pull requests ever and 639 accepted; 215 are open with a median age of 905 days and thirty-five older than five years, while the lightweight compiler-team process beside it has taken 585 proposals and holds seventeen open. Kubernetes has 343 enhancement proposals in a non-terminal state, fourteen ever explicitly killed, and 184 with nothing shipped since late 2024; the issue asking to streamline the process rotted out under a stale bot after two and a half years.

The IETF's editorial stream produced three documents in four years, and its newest process document is the genuine retrospective, admitting no appeal pathway existed and that never changing a published document was unworkable [RFC 9920]. The only throughput measurement anywhere is twenty documents from 2018 averaging 1,200 days from draft to publication, eighty-three percent of it inside working groups [RFC 8963]. Python did not fix its process; it shrank what a proposal must decide by standing up four domain councils. Oxide's requests for discussion publish no metrics and the public index now redirects to a login. The best critiques are opinion: proposal processes lack an explicit decide step and so default to no [Kaplan-Moss 2023]; one template for all domains and no tracking of whether an accepted proposal shipped [Cameron 2022]. Seen, computed by the agent on the day; the critiques preferred.
