# Beyond prose: the typed lineage and its graveyard

This is for the code's fifth section, read from the sweep: the systems that tried to make structure itself the medium, the semantic web, typed and versioned stores, schema evolution, permission, structure editors and interfaces as data. It gives you what died, what lived, and the shape the survivors share, so that whatever is built next starts from the record rather than repeating it.

## 1. Vocabularies outgrow their use, hierarchies rot, validation beat reasoning

The open web's own numbers say how much vocabulary is used. Of schema.org's 958 types and 4,587 properties, twelve types reach ten million domains and about seventy-seven percent of the vocabulary sits below a thousand; a closed vocabulary of a dozen kinds is roughly what the web converged on anyway. A curated, principled, governed registry of biomedical ontologies is twenty-nine percent inactive or orphaned. Transitive subclassing is the thing that rots under many hands: about 2.39 million Wikidata classes are simultaneously marked as first- and second-order, with 1.9 million problematic pairs, the root cause being editors' confusion of instance-of with subclass-of [Patel-Schneider and Doğan 2024]. Wikidata split its query service in two in May 2025 because sixteen billion triples growing a billion a year took one to two months to reload; the flagship graph hit its ceiling and bolted on a vector index. The most successful W3C vocabulary made hierarchy non-transitive on purpose in 2009, with the transitive closure a separate opt-in property [SKOS 2009]. Cyc spent about two hundred million dollars and two thousand person-years on thirty million assertions and invented microtheories because the whole could not be kept consistent; the contexts proliferated rather than consolidated. Measured and seen.

The reasoning layer is effectively dead and the validation layer won. The OWL reasoners' repositories were last pushed in 2017, 2022 and 2025; the Python SHACL validator's downloads went from 423 thousand to over a million a month in five months, thirteen times the OWL library's, and the W3C is now standardising inference as datalog-style rules under the shapes working group, not description logic under an OWL one. RDF 1.2 is structurally blocked: its concepts document cannot advance until a concrete syntax leaves candidate stage, and none has entered. The claim that a major financial ontology uses SHACL did not survive checking. The canonical argument for shapes over OWL is by the chief technologist of a company that sells shape tooling, and is nonetheless the one everyone silently reproduces. Datalog, in the same key, wins as an internal representation and loses as a surface: the datalog-first bitemporal database rewrote itself around SQL and its homepage no longer mentions datalog; two pure datalog engines are archived or abandoned; and Rust's new borrow checker shipped in August 2026 with a non-datalog reformulation because the datalog one was a non-starter on performance. Seen.

## 2. Graphs built by models lose to plain retrieval; declared vocabularies win

Independent benchmarks converged in 2025 and 2026. Not all graph-based retrieval methods beat plain retrieval; the global variant needs fifty-seven times the time and two hundred and ten times the tokens per query, about nine minutes and three hundred thousand tokens; and the method that most often wins has no entity graph at all, and does as well with plain clustering [Zhou et al. 2025, *PVLDB*]. Graph retrieval frequently underperforms vanilla retrieval on real tasks [GraphRAG-Bench 2026]. On summarisation, its home turf, plain retrieval scored higher; only about sixty-five percent of answer entities appear in the constructed graphs; and the original win rates used a judging method later shown position-biased [Han et al. 2026, *KDD*]. Microsoft put its reference implementation into maintenance mode in August 2026, citing the changed capabilities of frontier models; the cheap variant it announced never shipped as code. The much-cited claim that knowledge graphs make models three times more accurate is forty-three questions, vendor-authored, against a zero-shot baseline, with a hand-built ontology; it is evidence for human-built semantic layers, not extracted graphs. Measured.

The distinction that survives is who declared the types. A model reconstructing column metadata from values scored 0.223 on ninety-four percent coverage, and 0.475 on the forty-two percent it would commit to inside a code-enforced gate, the gate being a competence detector, not a competence amplifier; abstention requested in the prompt did not transfer between models [Rosetta 2026]. Schema compliance is near-perfect across twenty-one models while value accuracy is eighty-three percent on text and twenty-four on audio; a 369-field financial schema produced zero valid outputs from every frontier model. On the other side, an ontology built with no unconstrained generation step and a provenance chain on every term beat direct generation on competency coverage, 0.85 against 0.63; thirteen predefined memory categories with no extraction beat graph and vector memory on both standard benchmarks; a governed semantic layer took thirty-seven of thirty-eight enterprise questions on every run against twenty-one for direct schema-to-SQL. For many writers, a status CRDT reconverged in thirty of thirty partition heals where last-writer-wins managed eleven, preserving contradictions rather than resolving them silently. Induced vocabularies lose; small declared vocabularies win. Measured, paper-only, independent.

## 3. Versioned, typed stores: the exact idea exists, and it is fragile

The claim that nobody combines version control with knowledge structure has been false since 2019. TerminusDB is git-style commits, branches, diff and merge over a schema-constrained document graph with a datalog query language; it is the closest existing product to a versioned typed knowledge store, and it is one maintainer, handed to a third-party company in 2025 after a twelve-month release gap, with its marketing site down. It is the only system that models schema change as a first-class branchable operation, through weakening, a change that cannot invalidate existing data, with everything else an explicit replayable migration; and its operation for migrating the inheritance hierarchy, the most structural change there is, is unimplemented. Fluree is the second instance, and it already does eighty percent of a query-as-permission design: a policy is a where-clause transacted as data and evaluated per datum inside query execution, so aggregates over a filtered set follow for free. Seen.

Nobody merges structure; they serialise it. Dolt, the thriving versioned relational store, separates data conflicts, resolvable row by row, from schema conflicts, which prevent the merge from completing and resolve by taking the entire table from one side. Iceberg has branches and tags and no branch merge, only fast-forward. Datomic, the strongest-validated accretion store, with no safety bugs found at ninety-four million users and 2.3 billion transactions a day, documents that every datom is stored at least three times, that excision costs time proportional to the whole database, and that a common modelling mistake is to assume everything temporal belongs in it, recommending no-history on most attributes.

Willow and Meadowcap are the formalised version of intersection-as-permission, a capability granted over an area expressed in the same formalism as the sync protocol, and their reference implementations stalled in early 2025 with no commercial owner. ATProto has typed records rooted in DNS and no commit graph, its previous-commit pointer virtually always null. Postgres row-level security is the boring correct answer to uniform filtering, policies as predicates so counts follow, per table and with documented covert channels; Elasticsearch's document-level security states outright that it does not affect index statistics and that a restricted user can count how many inaccessible documents contain a term.

Consolidation took the independents: Gel to Vercel with the repository silent since, Electric to Databricks, Permify absorbed, Kuzu archived, Oso's open-source engine deprecated. The four-part conjunction, membership-only typing, owner-scoped contracts, one-hop permission and branch versioning of the type system itself, was found in no system; the thinnest literature is branching an owner-scoped contract, where every shipped system gives up. Seen and measured.

## 4. Permission: fan-out, not depth

Google's authorisation system names transitive membership as the hard problem in its own words: recursive pointer chasing has difficulty maintaining low latency with groups that are deeply nested or have many children; its denormalised index turns a single tuple write into potentially tens of thousands of index events and is applied only to selected namespaces; check latency runs from three milliseconds at the median to ninety-three at the 99.9th percentile [Pang et al. 2019, *USENIX ATC*]. The clones invented depth limits, fifty and twenty-five hops, with no dedicated cycle detector in one, and are now removing them as the wrong metric, because a shallow recursive graph can cost more than a deep linear one; the cost driver is fan-out. Their list-filtering escape hatches are check-then-filter loops or a materialised permission table for search, analytics and retrieval, which is the same denormalisation reinvented commercially. Seen, with vendor-authored numbers.

The vendors of shared drives conceded the same thing from the other side. Google Drive removed the ability to give someone less access to a file than to its parent folder. Notion respects the broadest level of access given. Microsoft's own documentation calls its restricted search a short-term solution that is not intended or scalable for long-term use and not a security boundary, blocks new enablement from July 2026, and gives as its worked example a budgeting site whose owner has not set up proper permissions and whose contents a copilot therefore returns. Latent transitive permissions were harmless while discovery was manual; a retrieval layer performs the closure exhaustively, so the model did not create the leak, it enumerated it. Post-filtering a vector search by permission wrecks recall by construction. Seen; the last sentence is the sweep's reasoning.

## 5. Schema evolution: forbid, do not translate

Every production system that survived schema change bought safety by forbidding things, and none by translating. Avro sends the writer's schema with the data and resolves at read, matching fields by name, filling absent reader fields from defaults, ignoring absent writer fields, erroring where a reader field has no default; every field must have a default or can never be removed, and every write's schema must be retrievable forever. Protobuf forbids reusing a field number, listing the consequences as a parse error at best and leaked personal data or corruption at worst, and removed required fields from the language entirely, because a constraint you can never relax is a liability that compounds. Datomic says you can never alter a value type, and that travelling back in time does not take the schema back in time. ATProto's rule is that all old data must be valid under the new schema and new data valid under the old, enforced by four prohibitions, new fields optional, no removing required fields, no changing types, no renames, with the escape hatch of a new name in a new namespace; data that fails to validate is entirely invalid and not to be repaired.

Kafka's compatibility modes are really an upgrade-order decision, and its default, backward and non-transitive, lets a chain of pairwise-compatible changes be globally incompatible; for a federation where nobody's upgrade can be ordered, full-transitive is the only honest setting, which is ATProto's rule in prose. The largest public GraphQL API abandoned deprecation-only for quarterly versions and delists apps that keep calling removed resources; deprecation-only survives while you can nag every consumer. Tolerance in what a peer may hand you now entrenches errors [RFC 9413]. Seen.

The option space for two stores whose contracts diverge, ranked by how well it survives peers who never upgrade: namespace the divergence into a different contract; freeze the format and change by new container plus pointer; restrict changes to the intersection of backward and forward; refuse, correct and useless at scale; translate, the only option that preserves both sides and the only one with no production evidence. The sweep's schema-evolution agent, going deeper on Cambria, concluded that lenses should not be built, and that stamping every write with the contract it validated against is the highest-value, lowest-cost move, since a body validated in one year is otherwise indistinguishable at read time from a body that would fail the contract three years later. The eager-versus-lazy question for re-derived indexes has a measured answer: create new views empty and populate on demand, which completes immediately, against a minute of degraded service for eager materialisation [Noria 2018]. Reasoned, on measured pieces; [the kin file](hypertext.md#22-cambria-and-what-it-actually-taught) carries what Cambria itself found.

## 6. Code as a database, and the editor the model took

Three ventures that stored code as structure rather than text converged on the same funding answer within a year: consulting. Unison, which stores code in a database because other tools try to recover structure from text, shipped 1.0 in November 2025, eight years in, and in February 2026 announced a consulting group, aiming to be cash-flow positive within twelve months, with release cadence halved. Glamorous Toolkit, objects, not text, is consultancy-funded and repositioned onto making sense of systems now that code generation is fast. Dark Inc ran out of money in June 2025, and its founder's post-mortem is the real story: the product was on track until ChatGPT arrived and it became obvious that an online structured editor did not make sense when the model generates the code, in a separate place from where people now work; the editor had been half the codebase and too fancy for our own good; the successor company shipped a version a week in August 2026 with the model as its pitch [Biggar 2025; Stachu 2024]. Seen; the words are the founders'.

The research side says the missing piece is theory. Jonathan Edwards's retrospective calls Subtext a series of overambitious failed experiments in interlocking hard problems, lacking an underlying theory to map a path the way languages have lambda calculus and databases have relational algebra, and asks of the substrates community, are we even a field. Hazel is the live one, with typed holes that contextualise a language model's completions with what the type checker knows [OOPSLA 2024], and a strategic turn toward classroom proof assistance. Webstrates' Varv, the sharpest concept model for interactive software as a declarative data structure, has been build-bot-only since October 2024; Mavo has 2,800 stars, 277 open issues and no maintainer commit in two years; Dynamicland's public material stops at 2024. Seen; Edwards's assessment preferred.

## 7. Interfaces as data: the shape everyone converged on

Five companies built server-driven interfaces at scale and hit the same wall. Airbnb's platform dispatches by an explicit discriminator the server names, keeps layouts as a closed set with a native renderer per client, and listed nested sections and design-tool editing as future work in 2021 with no evidence either shipped. Plaid's versioning says it flatly: adding a new kind of pane is a major version requiring a client upgrade, so the promised benefit holds only within the vocabulary already shipped. DoorDash found the obvious design wrong, fallback components were unnecessary because a new component is a specific new design, and gated its untyped escape hatch with difficulty until the design system became a typed vocabulary. Lyft named the escape hatch as an architectural tier and admitted the line is hazy.

Uber's Screenflow came closest to a compiled model, a restricted subset of TypeScript with definitions carrying types and defaults, and its versioning answer is the correct one: the build recompiled every screen against every previously shipped runtime; after two years it had sixteen flows in production and the department was shut down in July 2020. The 2021 practitioner critique still stands: a stripped-down HTML, caching, animation, and coupling every client to the backend. Spotify's framework is deprecated, Nubank's deleted, Yandex's and Shopify's alive, the latter mapping remote components to native implementations over a message channel at six hundred thousand downloads a month. Seen.

The protocol race for generated interfaces ended in January 2026 with an opaque winner: a sandboxed HTML iframe with no props schema, data arriving by notification, twelve million downloads a month, eleven hosts, the two prior contenders folded in. Typed component trees lost at the protocol layer and thrive one layer up, where a compact streaming language parses into a typed element tree validated against a schema generated from the application's own component library. The product view layers all have an extensible instance schema and a closed set of view kinds, ten in Notion, six or seven in Airtable, six dataview types and twenty-eight object layouts as closed enums in Anytype's protobuf, and one level deeper, no user-defined property types either; view configuration is everywhere a union discriminated by the view-kind tag, which is precisely what a declaration-and-call model replaces.

Whether a component's accepted types are queryable at runtime is answered yes in exactly one mainstream system: WordPress's block registry declares attributes, allowed parents, ancestors and children, and exposes which block types a set of blocks can be transformed into, with the highest-priority matching transform winning. The form world's ranked-tester registry is the same algorithm, and its maintainer wrote in May 2026 that logic leaks into the renderers, that customising anything means overriding every renderer, and that the answer is to abstract away from the schema. React removed its only runtime prop contract in version 19, silently ignored. The manifest format that could have become the cross-framework answer has not changed since May 2024 and is read only by documentation viewers. Seen.

## 8. Low-code: bifurcation, and where the money went

The load-bearing datum for any derived-interface bet is Retool's reversal of June 2026. It relaunched on React and TypeScript, saying its own visual abstraction had become a liability because it prevented language models from working fluently, and repositioned as the governance layer that imports applications built elsewhere. Read precisely, that is a verdict against closed, non-inspectable derivation, not against derivation; every survivor's remaining moat is typed contracts, data access rules, permission inheritance, schema. The rest of the market bifurcated. Airtable sold for 1.28 billion dollars on about 480 million of recurring revenue, from a peak mark of 11.7 billion. Wix cut a fifth of its staff naming a slowdown among the agencies that build for others. Pegasystems told the regulator that unprecedented changes in the AI market caused clients to delay purchasing decisions. Appian grew nineteen percent and deleted the words low-code from its boilerplate. The money relocated: Lovable reached five hundred million of annualised revenue in eighteen months and raised at 13.3 billion, Replit at nine billion, both self-reported on run-rate, and a six-month-old builder sold for eighty million. Directus put a licence key in front of its self-hosted defaults. Seen; the reading of Retool is the sweep's.
