# Beyond prose: what has been tried

This is what shipped systems and 2026 studies say about the code's [§5, how far this goes](../code.md#5-how-far-this-goes): a medium in which a program, a screen and a model can each say what it takes and gives, and be joined by that. It stands on [the adversarial stress test](../../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/aa5f5baaa66cc8bf1.md), [the semantic-web report](../../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/adc9dab44aefdde97.md), [the typed-substrate landscape](../../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/abffcf492c6f3d77d.md), [the schema-evolution deep read](../../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/ad341d6da8eaac361.md), [the agent-memory delta](../../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/a187277ed3a006fbc.md), [the server-driven-UI report](../../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/a57740ddd62e39ab6.md), [the schema-driven-UI report](../../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/a458ace67969949f3.md) and [the report on who else argues this](../../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/a09867b88abcb610b.md), which hold the system links, the exact wording and the full numbers behind every finding here. Reading it, you know which parts of that hope have precedent that worked, which have precedent that died, and which nobody has built.

Three things worked: describing what surrounds a text rather than the text, with a small vocabulary people declared; hierarchies and permissions that do not chain; and versioning built into storage rather than into a product. Six things died: describing the content itself with types; anything that chains, at scale; translating data automatically between versions of a schema; finding things by combining properties instead of walking a tree; screens sent as data with an open set of components to draw them; and editors that hold code as structure, once models could write the text. Four things are not built: merging two versions of a schema; a component chosen at run time by asking a shared registry what can draw a thing; a model's answer that can be re-derived from an addressable context; and the medium itself. Reasoned, on the sections below, where each is given with its ground.

## 1. Text won the payload; type the edges

The field moved away from typing the content and toward typing its edges, what surrounds a text: who wrote it, where it lives, who may read it, when it was last true. Every 2026 system that won typed those and left the body as prose or code; every loser typed the body. Seen, across the systems in this file; the reasoning is [the stress test's](../../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/aa5f5baaa66cc8bf1.md), which carries the argument in full.

The clearest proof is the July 2026 revision of MCP, the protocol by which models reach tools. It dropped everything conversational, sessions and the back-and-forth a server could start with a model, and at the same time required every server to describe itself, every result to carry its type and every list to say how long it may be cached. A protocol that stopped trying to be the model's conversation became a typed description of what is on offer, underneath it. Seen, against the published changelog.

Machine-facing descriptions move outcomes where human-facing overviews do not. Rewriting the descriptions of tools, of which 97 percent carry at least one defect across 856 tools, raised median task success 5.85 points at 67 percent more steps and with regressions in a sixth of cases *[Hasan and others 2026]*; descriptions that follow the standard are selected 72 percent of the time against 20 for ones that do not *[Wang and others 2026]*. Skills, packaged instruction folders a model loads on demand, are adopted in the tens of thousands, and the only controlled benchmark found them lowering pass rate by one to four points at 72 to 394 percent more tokens; every one of 105 software release transitions in a second study invalidated part of a skill set silently. Measured, by others; single papers each.

A type that must be guessed is worse than prose. In a 2026 study called Rosetta, a model asked to reconstruct the meaning of database columns from their values was right 22 percent of the time when it answered 94 percent of them; the same model behind a check that let it decline was right 48 percent of the time on the 42 percent it committed to, and wrote no better descriptions where both spoke, "a competence detector, not a competence amplifier", in the authors' words. Across 21 models in a second study the output always fit the schema while the values in it were right 83 percent of the time for text, 67 for images and 24 for audio, and in a third, on a 369-field financial schema, every frontier model produced nothing valid. A wrong type answers confidently where incomplete prose fails visibly, and letting the model decline has to be enforced in code, since asking for it in the prompt did not carry across models. Measured, by others; single papers, several of them, named in the stress test.

Small declared vocabularies win where induced ones lose. Thirteen predefined memory categories with no extraction of names from the text beat systems that build a graph from it, on the long-memory benchmarks; a typed language of fifteen relational operations beat free-form SQL and heavy pipelines; a hand-curated description of an enterprise's data answered 37 of 38 questions on every run against 21 for the raw schema, with the authors declining the causal claim. The open web converged the same way: of the 958 types in the shared vocabulary for web pages, twelve reach ten million sites and three quarters sit below a thousand. Measured and seen; the three studies are Memanto, ReAct-SQL and SPC, all 2026, in the stress test.

The stress test's decision rule is where a decision to type something should start. Structure wins when a rule must hold even if the model is wrong, when the query is a count or an intersection rather than a find, when there are many writers and no coordinator, when someone who was not there must answer later, or when cost must stay proportional to the size of the catalogue; prose wins when the payload is read once by one reader who will exercise judgment. The hard gate over all five: if the type would have to be inferred rather than declared or observed, do not type it. Reasoned, by the stress test's author, on the studies in this section.

## 2. Anything transitive rots

A chain of "is a kind of" fails under many-owner editing, and the successful vocabulary refused to chain on purpose. Wikidata, the largest collaborative knowledge base, holds about 2.39 million classes marked as both a class and a class of classes, and 1.9 million pairs in conflict, from editors confusing "is an instance of" with "is a kind of" *[Patel-Schneider and Doğan 2024]*. SKOS, the W3C's vocabulary for thesauri and the most deployed of its kind, defines "broader" as a direct link only, with chaining a separate opt-in. The flagship graph split its query service in 2025 because 16 billion facts could no longer be reloaded, and the tooling for logical inference has decayed while the tooling that checks data against declared shapes grows several-fold. Measured and seen.

The same holds for a group inside a group. Google's authorisation system says in its own paper that walking nested groups "has difficulty maintaining low latency with groups that are deeply nested or have a large number of child groups", and its index of pre-computed memberships turns one write into "potentially tens of thousands" of index events *[Pang and others 2019]*. Its open-source descendants capped nesting at 50 and 25 levels, and one is now removing the cap because depth was the wrong measure and the number of branches is the cost. Seen, in the paper and the vendors' documents.

Retrieval by a model enumerates what nesting hid. Google Drive no longer lets a file be narrower than its folder; Notion grants the broadest access a person has anywhere above; and Microsoft documents that its restricted search for its assistant "isn't a security boundary" and is being retired, because a model that answers from everything a person may read follows every inherited permission at once. The model did not create the leak; it enumerated it. Seen, in the vendors' own documents; the detail is in [the permission sweep](../../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/aea19b144c46bfc26.md).

## 3. Schema change is the unsolved problem

The one serious attempt at automatic translation between schemas stopped, and its real result was a pivot. Cambria, a project of the research lab Ink & Switch, defined lenses (two-way translations between two versions of a schema, written once and run in either direction). It began by translating on write and found writes racing newly registered schemas, so it switched to tagging every write with the schema it was written under and translating on read, which is the rule the Avro data format had used for years; its flagship operator turned out not to be a lens; its performance was never measured; and the lab lists it as completed in 2020 with the code last touched in 2024. The mathematics beneath it shows why: translations that can be safely chained cannot include the operations you would want, such as merging or reshaping lists *[Foster and others 2007]*. Seen, in the essay's own findings; the detail is in [the schema-evolution deep read](../../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/ad341d6da8eaac361.md).

Everything that survived schema change bought safety by forbidding, not translating. The formats and databases that have lived longest under change all forbid the same things: a field's number may never be reused, a field's type may never change, a field may not be renamed, and anything larger takes a new name. Kafka's schema registry checks a new version against the previous one only, by default, and only its strictest mode lets writers and readers upgrade independently; Shopify abandoned deprecation-only for quarterly versions. Every sync engine forces the client to upgrade, one by reloading the page. Seen.

For a store like the one the code's §5 imagines, where a type is a record carrying a contract, the transferable rule is three lines: stamp every write with the version of the contract it was checked against; apply changes that only widen a contract in place, and give any change that narrows one a new contract; and do not build lenses. Reasoned, on the systems above.

Nobody merges structure. Dolt, a database with git's branches, blocks a merge on a schema conflict and resolves it by taking a whole side; Iceberg, the table format of the data lake, has tags and fast-forward and no branch merge; the one store that models schema change as a branchable operation, TerminusDB, has the migration of its class hierarchy explicitly unimplemented. Structure merges only inside a set of widening changes, and everywhere outside it the shipped answer is stop, take one side, or make a new table. Seen.

## 4. Versioned knowledge stores exist and are fragile

Version control over structured knowledge has shipped since 2019, and what happened to it is instructive. TerminusDB does commits, branches and diff over a typed document graph; it went a year without a release, was handed to a third-party maintainer in 2025, and its marketing site is down. Fluree does an immutable typed ledger with permission written as a query and evaluated on every fact read. Dolt made versioning a property of storage under four database wire protocols, which is the one form of it that is thriving. Willow and Meadowcap, a peer-to-peer protocol pair, specify permission and sync in the identical formalism, and their reference implementations are orphaned. Seen; the map is [the typed-substrate landscape](../../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/abffcf492c6f3d77d.md).

## 5. Retrieval over structure lost to plainer things

Finding things by combining properties died for want of the field, not the idea. Semantic file systems, Microsoft's cancelled WinFS, faceted browsers: choosing by combining properties needs clean typed metadata on every item, and nobody ever had it, so folders plus full-text search won on cost. Seen.

What changed in 2026 is that metadata can be produced cheaply and wrongly. A model can fill the fields, at the 22 to 83 percent accuracy [§1](#1-text-won-the-payload-type-the-edges) measures unless it is allowed to decline, which is a new failure mode and not the old one solved. Reasoned, on §1.

Retrieval over a graph a model extracted from text loses to retrieval over plain text embeddings on simple questions, and costs 57 times the time and 210 times the tokens per query; the method that wins the independent benchmark has no graph; Microsoft's flagship graph project went to maintenance mode in August 2026, and its cheaper successor was announced with a cost figure and never shipped *[Zhou and others 2025]*. Measured, by others; the detail is in [the GraphRAG evaluations](../../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/a7ea3bc0963c0e185.md).

Datalog, a logic query language, wins as an internal representation and loses as a surface. The Datalog-first database rewrote itself around SQL and no longer mentions it, and the Rust compiler's ownership checker shipped a non-Datalog reformulation in August 2026 because the Datalog one was "a non-starter" on performance. Seen; the census is in [the Datalog and versioning sweep](../../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/a96d8d0c3161e997c.md).

## 6. Screens sent as data ship with a closed set of components

Five companies hit the same wall and built the same fix. Airbnb, Plaid, DoorDash, Lyft and Shopify all send screens from the server as data, and all converged on the client saying which kinds of component it can draw and the server sending nothing else; none solved shipping a new kind of component to an old client, and Plaid's specification makes a new one a major version requiring an app upgrade. The server names the component by a fixed key rather than asking which component can draw the data; the escape hatch is a named tier, an untyped field or "semantic components", and its boundary erodes. Uber's Screenflow, a restricted TypeScript compiled to an intermediate form with a build that recompiled every screen against every runtime ever shipped, is the closest anyone came to the code's model, and it was shut down in 2020 with sixteen flows. Seen.

The product tools all stop one level short. Notion's views, Airtable's field types and Anytype's layouts are closed lists in the protocol: a user may add kinds of thing and properties on them, and may not add a kind of view or a kind of property, and nobody lets a third party register a component that offers to draw a type. The exception is a content management system: WordPress blocks declare what they accept and what may contain them, and the registry answers which block types a piece of content can become, at run time, by ranked predicate; a form library does the same with ranked testers, and its maintainer wrote in 2026 that the abstraction leaks and the schema should be demoted. The one protocol that consolidated interfaces generated for models, MCP Apps in January 2026, chose an embedded web page with no declared inputs, twelve million downloads a month, and typed component trees thrive one layer up, where the app owns both model and component. Seen.

Retool's reversal is a real story with the code's lesson in it. For a decade Retool sold a visual abstraction for building internal tools. In June 2026 it relaunched on React and TypeScript, saying its own abstractions had "prevented LLMs from working fluently", and repositioned as the governance layer that imports apps generated elsewhere and wraps them in permissions and audit. What it kept as the moat was the typed layer, schema, access rules, connections; what it threw away was the closed, non-inspectable derivation. Seen, in vendor posts and filings; the reading is reasoned, and the market it sits in is [the low-code reckoning](../../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/ae0322198c23f4ec2.md), not brought in.

## 7. The harness did not dissolve

Nobody argues the whole thesis, and its nearest neighbours reject the parts they do not hold. Ink & Switch's Patchwork has the interface model running: tools declare the data types they support, a document may pin a tool, and which tool can draw a thing is a query over a registry, with the lab shipping an agent skill that teaches a coding agent to write tools for it. Its own findings: do not standardise the contract before you have users, since the typed-blocks standard before it is paused; and schema change will be your hardest problem. A fact-with-lineage database, a memory-in-the-harness vendor and an enterprise ontology each hold two or three of the code's claims. The one claim nobody makes is that the harness dissolves; the field made a common noun of "harness" in 2026 and shrank it, and the memory vendor's stated position is that memory "cannot be bolted on", it is the harness's job. Seen; the survey is [the report on who else argues this](../../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/a09867b88abcb610b.md).

Branching shipped; reproducibility did not. Both leading coding harnesses fork, branch and rewind sessions, and one reuses the cached shared prefix so a branch is cheaper than a rerun. Nobody in production can say a completion came from exactly this context, re-derivable: summarising the context on the server and consolidating memory in the background make the recorded context non-reconstructible by design, and the one formal treatment of safe checkpoint, fork, restore and merge, a 2026 paper the memory report gives by its arXiv number, 2608.22928, notes that shipped systems do these without deriving what each must preserve. Seen.

Memory went to files, and the holes are the ones the code names. The largest vendor's agent memory is a workspace of text documents at paths, every change an immutable version attributed to the session; the second's is generated memories with revisions and access conditions. What no lab builds is memory shared across harnesses; three memory vendors rushed to build exactly that within six weeks of each other. Poisoning is unsolved: in a 2026 study the memory report gives as arXiv 2608.21230, poisoning 1.2 percent of a memory corpus dropped accuracy from 85 to 30 percent, a screening pipeline rejected none of 360 poisoned memories, and weighting retrieval by where a memory came from was indistinguishable from no defence. Seen for the products; measured for the paper.

## 8. The opposition, and what models did to structured media

The opposition is worth holding in full. The bitter lesson, the argument from machine learning that general methods fed with compute beat hand-built structure, applied to representation: three years of ways to extend a model, each less structured than the last, ending in Markdown folders, and every year the "paste it in" option cheaper, with million-token context general on frontier models from March 2026. Formality considered harmful, the essay in [what documentation does, §3](documentation.md#3-records-of-decisions-are-written-once-and-left): typed structure imposes a cost at capture that users route around, unrefuted since 1999. And the inverse principle of Dynamicland, Bret Victor's research lab, "to maximize agency, minimize what the computer knows": a rich typed substrate that programs consume and people do not read is another opacity. Seen, in the sources' own words.

The stress test's answer locates the objection. Structure costs agency when imposed as a tax at authoring and buys it when offered as an inspection surface at decision time; one small user study of 16 people found that decomposing an agent's spreadsheet work into typed, inspectable actions improved their comprehension of the task, and one study of model monitors found legibility gains that grow with the monitor's strength. Reasoned, by the stress test's author, on thin measurement.

Three real stories carry the cause of what models did to structured media. Dark Inc, which sold a structured code editor, ran out of money in 2025, its founder writing that "our online structured editor didn't make sense when the LLM is generating the code". Logseq moved its notes from files to a database and shipped four years late, in July 2026, into a market that had just re-valued plain files because agents can read them, and Obsidian, files plus a view layer over them, is the default winner. The venture-funded note-taking tools in between, Tana, Mem and Notion among them, moved their products to meetings and agents. Seen, in the founders' and communities' own words; the detail is in [the tools-for-thought report](../../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/a3f6ddd86cf882a6d.md) and [the structure-editor status report](../../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/a5437aa6a38bae10c.md).

## 9. Open

The crux experiment does not exist: structure declared by people with a model's help, against a well-written prose document, on the same corpus, read by the same frontier model; every 2026 win beat an extracted or unstructured baseline, never a good prose one. Whether a branched, owner-scoped contract can be merged is where the literature is thinnest and every shipped system capitulates. Whether any Patchwork tool has a user outside the lab, and whether the type-production bottleneck clears at higher precision, are unestablished. And the lineage the code draws, Unix's pipes, the web's links, then briefs joined by links, is drawn by nobody else the sweep could find; the half of it that is argued, applications enclosing what a medium once shared, is Ink & Switch's.
