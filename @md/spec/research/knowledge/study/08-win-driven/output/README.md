# What the code stands on

[The code](../code.md) asks every rule to say how strongly it holds, and says its grounds are not yet written. This is a first cut of those grounds from one source: a web sweep run on 2026-08-26, forty-five reports by research agents, each on one subject, read whole. Reading it, you know which of the code's rules the world's evidence carries (supports, with a measured effect behind them), which it bounds (supports, but only so far), which it says to stop claiming, and, for the code's [§5, how far this goes](../code.md#5-how-far-this-goes), what has been tried beyond prose. It is written for whoever writes under the code, or is deciding to, and wants to know where they stand on measurement and where on preference.

The code is firmest where it is least ambitious. Its rules about words, everyday words, one point to a paragraph, a story where it carries the causes, are carried by the largest and steadiest effects in the reading literature, and those effects hold for experts as much as for novices.

Its rule that confidence is written is carried not by a study but by a record. Nearly every number the software field repeats about documentation, the cost of late change and how much a head can hold traces to nothing, or to something else, and became law by being unmarked.

Its rules about structure, the why first, the brief that stands alone, the holarchy, are carried too, but by small effects measured on students reading short passages once. The effects shrink for the readers the code most wants, people who know the field and read at their own pace, and vanish a week later. Nobody has measured how deep or how wide a hierarchy should be.

For a model the evidence splits along a different line. Structure helps a session find and does not help it understand: a file that says where things are moves an agent's success, a file that explains the repository does not, and costs more. So the code's claim that writing for people and building context for models is one problem holds for the contract, the holarchy and the link, and fails for the explanation.

Beyond prose, the code hopes for a medium in which a program, a screen and a model can each say what it takes and gives, and be joined by that. What has been built keeps every body of content as plain text and describes only what surrounds it, who wrote it, where it is, who may read it. The two hardest parts of the rest, changing a description once data exists under it and merging two versions of one, are unsolved everywhere.

## 1. What a number here is worth

The sweep is forty-five reports: eighteen named runs, each on one subject, and twenty-seven sub-agents they spawned. Each report follows a fixed skeleton, headline, map, findings with a link and a date, what moved, what could not be established, leads. [The sweep's register](../../../../sweep-2026-08/README.md) names the runs and their subjects; each file in this folder opens by linking the reports it stands on, which hold the paper links, the exact wording and the numbers behind it. Seen.

xTwo things bound every figure. Most agents exhausted their web-search budget early and fell back to fetching primary sources directly, so the sweep over-samples what has an open PDF or a public API, standards bodies, GitHub, preprint servers, and under-samples blogs, paywalled journals, trade press and anything not in English. And two agents independently caught an automated summariser inventing quotations and sample sizes, and re-verified their findings against the primary texts. So a figure here is worth what its report's verification note says, and no more. Seen.

Every claim in this folder closes with one of the code's marks. Measured means a study with a stated population, verified by the sweep against its primary text unless the mark says otherwise; where a study is a single paper not yet replicated, the mark says so in plain words. Seen means a shipped system, a document or a record read directly. Reasoned means an inference made here. Where a figure is folklore, the code's failure case, an unmarked guess that became law, it is named as such. Preferred.

## 2. How people read

A short framing at the top helps, a little, and helps most with what it names. Bounded units and few links help, and a hierarchy beats a network, though the effects are smaller for readers who set their own pace and fade within a week. What holds strongly is words: complex sentences tax experts as much as anyone, a specific instruction is followed far more than a vague one, and a story is understood and remembered better than the same content as statements, by the largest margin in the literature. [How people read](reading.md) gives each finding with its size and its population, and says what it does to the rule that stands on it.

## 3. What documentation does

No study has manipulated documentation and measured what an organisation got from it. What exists is small and old, a survey multiplier withdrawn by its publisher, and a record of decision documents mostly written once and left. Insight fails to travel mainly because the receiver cannot absorb it, and a document stays true only while something consumes it. [What documentation does](documentation.md) gives the evidence and what it bounds in the code's promise that nobody has to be walked through it.

## 4. What a model reads

For a session, what is in the context matters more than how much, and a well-organised long document is not a better one. Rules are lost when the context is summarised unless they are kept outside the summary. One level of pointers pays and a second does not. Prose that explains a repository does not move an agent's success; pointers to the right files do; and the chain of headings above a piece of text, carried with it, helps it be found. [What a model reads](agents.md) gives the studies and the line where the code's one-problem claim breaks.

## 5. The numbers not to cite

Some thirty figures circulate as measured and are not: the 1:10:100 cost of late change, the ten-to-one ratio of reading to writing, seven plus or minus two, the documentation multipliers, the curve that says how many users a card sort needs. [The numbers not to cite](folklore.md) lists each, what it traces to, and what is measured in its place.

## 6. Beyond prose

The field moved away from typing content and toward typing what surrounds it. A type a machine has to guess is worse than prose; a small vocabulary people declared beats a large one a model induced. Anything that chains, a class under a class under a class, a group inside a group, rots at scale. Changing a schema defeated the one serious attempt at automatic translation, and no system merges structure. Screens sent as data ship only with a fixed set of components to draw them. And the program around the model did not dissolve; it got typed underneath. [Beyond prose](substrate.md) gives the systems, the studies and the graveyard.

## 7. What the code should keep, restate and owe

Keep, as they stand, the prose principle and the story principle ([plain words](reading.md#4-plain-words-are-carried-for-experts-too), [the story](reading.md#5-a-story-is-the-largest-effect-in-the-literature)) and the law that confidence is written ([the numbers not to cite](folklore.md)). Keep the law that a fact has one home as a law of the ground, maintenance and correctness, and not as a comprehension finding ([the whole brief](reading.md#2-the-whole-brief-and-the-few-links-are-carried)). Keep the contract and the why first as what the evidence shows them to be, an allocator of attention, real and small ([the why first](reading.md#1-the-why-first-is-carried-and-small)).

Restate three things. "Writing for people and building context for models are one problem" is one problem for finding and two for explaining, and the code should say so ([where the claim breaks](agents.md#5-where-the-codes-one-problem-claim-breaks)). "Keeping it in order stops being a job of its own" holds only where something consumes the knowledge, and the code should name what consumes it, a session bootstrapped on it being the obvious candidate ([what gets maintained](documentation.md#5-what-gets-maintained-is-what-something-consumes)). The practice's lean of three heading levels is a preference; the evidence, all at fixed item counts, leans wider and shallower than most writers go, and no head-size number should be cited for it ([how deep and how wide](reading.md#3-nobody-knows-how-deep-or-how-wide)).

Owe: four of the six sources the story principle cites, since the sweep reached only two ([the story](reading.md#5-a-story-is-the-largest-effect-in-the-literature)); the grounds document itself; and the one experiment nobody has run, a well-written prose corpus against the same corpus with declared structure, read by the same model ([beyond prose, open](substrate.md#9-open)). Reasoned; proposed, not in force.

## 8. Open

What the sweep could not settle, and nobody else has: how deep and how wide a holarchy should be at any size beyond a few hundred items; whether letting a reader skip a nested brief does the work of writing a text twice, once for the newcomer and once for the expert; whether the rule to rewrite a broken brief whole still works once a body of knowledge is larger than one rewrite can hold in view; how professionals' use of a long document changes over months, since no study has watched one; and the experiment owed in [§7](#7-what-the-code-should-keep-restate-and-owe). Each file's last section sets down its own.
