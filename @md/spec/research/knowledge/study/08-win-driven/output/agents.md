# What a model reads

This is what the 2025 to 2026 evidence says structure does for a session: where it helps a model find and where it fails to help it understand, what the harness (the program around the model that assembles its context and runs its tools) now does with that context, and where the code's claim that writing for people and building context for models is one problem breaks. It stands on [the context-engineering report](../../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/aafbf4f4a32e619af.md), [the coding-agent documentation sweep](../../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/af750ce80e754223b.md) and [the documentation-platform report](../../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/a1c816c1f7cbcfdf9.md), which hold the paper links, the exact wording and the full numbers behind every finding here. Reading it, you know what a brief under the code does for a session and what it does not.

## 1. Composition matters more than length

A model does not use its context uniformly, and degradation begins far below the limit. In a test of 18 models on finding one fact in a long text (measured in tokens, a token being about three quarters of a word), a single unrelated passage placed near it reduced performance; facts phrased unlike the question were found less often as the text grew; and, against intuition, a text whose passages were shuffled was searched better than the same text in its original order, which the authors attribute to structural patterns steering attention *[Hong, Troynikov and Huber 2025]*. On a long-memory benchmark a 300-token focused prompt beat the same content inside 113,000 tokens for every model. Independently, on a 2025 benchmark called NoLiMa where the fact shares no words with the question, 11 of 13 models fall below half their short-context score at 32,000 tokens. So a well-organised long document is not, for a model, a better document; a selected short one is. Measured, by others; two single papers agreeing.

Compaction, summarising a long context to make room, silently deletes rules. With full policy context, seven model families violated policy 0 percent of the time; after compaction the average rose to about 30 percent and the worst family to 59, across 1,323 episodes, and an attack that biases the summariser into dropping policies defeated every model tested *[Chen 2026]*. Keeping the rules outside the summary restored 0 percent. The strongest shipped harness already does this by type: on compaction it reloads the project's instruction file, memory, plan and rules from disk, and summarises only the conversation. Measured for the paper, one author; seen for the harness.

## 2. Routing pays once

One level of pointers beats reading everything; a second level buys nothing. The only controlled test of progressive disclosure, giving a model a short index and letting it open what it needs, found one level degrading gracefully and eventually beating reading the corpus raw, and a second level of index giving no benefit and sometimes hurting *[He and others 2026]*. Presenting tools as a folder tree to browse rather than a flat list is the biggest reported win in the field, and the number attached to it is an example, not a measurement; the measured versions of the same mechanism, in two 2026 papers the context-engineering report lists, are six points more of the right tool found at one ninth of the tokens, and on a small model a change of how tools are described that took it from zero to 84 percent accuracy at half the tokens, with the gain shrinking to a few points on frontier models. So the code's holarchy, for a session, is an index: the first level is where the payoff is, and depth beyond it is speculative. Measured, by others; the example is traced in [the numbers not to cite, §5](folklore.md#5-the-ai-era-numbers).

## 3. Repository prose does not move success; pointers do

A team writes the instruction file its vendor recommends, a text file at the repository root telling the agent what the project is, how it is laid out and what the conventions are. The agent reads it and follows it. On the next task it fails where it failed before, on the wiring of the feature, and the run costs a fifth more because the file was in every request. The story is archetypal and carries no confidence of its own; the two paragraphs after it do.

Two independent studies converge on the null. On a benchmark of real repository issues, with instruction files generated as vendors advise and with fresh issues from repositories whose developers had committed their own, context files did not generally improve task success and raised inference cost by over 20 percent; the agents followed the instructions, and the repository overview, the content most recommended, did not help *[Gloaguen, Mündler, Müller, Raychev and Vechev 2026]*. A 288-run test on two frontier agents bounded any effect at ten to fifteen points and found failures were of implementation skill, not missing repository knowledge; the real instruction file "never converts a near-miss to a pass" *[Khatri 2026]*. Measured, by others; preprints, the second underpowered.

What helps is getting to the right file. Guidance refined by trial raised the share of issues resolved from 25.5 to 33.0 percent on a mid-sized model, and the whole gain came from agents reaching the correct files, while the quality of the fixes they wrote stayed flat *[Shepard and Albrecht 2026]*. Given the right files, a summary of them in plain language answered four out of 45 questions about how the code behaves, where the source code itself answered 27, and a frontier model's summaries scored no better than a small model's, so the gap belongs to the form and not the summariser *[Sam-Bodden 2026]*. Measured, by others; preregistered.

Small effects in this literature are under a noise floor. The Sam-Bodden paper also found that running the same model on the same input twice, with randomness turned off, still flips about 9 percent of outcomes; any single-run claim of a few points from documentation sits under that. Measured, by others.

The most-adopted convention rests on almost no measurement. The instruction file is in tens of thousands of repositories and stewarded by a foundation, and the only published positive measurement is five runs on one repository, which its author calls one data point. A 124-pull-request study found shorter runs and fewer output tokens with the file present, which could fit the cost finding above if the file adds input and cuts wasted output, and nobody has reconciled the two *[Lulla and others 2026]*. Seen for adoption; measured, thin, for effect.

## 4. A heading trail carried with the text helps it be found

Structure helps a model find when it travels with the piece of text retrieved. Prefixing each piece with its chain of headings, with no extra model calls, lifted a standard retrieval score (mean reciprocal rank at five, how high the right piece lands in the top five) from 0.374 to 0.463 on 1,600 queries over a production Markdown knowledge base, and from 0.828 to 0.925 on the queries that had an answer *[Yang 2026]*. In a second study, the same records presented to a model as readable cards, each with its fields laid out, nearly doubled answer quality against letting the model query the database they came from *[Marchioro and others 2026]*. Cutting text at meaning boundaries rather than at headings was found not worth its cost in three studies from 2024 to 2026 the platform report lists; a heading is free and does the work. Measured, by others; the first is one author, one knowledge base, not peer-reviewed.

A map of where things are cuts an agent's dead ends. In 2,400 trials across 20 documentation sites and two agents, serving plain Markdown was worse than HTML because the agents invented paths, and a link to an index file of the site cut dead URLs by about 90 percent with accuracy flat in the high nineties, in a vendor-run benchmark with an open design. Seen; the mechanism is the finding of [§3](#3-repository-prose-does-not-move-success-pointers-do), finding not understanding.

## 5. Where the code's one-problem claim breaks

The evidence says people and models converge on finding and diverge on explaining. Both benefit from where things live, how to run the tests and which conventions cannot be inferred; only the person benefits from what the code means. The code's contract, holarchy and link are the finding, and for those the claim that one text serves both holds; the win itself, the prose that carries the why, serves the person and not, where the model is editing, the model. Reasoned, on [§3](#3-repository-prose-does-not-move-success-pointers-do) and [§4](#4-a-heading-trail-carried-with-the-text-helps-it-be-found).

The case for structure in a context cannot rest on the model reading it better. The format a prompt is written in swung results by up to 40 percent on a 2023-era model and much less on the next generation, so that sensitivity is shrinking with capability *[He and others 2024]*; what remains for structure to buy is choosing what goes in and knowing where it came from, which is where [beyond prose, §1](substrate.md#1-text-won-the-payload-type-the-edges) places it. Measured for the swing, by others; reasoned for the rest.

The code's [read by a fresh session](../code.md#451-the-read-against-the-code) has a small ground. A session with no history of the work, reviewing a technical document, found more of its errors than one that had the history, 28.6 against 24.6 percent on a score combining what it caught and what it caught wrongly, in one 2026 study the context-engineering report gives by its arXiv number, 2603.12123. That supports the fresh read as a cheap step and not as a judge. Measured, by others; one study.

## 6. Open

Whether a coherent document written under the code is, for a session, worse than the same content shuffled is the direct test of the composition finding against the code, and it has not been run. Whether one artefact serves both readers or a derived list of claims is needed for the machine is the same question from the other side. No independent study varies a human document set's organisation, page kinds, heading depth, link density, holding content constant, and measures a model's answers. And the two cost findings on instruction files point in opposite directions and nobody has reconciled them.
