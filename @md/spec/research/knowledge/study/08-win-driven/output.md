# What the code stands on

This is the debrief of one web sweep, forty-five agent reports gathered on 2026-08-26, read against [the code](code.md). It is for whoever is about to commit to the code: to write a team's knowledge under it, or to build the medium its fifth part describes. Read it and you will know which of the code's claims the world's evidence holds up, which it corrects, and what nobody has shown, so that what you write or build stands on ground and not on habit.

The raw material is [the sweep](../../../sweep-2026-08/README.md), whose entry says what each of the forty-five files is and carries two cautions worth repeating: the agents ran out of web search and fell back on open sources, and two of them caught automatic summarisers inventing quotations and sample sizes. So every number below carries the grade its report gave it. Where a report says measured, there is a study with an N; where it says seen, a shipped system was read at source; where it says folklore, a circulating number was chased and no source was found. Where the joining is mine, I say reasoned. The sweep's own limits are at the end of [what nobody has shown](#6-what-nobody-has-shown). One more caution: the sweep tested an earlier edition of the code, one with six principles and a fifteen-rung ladder beside it. The code you hold has two laws and five principles. The mapping from the old to the new is mine, and I name it where it matters.

## 1. The whole: the shape holds, the numbers fall, and the artifact is not the lever

Three things came back from the sweep, and everything below breaks them down. The code's shape, why first, bounded parts, few links, plain words, a story where a story fits, is supported by comprehension science as far as that science reaches, which is students reading short passages once. Nearly every number the code's lineage inherited is folklore: the cost curve, the seven, the hundred and fifty, the two levels, the ten to one. And a written artifact on its own moves outcomes less than anyone in this field likes to say; what moves them is whether a reader can find the part they need, whether something downstream consumes the artifact so it stays true, and, for models, whether the structure sits at the edges of the text rather than in it.

Picture the team the code opens with. They write everything down, carefully, under a good scheme. A year on, the new colleague reads the top and still walks over to ask. The sweep says why, and it is not that they wrote badly. The colleague could not absorb what they had no prior ground for, the document could not repair a misunderstanding the way a conversation can, and half of what they wrote had quietly gone stale because nothing but a reader ever touched it. The code's bet is that a fresh session, which reads the top and becomes what it reads, is the consumer that keeps the writing load-bearing. That bet is reasoned, not shown, and this piece is the ground for judging it.

## 2. What the writing stands on

The code's laws are claims about comprehension, and comprehension has a literature: advance organizers, segmenting, signalling, hypertext, cognitive load, narrative. The sweep graded the earlier edition's six principles against it one by one, and the full verdicts are in [the comprehension report](../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/adc8fe8e64ae23f70.md), which also names what the principles miss. The short form: the ordering claims are supported and small, the narrative claim is supported and large, the sizing claims have no evidence, and the one-home claim was justified by a finding that does not say what it was taken to say.

### 2.1 Why-first holds, as an allocator of attention, and the opening should be concrete

The code's thesis, the biggest win as early as possible, has direct support in the levels effect: relations placed at the top third of a text's structure "dramatically" shape what is understood and recalled, while the same relations placed low barely register *[Meyer 1975]*. The ancestor of the idea, the advance organizer, helps too, but by less than its reputation: a mean effect of .21 across 135 studies, which the authors themselves frame as the average reader beating 58 percent of the control group *[Luiten, Ames and Ackerson 1980]*.

Two qualifications bound the win. First, framing a text by its purpose improves learning of exactly the content the frame names and nothing else: a preregistered meta-analysis found g = 0.54 for the material a prequestion covered and g = 0.04 for the rest of the same text. So the contract, the title and first paragraph that say what you will understand, buys the reader a decision, which is what the code asks of it, and not a better grasp of what follows. Second, the earlier edition said the opening should be "deliberately more abstract" than the body, and that is the one part of Ausubel's model the evidence specifically failed to confirm: high-effect studies did not use the abstract, subsuming organizers his theory predicted, and graphic, concrete organizers beat expository prose ones *[Stone 1983]*. The current code no longer says abstract; it says give the win in what the reader already holds. That is consistent with the evidence, and a concrete or scenario opening is the safer reading of it.

Measured, in students, on short passages; the placement claim replicated, the abstraction claim contradicted.

### 2.2 Parts should be bounded, and no number says how big

Breaking text into units a reader takes one at a time helps: a segmenting effect, small to medium, across 56 investigations *[Rey and others 2019]*. The mechanism is that the reader controls the pace, not that the unit is conceptually whole, and the same body of work finds that design matters least exactly where documents live, in self-paced reading by people who can go back *[Noetel and others 2021]*. The holarchy is supported as bounded units. It is not supported as a particular size.

Every size rule the lineage carried was checked at source by [the citation audit](../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/a3f4c332843acf946.md), which gives the verbatim text of each paper and a list of safe formulations, and none of them yields a rule.

- Miller's seven is, in his own words, "only a pernicious, Pythagorean coincidence", and his paper is about chunking raising the limit *[Miller 1956]*.

- Cowan's four is a pure capacity visible only when chunking is experimentally blocked; a browsable, re-readable hierarchy blocks nothing *[Cowan 2001]*.

- The menu studies fixed the item count and found the best tree at that count: eight by eight over 64 targets *[D. P. Miller 1981]*; four top-level items holding sixteen each, for Kiger, whose fastest tree had the narrowest top level tested; and sixteen by thirty-two over 512 items, with three levels of eight significantly slower and more disorienting, and no reliable difference between the two two-level trees *[Larson and Czerwinski 1998]*. The winning breadths, 8, 16 and 32, all exceed the working-memory numbers the rules were derived from.

- Luhmann's slip box, cited as an existence proof for shallow structure, ran to about 90,000 slips with up to four sub-levels, 108 divisions in the first box and one division with 120 subdivisions, and the archive says it was never a strict hierarchy at all.

- Wikipedia's split trigger is not fifty kilobytes; the size rule now runs on words, 8,000 as a "starting point" at which an article "may" be too long.

So the proof of concept's lean, three heading levels before a folder takes over, is a preference and should keep saying so. One finding does bear on depth for a model reader, and it points the same way: in the only controlled ablation of progressive disclosure (a top file that routes to deeper files loaded on demand), one routing level beat raw navigation and a second level gave no benefit and sometimes hurt, per [the context-engineering report](../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/aafbf4f4a32e619af.md), which carries the field's state and its dated timeline.

Measured, on fixed item counts and single sessions; no study reaches a corpus of the size a team's knowledge becomes.

### 2.3 A story is the largest effect in the literature

The code's fourth principle says a story is read faster and remembered longer, and the sweep found this the best-supported claim of the six. Across more than 75 samples and 33,000 participants, narrative was better understood and better recalled than exposition, "robust" and "not moderated by various study characteristics" *[Mar, Li, Nguyen and Ta 2021]*. The bound the code puts on it also holds: details that interest but do not serve the point reliably harm learning, the seductive-details effect, so the operative variable is relevance rather than concreteness.

Two things the code does not yet say sit beside this and are cheap. Signal words, "the problem is", "in contrast", "as a result", are the least expensive supported intervention, r = .17 for comprehension and transfer across 27 studies, larger for readers with less prior knowledge *[Richter, Scheiter and Eitel 2016]*; they are what switches a weak reader out of reading a list into reading a structure. And a text that asks the reader to explain something to themselves gains g = 0.55 across 64 reports *[Bisra and others 2018]*; none of the code's principles has the text ask anything of the reader.

One debt stays open. The four sources the code's story principle cites, Trabasso and van den Broek, Willingham, Mandler and Johnson, Bransford and Johnson, were not in the sweep; the code marks them as unverified, and they still are. Mar and colleagues is the verified support, and it is stronger than the four.

Measured, and the largest and least qualified effect the sweep found.

### 2.4 One home is a rule for maintenance, and the code should say so

The earlier edition justified "every fact in one place" by the redundancy effect, and the sweep says that is a misreading. The redundancy effect is about the same content in two channels at the same moment, written and spoken together inside one working-memory episode, with 25 technical apprentices *[Kalyuga, Chandler and Sweller 2004]*. It says nothing about a fact stated in one document and restated in another read weeks apart. If anything, the comprehension literature leans the other way: the more coherent, more elaborated, partly restating texts won for low-knowledge readers, and spaced restatement is one of the most robust effects in learning.

The current code grounds one home differently, in drift: "three copies of a rule quietly become three rules." That is the right ground, and it is the ground the organisational evidence supports, as [what gets kept](#33-what-gets-kept-is-what-something-consumes) shows. The one carve-out that has comprehension backing is restatement at a coarser level, which is what a contract is: an organizer is a restatement one level up, and it measurably helps.

Reasoned, from measured pieces; the maintenance ground is seen, in [what gets kept](#33-what-gets-kept-is-what-something-consumes).

### 2.5 Fewer links, in a hierarchy, with what a link gives said before it is followed

Hypertext research is unflattering to links. Decision-making at each link impairs reading, learning is better with fewer links, and readers low in working memory or prior knowledge suffer most *[DeStefano and LeFevre 2007]*. A 2020 study of 54 trainees found network structures produced far more disorientation than hierarchical ones and hurt low-knowledge readers most (d = 2.47 against 1.58). So the code's two rules, a link points out of a brief only where the brief cannot carry the ground, and the holarchy's order keeps forward links few, sit on the best-evidenced ground of the six. The third rule, that a link says what following it gives, has no direct test; it is a plausible extension of the signalling literature and nothing contradicts it.

The strongest outside challenge to the brief was thought to be Diátaxis, the four-mode scheme that would split a fold into typed files. [The documentation-systems report](../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/a26ae7e524c53262a.md) found the challenge weaker than advertised: the scheme's author disclaims file-per-type as "a typical outcome of the good practice, not its end," and the one documented adoption at scale reversed. Pigweed, at Google, adopted Diátaxis "as a literal blueprint" in 2023 and found "too much fragmentation": the explanation for a module on one page, its tutorial on another, and users who "won't click those links" never reached the theory. They retreated to sections within a page. That is the holarchy: one brief, its parts beneath it. Seen, one named organisation, no metrics.

For a model reader, the same conclusion arrives from retrieval. Prepending a chunk's heading chain, its place in the hierarchy, lifted retrieval on a production markdown knowledge base from 0.374 to 0.463 (paper-only, single author, 1,600 queries). The gain came from carrying hierarchy into the chunk, not from splitting the document.

Measured for fewer links and hierarchy; seen for the fragmentation; untested for the announcing rule.

### 2.6 Experts are not a separate audience

The worry that scaffolding for the newcomer costs the expert has a name, expertise reversal, and a 2025 meta-analysis of 60 studies. Its finding is asymmetric: novices gain from assistance at d = 0.505; experts are harmed at d = −0.428 overall, but that harm is non-significant inside every education stratum, and the analysis contains no practising professionals at all, because no such studies exist to code, per [the professional-readers report](../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/a4071d48c3f5d869c.md), which censuses every professional-population study the sweep could reach, in aviation, medicine and law. The one professional-population experiment points the other way: 105 lawyers read contracts in legal and in plain register, and legal training raised the whole curve without buffering against complexity; simplified contracts were rated equally enforceable and higher in quality by another 102 *[Martínez, Mollica and Gibson 2023]*. Expertise is a main effect, not a shield.

Two more findings from professionals bound how the code should treat its readers. Comprehension time falls with experience, from 66 to 44 percent of working time across 78 developers over 3,148 instrumented hours, which is what expertise is supposed to buy, and it is not reversal *[Xia and others 2018]*. And preference is not comprehension: 181 physicians preferred a layered guideline format 72 to 16 percent, while correct understanding moved from 58 to 72 percent and missed significance *[Brandt and others 2017]*. So the code's refusal to fork prose for novice and expert is not refuted, it is merely untested, and its plainest-word rule is what the professional evidence supports. Readers also do not read in order, and less so with experience: six professionals followed a code's linear order about 60 percent of the time against novices' 70 *[Busjahn and others 2015]*, which is one more reason a brief must leave the reader whole wherever they stop.

Measured, in the few professional studies that exist; the fork question untested.

### 2.7 Where this evidence comes from, and why it may not reach you

The generalisation gap is itself a finding, and the comprehension report calls it severe. The populations are school children and undergraduates; the materials are passages of a few hundred to a couple of thousand words, read once, in a session of fifteen to thirty minutes; the outcomes are immediate recall and multiple choice. Text-structure effects that look strong at immediate test are all non-significant on delay *[Bogaerds-Hazenberg and others 2021]*. Nothing in the literature studies a 5,000-word specification, let alone a corpus of linked ones read repeatedly over months, and the professional sweep found no longitudinal study of any kind. The gap runs against the code: professionals reading specifications are the high-knowledge, self-paced, goal-directed readers, which is exactly the corner where the measured effects shrink.

What the professional literature does establish is narrower and useful. Retrieval, not comprehension, is the failure mode: practitioners could read a clinical guideline but could not find the applicable recommendation; pilots face flow lines across pages and unexplained jumps. Specificity correlates with use: vague recommendations followed 36 percent of the time against 67 for clear ones, across 61 doctors and 12,880 decisions, correlational and confounded *[Grol and others 1998]*. And syntactic complexity taxes everyone.

Seen, across the sweep; the gap is reasoned by the reports and by me.

## 3. What a written artifact can do, and what it cannot

The code opens on friction, the work of joining what each of us carries, and the sweep went to the organisational literature to ground it. It came back with a defensible restatement, a ceiling, and a list of numbers to stop repeating. [The organisational-knowledge report](../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/abdf5bfa14a7ddd82.md) has the restatement in full and the primary sources; [the documentation-effectiveness reports](../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/a7b21ac3d71f8c8f6.md) and [the minimalism report](../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/a5e1231b95c4c9ac5.md) have the census of what was ever measured.

### 3.1 Insight fails to travel for knowledge reasons, not for want of will

Across 122 transfers of best practice in eight firms, the barriers that dominated were the recipient's lack of absorptive capacity, the ambiguity of what was being transferred, and an arduous relationship between the parties; motivation ranked low *[Szulanski 1996]*. Absorptive capacity is the receiver's prior related knowledge, so a reader absorbs as a function of what they already know, not of how well the sender writes *[Cohen and Levinthal 1990]*. That is the code's second force, comprehension built only on comprehension already held, stated as an organisational law, and it also fixes the code's opening: a document reaches only as far as the ground its reader already stands on.

Two more pieces bound the artifact. A document is maximal in reviewability and zero in cotemporality, so the cheap repair loop that makes conversation efficient is unavailable and misunderstandings compound silently *[Clark and Brennan 1991]*; the least collaborative effort is usually to go and ask. And where a boundary is one of interests, client and vendor rather than two colleagues, shared vocabulary is insufficient by construction; such boundaries need both sides to transform what they know, not translate it *[Carlile 2004]*. The strongest hard evidence in the lane is not about documents at all: on Windows Vista, organisational structure predicted failure-prone binaries at 86 percent precision, beating every code metric *[Nagappan, Murphy and Basili 2008]*.

Measured; Szulanski is a strong single study never properly replicated, the rest replicated or well-theorised.

### 3.2 No study shows documentation causing an outcome

Across software engineering, technical communication and information design, the sweep found a handful of experiments in which a document was the manipulated variable and performance was measured, and no meta-analysis. The oldest is the best: a minimal manual cut learning time by 40 percent with nineteen office temps in 1987, replicated by its main critic in 1993, and pooled to d = 1.12 across 13 effects and 288 people in a conference paper that was never peer-reviewed and whose authors suspect the active ingredient was cutting redundancy, not the theory *[Carroll and others 1987]*. The cleanest modern one: 58 students given a platform of correct crypto examples were 73 percent more effective and wrote 66 percent fewer vulnerabilities. Everything else, the API-documentation surveys, the issue taxonomies, is description of what is wrong with documents, never what good ones buy.

The best-controlled result on format says format does not matter: 65 people randomised to a narrative essay or a structured document about an architecture showed no significant difference in understanding; prior exposure to the code dominated *[Ernst and Robillard 2023]*. And the one large-N link to outcomes retracted itself. DORA reported in 2021 that teams with quality documentation were 2.4 times more likely to perform well; in 2022 the same construct came out negative, in 2023 "no effect" for the second year, and in 2025 the capability was dropped and the report declined to use the word "effect" at all, per [the DORA reading](../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/abeeefde341d33a2d.md), which quotes each year's report and instrument. In free-response onboarding data from 411 developers at Microsoft, mentorship came first and documentation second *[Rastogi and others 2015]*.

The surgical checklist is the cleanest natural experiment on whether a well-designed procedural document changes outcomes, and it is a chain of causes worth holding. Eight hospitals introduced one as an engaged programme and deaths fell from 1.5 to 0.8 percent *[Haynes and others 2009]*. Ontario then mandated it across 101 hospitals and 215,000 procedures, and nothing moved *[Urbach and others 2014]*. The only randomised trial, in two hospitals, found complications fall by 8.4 points where the checklist was implemented with care *[Haugen and others 2015]*. The artifact does not carry the effect; the implementation does. Measured, all three.

### 3.3 What gets kept is what something consumes

The finding a documentation project must sit with comes from 53 practitioners in six automotive companies: artifacts that generate something downstream, code, tests, other artifacts, get maintained; artifacts that only describe do not, and the guideline that follows is to produce descriptive documentation "as late as possible and only when actually needed" *[Wohlrab and others 2019]*. The same shape recurs everywhere the sweep looked. Of 6,362 architecture decision records on GitHub, 51 percent were committed once and never touched again, half of adopting repositories stopped within five records, and 4.6 percent showed sustained team use; a 2026 follow-up on the same 921 repositories found "at most modest" correlations with quality and that 63 percent of records were born "accepted", skipping the deliberation they exist to record, per [the decision-records report](../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/aae121db667d39f2d.md), which has the counts and the proposal-process numbers beside them. When Google retired its internal wiki, about 90 percent of pages had no views or edits in the preceding months. Heavyweight proposal processes have no exit path: 215 open Rust RFCs with a median age of 905 days, 343 Kubernetes enhancement proposals alive and 14 ever killed; the only intervention with a visible before and after is Python's, which shrank what a proposal must decide and routed around the process.

This is where the code's one-home law and its debrief duty find their ground, and where the code's central bet is exposed. Copies drift because nothing consumes them; a fact with one home is a fact something can consume. And a debrief that ends a session is load-bearing only if the next session reads it. The code says it will: a fresh session "holds nothing else, so it becomes what it reads." If that is true, the debrief is prescriptive in Wohlrab's sense, the model is the downstream consumer, and the artifact escapes the decay every descriptive artifact in this section suffered. Nothing in the sweep tests it. It is the code's own hypothesis, and [the coding-agent brief](#42-for-coding-agents-the-map-helps-and-the-prose-does-not) says what the nearest evidence does to it.

Measured for the decay; reasoned for the escape.

### 3.4 The numbers the code must not carry

The sweep chased every figure the lineage had ever leaned on, and the code's own §1, which speaks of friction and wins without a multiplier, is already clean. Anyone writing under it should stay clean, which means not reaching for these.

- The 1:10:100 defect-cost ratio and its "IBM Systems Sciences Institute" source: the institute was an IBM customer-training unit in Los Angeles, not a research body, and no study exists; the largest test of the delayed-issue effect, 171 projects, found no evidence for it *[Menzies and others 2016]*. Boehm and Basili themselves wrote in 2001 that the factor is "more like 5:1 than 100:1" for small non-critical systems.

- The seven, the four, and Dunbar's 150: [the sizing brief](#22-parts-should-be-bounded-and-no-number-says-how-big) for the first two; the third rests on confidence intervals of 4 to 520 and 2 to 336, from which "a cognitive limit on human group size cannot be derived" *[Lindenfors, Wartel and Lind 2021]*.

- "Developers read ten times more than they write": Robert Martin's recollection of watching editor playbacks, no instrumentation, and the playback was of scrolling. What is measured is 58 percent of effective working time on comprehension, of which a large share is browsers and documents, not code *[Xia and others 2018]*.

- DORA's 2.4 times and its 656 percent table: [the documentation brief](#32-no-study-shows-documentation-causing-an-outcome).

- "Reuse saves 30 to 40 percent" and every translation-savings figure for structured authoring: no primary source anywhere the sweep could reach; the standard's own beta is six years late and written by one person.

- Nielsen's fifteen participants for card sorting: numbers read off a graph in a non-peer-reviewed 2004 paper, and the one direct validity test found the match between elicited mental model and site structure "has no effect on browsing performance" *[Schmettow and Sommer 2016]*.

- "Knowledge compounds" as Romer's result: unbounded growth is "more like an assumption than a result of the model," in his words, and he denies that population suffices.

- Anthropic's "150,000 tokens to 2,000": an illustrative example in a blog post, with no method; the mechanism is independently supported at one to two orders of magnitude smaller effect.

- GitLab's 605,000-word handbook: no primary source; "over 2,000 pages" is what GitLab says.

Seen, each chased to its origin by the reports named above and by [the developer-folklore trace](../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/aec803bcd71ce9745.md).

## 4. What a model reads

The code says writing for people and building context for models are one problem. The sweep tested the half of that claim that can be tested today, in coding agents and in retrieval, and the answer is: one problem at the level of the map, two problems at the level of the prose. [The context-engineering report](../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/aafbf4f4a32e619af.md) carries the field's state; [the coding-agent report](../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/af750ce80e754223b.md) carries the null results; [the structure stress-test](../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/aa5f5baaa66cc8bf1.md) carries the verdict on typing.

### 4.1 Composition, not length, and a coherent document is not automatically better context

Degradation begins far below a model's context limit and is driven by what is in the window, not how much. A single distractor reduces performance; a 300-token focused prompt beat the same content inside 113,000 tokens for every model tested; and, the finding nobody cites, shuffled haystacks outperformed coherent ones across all eighteen models, which the authors attribute to structural patterns steering attention. Compaction, the summarising a harness does when its window fills, is now a documented safety failure: policy violations rose from zero with full context to about 30 percent after compaction, and the fix that restored zero was to pin the constraints outside the lossy summary. Both are paper-only, the first replicated in spirit by a second benchmark.

Two consequences for the code. The first supports it: a bounded brief, read as a unit, is exactly the "focused prompt" that wins, and the code's bet on the top staying short is the right bet for a model. The second cautions it: a well-written document is not automatically good context, because coherence itself can mislead attention. The code's answer, that the brief's contract lets a reader decide without reading, is the mechanism that keeps a model's window small; it is not evidence that the prose inside the window helps.

Measured, paper-only; the composition finding replicated.

### 4.2 For coding agents, the map helps and the prose does not

Two independent studies converge on a null for repository context files. Across SWE-bench tasks and fresh issues from repositories with developer-written files, context files "do not generally improve task success rates, while increasing inference cost by over 20 percent," and repository overviews, the content vendors recommend most, were unhelpful *[Gloaguen and others 2026]*. A 288-run ablation on two frontier agents bounded any effect at 10 to 15 points and triaged the failures: implementation skill, "not missing repository knowledge that a context file could supply." Meanwhile the one positive result isolates the mechanism. Empirically refined guidance raised resolution from 25.5 to 33 percent, and the gain came entirely from agents reaching the correct files; patch quality stayed flat. And a preregistered ablation found natural-language summaries of code answer almost none of the questions the source answers, 4 of 45 against 27, with a frontier summariser scoring exactly as poorly as a 3-billion-parameter one: the gap belongs to the representation.

The same shape holds for documentation served to agents. Serving a link map cut dead URLs by about 90 percent across 2,400 trials with accuracy flat, per [the AI-turn documentation report](../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/a1c816c1f7cbcfdf9.md), which grades every vendor benchmark in the space; the win was finding the file. Carrying the heading chain into the chunk lifted retrieval; splitting documents did not. And a noise floor sits under all of it: temperature-zero inference flips about 9 percent of per-instance outcomes between identical runs, so any single-run claim of a few points from documentation is unfalsifiable.

For the code this is the sharpest correction in the sweep, and it should be stated plainly. What people and models share is the need for the map: where things live, which door to open, what a link gives. That is the contract and the entry file, and the evidence says they pay. What only people need, on current evidence, is the why: the explanation, the descent from win to detail. A coding agent editing a repository does not measurably use it. The code's claim that "the difference people blame on models is mostly a difference in ground" is untested for knowledge work, which is where the code aims, and refuted for repository edits, which is where it was measured. The bet in [what gets kept](#33-what-gets-kept-is-what-something-consumes), that the next session's reading keeps the debrief load-bearing, stands or falls on whether knowledge work behaves like the map or like the prose. Nobody has run that experiment.

Measured, paper-only, two nulls and one mechanism; the extension to knowledge work reasoned.

### 4.3 Type the edges, leave the body as text, keep the vocabulary small and declared

The stress test of the earlier thesis, that text as a medium must be superseded by typed structure, returned a split verdict, and the half it loses is the half that was stated most confidently. Typed, self-describing structure is the right substrate for a narrow set of properties, identity, permission, provenance, freshness, address, action space, and only when the types are declared or deterministically observed, never inferred at breadth. Every 2026 system that won typed the edges around a prose or code body; every loser typed the body. Prose is the winning medium for the payload.

The evidence for the inference gate is the hardest in the sweep. A model reconstructing column metadata from values scored 0.223 accuracy at 94 percent coverage; the same model behind a deterministic gate scored 0.475 on the 42 percent it committed to, and the authors' line is that the gate "is a competence detector, not a competence amplifier." Frontier models produced zero valid output on a 369-field schema. Schema compliance is near-perfect while value accuracy is 83 percent on text and 24 on audio: form is free, content is not. On the other side, small declared vocabularies win: thirteen predefined memory categories beat graph memory on both long-memory benchmarks with no ingestion cost; a typed language of fifteen relational operations beat free-form SQL; and of schema.org's 958 types, twelve reach ten million domains and three quarters sit below a thousand. A typed store with wrong types is worse than prose, because prose degrades visibly and a type keeps answering.

Two findings bear on the code directly. Putting instructions in a schema underperformed putting them in a system prompt by 11 to 13 points, and when the two conflicted accuracy fell by up to 45: typing your instructions makes a second channel that silently overrides the first, which is a reason the code's "nothing here is typed" is right for the writing. And the most adopted new authoring artifact, the agent skill, a markdown folder with front matter, has adoption in the tens of thousands and a controlled benchmark showing target skills lower pass rates by 1 to 4 percent at 72 to 394 percent more tokens, with 105 of 105 release transitions silently invalidating part of the skill set. Structure decays silently; text rots loudly.

Measured, paper-only, many studies pointing the same way; the decision rule in the stress-test report is reasoned from them.

### 4.4 The harness moved under the code's fifth part

The code's fifth part says a context "belongs to one tool, and runs through a closed function over a linear transcript," with no branch and nowhere to step back to. Half of that was overtaken by shipped product before the sweep ran. Both leading coding agents branch and rewind; one publishes a typed table of what survives compaction, re-injecting project files, memory and rules from disk and summarising only the conversational middle. Memory stores shipped as product from Anthropic in April 2026, as markdown documents at paths with immutable versions attributed to the session that wrote them, and Google shipped a matched stack with memory revisions. Every memory vendor then converged on the same three features, background consolidation, cross-harness memory, and provenance, with three of them shipping cross-harness memory within six weeks of each other, per [the agent-memory report](../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/a187277ed3a006fbc.md), which tables the delta system by system. The protocol the code names, MCP, deleted its sessions and handshake in July 2026 and grew a required discovery call, required cache fields on every read, and full JSON Schema on tools: it moved down a layer and got more typed on the way.

What remains absent is exactly what the code says is absent. Nothing lets you ask what is in a context or address a past state by identity; branches are transcript copies, not shared structure; and reproducing a completion from a recorded, addressable context is unsolved in any shipped product, with server-side compaction and background consolidation making the recorded context actively non-reconstructible. Provenance exists over the memory store, not over the world: nothing answers "which run edited this file, under what permissions, from what context." The paper that names the join, checkpoint, fork, restore and merge as execution edits, notes that existing systems perform them "without deriving what each edit must preserve." So the fifth part's claim should be re-cut: the branch and the step back exist; the address, the query and the re-derivation do not. Two more corrections from the same lane: the flagship memory operating system retired its own server and became a coding harness, and competent lexical search over a raw log matched graph memory on the agent benchmark, so the null hypothesis the memory field skipped is now on the table.

Seen, at source, in release notes, changelogs and specifications; the papers paper-only.

## 5. What the medium would meet

The code's fifth part is for engineers, and the rest of the code does not depend on it. It describes a unified medium in which programs say what they take and give as structure, so that a screen, a model and a person are all citizens of one field. Most of the sweep's forty-five reports are about the pieces such a medium would be built from, and the honest summary is that every piece has a neighbour, several neighbours died, and one problem, changing a contract after data exists, has no shipped answer anywhere.

### 5.1 Nobody holds the whole thesis, and every part has a neighbour

[The neighbours report](../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/a09867b88abcb610b.md) took the earlier thesis apart into six claims and found no one arguing the whole. Ink & Switch make the app-as-enclosure half without the medium lineage, and their Patchwork system already runs the interface model, tools as plugins declaring which document types they render, composition by embedding; Pentad Labs states context and lineage as slots on every fact, as an essay and a pre-release product; Palantir's ontology is the one instance at national scale and is itself a monolith. The claim that text must be superseded is the loneliest and is losing in public: the extension mechanisms for models have swung away from structure twice, from plugins to instructions and from tool schemas to markdown skill folders. And the strongest opposition is not that structure is hard; it is Dynamicland's inverse principle, "to maximize agency, minimize what the computer knows," which says a richer typed model that programs consume and people do not read is another opacity.

The ventures nearest the "code as structure, not text" idea converged on one funding answer within a year, per [the structure-editor report](../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/a5105d288fc90070c.md), which has the founders' own words. Unison shipped 1.0 after eight years and opened a consulting group to reach cash flow; Glamorous Toolkit is consultancy-funded; and Dark Inc ran out of money in June 2025 with a post-mortem that names the cause: "our online structured editor didn't make sense when the LLM is generating the code," and the editor was half the codebase. The research side names the missing piece as theory rather than engineering: Subtext's author calls twenty years of it "overambitious failed experiments" that lacked "a Theory of Change" the way databases have relational algebra. Seen, all at source; the post-mortems are the founders' own words.

### 5.2 Files won, for a reason nobody predicted

Plain markdown became a moat in 2026 because coding agents can read it, and the clearest evidence is a product that moved the other way. Logseq had promised a database rewrite for years, canonical SQLite, nodes unifying pages and blocks, typed properties, tags as classes, and shipped it in July 2026 after a thirteen-month hole in releases, into a market that had just re-valued the thing it gave up. The top reaction was "too little, too late," and long-time users described leaving for Obsidian because plain text was non-negotiable and their agents could no longer read the files. Obsidian, meanwhile, added typed views over files without a new store, went free for commercial use, and shipped a command line as its agent surface. [The tools-for-thought report](../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/a3f6ddd86cf882a6d.md) calls this the single most adverse fact for a "structure not files" thesis, and the twenty-seven-year-old critique behind it still stands: formality imposes a cost at capture time that users route around, and nothing since refutes it *[Shipman and Marshall 1999]*. The rest of the category told the same story from the funding side: the venture-backed middle evacuated note-taking for meetings and agents, and what remains as pure tools for thought is bootstrapped and small, or dead.

For the code this is confirmation of its own choice, markdown in directories, and a constraint on the fifth part: whatever the medium becomes, it must project to files as a first-class, two-way surface, or its users will leave for the tool that does. Seen; the constraint reasoned.

### 5.3 Choices with precedent that held

Several of the earlier design's decisions repeat a known path, and the path worked. [The formal-representation report](../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/adc9dab44aefdde97.md) and [the permission report](../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/aea19b144c46bfc26.md) have the sources.

- Hierarchy that is not transitive: SKOS, the most successful W3C vocabulary, made `broader` a direct link only in 2009 and put the closure in a separate opt-in property; the transitive subclass tree is what rots, with 2.39 million Wikidata classes marked both first and second order.

- Permission that stops at one hop: Zanzibar's own paper says recursive pointer chasing "has difficulty maintaining low latency" with deep or wide groups, and its fix turns one write into tens of thousands of index events; OpenFGA is removing its depth limit because depth was the wrong metric and fan-out is the cost; Google Drive removed the ability to grant less than a parent folder; and Microsoft's own documentation for its search restriction calls it "a short-term solution" that "isn't a security boundary," with a worked example in which the assistant answers from a site whose owner "hasn't set up proper permissions." The report's reading is that the model did not create the leak; it enumerated a closure that manual discovery never reached.

- Validation instead of inference: shape validation won over description-logic reasoning on every activity measure, the Python validator at 1.2 million monthly downloads against the reasoner's 92,000, and the W3C is now standardising inference as datalog-style rules under the shapes group, not description logic.

- Permission written as a query, inside the engine: Postgres row-level security has done it for a decade, and Fluree does it for a knowledge graph, policy as a where-clause evaluated per datum. What almost nobody achieves is full-text search under the same filter; the standard search tier leaks term counts by its own documentation.

- A small closed vocabulary: what schema.org became in practice.

Seen, at source, each; the Zanzibar figures paper-only and shipped inside Google.

### 5.4 Choices with precedent that died

Other decisions repeat a path that ended badly, and the reports say why.

- Owner-scoped contexts as the fence against a global ontology: Cyc's microtheories, two thousand person-years, and contexts that multiplied rather than consolidated. The failure of the semantic web was never a global vocabulary; it was that per-owner vocabularies never aligned, and alignment was never automated.

- Commits and branches over a typed graph: TerminusDB, the closest prior art, resurrected by a third-party maintainer after a year without releases, its marketing site down, and its operation for migrating an inheritance hierarchy unimplemented.

- Lenses for schema translation: [nobody merges structure](#55-nobody-merges-structure-stamp-every-write).

- A logic language as the user surface: XTDB rewrote itself around SQL and stopped mentioning its datalog heritage; the Rust borrow checker's datalog formulation was "a non-starter" on performance and shipped as a different algorithm; the pure datalog products are archived. Datalog wins as an internal representation and loses as something users type, per [the datalog-and-versioning report](../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/a96d8d0c3161e997c.md), which has the engine census and Datomic's own accretion limits.

- Navigation by intersecting facets: MIT's semantic file system, Xerox's placeless documents, WinFS, all died for the same reason: intersection needs clean typed metadata on every item and nobody had it. That is the one thing 2026 changed, and [the typing brief](#43-type-the-edges-leave-the-body-as-text-keep-the-vocabulary-small-and-declared) says it changed into dirty metadata at 20 to 85 percent wrong rather than into clean.

- Retrieval without vector similarity: independent benchmarks have plain vector retrieval beating graph retrieval on factual recall, and the graph method that wins on the largest benchmark has no entity graph at all and an abandoned repository, per [the graph-retrieval report](../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/a86776519f614dd3a.md), which has the benchmark numbers and their costs per query. Microsoft's own GraphRAG went to maintenance mode in August 2026.

Seen, at source; the Cyc account is well-sourced opinion.

### 5.5 Nobody merges structure; stamp every write

Changing a contract after data exists under it is, in the sweep's words, "the confessed unsolved problem, and the lab that named it has closed the file." [The schema-evolution deep read](../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/ad341d6da8eaac361.md) is the best single piece in the sweep and should be read whole by anyone building here; what follows is its spine.

Cambria's real result is not lenses. Ink & Switch began by translating data on write and abandoned it: too much translation happening eagerly, and a write racing a newly registered schema. They switched to tagging every write with the schema it was written under and translating on read, which is what Avro has done for eighteen years, and the essay concedes its flagship operator is not a lens at all and that performance was never measured. The bidirectional-transformation literature then proves the wall: the composition law you would need to chain migrations safely fails for map, flatten, merge and conditionals "for reasons that seem pragmatically unavoidable." Lenses did not solve the view-update problem; they gave it a type system.

Every production system that survived schema change bought safety by forbidding, not translating. Protobuf forbids reusing field numbers and removed required fields entirely; Datomic never alters a value type and concedes that time travel does not take the schema back in time; ATProto's rule is that old data stays valid under the new schema and new data under the old, with types unchangeable and fields unrenamable, and a breaking change means a new name. Dolt blocks a merge on schema conflict and resolves by taking an entire side; Iceberg has no branch merge, only fast-forward; the industry-standard sync engine handles a schema change by reloading the page. Nobody merges structure. They serialise it, or namespace the divergence away.

So the report's first recommendation is the one that matters: stamp every write with the contract commit it validated against, because write-time-only validation is safe only when the writer's schema travels with the datum. Then split contract edits into widening, applied in place, and narrowing, which mints a new contract with a new id; support alias as the one blessed rename; and do not build lenses. For indexes, neither eager nor lazy: invalidate at one cost by bumping an epoch, re-derive on read, backfill in the background, which is the measured shape from Noria. In a store where every write is a commit, eager fan-out is worse than anywhere else, because it writes history that carries no intent.

Seen, at source, in the systems' own documentation; the recommendations reasoned from them by the report.

### 5.6 The renderer is nowhere data, and the opaque frame won the wire

The fifth part says what draws a screen should declare what it takes in the same medium as everything else. [The server-driven-UI report](../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/a57740ddd62e39ab6.md) and [the schema-driven-UI report](../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/a458ace67969949f3.md) went looking for anyone who had done it at scale. Five teams, Uber, Plaid, DoorDash, Lyft and Shopify, hit the same wall and invented the same fix, which is not the one the thesis proposes: the client tells the server which component types it can render, and the server refuses to send anything else. Nobody solved shipping a new component to an old client; Plaid's spec makes it a major version and an SDK upgrade. The escape hatch is a named tier, Lyft's "semantic components," DoorDash's untyped custom struct, and both report the boundary eroding. And dispatch is everywhere a pinned key, a discriminator the server names, never a query over the data asking which component can draw it.

Uber's Screenflow is the closest anyone came, and it is a shutdown. A restricted subset of TypeScript, components as the only top-level definitions, compiled to an intermediate form with definition files carrying types and defaults so native code could be generated. Its versioning answer was the correct one and the expensive one: continuous integration recompiled every screen against every runtime version ever shipped. After two years there were sixteen flows in production, and the department closed in July 2020. The lesson the report draws is that "a mount is a call validated by the matching rule" is right, and validating every call site against every shipped signature set costs a compiler, a monorepo and a build matrix.

Two shipped facts bound the space today. The generative-interface protocol race consolidated inside MCP in January 2026, and the winner is opaque: a sandboxed HTML frame with no property schema, data arriving by notification, at twelve million downloads a month. Typed component trees lost at the protocol layer and thrive one layer up, where the application owns both model and renderer. And the one mainstream system that answers "which components can render this?" at runtime is WordPress's block registry, with declared containment contracts and a ranked-predicate query; the industry's dominant component model went the other way and deleted its only runtime contract in React 19. Every product view layer the sweep checked, Notion, Airtable, Tana, Anytype, has an extensible instance schema and a closed set of view kinds, and none lets you define a new property type either. The nearest running instance of the thesis is a research platform: Patchwork's plugin registry, whose engineering notes encode the constraints a mount must obey.

Seen, at source and in the teams' own post-mortems; the consequence for the code reasoned by the reports: ship the typed layer, treat the opaque frame as one adapter, and keep the vocabulary of what can be mounted small.

### 5.7 The contract is the asset; a closed runtime is the casualty

The low-code market did not die under code generation; it bifurcated, with one real casualty class, per [the low-code reckoning](../../../sweep-2026-08/raw/ace1709b4a562f194.md), which has the filings. What is dying is the proprietary visual runtime sold to prosumers and agencies: Airtable sold for 1.28 billion on 480 million of revenue after a peak above eleven; Wix cut a fifth of its staff naming its agency channel; Pegasystems told the SEC that "unprecedented changes in the AI market caused clients to delay their purchasing decisions." What is fine is governed process automation over regulated data: Appian grew 19 percent and deleted the words "low-code" from its boilerplate. And the market relocated rather than vanished: two code-generating tools created roughly Airtable's entire revenue in twenty-four months, on company-claimed figures.

The datum that matters for the fifth part is Retool's reversal of June 2026. It abandoned its own visual abstraction for React and TypeScript, saying the abstraction "prevented LLMs from working fluently," and repositioned as the governance layer that imports apps built elsewhere and wraps them in permissions and audit. Read carefully, that is not a verdict against deriving an interface from typed data. It is a verdict against closed, non-inspectable derivation. Every survivor's remaining moat is typed contracts: data access rules, permission inheritance, schema. Every code-generating winner's admitted weakness is maintenance. So the sweep's reading of the bet is that deriving a screen from a typed contract is right in 2026 on one condition: the derivation must emit inspectable artifacts in a standard language, and the renderer must not be a walled garden.

Seen, from filings and company statements; the reading reasoned.

## 6. What nobody has shown

The code says a claim of coverage is judged by its omissions, and the sweep's reports were unusually good at naming theirs. The ones that bear most on the code are these.

- The crux experiment does not exist: no head-to-head of human-declared, model-assisted structure against a well-written prose document, on the same corpus, with the same frontier reader. Every structure win in the sweep beat an extracted or unstructured baseline, never a good prose one.

- No causal study of documentation of any design, and no longitudinal study of professionals re-reading a long technical document over months. The code's whole use case sits in that gap.

- No evidence on written confidence marks. Nothing in the comprehension literature tests author-assigned status labels on claims, so the code's second law is unevaluated, not supported.

- No test of whether skippable nesting substitutes for prose forked by audience. The code rejects forking; the literature has not compared them.

- No study of whether a typed or structured input beats prose on a frontier model. The only controlled work is on older models and shows the effect shrinking with capability; the case for typed context rests on selection and provenance, not on parsing.

- No verification of the four sources the code's story principle cites, and none of Kiger 1984 at source, Miller 1981's sample, or Cambria's peer-reviewed paper; the paywalls held.

- No knowledge-work version of the coding-agent nulls. Whether a fresh session's reading of a debrief moves any outcome has not been measured by anyone.

The sweep's own limits are three. Web search ran out for most agents, so the evidence over-samples what has an API: W3C, GitHub, arXiv, npm, and under-samples blogs, talks and non-English work. Two agents caught summarisers fabricating verbatim quotations and a sample size, one inventing "N=22, 10 companies" for a study whose real figures are 271 observations, 122 transfers and 8 firms, and re-verified their findings from primary PDFs; the reports that did not do this may carry what those did not catch. And of the forty-five files, twenty-seven are sub-agents whose commissioned topics were not recorded, and one named run did not survive the harvest, so the sweep's own coverage map has holes it cannot name.
