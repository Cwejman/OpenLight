# Evidence: whether the medium needs a kernel

*The author's question, marked open in the ontology on 2026-09-05 and explored the same evening: does the medium need a kernel, or is the engine a primal thing of the medium, one of many, so that the core is unbound? Technique: one fresh session read the ontology's platform entries and the pilot's substrate and engine specifications, listed every duty the pilot's one process performs, asked of each whether an engine that reads the law as data could do it for itself, named what cannot be distributed, described three shapes with prior art verified on the web, and stated the dynamic underneath in the ontology's words. The report is in git at the commit that adds `passes/round-5/r5-kernel.md`. Strength: **reasoned**, one session, with prior art verified.*

## What was found

Thirty duties in the pilot's one process. Seventeen an engine can do for itself once the law is data it reads. Ten need a shared library or a store-level rule. Three cannot be distributed, and they are one thing: at a store, someone must see every commit, in order, and judge each under a boundary before it stands.

Prior art agrees on the shape. The web has no kernel above its origins, but each origin has a server that judges writes. Git has no daemon, only a format and many clients, and a shared remote still has one receiver that orders pushes. SQLite is a library and no server, with one exclusive writer per file. The pattern is one judge per store and none above the stores.

## The dynamic

For many engines to share one medium safely: the law must be in the field, so every engine reads the same law from the same address; every act must become a commit, so nothing is outside the trail; and at a store, commits must stand in one order, each judged before it stands. Engines execute, the law judges, the substrate records. Whether the judging is done by a library every engine links or by one small process the store keeps, the dynamic is the same.

## What it did to the ontology

Kernel was retired as a word. It had named this role, the law's judging at a store, and the operating-system picture had made a thing of it, one process above everything. The role moved to the substrate's entry: each store one order, each commit judged before it stands, one judge per store and none above. Engine became the primal thing of the medium that executes under the law, one of many, reached by its contract, hosting its own runs. The pilot's one binary is then a judge fused with a program engine, and the seam between them is the first thing the next specification draws.

## What this does not reach

One session, one reading of the pilot's duties. The exploration was briefed on the engineering spec, which is the bottom-up ground the author wants out; only its dynamic was taken, not its inventory. Whether a library or a process plays the judge is a mechanism, and mechanisms are the specification's. The author's own reach for the idea, that unbinding the core lets others bring engines, goals and ideas, is marked underexplored in the engine entry and stays so.
