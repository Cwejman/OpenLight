# Ontology

*The words the code and the platform built for it are made of, and the dynamics between them: what is, the parts of meaning that make it, the right words in their right relations, and nothing more detailed than that. Its goal is the code's, that knowledge reaches whoever needs it next, person or model, and every word is graded toward it. Each word is a brief: its essence first, then the relations it stands on, one per heading, the heading a prose title with the word it reaches linked. A relation lives under the word that depends and nowhere else; what stands on a word is not written as a relation under it, because the trail shows it. Words are ordered by win toward that goal: the biggest gain toward knowledge reaching whoever needs it next comes first. That order is a reader's judgment, observed; how many words stand on each is counted beside it as the check, in [`evidence-reach.md`](evidence-reach.md), and the two agree where value and dependence coincide and part where they do not. A web has no order in which every word is given before use, so this file borrows forward and declares it here, which the first law allows; the nearest line is in [`evidence-series.md`](evidence-series.md). Confidence is in the writing: **measured** means numbers exist, **observed** means seen in the work, **reasoned** means argued only, **ruled** means the author decided; a word whose name is not settled says so, and rests on its notion; a better word comes when it comes, and nothing waits on it. Who changed what is in the git log.*

## Code

The working rules for how knowledge is written and arranged so that it reaches whoever needs it next. What this ontology is the words of.

### Has one value, [comprehension](#comprehension), and one unit of it, the [win](#win)

Everything the code asks is measured in wins. A rule that does not raise comprehension per reading is not the code's.

### Has two laws, and everything else follows from them

The first: nothing arrives before its ground; the reader's order is the writer's order. The second: the ground stays true; whatever is stood on holds for whoever stands on it next. Each has its home under the word it binds, the [gradient](#gradient) and the [ground](#ground).

### Runs on prose in files today, and on the [field](#field) when it is built

Nothing in the code needs new technology: markdown in directories, tracked in git, holds it, and the code's words are in use today. The [platform](#platform)'s words are what the same goal requires once programs are readers too; they are specified, and only first cuts of them are built.

### Stands in a study, under the project's entry

This file is one lab's arc in a study of how knowledge is best structured ([`../README.md`](../README.md)); the project's entry is its README ([`README.md`](../../../../../../README.md)), whose claims the words here carry; what is only an idea waits in [`sketches.md`](../../../../../sketches.md). The platform was first specified bottom-up, from engineering; that tree is not this file's ground.

## Comprehension

Understanding a thing well enough to act on it. The one value the code measures everything by.

### Is not information

Information is what is on the page; comprehension is what the page lets form, built from the text together with what the reader already holds and settled into one whole [Kintsch 1988]. A page rich in the first and poor in the second holds little knowledge.

### Compounds by joining

An insight is worth more once it meets what the reader already holds, and two people's understandings are worth more again once they meet. The friction of that meeting, across roles, backgrounds and processes, is the wall the code exists to take down.

## Knower

Whoever comes to the knowledge and is changed by it: a person, or a model in a session. Bounded, and both reader and writer. The knower's part in every act of knowing is not a flaw but a component of it [Polanyi 1958].

### Is where [comprehension](#comprehension) lives, and nowhere else

A head, or a model's context, and neither holds everything. Knowledge is comprehension written down so that it can form again in a knower other than the one it first formed in.

### Is bounded, which both laws answer to

A person forgets; a model's context fills and gets worse as it fills. So a knower holds what matters and reaches for the rest, and every rule of the code is written for a knower that cannot hold the whole.

### Acts in two ways, reading and writing, and is bounded in both

A read changes the knower; a write changes the field. Whoever makes a brief is bounded the same way as whoever reads it, so every limit on the reader binds the writer first. Time enters only as the order acts stand in.

### Is a session, when it is a model

A session is one model-knower's lifetime, opened holding nothing and closed when its context is spent. What it does not write is lost with it, so a session debriefs before it ends.

### Is named for now

The code says reader, which fails for the writer. Rest on the notion, one bounded thing that reads and writes; the word iterates.

## Win

One gain in comprehension: what you understand after reading that you did not before, with what it is good for. The unit of value.

### Is measured as [comprehension](#comprehension) gained against reading spent

A long page that gives little is a small win; a line that changes what you can do is a large one. The ratio has no unit yet: two wins are compared by a reader, not by a count, and this file's order was made that way. Observed.

### Is unequal, which is what makes an order possible

A few wins carry everything else; most details matter only to their own subject. If every piece mattered the same there would be nothing to put first.

### Is the test of writing

A piece that gives no win is not knowledge yet, however true. It is material, waiting to be understood.

## Brief

A piece of writing that hands over one win and stands on its own. Essence at the surface, detail within. The unit of writing.

### Hands over one [win](#win), at the surface

The reader came to be briefed, not to relive the work, so the win lands at once. A brief holding two wins is broken into briefs nested inside it or beside it until each lands alone; the detail moves a level deeper, not away.

### Is made by debriefing: conclusion to the surface, detail within

When a stretch of work ends, the ground covered gives up its conclusion, the conclusion goes to the surface, and the detail stays within. Nothing is lost; the reading is contracted. The verbs are everyday: brief me, debrief this session, unpack that.

### Stands on [ground](#ground), and becomes it

A brief is whole on its own but not from nothing; the wins it assumes are its ground. Once read it is ground for the next reader, and lives that second life longer than the first.

### Is a [holon](#holon)

Whole on its own and part of the one above, at every depth, which is why it breaks down without breaking. It can be a whole file or a section in one.

### Explains or points out everything it uses

Everything a brief uses it gives in place or links out of itself, and following the link is optional, so a reader who has what they came for can stop and leave whole.

### Is listed under its ground by its title, so a linking title must read as a sentence

The ground says nothing back; the [trail](#trail) shows, under a word, every brief that names it, by title and first paragraph. So a title that links must read whole from the linked word's side, the word above it the subject and the title the predicate: "Amplify: feeds on true ground" is one. Observed in two heads.

## Ground

What a brief stands on: the wins its reader is assumed to hold already. Common ground, the mutual knowledge two parties can assume, grounded as far as the purpose at hand needs [Clark and Brennan 1991]; and what a symbol must be tied to before it means anything to the one holding it [Harnad 1990].

### Is what a model is, since a fresh session holds nothing else

A session started against a set of briefs becomes what it reads, and is the engineer or the planner by its ground alone. The difference people blame on models is mostly a difference in ground.

### Must stay true for readers not yet met, which is the second law

A team's knowledge compounds only as far as its briefs are safe to stand on. So every fact keeps one home and is pointed at from everywhere else, since three copies of a rule quietly become three rules; and settled means settled for now, given what it stands on.

### Carries confidence when it describes, and status when it directs

A brief that describes the world is true or false, and carries its confidence in the writing, never as a feeling the reader is left to sense. One that directs the world, a goal, a plan, a decision, is in force, fulfilled or superseded, and carries that status instead. The split is the direction of fit [Searle 1979]: word to world for the first, world to word for the second. Reasoned.

## Link

The one way briefs join beyond nesting. An offer, never a missing piece. Nelson's word for the join of hypertext [Nelson 1965].

### Joins [briefs](#brief) without breaking them

The brief reads whole without the link, and the sentence around it says what following it gives, so nobody follows blind.

### Names [ground](#ground) so it is never restated

A reader who lacks the ground is one step from it, and the ground is never said twice.

### Opens a door past the brief

For the reader who wants more than the brief says, the link is where more is.

### Needs no target yet, in prose

A pointer to a source unread or a question unanswered is the brief nobody wrote; the text reads whole and the door stands. Written as a name and a year in brackets it is a citation, and the same thing.

## Holon

A whole that is also a part, the same law holding at every level, Janus-faced [Koestler 1967]. What a brief is, and what the field is made of.

### Is within one whole, in prose, and placed in many, in the field

Nesting is being part. In prose a part has one whole, and that is the one relation a document's line can show, so every other relation is a link. In the field a holon is placed in many dimensions at once, and nesting is one of them. Reasoned.

### Has a surface and a depth, which are one holon seen twice

The surface is what it says itself, apart from its parts; the depth is its parts, holons in their own right.

### Has an address, which is what a [link](#link) points at

One thing carries one name, and its place in the nesting supplies the everyday form of it, a path. Identity is the name and the path is its spelling, so a holon that moves keeps its identity while its path changes.

### Has its name from Koestler, for a part that is whole

Greek *holos*, whole, with the suffix *-on*, which as in proton suggests a particle, a part [Koestler 1967]. A nesting of holons is a holarchy, his word too.

## Gradient

The shape wins take together: the biggest first, breaking down to the detail that grounds them. Minto's pyramid, where every idea is a summary of the ideas grouped below it [Minto 1978].

### Lets a reader stop whole at any depth

Comprehension descends as reading deepens, and a reader who stops holds the biggest wins and leaves the rest reachable. The slope is the inequality of [wins](#win); that is what gives the shape an order at all.

### Runs one way, which is the first law

Every connection runs from a bigger win to the smaller wins inside it, never the reverse unless it says so: nothing arrives before its ground, purpose before machinery, and every meaning given before it is used. Where a subject forces the other order, the debt is declared in a line. Borrowing is allowed; silent borrowing is not.

### Descends from its entry's why, so one web holds many gradients

Which win is biggest depends on what the reader came for, and the entry states it: a document's first brief is its why, and every win below is sized against that. The same briefs entered for another purpose fall into another order, and an interface that knows the reader's why can lay the same field out afresh. Ruled by the author, 2026-09-05.

### Is checked by reach, which the [trail](#trail) can count

A [brief](#brief) that many stand on sits near the centre and carries the comprehension; one that few need sits at the edge, reachable from wherever it matters. How many stand on a brief is the length of its trail, so reach is measured, and it checks the gradient without replacing it: reach counts what depends, the gradient sizes what is gained. Measured.

### Keeps order without discipline

Because the slope is in the shape, each addition lands where its win belongs and the top stays short as the whole grows, so keeping knowledge in order stops being a job of its own.

## Medium

Briefs joined by links, held in common. What the code turns written knowledge into; untyped, because it is prose. A medium shapes what passes through it more than any message does [McLuhan 1964].

### Is made of [briefs](#brief) and [links](#link), and briefs of words

Both its readers, person and model, read prose, so no schema is needed for the medium to hold. Types arrive with the field.

### Is where [comprehension](#comprehension) meets, across knowers

Each piece lands as ground for the next, and two knowers in one medium compound the moment either writes, because the write has an address.

### Holds everything that gives work its direction

The goals and plans, the decisions and their whys, the record each session leaves. All of it is knowledge, all of it the same briefs and links.

### Makes forgetting safe

Nobody holds the whole. A reader holds the briefs that matter and lets the rest go, because the brief above holds the point and the link leads back. Dropping context is not new; dropping it and staying whole is, and it is the medium that provides it, not the tool.

## Trail

What leads to a brief and what it led to: every brief that names it, every change it had, and every read made through the field. Never written as a trail; derived from what is recorded. Bush's memex tied items into trails that did not fade [Bush 1945]; his trail was stored, this one is derived, and that is the departure.

### Runs the [links](#link) backward

A link is written once, at the brief that stands on the ground. The trail is the same links read from the ground's side: under a brief, everything that names it.

### Is shown by the [interface](#interface), never written in the [medium](#medium)

The medium holds one direction, and a file's order supplies the other for the reader who reads down. Every other way back is derived by what shows the field, from links and commits it can already see; writing it down would be a copy. Observed in two heads.

### Is derived from a run's record: what was read, in what order, against which state

A read leaves nothing in the medium, but a read made through the field is recorded with the run it belonged to, so an answer can be walked back to what it stood on. This is the trail the transcript could not keep.

### Is walked by [knower](#knower) or by address in the [space](#space)

Everything one knower wrote, or everything said at one address.

### Is named for now

A notion may enter before its name; this one did. Rest on the notion.

## Word

The smallest holon the medium is built on: the minimum free form, what can be uttered alone with meaning [Bloomfield 1926]. Can be looked up, so a precise word is a link to its meaning.

### Is the smallest [holon](#holon)

Whole in itself, since it means something alone, and part of every brief that uses it. Below it nothing stands alone.

### Is a [link](#link) when it is precise, so the exact word is allowed

A word outside everyday English is reading spent, and the code once asked for the plainest word for that reason. But a word whose meaning is exact is an offer of the same kind: the sentence reads, and the meaning is one lookup away. So the code asks for the word whose meaning is exact, and the plainest such word. Ruled by the author.

### Puts a lookup one step below every brief

Once every word can be opened, the [medium](#medium) reaches below the brief, and a reader who meets a word they do not hold is one step from holding it. The code's shape at the smallest scale there is.

## Amplify

To give back more than was put in. What the code does to comprehension, and what the harness did not. The name is Engelbart's, augmenting the intellect to gain comprehension to suit one's needs [Engelbart 1962], and behind it Ashby's amplifying of regulation [Ashby 1956].

### Is what a [win](#win) counts, one reader at a time

Understanding out against reading in is gain, and a win is that gain measured at one reader.

### Works by contraction, which the [brief](#brief) does

A brief contracts the reading, never the reader, and what is contracted at the surface expands again in whoever reads it, larger than the words.

### Feeds on true [ground](#ground), and amplifies error as fast as truth

The more true ground a reader gains per reading, the greater the gain. A wrong brief stood on by many spreads as fast as a right one, which is why the second law is not a rule laid on top: it is what amplification costs.

### Is multiplied by every knower reading at one write's address

A write with one home and an address is picked up by every [prism](#prism) tuned to it, so one write is amplified as many times as there are knowers standing there. Resonance is that, seen from the knowers' side, and the word is Grossberg's for a pattern that settles only when input and expectation agree [Grossberg 1976]. Reasoned.

### Replaces the [harness](#harness), which bounded what this expands

A harness bounds a model to a transcript, a closed function run once. The medium gives a model more ground per reading than any transcript could, and its output becomes ground for the next. We do not bound; we expand.

## Consolidation

Bringing what is scattered into one whole that stands: many sessions, many heads, one home. The author's word, and memory's: what is learnt takes time to fix and can be lost until it is [Müller and Pilzecker 1900].

### Does for the medium what a [brief](#brief) does for one stretch of work

A brief consolidates one dive into one surface. The medium consolidates every brief, so what many knowers found separately stands once, and stands for all of them.

### Lets an answer be re-derived from the same state of the [field](#field)

An inference made from consolidated ground stands on one addressable state, a [commit](#commit), and can be made again from that state. What is repeatable is the ground; a sampled model's answer is its own. Reasoned.

### Is amplification seen from the field, as resonance is from the knower

Consolidated inference and amplified resonance are one dynamic seen from two sides: the field settles, and the knowers standing on it gain at once. This reading of the pairing is reasoned; the word is ruled by the author.

## Commit

One declaration, kept whole: what changed, by whom, why, and after what. Gray's notion, all of a transaction or none of it and, once committed, never abrogated [Gray 1981]; git's word for it, and kept as git's.

### Is a [holon](#holon) with a place in time

Its surface is what changed and why; its changes are read as two states of the field compared; its address is its place in the order of commits, and it stands on the commit before it. A change is read the way knowledge is read.

### Is on a branch

A branch is what the transcript lacked: somewhere to step back to, a fork to take. Work stands on a branch before it is merged.

### Is the ratification, which is this document's rule

Whoever makes a change commits it, standing on what it changes, and the other side ratifies by committing on it in turn. Talk settles nothing; the trail does. A change left uncommitted is outside the trail and can be lost. Observed once.

### Changes one [brief](#brief) whole, or one [word](#word) in place

A small fix, a word or a name, is made in place. A change to what a brief says rewrites it whole, because a whole is checkable only when one bounded knower holds it at one moment. One pass is never enough, for the same reason.

### Publishes by placement, not by a second act

To place a holon where others' [boundaries](#boundary) reach is to publish it; there is no second act.

## Comment

A write that stands on a brief without changing it.

### Stands on a [brief](#brief) and leaves it whole

The author's text stays the author's and the reader's response stays the reader's, and the two are visible together. A comment that asks for a change is folded in by the author and retires; the trail still shows it was said.

### Is free from the reader's own place, and placed onto the brief by leave

From the reader's own place a comment is free to anyone who may read the brief, and reaches the brief's readers through the trail; placed onto the brief itself it needs the author's leave, within the [boundary](#boundary). A published brief may be commented on, and that is the line between custodian and stakeholder, a team and its client.

### Has no home yet when the brief is a file

A file cannot hold a comment without ceasing to be the author's alone; where it lives instead is held open in the sketches.

## Platform

What the medium must become for a program to be a knower's peer: the same briefs and links, given addresses, contracts and a law that is data, so that programs read and write what people and models read and write. The entry of the second half, and its why is the code's, met for citizens that cannot read a sentence.

### Exists so that programs compound as knowers do

Whoever needs it next includes programs. A program cannot read prose, so for it the medium must be data; once it is, a program's output is ground the next reader stands on, person, model or program alike, and the walls between them fall.

### Is derived from that goal, not from what was built

The words below are what the goal requires and nothing else. Where a built thing has no word here, it is a mechanism, and mechanisms belong to the [specification](#specification). Reasoned.

### Names dynamics, not mechanisms

Each word says what must be true, not how. An example may name a technology; the words do not, because mechanisms change and the dynamics do not.

## Field

What the medium becomes when every holon has an address and a contract: what is at every address, read from a point. Maxwell's word, for a state assigned to every point of a space [Maxwell 1865].

### Is the [medium](#medium) made readable by a program

A program cannot read prose; it can read data at an address under a contract. So the field is the same briefs and links, each holon addressed and its shape declared, and nothing in it is pruned. Where Unix had text joined by the pipe and the web the page joined by the link, the field has the holon joined by the link, and text is not data.

### Is read from a point, which makes a completion's ground repeatable

A knower reads from where it stands. A model's completion from a point in a field, not from a pasted transcript, is the project's first claim: the point makes the ground addressable, and the answer is the model's own.

## Citizen

Anything that reads the field and writes back into it on the same terms as everything else. Aristotle's citizen shares in decision, not only in residence [Aristotle c. 350 BC], and the ontology admits programs, which is the departure.

### Compounds directly, where a monolith compounds only its glue

A monolith composes only inside its own frame; its seams are for translation, so what compounds is the glue and never the parts. A citizen's output is ground the next citizen stands on. The project's entry says the monolith becomes a citizen; widening the word to programs and engines is this file's.

### Is a [knower](#knower), or a program or an engine, which cannot comprehend

A person and a model are citizens that know; a classical program, or an engine with its own mechanics, is a citizen that only matches. Citizenship is the terms; knowing is not required for it.

## Program

The unit of execution in the field, headless, carrying its contract in its body. Made to do one thing well, its output the input of a program not yet known [McIlroy 1978].

### Is a [citizen](#citizen); a model run as one is still a knower

Its context a selection of the field, its output landing back as structure.

### Is called by matching its contract, never by name

A call is matched against the contract in the body, which is what [amplifies](#amplify) programs: attach a peer's store and its programs compound with yours at once.

### Runs under a [boundary](#boundary) and leaves a [trail](#trail)

A boundary before it, a trail after, so every write walks back to the program that made it and the person who ran it.

## Contract

A statement in the field of what a thing takes and what it gives, so things meet by matching rather than by custom glue. Meyer's word: precondition the client's duty, postcondition the supplier's [Meyer 1992].

### Is a [brief](#brief) of a kind

The essence a caller needs at the surface, the implementation within.

### Is one value that every reader reads

Types are data, so a model reads the same contract a person composes with and a program checks it. Types are the plainest exact words for the [citizens](#citizen) that cannot read a sentence.

### Lives in the [field](#field) it governs

So whatever holds contracts holds its own law as data.

### Declares what draws, as a component

What draws a screen declares what it takes, as a program declares what it takes; a component is a contract that draws.

## Substrate

What the field rests in and what outlives every engine: the law of laying it down, and the record of every commit.

### Holds the [field](#field) at rest

A store is the substrate's unit, one field at rest. Any engine that reads the substrate can open it, and closing one loses nothing; the commits are kept here.

### Gives each store one order, and judges each [commit](#commit) before it stands

For many engines to share one field safely, commits at a store must stand in one order, each judged under a boundary before it stands. That judging is the law's role at the store, not a thing of its own: today one process plays it, fused with the engine that runs programs, and any process that reads the substrate and the law may play it instead. One per store, and none above the stores. The word kernel named this role and was retired for it, as harness was; the role stays, the thing does not. Reasoned, from an exploration on 2026-09-05.

### Holds the [holon](#holon) as a chunk

A chunk is the substrate's holon, with no duty to be true; whether the specification takes the word holon is held open.

### Keeps the law as data, which is the lever

The contracts that govern the field are in the field, read where the data is, by the same readers. That one fact is what the project's entry calls the lever: it is why programs meet by matching, why the interface is a read of the field, and why no separate harness exists. Ruled by the author.

## Engine

A primal thing of the medium that executes: it reads the field's law and runs under it, with mechanics of its own, one of many. Babbage's engine had a store and a mill of its own [Babbage 1837].

### Executes under the law, which it reads as data

Engines execute; the law judges; the substrate records. An engine reads the law from the field as any citizen does and commits through the store's judging, so nothing it does is outside the trail, and no engine needs to know another exists.

### Is one of many, so nobody owns the centre

The engine that runs programs is one. A DSP engine for music, with its own clock and its own arithmetic, is another. None is the platform: the medium has a law and many engines, as the web has a law and many servers, so more people bring their own engines, goals and ideas to one medium and no centre binds them. Ruled by the author; the reach of it underexplored.

### Is reached by its [contract](#contract), whatever happens inside

What an engine takes and gives is in the field, so the field can call it and stand on what it returns. Interoperability is the field's gift, not a runtime the engine carries.

### Hosts its own runs

Its live state is its own: what it runs it also watches and stops, and what another engine asks of it arrives as a commit it honours.

## Boundary

A rule on acts over a region of the space: who may read there, who may write, what may run.

### Draws a region of [space](#space) by permission

The field is one; a boundary is what a knower or a run may reach in it. Reads reach what it admits, writes land where it allows, runs start what it names.

### Is set before a run and stays live during it

What a boundary names is a selection of the field, and the field keeps moving, so the licence stands while its members change. Trust is native, because the rule is the law's and not the writer's.

### Is what a private region is, seen from outside

A knower may work on a branch of its own before anyone sees; what keeps others out is that their boundaries do not reach it. Nothing is private by a wall of its own.

## Prism

What a knower holds at one moment: for a model its context, for a person what they have read and still hold. Newton's prism separates what was already in the light and alters none of it [Newton 1672].

### Is cut from the [field](#field) and loses none of it

A glass prism lays light out by colour without losing any. This one lays the field out by kind, and the knower takes the band the work at hand needs; the rest stays at its address, and the prism is recomputed as the work moves.

### Is taken under a [contract](#contract) by a model, by choice by a person

A model's prism is agreed in advance, what the session takes in and gives back. A person's follows the offers the links make. This is the one place the code treats the two knowers differently.

### Is a selection, as a boundary and a call's argument are

Three things that look different are one form: a selection of the field, read as what a knower holds, what a run may reach, and what a call is given. Reasoned.

### Is named for now

The code's own word for the model's case is context. Rest on the notion, a bounded selection recomputed as the field moves.

## Space

The addresses, across dimensions, at which holons are placed. Where a thing is, never what it is.

### Gives every [holon](#holon) an address

An address is a point in a space, and a space is made of dimensions. A dimension is a holon that other holons are placed in, so every holon is placed in some and is itself one. Ruled by the author.

### Is where, never what

As different as coordinates and what stands at them; the [field](#field) is the what. Medium, space and field are three words because they are three things.

## Interface

Where a knower meets the field: what it reads and writes through, and what shows the trail. Simon's meeting point between an artifact's inner and outer environment [Simon 1969].

### Rests on the [medium](#medium)'s law, and reads the [field](#field) for the rest

The interface the code needs today rests on briefs and links under a gradient, and on the files and their history, which is why it can be built before the platform is settled. Over the field, the interface is itself a read of the field: which components can draw a thing is asked of the field, not configured beside it. Ruled by the author.

### Is built next, since it has to be built to be felt

Nobody has had the experience yet. Ruled by the author.

## Specification

A brief for what is to be built: the shortest text from which the built thing could be deleted and rebuilt in one pass, and no further than that. Parnas's rule, precise and complete enough that others can build against it and no more [Parnas 1972].

### Is a [holon](#holon) whole only in its goals, background and taste

A specification has preceding matter, other wholes to be whole in, and the platform's is written from that [ground](#ground): the code's holons are briefs, the platform's are specifications, and both stand under one gradient.

### Is written from these words downward

Each word's dynamic becomes a requirement, and the mechanism is chosen last; where an earlier specification and this file disagree, this file is the ground.

## Processor

The circuitry that computes: the floor. Von Neumann's central arithmetic and central control, apart from the memory [von Neumann 1945].

### Runs everything above it and nothing below

The one layer with no ground beneath, beside the [substrate](#substrate) that keeps.

### Holds no [comprehension](#comprehension)

It executes. The win begins one layer up.

## Harness

What a tool builds around a model to bound it: a transcript, a closed function, a context made for the occasion. Retired.

### Leaves no [trail](#trail)

Nobody can say an answer came from exactly this state, and nobody can re-derive it.
