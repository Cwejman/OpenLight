# Ontology

## Code

The code is the whole body of prose that a knower reads to work: it is the project itself, held as writing. It is defined by one value and two laws, and everything else in this document is what follows from them.

### Has one value, [comprehension](#comprehension), and one unit of it, the [win](#win)

The code exists to produce comprehension in whoever reads it, and nothing else counts. Comprehension is not measured loosely; it is counted in wins, each win being one piece of comprehension handed to one reader. Every other thing named here is either a way of producing wins or a way of protecting them.

### Has two laws, and everything else follows from them

Two laws govern the code: the gradient runs one way, and ground must stay true for readers not yet met. Nothing else in this document is a rule in its own right; each entry below is a consequence of these two laws applied to some part of the medium. If a later statement seems to conflict with either law, the law wins.

### Runs on prose in files today, and on the [field](#field) when it is built

Today the code is markdown in a repository, read by people and by models in sessions. The field is the same code once a program can read it as data, with addresses and laws held inside it rather than in a reader's head. The ontology is written so that nothing in it depends on which of the two carries it.

### Stands in a study, under the project's entry

The code is not read from anywhere; it is read from a study, which is the project's entry point and the top of its gradient. Every brief descends from that entry's why. A reader who lands on a brief without the study still has the trail back to it.

## Comprehension

Comprehension is the single value of the code: what a knower holds after reading that lets it act well. It lives only in knowers and compounds when pieces of it join.

### Is not information

Information is what a file holds; comprehension is what a reader holds after the file has done its work. A brief can be full of information and produce no comprehension, and a short one can produce a great deal. The code is measured by the second, never by the first.

### Compounds by joining

One piece of comprehension joined to another yields more than their sum, because the join itself is comprehended. This is why briefs link rather than repeat, and why a reader who has ground already gains more from the next brief than one who has not. Compounding is the mechanism that makes a win worth chasing.

## Win

A win is one unit of comprehension gained by one reader, set against the reading it cost. It is the measure of every brief and the test of every act of writing.

### Is measured as [comprehension](#comprehension) gained against reading spent

A win is a ratio, not a quantity: how much the reader now comprehends, divided by how much it had to read to get there. A brief that says the same thing in half the words scores twice. Reading spent counts every word, including those the reader had to skip.

### Is unequal, which is what makes an order possible

Not every sentence wins equally, and this inequality is what lets briefs be ordered. The sentence that wins most goes first; the one that wins least goes last or is cut. If all sentences won alike there would be no gradient, only a list.

### Is the test of writing

Writing is judged by one question: did the reader win, and how much per word. Style, completeness and correctness matter only as they serve that. A writer who cannot say what the reader wins from a paragraph has not finished it.

## Brief

A brief is the unit of the medium: one whole piece of prose that hands one win to its reader at the surface, with detail beneath. It is made by debriefing, stands on ground, and becomes ground for what follows.

### Hands over one [win](#win), at the surface

A brief carries exactly one win, and puts it in its first lines so that a reader who stops there has received it. Everything after the surface is depth: the how and the evidence, for readers who want it. A brief that needs to be read to the end to give its win is not yet a brief.

### Is made by debriefing: conclusion to the surface, detail within

A brief is written after the work, not during it, by asking what was concluded and putting that first. The detail that led to the conclusion goes within, in the order a reader would need it. Debriefing is the act of contraction that turns a stretch of work into one readable holon.

### Stands on [ground](#ground), and becomes it

Every brief assumes ground the reader already holds, and names it by link rather than restating it. Once a brief is written and ratified, it is ground in turn for the briefs that come after. The medium grows by this: each brief stands on the last and holds up the next.

### Is a [holon](#holon)

A brief is whole on its own and a part of the larger web at once. It can be read alone, and it can be linked into from anywhere. Its surface and depth are one thing seen from two distances.

### Explains or points out everything it uses

A brief may not lean on something it neither explains nor links to. If a term is needed, the brief either defines it in place or points to the brief that does. This is the second law applied to one brief: a reader not yet met must be able to follow it.

### Is listed under its ground by its title, so a linking title must read as a sentence

Briefs are listed beneath the ground they stand on, and the list is made of titles. A title therefore has to carry meaning on its own, and a title that is also a link has to read as a sentence so the list reads as prose. The headings of this document are the example: each is a claim, not a label.

## Ground

Ground is what a reader already holds when a brief begins. For a model in a fresh session, ground is the whole of what it is, so ground must stay true for every reader that will ever arrive.

### Is what a model is, since a fresh session holds nothing else

A model in a new session has no memory of the last one; the ground it has read is the entire state it acts from. Ground is not context for the model but identity. Whatever the ground says, the model is.

### Must stay true for readers not yet met, which is the second law

Ground is written once and read by readers the writer will never know, in sessions that have not started yet. So it must be true not only now, for this reader, but later, for any reader. This is the second of the two laws, and it is why ground is edited rather than appended to.

### Carries confidence when it describes, and status when it directs

Ground comes in two voices. When it describes what is, it carries a confidence: how sure the writer was. When it directs what to do, it carries a status: whether it is proposed, live, or retired. A reader uses the one to weigh and the other to obey.

## Link

A link is the joint of the medium: it joins one brief to another without breaking either, names ground so it need not be restated, and opens a door the reader may or may not walk through.

### Joins [briefs](#brief) without breaking them

A link connects two briefs while leaving each whole. Neither brief absorbs the other; the reader can follow the link or not and still have read a complete thing. This is how the medium is a web and not one long document.

### Names [ground](#ground) so it is never restated

When a brief needs ground, it links to the brief that holds it rather than repeating it. Restating ground makes two copies that can drift, and doubles the reading spent. A link costs one word and keeps the truth in one place.

### Opens a door past the brief

A link is an invitation, not an instruction. The reader who has enough stops; the reader who wants more goes through. The brief is whole on either side of the door.

### Needs no target yet, in prose

In prose, a link may point at a brief that does not exist yet. The word is placed, the door is drawn, and the target is written when it is needed. The field will resolve such links by address; today they resolve by the reader's judgement.

## Holon

A holon is something that is whole on its own and a part of something larger at once. Briefs, words, commits and specifications are all holons; it is the shape everything in the medium takes.

### Is within one whole, in prose, and placed in many, in the field

In a file, a holon sits inside exactly one parent: the brief in its study, the word in its brief. In the field, the same holon has an address and can be placed into many wholes at once without being copied. The holon does not change; only the number of places it can be seen from does.

### Has a surface and a depth, which are one holon seen twice

Every holon has a surface, which is what you see from outside, and a depth, which is what you see from within. These are not two parts; they are the same holon seen from two distances. A brief's first lines and its body are the example.

### Has an address, which is what a [link](#link) points at

A link does not point at text; it points at a holon's address. The address is what lets the holon be placed in many wholes and still be one thing. In prose the address is a path and a heading; in the field it is given by the space.

### Is Koestler's word

The term comes from Arthur Koestler, who coined it for things that are simultaneously wholes and parts. It is borrowed here without alteration. The name is kept so that a reader who knows it needs no explanation and one who does not can look it up.

## Gradient

The gradient is the order in which a reader meets things: from the win at the surface down to the detail, from the entry's why down to every brief. It runs one way, and that is the first law.

### Lets a reader stop whole at any depth

Because the gradient runs from most to least, a reader can stop anywhere and hold a complete thing. Stopping early loses detail, never the conclusion. This is what makes reading spent a cost the reader controls.

### Runs one way, which is the first law

The gradient never asks the reader to go back up for something it needed below. Everything a point depends on is above it. This is the first of the two laws, and every ordering rule in the medium is a case of it.

### Descends from its entry's why, so one web holds many gradients

Each study has an entry, and the entry states a why; the gradient descends from that why. Different entries have different whys, so one web of briefs holds many gradients, one per entry. A brief can sit on several of them at different depths.

### Is checked by reach, which the [trail](#trail) can count

Whether a gradient holds is tested by reach: how far a reader had to go to get its win. The trail records what was read and in what order, so reach can be counted rather than guessed. A brief whose win needs a long reach has a broken gradient.

### Keeps order without discipline

A correct gradient does not depend on the reader behaving well. The reader may stop, skip or wander, and still receive things in a usable order, because the order is in the medium and not in the reader. Discipline is required of the writer once, not of every reader forever.

## Knower

A knower is whatever holds comprehension: a person, or a model in a session. It is bounded in what it can read and what it can write, and both laws exist because of that bound.

### Is where [comprehension](#comprehension) lives, and nowhere else

Comprehension is not in the file, the field or the substrate; it is in the knower that has read them. The medium can carry it between knowers but cannot hold it. Everything the medium does is in service of putting comprehension into a knower.

### Is bounded, which both laws answer to

A knower can hold only so much at once and read only so fast. The gradient runs one way so that a bounded reader can stop whole; ground stays true so that a bounded reader can trust what it did not verify. Both laws are answers to the bound.

### Acts in two ways, reading and writing, and is bounded in both

A knower reads the medium and writes to it, and is limited in both directions. It cannot read everything, so the gradient orders what it reads; it cannot write everything, so the brief contracts what it writes. The two acts are the two halves of amplification.

### Is a session, when it is a model

When the knower is a model, the unit of knower is the session: one context, one run, one set of ground read. The next session is a fresh knower that holds nothing until it reads. This is why ground is what a model is.

### Is named for now

Knower is a provisional name, kept until a better one appears. The concept is stable; the word is not. A later commit may rename it without changing what it means.

## Trail

The trail is the record of a run: what was read, in what order, against which state. It runs the links backward, is shown by the interface, and is never written into the medium itself.

### Runs the [links](#link) backward

A link points forward, from a brief to what it opens. The trail is the same links seen in reverse: from what was reached back to where the reader came from. It is how a reader arriving at a brief can find the entry it descends from.

### Is shown by the [interface](#interface), never written in the [medium](#medium)

The trail is derived, not authored. The interface computes and displays it; nothing in a brief records it. Writing the trail into the medium would make it ground, and it would go stale at the next read.

### Records a run: what was read, in what order, against which state

A trail is specific to one run: which briefs were read, in what sequence, and at which commit of the field. Two runs from the same entry can leave different trails. The record is what lets reach be counted and a completion be repeated.

### Is walked by [knower](#knower) or by address in the [space](#space)

A trail can be followed by a knower, reading brief by brief, or by a program, resolving address by address in the space. Both traverse the same links. The second is what makes the trail a thing a program can check.

### Is named for now

Trail is a provisional name, kept until a better one appears. The concept is stable; the word is not.

## Medium

The medium is the whole of what knowers write and read: briefs joined by links, briefs made of words. It is where comprehension meets across knowers and what makes forgetting safe.

### Is made of [briefs](#brief) and [links](#link), and briefs of words

The medium has two kinds of thing in it, briefs and the links between them, and the briefs are made of words. There is nothing else: no metadata, no index, no schema that is not itself a brief. Whatever a program later adds is derived from these.

### Is where [comprehension](#comprehension) meets, across knowers

Two knowers cannot share comprehension directly; each holds its own. The medium is where one knower's comprehension is written down so another can read it into itself. It is the only channel between sessions and between people.

### Holds everything that gives work its direction

Whatever decides what to do next is in the medium: the whys, the contracts, the status of each direction. If a direction is not written it does not exist for the next knower. This is what lets a fresh session pick up where the last one left off.

### Makes forgetting safe

Because everything that matters is in the medium, a knower can forget without loss. A session can end, a person can leave, and the next reader reads the same ground. Forgetting is not a failure but the normal state between reads.

## Word

A word is the smallest holon: whole in itself and part of a brief. When it is precise it acts as a link, and it puts a lookup one step below every brief.

### Is the smallest [holon](#holon)

Below the brief is the word, and nothing is below the word. A word is whole because it means something on its own and a part because it is inside a brief. It has an address, so it can be pointed at.

### Is a [link](#link) when it is precise, so the exact word is allowed

A precise word names one thing, and naming one thing is what a link does. So a brief may use the exact term even if it is unusual, because the term itself is the door to its definition. Vagueness is what forces restating; precision is what allows linking.

### Puts a lookup one step below every brief

Since every precise word is a link, every brief has a layer of definitions one step beneath it. A reader who does not know a word goes down one step, reads, and comes back. The brief does not have to explain its words in place; the words explain themselves.

## Amplify

To amplify is what a win does across readers: one write, contracted into a brief, is read by many knowers, and each gains more than the reading cost. It feeds on true ground and multiplies error as readily as truth.

### Is what a [win](#win) counts, one reader at a time

Amplification is measured in wins, and each win belongs to one reader. There is no amplification in the abstract; it is the sum of wins across every knower that read the brief. A brief nobody reads amplifies nothing.

### Works by contraction, which the [brief](#brief) does

A stretch of work is long; its brief is short. Contracting the work into the brief is what lets the next reader gain the work's comprehension at a fraction of its cost. The gain per reader is the ratio of work to brief.

### Feeds on true [ground](#ground), and amplifies error as fast as truth

Amplification does not check what it multiplies. A false brief read by many knowers puts the falsehood into every one of them at the same rate a true one would. This is why the second law exists and why ground carries confidence.

### Is multiplied by every knower reading at one write's address

One brief has one address, and every knower that reads it reads the same thing. The writer wrote once; the readers are as many as arrive. The multiplier is the number of reads at that address.

### Replaces the [harness](#harness), which bounded what this expands

A harness confines a model's session to a fixed context and discards what it learned. Amplification is the opposite: what one session writes stays at an address for every session after. The field harnesses everything by holding it, and so dissolves the harness that bounded it.

## Consolidation

Consolidation is the brief's act done to the whole medium: reducing many writes to one ground so the field stays readable. It is amplification seen from the field's side.

### Does for the medium what a [brief](#brief) does for one stretch of work

A brief contracts one run into one holon. Consolidation contracts many briefs, over time, into fewer and truer ground. Without it the medium grows by accretion and the gradient lengthens until reach fails.

### Lets an answer be re-derived from the same state of the [field](#field)

After consolidation, the ground that produced an answer is in one place at one commit. Anyone reading from that point gets the same ground and can derive the same answer. This is what makes a completion repeatable rather than a lucky run.

### Is amplification seen from the field, as resonance is from the knower

From the knower's side, amplification feels like resonance: what it reads fits what it already holds. From the field's side, the same event is consolidation: many reads converging on one ground. They are one dynamic named from two positions.

## Commit

A commit is a holon with a place in time: one whole change to the medium, on a branch, ratified by being placed. It is this document's rule for how anything becomes ground.

### Is a [holon](#holon) with a place in time

A commit is whole, is part of a history, and has a position in that history that nothing else has. It is the holon that gives every other holon a when. Two states of the field differ by the commits between them.

### Is on a branch

A commit is not made against the field directly but on a branch, which is a proposed line of history. Branches let a change be written whole before it is judged. The main line is the ratified one.

### Is the ratification, which is this document's rule

A change becomes ground when it is committed, not before and not by any later act. This document is itself ratified by that rule: what is committed here is the ontology; what is not, is not. There is no separate approval step.

### Changes one [brief](#brief) whole, or one [word](#word) in place

A commit either replaces a whole brief or corrects a single word where it stands. It does not half-edit a brief, because a half-edited brief is not whole. The two grain sizes match the two holons the medium is made of.

### Publishes by placement, not by a second act

There is no publish step after commit. Placing the commit on the main line is what makes it readable by every knower. Anything requiring a second act to become visible is not yet in the medium.

## Comment

A comment is a remark that stands on a brief without altering it, written from the reader's own place and attached to the brief only by leave. In files it has no home yet.

### Stands on a [brief](#brief) and leaves it whole

A comment is about a brief and is attached to it, but changes nothing in it. The brief remains ratified ground; the comment is one reader's note beside it. This keeps commentary from leaking into ground.

### Is free from the reader's own place, and placed onto the brief by leave

Anyone may write a comment in their own region without permission. Making it visible on the brief itself requires the brief's leave, since the brief's readers will see it. The two steps keep freedom to remark separate from the right to be read.

### Has no home yet when the brief is a file

In prose files there is nowhere to put a comment that is neither inside the brief nor lost. The field will give comments an address beside the brief's. Until then, comments are made in review or in conversation and do not persist.

## Prism

A prism is a view cut from the field that loses none of it: a selection of holons a reader takes, under a contract if a model and by choice if a person.

### Is cut from the [field](#field) and loses none of it

A prism selects some of the field for a reader, but the selection is a view, not a copy. Everything left out is still there and still addressable. Nothing is lost because nothing was removed.

### Is taken under a [contract](#contract) by a model, by choice by a person

A model does not choose its prism; its contract sets what it reads. A person chooses freely. The prism is the same kind of thing either way; only what decides it differs.

### Is a selection, as a boundary and a call's argument are

A prism, a boundary and a call's argument are all selections from the space. The boundary selects what may be touched, the argument selects what is passed, the prism selects what is read. One mechanism, three uses.

### Is named for now

Prism is a provisional name, kept until a better one appears. The concept is stable; the word is not.

## Platform

The platform is the part of this ontology that lets programs, and not only knowers, compound. It is derived from that goal alone and names no technology.

### Exists so that programs compound as knowers do

Knowers compound by reading briefs that stand on ground. The platform exists so that programs can do the same: stand on contracts, be called by them, and leave trails. Without it programs are glue between knowers rather than citizens among them.

### Is derived from that goal, not from what was built

Nothing in the platform is there because it already exists in code. Each part is derived from the goal of compounding programs and then checked against what has been built, not the reverse. What was built that does not serve the goal is not in the platform.

### Names no technology, because a dynamic is not a mechanism

The platform describes what must happen, never which tool makes it happen. Compounding is a dynamic; a database or a runtime is a mechanism, and any mechanism that yields the dynamic will do. Naming one would bind the ontology to a choice it does not need to make.

## Field

The field is the medium made readable by a program: the same briefs and links, held as data with addresses and with its own law inside it.

### Is the [medium](#medium) made readable by a program

The medium today is prose read by knowers. The field is that prose given addresses and structure so a program can traverse it too. Nothing is added to the meaning; only who can read it changes.

### Keeps its own law as data

The two laws, the contracts and the boundaries are held in the field as holons, not in the code of whatever runs it. An engine reads the law it executes under. This is what lets the law be changed by commit like anything else.

### Is read from a point, which makes a model's completion repeatable

Every read of the field is from one commit. A model's completion given that read is a function of that point, so the same point yields the same ground and a repeatable result. Reading from "latest" gives no such guarantee.

## Space

Space is what gives every holon an address. It says where a thing is, never what it is.

### Gives every [holon](#holon) an address

Every brief, word, commit and contract has one place in the space, and that place is what links point at. Placement in many wholes is possible because the address is one. The space is the field's coordinate system.

### Is where, never what

The space holds positions and nothing else. Meaning is in the holons; the space only locates them. Asking the space what something is gets no answer; asking where gets one.

## Citizen

A citizen is anything that lives in the field and compounds directly: a knower, or a program or engine, which cannot comprehend but can still stand on contracts.

### Compounds directly, where a monolith compounds only its glue

A citizen gains from every other citizen's contract without being rebuilt. A monolith gains only in the code that joins its parts, because its parts are not addressable from outside. The field makes every part a citizen.

### Is a [knower](#knower), or a program or an engine, which cannot comprehend

Citizens come in two kinds. Knowers hold comprehension; programs and engines do not, and act only by contract. Both are citizens because both compound; only knowers win.

## Contract

A contract is a brief of a particular kind: one value that every reader of it reads the same way. It lives in the field it governs, and for a component it is what draws its boundary.

### Is a [brief](#brief) of a kind

A contract is written, ratified and linked like any brief. What sets it apart is that it is read by programs as well as knowers, so its surface must be exact. It is still a holon with a surface and a depth.

### Is one value that every reader reads

A contract states one thing, and every reader, knower or program, takes it to mean the same. Two readers who read a contract differently have found a broken contract. This is what makes matching on contracts possible.

### Lives in the [field](#field) it governs

A contract is not stored beside the field or outside it; it is a holon inside the field, with an address. Changing it is a commit. Reading from a point reads the contract as it stood at that point.

### Is what draws, when it is a component's

A component's contract is what marks the component's edge. Everything the contract names is inside; what it does not name is outside. The boundary of a component is its contract seen as a region.

## Engine

An engine is a program that executes under the field's law, reading that law as data. It is one of many, reached by its contract, and hosts its own runs.

### Executes under the law, which it reads as data

An engine does not embody the law in its code; it reads the law from the field and obeys it. Change the law by commit and the engine behaves differently without being rebuilt. This is what keeps the core unbound.

### Is one of many, so the core is unbound

No engine is the engine. Several can execute the same field under the same law, and none is privileged. The core of the platform is therefore not bound to any implementation.

### Is reached by its [contract](#contract), whatever happens inside

A caller reaches an engine by matching its contract, not by knowing what it does inside. Two engines with the same contract are interchangeable to every caller. The inside is the engine's own affair.

### Hosts its own runs

An engine runs programs, and each run happens inside the engine that hosts it. The run's boundary and trail are the engine's to enforce and record. A run does not exist outside some engine.

## Boundary

A boundary is a region of space drawn by permission: set before a run, live during it, and what a private region looks like from outside.

### Draws a region of [space](#space) by permission

A boundary is a set of addresses a run may read or write. It is drawn by whoever grants the permission, not by the run. Everything outside it does not exist for the run.

### Is set before a run and stays live during it

The boundary is fixed before the run begins, so the run cannot widen it. It stays in force for the whole run, so a run cannot drift past it. Changing a boundary is a new run.

### Is what a private region is, seen from outside

A private region is one whose boundary excludes everyone but its owner. From outside, that looks like a boundary; from inside, it looks like privacy. They are one thing seen from two sides.

## Substrate

The substrate is what holds the field at rest: the stores that keep it, the order they give each commit, and the chunk that may become the holon. It is the lever, typed against itself.

### Holds the [field](#field) at rest

When nothing is reading or writing, the field is the substrate. It is the durable form of the medium. Engines and interfaces come and go; the substrate persists.

### Gives each store one order, and judges each [commit](#commit) before it stands

Each store in the substrate has a single linear order of commits. Before a commit takes its place in that order, the substrate checks it against the law it holds. A commit that fails does not stand.

### Holds the chunk, which may become the [holon](#holon)

The substrate stores in chunks: units of data it can address and move. A chunk is not yet a holon, but the holon may be built on it, so that the smallest unit of storage and the smallest unit of meaning coincide. Whether they do is not yet decided.

### Is the lever, typed against itself

The substrate's types describe the substrate. Its schema is a holon it holds, and changing that holon changes the substrate. This is the lever: the one place where the field can move itself.

## Program

A program is a citizen that does not comprehend: it is called by contract, runs under a boundary, and leaves a trail. A model run as a program is still a knower.

### Is a [citizen](#citizen); a model run as one is still a knower

A program compounds like any citizen, by standing on contracts. When a model is run as a program, it takes a contract and a boundary like one, but it still holds comprehension, so it remains a knower. The two labels are not exclusive.

### Is called by matching its contract, never by name

Nothing calls a program by its name. A caller states what it needs, the contract that matches is found, and the program behind it runs. Names are for people; contracts are for calls.

### Runs under a [boundary](#boundary) and leaves a [trail](#trail)

Every run of a program is confined to a boundary set before it starts and leaves a trail of what it read and wrote. The boundary is what makes a run safe; the trail is what makes it checkable. A run with neither is not a run in the field.

## Interface

The interface is what a knower sees: it rests on the medium's law and reads the field for everything else. It is built next, because it can only be judged by being used.

### Rests on the [medium](#medium)'s law, and reads the [field](#field) for the rest

The interface hard-codes only the two laws. Every other behaviour, the gradient, the trail, the contracts, it derives by reading the field. Change the field and the interface changes with it.

### Is built next, since it has to be built to be felt

The interface cannot be specified into existence; whether it gives wins is felt in use. So it is the next thing to build after this ontology. Its brief will be written by debriefing what building it taught.

## Specification

The specification is the holon this document belongs to. It is whole only in its goals, background and taste, and everything in it emanates from this file.

### Is a [holon](#holon) whole only in its goals, background and taste

A specification is not whole in its detail, which changes as it is built. It is whole in what it wants, where it came from, and what it prefers. Those three are what a reader needs to make every remaining decision the same way the writer would.

### Emanates from this file

The ontology is the root of the specification. Every other spec brief descends from a word defined here and must be consistent with it. If a spec brief and this file disagree, this file is ground.

## Processor

The processor is what runs everything above it and nothing below: the machine. It holds no comprehension.

### Runs everything above it and nothing below

Substrate, engines, interface, programs all run on the processor. Nothing in the ontology is beneath it. It is the floor.

### Holds no [comprehension](#comprehension)

The processor computes and stores but comprehends nothing. Comprehension lives only in knowers. This is the line between a knower and the machine it runs on.

## Harness

The harness is what confines today's model sessions: a fixed context that leaves no trail and forgets at the end. The field dissolves it by holding everything a harness discarded.

### Leaves no [trail](#trail)

A harnessed session reads and writes with no record of what it read or in what order. When it ends, its trail is gone with it. Nothing about the run can be counted or repeated.

### Is dissolved by the [field](#field), which harnesses everything

The field holds every read and write at an address, so nothing a session does is lost. What the harness confined, the field keeps. The harness does not need to be removed; once the field exists there is nothing left for it to hold.
