# Ontology

## Code

The code is the small set of rules that governs how knowledge is written, held and read in this project. It has one value, two laws, and a home that is prose today and the field once that is built.

### Has one value, [comprehension](#comprehension), and one unit of it, the [win](#win)

Everything the code asks for is asked for the sake of one thing: comprehension in a reader. There is no second value to trade it against, so every rule is a way of buying more of it. The unit in which it is bought is the win, the comprehension a single reading yields, and the code is judged by wins and nothing else.

### Has two laws, and everything else follows from them

The code does not accumulate rules; it has two laws, and the rest are consequences. The first is that the gradient runs one way, so a reader can stop whole at any depth. The second is that ground must stay true for readers not yet met, so what is written cannot depend on who happens to be reading. Any further rule that cannot be derived from these is suspect.

### Runs on prose in files today, and on the [field](#field) when it is built

The code does not wait for its infrastructure. Today it runs on markdown files in a repository, with links that need no resolver and trails that no one records. When the field exists, the same code runs on it unchanged; only the addresses, contracts and memory become real.

## Brief

A brief is the unit of writing: a whole that hands over one win at its surface and keeps its detail within. It is what the medium is made of.

### Hands over one [win](#win), at the surface

A brief exists to give a reader one gain in comprehension, and it gives it at the top, before the detail. A reader who reads only the surface should already have the win; the rest is there for whoever needs the reasoning. A brief that needs to be read to the end to be understood has failed its form.

### Is made by debriefing: conclusion to the surface, detail within

Writing a brief is the act of debriefing a stretch of work: the conclusion is lifted to the surface and the detail that produced it is placed beneath. This is the opposite of the order in which the work happened, which is why it has to be done deliberately. The result is a document that reads conclusion-first and reasons-second.

### Stands on [ground](#ground), and becomes it

A brief is written against what is already known and settled; that is its ground. Once it is committed it joins that ground, and later briefs stand on it in turn. So a brief is both a reading of the ground and an addition to it, and this is how the ground grows.

### Is a [holon](#holon)

A brief is whole on its own and at the same time part of a larger whole. Read alone it makes sense; read in place it is one node in a structure of briefs. That double nature is what makes it addressable, linkable and stoppable at any depth.

### Explains or points out everything it uses

A brief may not lean on something the reader has not been given. Every term or fact it depends on is either explained in place or pointed to by a link. This is what makes a brief self-standing for a reader who arrives with nothing, which the second law requires.

### Is listed under its ground by its title, so a linking title must read as a sentence

Briefs are listed under the ground they stand on, and what is shown in that list is the title. A title that links to other words therefore has to make sense on its own, as a sentence, since the list is the first and often the only place it is read. This is why the titles in this document are prose, not labels.

## Holon

A holon is something that is a whole and a part at once. It is the shape shared by briefs, words, commits and specifications.

### Is within one whole, in prose, and placed in many, in the field

In prose, a holon sits inside exactly one larger whole: a section inside a document, a document inside a directory. In the field, the same holon can be placed in many wholes, because placement is by address rather than by containment. The one-parent tree is a limitation of files, not of holons.

### Has a surface and a depth, which are one holon seen twice

A holon is not split into a summary and a body; it has a surface and a depth that are the same thing seen at two distances. The surface is what a reader gets by stopping early, the depth is what remains if they go on. Neither is a separate object, which is why a link to the holon serves both.

### Has an address, which is what a [link](#link) points at

Every holon can be named by an address, and a link is nothing more than that address written down. In prose the address is a path and an anchor; in the field it is a location in the space. What a link points at is the holon, never a copy of it.

### Is Koestler's word

The term comes from Arthur Koestler, who coined it for things that are simultaneously wholes and parts, as cells are in organs and organs in bodies. It is borrowed here unchanged, because the same simultaneity is what briefs and words have. Naming the source keeps the word from being reinvented.

## Win

A win is the unit of comprehension: what one reading gives a reader, net of what it cost. It is the measure the whole code is judged by.

### Is measured as [comprehension](#comprehension) gained against reading spent

A win is not comprehension alone but comprehension per cost. A brief that gives a great deal but takes a great deal to read may win less than a short one that gives just enough. Reading is spent in attention and context, and the win is what remains after that spending.

### Is unequal, which is what makes an order possible

Not every reading wins the same amount, and that inequality is what allows briefs to be ordered. If all wins were equal there would be nothing to place first, and the gradient would have no direction. Because some readings give more for less, there is a best order, and the medium can be arranged along it.

### Is the test of writing

Whether a piece of writing is good is settled by the win it produces, not by its length, style or completeness. A brief that produces no win is not a brief, however careful. This gives the code a single test to apply to everything written under it.

## Ground

Ground is what is already known and can be stood on: the settled briefs a new one is written against. For a model, it is everything it has.

### Is what a model is, since a fresh session holds nothing else

A model in a fresh session brings no memory of its own; what it knows of the project is exactly the ground it is given to read. So the ground is not context added to the model, it is what the model is, for the duration of the run. This is why the ground has to be complete and true rather than merely helpful.

### Must stay true for readers not yet met, which is the second law

Ground is read by readers the writer will never see, human and model, in sessions that have not yet begun. It therefore cannot depend on shared history or unstated assumptions; it must be true on its own for whoever comes. This is the second law of the code, and it is what makes forgetting safe.

### Carries confidence when it describes, and status when it directs

Ground comes in two modes. When it describes how things are, it carries a confidence, since a description can be more or less certain. When it directs what should be done, it carries a status, since a direction can be proposed, accepted or superseded. A reader needs to know which mode they are in to know how hard to lean on it.

## Comprehension

Comprehension is the one value of the code: understanding held in a knower. It is not the same as information, and it grows by being joined.

### Is not information

Information can be stored, copied and transmitted without anyone understanding it. Comprehension is what happens when a knower takes information in and can act from it. The medium holds information; only knowers hold comprehension, and the code optimises for the second.

### Compounds by joining

Comprehension grows faster than the sum of its parts when pieces are joined. Two briefs understood together give more than each understood apart, because the relation between them is itself understood. Links are how joining is written down, and the medium is where the compounding accumulates.

## Link

A link is an address written into a brief. It joins without merging, names ground without restating it, and in prose needs no working target.

### Joins [briefs](#brief) without breaking them

A link lets one brief lean on another without absorbing it. Each brief stays whole; the link is the seam between them. This is how comprehension can compound across briefs while every brief still stands alone.

### Names [ground](#ground) so it is never restated

Rather than repeat what is already settled, a brief links to it. That keeps the ground in one place, so it can be corrected once and stay true everywhere. Restatement would drift; a link cannot.

### Opens a door past the brief

A link is also an invitation: the reader who wants more can go through it, and the reader who does not can stay. It is the mechanism by which a brief can be stopped whole at any depth yet still lead further. The door is optional, which is the point.

### Needs no target yet, in prose

In files, a link may point at a word or brief that does not yet exist, and nothing breaks. The address is a promise, and the target can be written later. In the field a link resolves against real addresses, but the prose stage does not wait for that.

## Trail

A trail is the record of a run: what was read, in what order, against which state of the field. It is shown by the interface and never written into the medium.

### Runs the [links](#link) backward

Where a link points forward from a brief to its ground, a trail runs the other way: from what was read back to what led there. Following a trail backward reproduces the path a reader took. It is the inverse of the link structure, seen from the reader's side.

### Is shown by the [interface](#interface), never written in the [medium](#medium)

The medium contains only briefs and links; a trail is not one of them. What a reader did is displayed by the interface, which can see the run, but it is not written into the knowledge it ran over. Keeping trails out of the medium keeps the medium about the subject, not about its readers.

### Records a run: what was read, in what order, against which state

A trail is specific: it names the briefs read, their order, and the state of the field at the time. Given those three, the run can be understood or repeated. Without the state, the same order over a changed field would mean something different.

### Is walked by [knower](#knower) or by address in the [space](#space)

A trail can be walked by a knower reading along it, or it can be walked mechanically, by address, through the space. The first produces comprehension; the second produces reach and counts. Both use the same trail.

### Is named for now, not settled

"Trail" is the current name for this thing and may change. The concept is held more firmly than the word. Flagging this keeps later readers from treating the term as fixed.

## Medium

The medium is what the knowledge is written in: briefs and links, and nothing else. It is where comprehension compounds and what makes forgetting safe.

### Is made of [briefs](#brief) and [links](#link), and nothing else

There are two kinds of thing in the medium: briefs, which are wholes, and links, which join them. Anything else, such as trails, comments or status, lives beside the medium, not in it. This minimality is what lets the medium be moved from files to the field unchanged.

### Is where [comprehension](#comprehension) compounds

Comprehension lives in knowers, but it compounds in the medium, because the medium holds the joins between briefs that knowers cannot hold across sessions. A knower reads the medium, gains, writes back, and the next knower starts higher. The medium is the accumulator.
[blind: conflict with Knower / Is where comprehension lives, and nowhere else]

### Holds everything that gives work its direction

Goals, background, taste and settled conclusions all live in the medium as briefs. Nothing that steers the work is kept outside it, in someone's head or in a chat. So a knower who reads the medium has all the direction there is.

### Makes forgetting safe

Because the medium holds everything that matters, a knower can forget without loss. A session can end, a model can start fresh, a person can step away, and the next reader recovers the state by reading. This is the practical payoff of the second law.

## Kernel

The kernel is the built layer that hosts the field, judges every act under a boundary, and attaches stores. It sits between the interface and the circuitry.

### Hosts the [field](#field) open, and rests in the [substrate](#substrate)

The kernel is what keeps the field open for reading and writing while a run is in progress. When nothing is running, the field rests in the substrate as stores, and the kernel rests with it. Hosting is the live state; resting is the durable one.

### Judges every act under a [boundary](#boundary)

No act reaches the field without passing the kernel, and the kernel judges each one against the boundary set for the run. What is outside the boundary is not seen and cannot be written. This is what makes private regions and permissions real rather than advisory.

### Attaches stores on declared terms

The kernel does not own storage; it attaches stores, and each is attached on terms that are declared, which is a contract. A store that does not declare its terms cannot be attached. This is how many kinds of storage can serve one field.

### Sits below the [interface](#interface), above the circuitry

The kernel is the layer the interface is a client of, and the layer that sits directly on the processor and substrate. It is neither what the user sees nor what the hardware does. Placing it precisely keeps each layer to its own job.

## Field

The field is the medium made real: the same briefs and links, with addresses and contracts, kept in full. It stands over a space and is read from a point.

### Stands over a [space](#space)

The field is content; the space is the set of addresses that content lives at. The field stands over the space the way a map stands over coordinates. Without the space there is no way to place a holon in many wholes.

### Is the [medium](#medium) with addresses and contracts, and keeps everything

The field adds to the medium exactly two things: real addresses, so links resolve, and contracts, so parts interoperate. And it keeps everything, so nothing read or written is lost. It is not a different medium; it is the same one, built.

### Is read from a point, which is what makes completion repeatable

Every read of the field is taken from a point in it: a state and a position. Because the point is named, the same read can be taken again, and a model completion run against it can be reproduced. This is what consolidation and re-derivation depend on.

## Boundary

A boundary is a region of space drawn by permission from the viewer's side. It is set before a run and stays live through it.

### Draws a region of [space](#space) by permission, on the viewer's side

A boundary is not a wall around content; it is the set of addresses a particular viewer is permitted to see and touch. It is defined from the viewer outward, so two viewers of the same field have different boundaries. The field is one; what each sees of it is bounded.

### Is set before a run and stays live during it

The boundary is decided before a run begins, so the run's reads are known in advance, and it remains in force throughout, so the run cannot enlarge it. The kernel judges every act against it as the run proceeds. This is what lets a trail be trusted: nothing outside the boundary was read.

### Is what a private region is, seen from outside

A private region is not a special kind of place; it is what a boundary looks like to someone who is not inside it. Privacy is the outside view of permission. This collapses two concepts into one.

## Gradient

The gradient is the one-way ordering of the medium that lets a reader stop whole at any depth. It is the first law.

### Lets a reader stop whole at any depth

Because briefs are arranged so that what comes first needs nothing from what comes later, a reader can stop at any point and still hold a whole. The surface of a brief is whole; the surface of the medium is whole. Nothing read so far is invalidated by what was not read.

### Runs one way, which is the first law

The gradient has a direction: from surface to depth, from ground to what stands on it, and never back. A brief may depend on what is above it, never on what is below. This is the first law of the code, and the stopping property follows from it.

### Places by reach, which the [trail](#trail) can count

Where a brief sits on the gradient is determined by how far it is from the surface in links, its reach. A trail records the path a reader took, and so can count reach directly. Placement is therefore measurable, not a matter of judgment.

### Keeps order without discipline

Because the gradient is a property of the structure, not of the writer's habits, order is kept even when no one is being careful. A brief written out of place is visible as out of place. The medium stays ordered because its form enforces it, not because its authors do.

## Contract

A contract is a brief of a particular kind: the declared terms under which a part of the field interoperates. It lives in the field it governs.

### Is a [brief](#brief) of a kind

A contract is not a separate kind of document; it is a brief whose win is the terms something can be used on. It is written, listed and linked like any brief. This keeps interoperation inside the medium instead of beside it.

### Is one value, read three ways

A single contract is read by a person as documentation, by a model as ground, and by the kernel as a rule to enforce. There is one text; the three readings do not diverge because they share it. Splitting it into three documents would let them drift.

### Lives in the [field](#field) it governs

A contract is stored in the field whose parts it governs, at an address there, not in some registry outside. Whoever can read the governed region can read its terms. This is why a substrate that keeps every contract is enough for anything to interoperate.

### Draws, when it is a component's

When a contract belongs to a component, it draws the region that component owns and the terms on which others may enter. In that case the contract is also a boundary. Other contracts describe; a component's contract draws.
[blind: title underdetermines]

## Space

The space is the set of addresses. It gives every holon a place, and it is where the field is what.

### Gives every [holon](#holon) an address

Every holon, from a word to a specification, has an address in the space. That is what a link points at and what a trail walks. Without the space, holons could only be contained; with it, they can be placed.

### Is where, while the [field](#field) is what

Space and field are two aspects, not two things. The space answers where something is; the field answers what is there. Keeping the distinction lets the same content be placed in many wholes.

## Citizen

A citizen is anything that acts in the field: a knower, a program, or an engine. Citizens compound directly where a monolith compounds only its glue.

### Compounds directly, where a monolith compounds only its glue

When the field is made of citizens, each one's gains are kept and reused by the next. A monolith gains only in the code that wires its parts together, while the parts themselves stay fixed. Citizens compound where a monolith cannot.

### Is a [knower](#knower), or a program or an engine, which cannot comprehend

There are three kinds of citizen. A knower comprehends. A program and an engine act in the field but do not comprehend; they run and are reached. The distinction matters because only the first can produce a win.

## Knower

A knower is where comprehension lives. It is bounded, acts by reading and writing, and when it is a model, it is a session.

### Is where [comprehension](#comprehension) lives, and nowhere else

Comprehension is held by knowers and by nothing else. The medium holds information; programs and engines hold state; only a knower understands. Every rule in the code is about getting comprehension into knowers.

### Is bounded, which the whole code follows from

A knower can hold only so much and read only so far. That limit is why briefs must be short, why gradients must let a reader stop, why ground must be linked rather than restated. The code is what follows from taking the bound seriously.
[blind: conflict with Code / Has two laws, and everything else follows from them]

### Acts in two ways, reading and writing, and is bounded in both

A knower reads the field and writes to it, and both acts are limited: it can read only within its boundary and write only within it. Neither act escapes the kernel's judgment. The two acts are the whole of what a knower does in the field.

### Is a session, when it is a model

When the knower is a model, the unit of it is a session: one context, one run, one trail. A new session is a new knower, holding nothing but the ground it is given. This is why the harness's notion of a persistent agent does not fit.

### Is named for now, not settled

"Knower" is a provisional name. It is used because "agent" and "user" both fail to cover both people and models. The word may change; the concept will not.

## Substrate

The substrate is where the field rests when not hosted: stores, contracts, chunks. It sits below the kernel and is the README's lever.

### Holds the [field](#field) at rest, as stores

When the kernel is not hosting it, the field exists as stores in the substrate. A store is durable and passive; the field becomes active only when the kernel opens it. Rest and hosting are two states of one field.

### Keeps every [contract](#contract), which is how anything interoperates

Every contract is kept in the substrate, so whatever needs to interoperate can find the terms. Nothing has to be pre-wired; it reads the contract and acts on it. This is how many engines and stores serve one field.

### Holds the chunk, which may become the [holon](#holon)

The substrate's native unit is the chunk: a piece of stored content. A chunk is not yet a holon, because a holon has a surface, a depth and a place. When a chunk is given those, it becomes a holon, and the substrate stops being merely storage.

### Is the README's lever, placed below the [kernel](#kernel)

The substrate is the layer the README points at as where to start building, because changing it moves everything above. It is placed below the kernel so that the kernel's judgments do not depend on how storage is done.

## Commit

A commit is a holon with a place in time. It is the ratification of a change to the medium, and this document's own rule.

### Is a [holon](#holon) with a place in time

A commit is whole and part, like any holon, but it is also placed on a timeline. It can be addressed both by what it contains and by when. That is what makes a state of the field nameable.

### Is on a branch

A commit belongs to a branch, and a branch is a line of commits with a holder. Branches are how parallel work stays separate until it is merged. The branch is the dimension a commit is placed in.

### Is the ratification, which is this document's rule

A change to the medium is not real until it is committed; the commit is what ratifies it. This document is itself governed by that rule: it changes only by commit, and its current form is its latest one. Nothing in it is settled by discussion alone.

### Changes one [brief](#brief) whole, or one [word](#word) in place

A commit either replaces a brief entirely or changes one word where it stands. It does not half-edit a brief, because a half-edited brief is not whole. This keeps every commit a holon and every state of the medium readable.

### Publishes by placing into a dimension whose holders can see

Publishing is not a separate act; a commit is published when it is placed into a branch or region whose holders can see it. Visibility is a consequence of placement and boundary. Merging to a shared branch is publishing.

## Word

A word is the smallest holon: a term with a settled meaning and an address. When precise, it is a link.

### Is the smallest [holon](#holon)

A word is whole, since it means one thing, and a part, since it is used in briefs. It has a surface, its title, and a depth, its entry. There is no smaller unit that is still both.

### Is a [link](#link) when it is precise, which moved the code's language rule

When a word has one settled meaning, using it is the same as linking to it. That discovery changed the code's rule on language: precision is no longer style, it is linkage. A vague word breaks a link; a precise one makes one.
[blind: title underdetermines]

### Puts a lookup one step below every brief

Because every precise word is a link, every brief has a layer of lookups directly beneath it. A reader who does not know a term goes one step down and comes back. This is how briefs stay short without losing anyone.

## Prism

A prism is a selection cut from the field that loses nothing of it. It is taken under a contract by a model, and by choice by a person.

### Is cut from the [field](#field) and loses none of it

A prism is a view of the field, not a copy of part of it. What is cut out is still there, still addressed, still reachable. The prism selects what is shown, not what exists.

### Is taken under a [contract](#contract) by a model, by choice by a person

A model's prism is fixed by a contract, so its view is declared and reproducible. A person's prism is chosen freely, as they navigate. The same mechanism serves both, but only one is bound by terms.

### Is one selection among three

A prism is one of three ways the field is narrowed for a viewer; the boundary and the point of reading are the others. Each narrows differently: by permission, by state, by selection.
[blind: title underdetermines]

### Is named for now, not settled

"Prism" is a provisional name, chosen for the image of a single field split into views. The word may change; the mechanism will not.

## Interface

The interface is what shows the field to a knower. It rests on the medium's law for the knowledge half and is a client of the kernel for the built half.

### Rests on the [medium](#medium)'s law for the knowledge half, and is a client of the [kernel](#kernel) for the built one

The interface has two halves. What it shows of knowledge follows the medium's laws: gradient, surface and depth. What it does in the built system it asks the kernel for. It invents neither; it renders one and calls the other.

### Shows what the medium does not write

Trails, status, confidence and boundaries are not in the medium, but a reader needs to see them. The interface shows them. That is its job: to display what the briefs and links cannot carry.

### Is what the arc builds next, since it has to be built to be felt

The plan of work builds the interface next, because its value cannot be judged from description. Only a built interface shows whether stopping at any depth, walking a trail, or seeing a boundary actually helps a reader.

## Amplify

Amplify is the effect a win has when it is read by many: one write, many readers, each gaining. It works by contraction and feeds on true ground.

### Is what a [win](#win) counts, one reader at a time

Amplification is measured in wins, and wins are had by one reader at a time. A brief read by a hundred readers amplifies a hundred times. There is no amplification without individual readers each gaining.

### Works by contraction, which the [brief](#brief) does

A brief amplifies by contracting: it takes a long stretch of work and gives its win in a short reading. The shorter the reading for the same win, the more it amplifies. Debriefing is the act of contraction.

### Feeds on true [ground](#ground), and amplifies error as fast as truth

Amplification is indifferent to truth. A false brief on the ground is read as many times as a true one and spreads as fast. This is why ground must stay true: the medium multiplies whatever it holds.

### Is multiplied by every knower reading at one write's address

One write at one address is read by every knower who reaches that address. The multiplier is the number of readers, and it grows without the writer doing more. Addresses are what make a single write reusable.

### Supersedes the [harness](#harness)

The harness gave each session its own context and lost it at the end, so nothing amplified. Once writes go to addresses in a field, amplification replaces the harness as the way work carries forward.
[blind: title underdetermines]

## Comment

A comment stands on a brief and leaves it whole. It is free in the reader's own room and placed onto the brief by leave.

### Stands on a [brief](#brief) and leaves it whole

A comment is attached to a brief without changing it. The brief remains a whole; the comment is beside it, addressed to it. This is how readers can respond without editing.

### Is free from the reader's own room, and placed onto the brief by leave

A reader may comment freely within their own region of the space. For the comment to appear on the brief itself, in the writer's region, it needs the writer's leave. Permission is the boundary, seen from the reader's side.

### Is held open for the file

In the prose stage a comment has nowhere to live but the file, so the mechanism is left open until the field can hold comments beside briefs.
[blind: title underdetermines]

## Harness

The harness is the current way models are run: a session with a context that is set up and then lost. It leaves no trail and is dissolved by the field.

### Leaves no [trail](#trail)

A harness session reads and writes and then ends, and nothing records what it read, in what order, or against which state. What it learned goes nowhere. This is the loss the field is built to stop.

### Is dissolved by the [field](#field)'s one fact

The field keeps everything. Once that holds, there is no need for a harness that sets up and tears down a context; the session reads from a point and writes to an address. The harness dissolves into a knower with a boundary.
[blind: title underdetermines]

### Still names today's sessions in the code

Until the field is built, the code still speaks of harnesses, because that is what today's sessions run in. The word is kept for accuracy about the present, not as a concept the code needs.

## Program

A program is a citizen that runs as a process. It is bound by the match, and leaves a trail in the kernel.

### Is a [citizen](#citizen); a model run as one is still a knower

A program acts in the field on the same footing as any citizen. When a model is run as a program, it does not stop being a knower; it comprehends while it runs. The kind of citizen is set by what it is, not how it is invoked.

### Is bound by the match, never by keyword

A program is invoked when its contract matches the need, not when a name is called. Nothing addresses a program by keyword. Binding by match is what lets programs be replaced without changing what calls them.
[blind: title underdetermines]

### Runs as a process, and leaves a [trail](#trail) in the [kernel](#kernel)

When a program runs, it is a process under the kernel, and the kernel records what it read and wrote. That record is a trail, held in the kernel rather than the medium. So a program's run is accountable in the same way a knower's is.

## Engine

An engine is a citizen that is reached rather than run: a service with a contract in the substrate. There are many.

### Is a [citizen](#citizen), and there are many

An engine acts in the field like any citizen, and the field expects many of them: models, indexes, stores, tools. None is privileged. The field is made to be served by many engines at once.

### Is reached by its [contract](#contract) in the [substrate](#substrate)

An engine is used by reading its contract from the substrate and acting on the terms there. Nothing needs to know an engine in advance; it finds the contract and reaches it. This is what makes engines interchangeable.

## Processor

The processor is the bottom of the stack: what executes, under the kernel and beside the substrate. It holds no comprehension.

### Fixes the bottom under the [kernel](#kernel), beside the [substrate](#substrate)

The processor is where the stack ends. The kernel sits on it; the substrate sits beside it. Naming it fixes the bottom so that nothing below the kernel is left undescribed.

### Holds no [comprehension](#comprehension)

The processor executes and stores; it does not understand. Comprehension is in knowers, and nothing at the processor level is a knower. This keeps the value of the code from being attributed to the machine.

## Consolidation

Consolidation is the medium's own debriefing: what a brief does for one stretch of work, done for the whole field. It lets answers be re-derived from a state.

### Does for the medium what a [brief](#brief) does for one stretch of work

A brief contracts one stretch of work into a win. Consolidation contracts the medium: it lifts what many briefs have settled into fewer, higher ones. It is debriefing applied to the field itself.

### Lets an answer be re-derived from the same state of the [field](#field)

Because the field is read from a point and keeps everything, a consolidation can be run again from the same state and produce the same answer. This is what makes it trustworthy: it is a function of the field, not of who ran it.

When a knower reads and pieces come together, that is resonance. When the field is consolidated and pieces come together in the medium, that is the same event from the other side.

## Specification

A specification is a holon that is whole only in its goals, background and taste. It is what the spec tree becomes when rewritten under the code.

### Is a [holon](#holon) whole only in its goals, background and taste

A specification is whole in what it wants, what it knows, and what it prefers. It is not whole in its design or implementation, which change beneath it. Those three are its surface; everything else is depth that may be rewritten.

### Is what the spec tree becomes when rewritten under the code

The project's existing tree of specifications, rewritten as briefs with gradients, links and ground, becomes a specification in this sense. The rewriting does not add content; it gives the tree the code's form so it can be read at any depth.
