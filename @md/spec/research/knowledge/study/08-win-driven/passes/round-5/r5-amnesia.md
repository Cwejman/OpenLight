## 7. The goal test

The five words that serve "knowledge reaches whoever needs it next" least:

1. **Processor**. By its own relation it "Holds no comprehension" and "The win begins one layer up". A word that admits it holds no win has no place in a file ordered by win.
2. **Harness**. Retired, defined by what it lacks ("Leaves no trail"), and its third relation exists only to explain why the word still appears elsewhere. It is a footnote to the code, not a part of meaning.
3. **Kernel**. Four relations built on a thing whose fourth relation says it "May not need to exist" and whose author "is skeptical of the word and the thing". Knowledge cannot reach anyone through a word the writer doubts.
4. **Comment**. "Has no home yet when the brief is a file"; its two other relations are permission rules for a feature that does not exist. Nothing a reader of the dynamics can stand on.
5. **Specification**. Two relations, one of which is about a study's "second mark" and the other about "taste". It names the platform's document type, which is content, not a dynamic.

Does the order follow the goal? For the first six words, yes: Code, Comprehension, Win, Brief, Ground, Link is a reader's descent from purpose to unit to piece to what it rests on. After that it does not. Knower, the "whoever" in the goal, sits eighth, after Holon and Gradient, which are shapes of what the knower reads. Word, the smallest unit and the one every other word is spelled in, sits eleventh. Medium, the thing the code "turns written knowledge into", sits tenth. From Commit onward the order is the platform's build order, not the reader's win: Kernel precedes Substrate though it "rests in" it, Processor precedes Program and Engine though it grounds them, and Specification and Harness trail as leftovers. The head calls this order "a reader's judgment, observed"; the judgment holds for the top third and stops.

## 8. Verdict

Not settled enough to build on. The first eleven words are a coherent account of writing under a gradient and could be built on today. From Commit down, the file becomes a platform sketch wearing the ontology's form: seventeen words never given, thirty sentences of mechanism, a kernel its author doubts, and three flagged contradictions left standing. The one change that would raise it most: cut the file at Word, and move everything from Commit to Harness into a second file named for what it is, the platform's words, with its own head, its own confidence, and no claim to be "nothing more detailed than that".
