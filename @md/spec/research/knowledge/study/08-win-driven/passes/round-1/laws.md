# Audit: ontology.md against the two laws

Files read, and only these: `knowledge/code.md` (ratified, two laws), `study/08-win-driven/code.md` (edition, §1–3 author's, §4 pointer), `study/08-win-driven/ontology.md` (under audit). Line numbers below are ontology.md's unless a file is named.

The ontology's own rule, from its head (l.5): each h2 opens with its essence, then h3 relations, one per heading, the word reached linked in the title; "every relation has one home, the side that depends, and nothing is said twice, since what stands on a word follows it in the order." That last clause is itself a test: a relation whose home is the depending side, with the order being centrality, should link *backward* (to a word already given). Forward links are where the rule and the document part company, and they are where most of the copies below come from.

## 2. Relations on the wrong side or on both sides

Test applied: under the head's rule, the h3 lives with the word that *depends on* (stands on) the linked word. Where the parent is the thing stood on, the relation is on the wrong side. Where each word carries the same relation toward the other, it is on both sides.

### Stated at both ends
1. **Comprehension ↔ Knower.** Comprehension > "Lives in a knower, and nowhere else" (l.11) and Knower > "Is where comprehension lives" (l.27). Same fact, both words. Per the head's own order rule (what stands on a word follows it), Knower follows Comprehension, so Knower's is the home; Comprehension's h3 is the copy.
2. **Space ↔ Field.** Space > "Is not the field, which has a different nature" (l.287) and Field > "Stands over a space" (l.295). Field depends on space ("To every address the field assigns what is there"); Space's "is not" relation states no dependence and carries the same where/what fact. Home: Field.
3. **Ground ↔ Amplify.** Ground > "Must stay true for readers not yet met, which is the second law" (l.139) and Amplify > "Amplifies error as fast as truth, which is why ground must stay true" (l.79) and Amplify > "Feeds on ground" (l.75). The duty is stated under Ground and twice under Amplify.
4. **Kernel ↔ Engine, Kernel ↔ Boundary.** Kernel > "Is the one thing every act goes through" (l.427) carries "it judges every act under the boundaries it keeps"; Boundary > "Is set before a run and kept by the kernel" (l.319) carries "every act is judged under one rule the kernel keeps"; Engine > "Reaches the field only through the kernel" (l.519) carries the same again. Engine and Boundary depend on Kernel, so theirs are the homes; Kernel's h3 is the general copy.
5. **Substrate ↔ Processor ↔ Kernel** on "two circuitries below the kernel": Substrate l.455, Processor l.463, Kernel l.435. Processor and Substrate depend on Kernel (they are placed relative to it); Kernel's "Sits below the interface" restating what is below it is the wrong-side copy.
