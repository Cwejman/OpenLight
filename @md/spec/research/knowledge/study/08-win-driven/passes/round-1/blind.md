# Ontology

## Comprehension

Comprehension is the understanding a reader actually holds, as distinct from the text that produced it. It is the quantity the whole document is built to increase.

### Lives in a [knower](#knower), and nowhere else

Comprehension exists only inside a knower. A file, a database, a transcript, or a program may carry the information that produced it, but none of them comprehends; the moment the knower is removed, what remains is marks, not understanding. Every later term in this document is placed relative to that fact.

### Is not information

Information is what can be stored, copied and counted; comprehension is what a knower has after reading it, and the two do not scale together. A long text may leave little comprehension and a short one much. The document treats the gap between them as the thing to be managed rather than as noise.

### Compounds by joining

Comprehension grows not by accumulation but by joining: a new piece taken in connects to what is already held, and the result is more than the sum. This is why order and adjacency of reading matter, and why the medium's job is to put the right things next to each other.

## Knower

A knower is whatever can hold comprehension: a person, or a model in the moment it is reading. It is the fixed point every other term is measured against.

### Is where [comprehension](#comprehension) lives

The knower is the only place comprehension can be, so all measurement of comprehension is measurement at a knower. Anything said to be "in" the medium is really a promise about what a knower will hold after reading it.

### Is bounded, which the whole code follows from

A knower can hold only so much at once, and reads only so fast. Every rule in this document, from the shape of a brief to the laws of ground and gradient, is a consequence of that bound: if a knower were unlimited, nothing here would need to exist. The bound is the axiom, and the rest is derivation.

### Acts in two ways, and time is the order of the acts

A knower reads and a knower writes, and those are its only two acts on the medium. Time, for the purposes of this document, is nothing more than the sequence in which those acts occur; there is no separate clock. History, trails, and consolidation are all defined over that order.

### Writes as well as reads

The knower is not only a consumer. What it comprehends it can write back as a new brief, and that write is how the medium gains ground. A knower that only reads leaves the medium where it found it.

### Is named for now, not settled

"Knower" is a working term. The author has not committed to it, and a better word may replace it without changing what the term points at.

## Win

A win is the net gain of a read: comprehension acquired against the reading it cost. It is the unit in which everything in the medium is valued.

### Measures [comprehension](#comprehension) gained against reading spent

A win is a ratio, not an amount. It takes the comprehension a knower ends up with and sets it against the words, time and attention that were spent to get it. A text that gives much for little is a large win; a text that gives little for much is a small one, however long or correct it is.

### Is unequal

Wins differ, and the difference is the point. Two briefs on the same subject can yield very different wins, and the same brief yields different wins to different knowers. Because wins are unequal they can be ordered, and that ordering is what the gradient later uses.

### Is the test of writing

A piece of writing is judged by the win it hands over and by nothing else. Style, completeness and length are not criteria in themselves; they matter only as they raise or lower the win. Writing that fails this test is not made better by being thorough.

## Amplify

To amplify is to make a win larger than the reading behind it would ordinarily allow, by concentrating what is true and making it reach further. It is the mechanism the medium exists to run.

### Is what a [win](#win) measures

A win is the reading of the amplifier: how much more a knower got out than it put in. Amplification is the cause, the win is the measurement, and the two are never separated in this document.

### Works by contraction, which the [brief](#brief) does

Amplification comes from cutting, not from adding. When a text is contracted so that only what raises comprehension remains, the reading spent falls while the comprehension stays, and the win rises. The brief is the form that performs this contraction.

### Feeds on [ground](#ground)

Amplification needs something to stand on. A brief can be short only because it names ground the knower already holds or can reach; without that ground it would have to restate everything and could not contract. The more ground there is, the more can be amplified.

### Amplifies error as fast as truth, which is why [ground](#ground) must stay true

The amplifier does not discriminate. If the ground a brief stands on is false, the contraction spreads that falsehood with the same efficiency it spreads truth, and the win becomes a loss the knower cannot see. This is the motive for the second law: ground has to stay true because the amplifier will magnify whatever it is given.

### Resonates through the [medium](#medium) once a write is [consolidated](#consolidation)

A single write amplifies for the knower who reads it. Once that write is consolidated into the medium, everything that links to it or stands on it is amplified too, so one good write raises many wins at once. The medium acts as a resonator for consolidated writes.

## Consolidation

Consolidation is the act by which a write stops being one knower's note and becomes part of the medium that other reads depend on. It is debriefing done at the medium's scale.

### Is what [debriefing](#brief) does at the scale of the medium

When a knower debriefs, it contracts what it has learned into a brief. Consolidation is the same act performed for the medium as a whole: what was scattered across many writes is contracted into ground that others can stand on. The medium debriefs itself through consolidation.

### Makes inference deterministic, because the [field](#field) has a point

Once a write is consolidated, the field has a definite point for every question that write settles, and any reader cut at that point gets the same answer. Before consolidation, inference from the medium may vary with which write a reader happens to find; after it, the inference is fixed. Determinism is the payoff of consolidation.

"Consolidation" is the author's own term for this act, chosen on the date given. It should be read as a coinage in this document's sense, not as the word's use elsewhere, and the date marks when it entered the vocabulary.

## Brief

A brief is a short piece of writing that hands a reader one win. It means what it means in ordinary speech: the concise version that lets someone act.

### Hands over one [win](#win)

A brief is sized to a win, not to a subject. It gives a knower one gain and stops; anything that would be a second gain belongs in a second brief, linked or nested. This keeps every brief judgeable by the one win it carries.

### Keeps [comprehension](#comprehension) high while cutting information

The brief's craft is to remove information without removing comprehension. It cuts what the knower already holds, what can be linked to, and what does not raise understanding, and keeps only what does. This is the contraction the amplifier works by.

### Can be found from the [ground](#ground) it names

A brief names the ground it stands on, and so it can be reached from that ground: anyone standing on the same ground can find the brief that uses it. This makes the medium navigable in both directions, from brief to ground and from ground to brief.

### Means what everyday speech means

No technical redefinition is intended. A brief is what a lawyer, an officer or a designer means by one: the short document that tells you what you need in order to act. The document borrows the word precisely so that it does not need to be explained.

### Can be a whole file or a section in one

The brief is not tied to a file boundary. A file may be one brief, or a file may hold several briefs as sections, each handing over its own win. What makes a brief is the win, not the container.

## Ground

Ground is what a brief stands on: comprehension the reader already holds or can reach by link, and so need not be restated. It is what lets briefs be short.

### Is stood on by [briefs](#brief)

Every brief presupposes something, and that something is its ground. A brief that stood on nothing would have to say everything and could not be brief. Ground is therefore not optional background but the structural support of every brief.

### Fills the bounded place [comprehension](#comprehension) lives in

The knower's capacity is bounded, and ground is what occupies it. What a knower has already taken in becomes ground for the next read, so a brief written for a knower with the right ground can be far shorter than one written for a knower without it. Ground is comprehension acting as capacity.

### Must stay true for readers not yet met, which is the second law

Ground is stood on by readers the writer will never meet, at times the writer cannot foresee. Because the amplifier spreads whatever ground carries, ground that goes false poisons every brief that stands on it. The second law of the medium is therefore that ground must stay true, and stay true for those readers, not only for the writer.

## Link

A link is a reference from one brief to another. It is how briefs join without merging and how ground is named instead of repeated.

### Joins [briefs](#brief) without breaking them

A link connects two briefs while leaving each whole and each still handing over its own win. Neither brief is cut open or absorbed into the other. This is what allows the medium to grow by joining rather than by rewriting.

### Names [ground](#ground) so it is never restated

Instead of repeating what the reader can already stand on, a brief links to it. The link says "this is my ground" and lets the reader who already holds it move on, while the reader who does not can follow it. Restatement is replaced by naming.

### Opens a door past the brief

A link is also an exit. It lets the reader who wants more leave the brief and go deeper, without the brief itself having to carry the depth. The brief stays short because the door is there.

### Is one of two ways briefs join, and the other is nesting

Briefs join either by link or by nesting, and these are the only two. Nesting places one brief inside another and carries the gradient; linking connects briefs across the medium and carries every other relation. Nothing else joins briefs.

### Needs no target yet

A link may be written before the brief it points at exists. It names ground that is still to be written, and it becomes a door only when that ground arrives. This lets writers name what they stand on without waiting for it.

## Trail

A trail is the record of how briefs have been reached and linked, read backward. It is not written into the medium but shown by the interface.

### Runs the [links](#link) backward

A link points forward from a brief to its ground; a trail is the same links followed in reverse, from a ground to everything that stands on it. Where links answer "what does this rest on," trails answer "what rests on this."

### Is shown by the [interface](#interface), never written in the [medium](#medium)

The trail is derived, not authored. The medium contains only briefs and links; the interface computes the trail from them and displays it. Writing trails into the medium would duplicate what the links already say and would go stale.

### Shows a [ground](#ground) its second life

A ground's first life is being written; its second is being stood on. The trail shows the second: every brief that has come to rest on that ground since. This is how a writer learns what a piece of ground has become.

### Carries history as well as reach

A trail records two things: how far a brief's influence reaches through the links, and the order in which it was reached. Reach is structural; history is temporal, since time is the order of the knower's acts. The trail carries both.

### Is walked by [knower](#knower) or by place in the [space](#space)

A trail can be followed per knower, giving that knower's own history of reads, or per place, giving everything that has arrived at one address in the space. The two walks use the same links and yield different views.

### Is named for now, not settled

"Trail" is a working term and may be renamed. What it refers to, the backward reading of links, is settled; the word is not.

## Gradient

The gradient is the ordering of briefs by the wins they hand over, running from the ground up. It is the structure that nesting carries and that keeps the medium ordered.

### Grades [briefs](#brief) over [wins](#win)

Because wins are unequal, briefs can be ranked by them, and the gradient is that ranking. A brief lower on the gradient is stood on by briefs higher up; a brief higher up hands over a win that presupposes the ones below.

### Runs one way, which is the first law

The gradient has a direction and never cycles: a brief stands on ground below it and never on ground above it. This is the first law of the medium. Without it, ground could depend on what depends on it, and no read would have a starting point.

### Is the one relation nesting carries, and the [link](#link) carries the rest

Nesting one brief inside another expresses exactly one thing: position on the gradient. Every other relation between briefs, reference, contrast, example, is expressed by link. This division keeps nesting meaningful and links free.

### Is a holarchy of [wins](#win), so [holons](#holon) are its parts

The gradient is a holarchy: each level is a whole made of parts that are themselves wholes. Its units are wins, and the things that carry wins are holons. So the gradient is not a tree of files but a nesting of holons, each whole at its own level.

### Places by reach, which the [trail](#trail) can count

A brief's height on the gradient is given by how far it reaches, how much stands on it, and how much it stands on. The trail, running the links backward, can count that reach. Placement is therefore computed, not declared.

### Keeps order without discipline

Because the gradient is computed from wins and links, the medium stays ordered without anyone enforcing a filing scheme. Writers write briefs and links; the order emerges. No discipline of categorisation is required of them.

## Holon

A holon is a thing that is a whole in itself and a part of a larger whole. It is the coined word for what a brief is.

### Names the nature of the [brief](#brief)

A brief is complete on its own, handing over one win, and is also a part of the gradient above it. That double nature, whole and part, is what "holon" names. Calling briefs holons says nothing new about them; it says what they already are.

### Has a surface and a depth, which are one holon seen twice

Seen from above, a holon is a surface: one unit handing over one win. Seen from inside, it is a depth: the nested holons that make it up. These are not two things but one holon regarded from two sides.

### Has an address, which a [link](#link) points at

Every holon sits somewhere in the space, and that somewhere is its address. A link is a pointer to an address. Without addresses there would be nothing for links to point at and the medium could not join.

### Is the coined word for it

"Holon" is borrowed and adopted here as the technical term for the whole-and-part nature of briefs. It is coined for this document's use, so its meaning here is fixed by the headings above rather than by its source.

## Word

A word is the smallest holon: the smallest unit that can carry meaning, be linked, and be changed in place.

### Is the smallest [holon](#holon)

Below the brief, the holarchy bottoms out at the word. A word is whole, carrying its own meaning, and a part of the brief it sits in. There is no smaller unit the medium treats as a holon.

### Is a [link](#link) when it is precise

A word used precisely, in the sense this document fixes for it, functions as a link: it names ground without restating it. This is why the h2 words of this document are linked wherever they appear in titles. Imprecise words are only words; precise ones are doors.

### Deepens the [medium](#medium)

Each word that is given a precise sense adds a level to the medium: what was a phrase becomes an address, and what stood on the phrase now stands on ground. The medium grows deeper by words becoming precise.

## Medium

The medium is the whole body of briefs and links a community of knowers reads from and writes to. It is where comprehension compounds and what makes forgetting safe.

### Is made of [briefs](#brief) and [links](#link), nothing typed

The medium has exactly two constituents: briefs and the links between them. It carries no types, schemas or fields; those belong to the field that stands over it. Keeping the medium untyped keeps it writable by any knower in plain language.

### Is where [comprehension](#comprehension) compounds

Comprehension lives in knowers, but it compounds through the medium: what one knower writes joins what another reads, and the join happens across the medium. The medium is the site of compounding even though it holds no comprehension itself.

### Holds everything that gives work its direction

Whatever tells a knower what to do next, the aims, the ground, the open questions, lives in the medium and nowhere else. Work has no direction stored outside it. This is why the medium can replace the harness: it holds what the harness used to hold.

### Makes forgetting safe

Because the medium holds direction and ground, a knower may forget without loss: what is forgotten can be read again. This is what allows bounded knowers, and models with no memory between sessions, to work in it. Forgetting becomes a cost of reading rather than a loss of comprehension.

## Space

Space is the addressing system of the medium: the set of places holons can be at. It is not the field, which has a different nature.

### Gives every [holon](#holon) its address

Space is what makes "where" meaningful for holons. Each holon has one address in space, and links, boundaries and trails all operate on those addresses. Space exists so that things can be pointed at.

### Is not the [field](#field), which has a different nature

Space is only positions; it says nothing about what is at them. The field stands over space and adds content, types and a point of reading. Confusing the two would put content into addressing or addressing into content.

## Field

The field is the medium given addresses and types, as it appears to something that reads it from a point. It is what a prism is cut from and what the kernel hosts.

### Stands over a [space](#space)

The field is defined on the space: at each address in space, the field has content. Space is the domain; the field is what is spread across it.

### Is the [medium](#medium) given addresses and types

The medium itself is untyped briefs and links. The field is that medium after every holon has an address and a type, so that programs and engines can operate on it. It is the medium made legible to citizens that cannot read a sentence.

### Is read from a point, which is what a [prism](#prism) is cut at

The field is never read whole; it is read from somewhere. A prism is the field as seen from one point, and that point is where the prism is cut. Reading from a point is what makes consolidated inference deterministic.

## Boundary

A boundary is a region of space with an edge: what a knower or a run may reach. It is set before a run and kept by the kernel.

### Bounds a region of [space](#space), not a kind of thing

A boundary is spatial, not categorical. It says which addresses are inside, not which kinds of holon are permitted. Anything at an address inside the boundary is inside, whatever it is.

### Can hold one [knower](#knower)'s own region

A boundary can be drawn around what one knower is working in, so that its writes stay its own until they cross out. This gives a knower a private region without needing a private medium.

### Is set before a run and kept by the [kernel](#kernel)

A boundary is fixed before a run begins and does not move during it. The kernel enforces it, since every act goes through the kernel. This is what replaces the harness's job of bounding where the medium expands.

## Commit

A commit is the act that ratifies a write into the medium. It is itself a holon, changes one brief whole or one word in place, and publishes when it crosses a boundary.

### Is a [holon](#holon)

A commit is a whole in itself, one ratified change, and a part of the history of the medium. Being a holon, it has an address and can be linked to and trailed.

### Is the ratification, under this document's rule

A write is proposed until it is committed; the commit is the moment it counts. The rule of this document is that nothing is ground until it has been ratified in this way. Consolidation and publication both follow from commits.

### Changes one [brief](#brief) whole, or one [word](#word) in place

A commit has two grains and no others: it replaces a whole brief, or it changes one word where it stands. This follows from briefs and words being the holons that carry wins and precise meaning respectively; a change of any other size would not be a change of one holon.

### Publishes when it crosses a [boundary](#boundary)

Within a boundary, a commit is private to that region. When a commit crosses a boundary, it becomes visible to whoever reads beyond it, and that crossing is publication. There is no separate publish act.

## Comment

A comment is a note that stands on a brief without changing it. It is held as a sketch, not yet built.

### Stands on a [brief](#brief) and leaves it whole

A comment attaches to a brief the way a brief attaches to ground: it stands on it. The brief is not altered; the comment is beside it, and the brief's win is unchanged.

### Is free to whoever may read within the [boundary](#boundary)

Anyone who can read a brief within a boundary may comment on it. Commenting requires no right beyond reading, since it leaves the brief whole and ratifies nothing.

### Is a [trail](#trail) too

A comment records that someone reached a brief and what they thought there; in that sense it is a trail, evidence of reach and history. Unlike other trails, it is written rather than derived.

### Is held as a sketch, not yet built

## Citizen

A citizen is anything that acts in the medium: a knower, or a program or engine that cannot comprehend. Citizenship is a condition of acting, not a right.

### Composes by being in the [medium](#medium)

Citizens do not need a separate integration layer to work together; being in the medium, addressable and linkable, is what lets them compose. Composition is a property of the medium, not of the citizens.

### Is [amplified](#amplify)

Every citizen benefits from the amplifier: knowers through comprehension, programs and engines through matching. Whatever is in the medium gets more out than it puts in, and that is what makes citizenship worth holding.

### Is a [knower](#knower), or a program or an engine that cannot comprehend

There are three kinds of citizen. Knowers hold comprehension; programs and engines do not, and act only on the typed field. All three are citizens, but only the first is a knower.

### Holds citizenship as a condition, not a right

A citizen is one while it meets the conditions: it is in the medium, it goes through the kernel, and it keeps its contract. Nothing is a citizen by entitlement, and citizenship can lapse.

## Prism

A prism is what a knower holds at one moment: the field as seen from one point, losing nothing of the field. It is a working term.

### Is what a [knower](#knower) holds at one moment

A bounded knower cannot hold the whole medium. What it holds at any one moment is a prism: the field seen from where it stands. The prism is the knower's working set.

### Is cut from the [field](#field) and loses none of it

A prism is not a subset that discards the rest; it is the whole field refracted through one point. Everything in the field is still reachable from within the prism, by link. The cut chooses a viewpoint, not a selection.

### Is taken under a [contract](#contract) by a model, by choice by a person

A model receives its prism as specified by a contract: the point and the terms are set for it. A person chooses their own point. The prism is the same thing in both cases; only the manner of taking it differs.

### Is named for now, not settled

"Prism" is a working term and may be renamed. The thing it names, the field cut at a point, is settled.

## Interface

The interface is what a knower reads and writes the medium through. It rests on the medium's law alone and its first job is to amplify.

### Rests on the [medium](#medium)'s law alone, on no tenet beneath

The interface derives from the two laws of the medium and from nothing else: no lower-level doctrine about kernel, substrate or processor reaches it. Whatever the interface does must be justified as serving the gradient and keeping ground true.

### [Amplifies](#amplify) first

Before anything else, the interface's job is to raise the win: to show the right brief, name the right ground, show the trail. Any other function it has is secondary to amplification.

## Kernel

The kernel is the one thing every act on the medium goes through. It hosts the field while running, rests in the substrate, and governs itself because its law is data.

### Governs itself, because its law is data it rests on

The kernel's rules are not hard-coded above it; they are contracts stored in the substrate the kernel rests on. So the kernel reads its own law from data, and changing the law is a write, not a rebuild.

### Hosts the [field](#field) while running, and rests in the [substrate](#substrate)

While the kernel runs, the field lives in it; when it stops, the field is held at rest in the substrate. The kernel is therefore the field's runtime and the substrate its store.

### Is the one thing every act goes through

Reads, writes, commits, and every act by any citizen pass through the kernel. This is what lets it keep boundaries, record trails, and enforce contracts: nothing bypasses it.

### Is federated by [contract](#contract)

There is no single kernel. Kernels interoperate by contract, and a contract is a brief of a kind, so federation is itself written in the medium. Two kernels that keep the same contract can exchange field.

### Sits below the [interface](#interface)

The interface faces the knower; the kernel faces the field. The interface rests on the medium's law alone and does not reach down into the kernel; the kernel does not decide what a knower sees.

## Substrate

The substrate is the circuitry that keeps: it holds the field at rest, keeps every contract, and holds the chunk that may become a holon. It sits beside the processor.

### Holds the [field](#field) at rest

When nothing is running, the field is in the substrate. This is storage in the plain sense, but it is storage of the typed, addressed field, not of loose files.

### Keeps every [contract](#contract), which is how anything interoperates

Contracts live in the substrate, so anything that can reach the substrate can read the terms on which it may interoperate. Interoperability is not negotiated at runtime; it is kept.

### Holds the chunk, which may become the [holon](#holon)

At the substrate's level the unit is a chunk: a stored piece with no meaning yet. A chunk becomes a holon when it is given an address and a win. The substrate holds chunks; the medium sees holons.

### Is circuitry that keeps, beside the [processor](#processor) that computes

The substrate and the processor divide the bottom of the stack: one keeps, one computes. Neither comprehends. Together they fix the bottom under the kernel.

## Processor

The processor is the circuitry that computes. It sits under the kernel beside the substrate and holds no comprehension.

### Fixes the bottom under the [kernel](#kernel), with the [substrate](#substrate) beside it

Below the kernel there is only the processor and the substrate. The processor is the floor of the stack: nothing in this ontology lies beneath it.

### Holds no [comprehension](#comprehension)

The processor computes, including running models, but comprehension lives in a knower and not in what runs it. A model comprehends while reading; the processor that runs it does not.

## Contract

A contract is a brief of a kind: the one typed brief, written for citizens that cannot read a sentence. It lives in the medium it governs.

### Is a [brief](#brief) of a kind

A contract hands over one win like any brief: the terms on which something interoperates. It is a brief specialised for programs and engines, but it is still a brief and lives among them.

### Lives in the [medium](#medium) it governs

A contract is not kept outside the medium as configuration; it is written into the medium it rules. This is what lets the kernel govern itself: its law is in the field it hosts.

### Is typed for the one [citizen](#citizen) that cannot read a sentence

Programs and engines cannot comprehend prose, so the contract is typed: it carries a structure they can match on. It is the only place in the medium where typing is required, and it is required because of who must read it.

## Program

A program is a citizen that cannot comprehend, and what a model becomes once it is a citizen. It carries its contract in its body and composes by matching.

### Is a [citizen](#citizen), and what a model is once it is one

A model outside the medium is a tool run by a harness. Once it is in the medium, going through the kernel and keeping a contract, it is a program: a citizen with an address and a trail.

### Carries its [contract](#contract) in its body

A program's contract is not kept elsewhere and looked up; it is part of the program itself. Reading the program is reading its terms.

### Composes by matching, which is the [amplifier](#amplify) in the technical arc

Programs compose when their contracts match: an output of one fits an input of another. That matching is what amplification looks like on the technical side, where there is no comprehension to compound but there is fit to exploit.

### Leaves a [trail](#trail) in the [kernel](#kernel)

Because every act goes through the kernel, a program's acts are recorded there, and that record is its trail. The kernel, not the program, keeps it.

## Engine

An engine is a citizen that runs: a runtime, a model host, a service. There are many, and each is made interoperable by its contract in the substrate.

### Is a [citizen](#citizen), and there are many

Engines are citizens in the same sense as programs, and there is no single engine. Any number may be present, each with its own contract.

### Is made interoperable by its [contract](#contract) in the [substrate](#substrate)

An engine's contract is kept in the substrate rather than in its body, because engines are not themselves in the medium the way programs are. The substrate holds the terms on which each engine can be reached.

### Reaches the field only through the [kernel](#kernel)

No engine touches the field directly. Whatever it reads or writes goes through the kernel, which keeps boundaries and records the trail.

### Runs a [program](#program) when that is its kind

Some engines are hosts: their job is to run programs, including models. That is one kind of engine among several, and only that kind runs programs.

A specification is a brief, whole and part, but it also carries what must be read before it: the matter that precedes. It is a holon with a front.

## Harness

The harness is the apparatus that today runs a model from outside the medium: the session, the prompt, the transcript. It is superseded by the amplifier and dissolved by the citizen.

### Bounds where the [medium](#medium) expands

Today the harness sets the limits of what a model may read and write, and so bounds where the medium can grow. That role passes to the boundary, kept by the kernel.

### Leaves no [trail](#trail)

What happens in a harness is not recorded in the medium. Sessions end and their links, reach and history are gone. This is the loss the trail is meant to prevent.

### Withheld the [ground](#ground) a model becomes

A model in a harness produces comprehension that is never written back as ground; the harness keeps it in the transcript and discards it. What the model could have become for later readers, ground, is withheld.
[blind: title underdetermines]

### Is dissolved by the [citizen](#citizen)

Once a model is a citizen, a program in the medium with a contract and an address, there is nothing left for the harness to do. The citizen dissolves it.

### Is superseded by the [amplifier](#amplify)

The harness's function, making a model useful, is done better by the amplifier: a model reading briefs on ground gets more out per read than a model prompted from outside. The amplifier supersedes the harness.

### Is left out with the transcript's other artefacts

The harness and what it produces, transcripts, prompts, session state, are not part of the medium. They are left out along with everything else that is not a brief or a link.

### Still appears in the code, meaning today's sessions

The harness is not yet gone. Today's sessions run in one, and the code refers to it. The term stays in the ontology as a name for the present arrangement, not as part of the design.
