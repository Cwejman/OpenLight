## What the authors correct

**Word.** The entry says "Below it nothing carries meaning." Bloomfield 1926: "A minimum free form is a word. A word is thus a form which may be uttered alone (with meaning) but cannot be analyzed into parts that may (all of them) be uttered alone", and below the word the morpheme, "a recurrent meaningful form which cannot be analyzed into smaller recurrent meaningful forms", still carries meaning. The correction sharpens the entry's own point: the word is the smallest holon not because nothing below it means, but because nothing below it *stands alone*. "Below it nothing stands alone" is what the essence needs, and it keeps "Whole in itself" true.

**Program.** The entry opens "The unit of execution, headless, carrying its contract in its body." Brinch Hansen 1970: "A sharp distinction is made between the concepts program and internal process. A program is a collection of instructions describing a computational process, whereas an internal process is the execution of these instructions in a given storage area." The program is the description; the run is the process. The ontology already holds this split elsewhere, "Boundaries before a run, inspection during, history after", so the essence should say the unit *of what runs*, or name the process, and not call the description the execution.

**Commit.** The entry says "Git's word, and kept as git's." Gray 1981: "either all actions are done and the transaction is said to commit, or none of the effects of the transaction survive", and the committed state is durable, "once a transaction is committed, it cannot be abrogated." Git took the word from databases; what the entry needs from it, "One write, kept", is Gray's atomicity and durability, and what git adds is the snapshot, the author, the message and the parent. The entry can keep git's *form* while crediting the notion.

**Trail.** The entry says "Never written, always derived." Bush 1945: "Thus he builds a trail of his interest through the maze of materials available to him", "And his trails do not fade", and the trail can be "photographed" and given to a friend. Bush's trail is a stored artefact; the ontology's is a view computed from links and commits. The citation would mark a deliberate departure, and the entry's "Writing it down would be a copy, and copies rot" is the argument against Bush, worth saying as such.

**Ground.** The entry says the ground is "the wins its reader is assumed to hold already." Clark & Brennan 1991: common ground is "mutual knowledge, mutual beliefs, and mutual assumptions", and grounding reaches a criterion "sufficient for current purposes". Two sharpenings: the ground is *mutual*, so the writer's assumption is only half of it, and the trail is where the other half is read; and "settled meaning settled for now" is the grounding criterion, which gives the second law a name.

**Kernel.** The entry says "Both program and memory." Brinch Hansen 1970: "We do not regard the system nucleus as an independent process, but rather as a software extension of the hardware structure." The nucleus is neither a program among programs nor a store; it is the layer that makes the hardware usable by processes. "Both program and memory" reads as a category the kernel refuses; "the software extension of the circuitry, holding the field open" is closer to Hansen and to the entry's own "Sits below the interface… Below it, only circuitry".

**Field and Space.** The entry says "medium, space and field are three words because they are three things." Maxwell 1865: the theory "has to do with the space in the neighbourhood of the electric and magnetic bodies", and the field *is* that space with a state at every point. Maxwell would not separate the field from the space as two things; the field is the space seen with what stands at each address. The ontology's Holon entry already has the form for this, "one holon seen twice"; the author's ruling that they differ in nature holds if the difference is the one between coordinates and the function over them, which is what the Field entry says a line later.

**Amplify.** The entry says "We do not bound, we expand." Engelbart 1962 rejected "intelligence amplifier" at first "on the grounds that in our view one's only hope was to make a better match between existing human intelligence and the problems to be tackled, rather than in making man more intelligent", then accepted it because "the entity to be produced will exhibit more of what can be called intelligence than an unaided human could." The correction: what is amplified is the *human plus system*, not the reader, which is the entry's "contracts the reading, never the reader" stated from the system's side, and it warns against reading "expand" as expanding the knower.

**Specification.** The entry says "Is the ground the engineering arc writes from." Parnas 1972 writes specifications so that "other pieces of software can be written to interact with the piece specified without additional information." Parnas's spec faces the *user* of the module; the ontology's faces the *builder*. Both are briefs, but they are different briefs, and the user-facing one is what the ontology calls a contract. The entry could say which it means, or say that a specification is a contract read from the inside.

**Knower.** The entry says comprehension "exists nowhere but in a knower." Polanyi 1958: "Such is the personal participation of the knower in all acts of understanding. But this does not make our understanding subjective. Comprehension is neither an arbitrary act nor a passive experience, but a responsible act claiming universal validity." The sharpening: located in a knower is not the same as private to one, and the ontology's compounding across knowers needs Polanyi's second sentence as much as his first.

**Citizen.** The entry says "Citizenship is the terms; knowing is not required for it." Aristotle 1275a: the citizen "is defined by no other thing so much as by sharing in decision and office." A program that only matches shares in no decision. The ontology's extension is a deliberate departure and the citation would say so; the entry might keep Aristotle by saying that a program's writes are decisions the medium admits.

**Consolidation.** The entry gives consolidation as "Bringing what is scattered into one whole that stands." Müller & Pilzecker 1900 say memory "takes time to be fixed (or consolidated)" and is vulnerable until then. Their sense is temporal; the ontology's is spatial. The two meet in the Commit entry, where a write "left uncommitted… can be lost", which is their vulnerability window; the Consolidation entry could name it, since it is where the author's word and the literature's agree.

## New address space

Once the brackets stand, each is an address, and the trail runs backward from it.

1. **`[Bloomfield 1926]`** — every relation that rests on the word as smallest holon. Returns: Word (essence and "Is the smallest holon"), Commit ("one word in place"), Link ("Written as a name and a year in brackets it is a citation"). What it shows: the three places where a one-word write is treated as a whole, and whether they agree on why.

2. **`[Engelbart 1962] AND [Ashby 1956]`** — briefs that stand on both. Returns: Amplify, Win, Harness ("Is superseded by the amplifier"), Interface ("Amplifies first"). What it shows: the lineage the arc inherits, and that the ontology's "amplify" splits Engelbart's "augment" in two, the reader unbounded and the reading contracted.

3. **`[Clark & Brennan 1991] NOT [Harnad 1990]`** — which sense of ground a brief is using. Returns: Brief ("Can be found from the ground it names"), Link ("Names ground so it is never restated"), Gradient ("Nothing arrives before its ground") on the common-ground side; Ground's "what you become" and Harness's "Withheld the ground a model becomes" on the symbol-grounding side. What it shows: the ontology carries two grounds under one word, one assumed and one absorbed, and the trail says which relation means which.

4. **`[Gray 1981]`** walked by place — everything standing at the address where a write becomes durable. Returns: Commit, Consolidation ("Makes inference deterministic, because the field has a point"), Trail ("Carries history as well as reach"), the document's own rule ("the commit is the ratification"). What it shows: the ontology's ratification rule is an atomicity-and-durability claim, and the four places that depend on it can be checked as one.

5. **`[Parnas 1972] near [Meyer 1992]`** — the seam between specification and contract. Returns: Specification ("Is a brief of a kind" is Contract's line, "Is the ground the engineering arc writes from" is Specification's), Contract ("Is typed for the one citizen that cannot read a sentence"), Program ("Carries its contract in its body"), Engine ("Is made interoperable by its contract in the substrate"). What it shows: which text faces the user and which the builder, so the spec tree's rewrite under the code knows which of the two it is writing.
