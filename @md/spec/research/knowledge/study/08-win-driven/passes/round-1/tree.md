# Audit: ontology.md against the spec tree

Files read: `spec/pilot.md`, `spec/substrate.md`, `spec/engine.md`, `spec/view.md` (component, mount, place, selection only), and the document under audit, `spec/research/knowledge/study/08-win-driven/ontology.md`. Quotes are verbatim; link markup inside quotes is the source's. Nothing under `@x/night` was modified.

Frame for reading the tables: the ontology's own header says the arc words "are the author's direction and not yet the spec tree's, which still holds the substrate as a tenet beside the engine and the engine as the platform's one runtime; the ontology puts the substrate below the kernel and makes the engine a citizen, plural." That is the announced divergence. The rows below are where the two texts actually collide sentence-by-sentence, including collisions the header does not name.

## 3. Where the ontology is right and the tree is behind

Stated as what the tree should adopt.

1. **Split the judge from the runner.** engine.md's *What the Engine Owns* lists nine duties; five are acts on the field (boundary construction, write governance, expression evaluation, protocol mediation, the attach registry) and four are running (process creation, program lifecycle, containment, source serving). The tree already draws the line it does not name: "Substrate ops — `read`, `commit`, `subscribe` — are protocol, not programs: every connected program has them, and they are walled by `read` and `write`, never by `run`." Adopt the ontology's cut: the kernel is "the one thing every act goes through"; the thing that owns `ProcessSlot`, `SpawnHandle`, timeouts and cleanup is one contracted engine among possible others.

2. **An engine is a contracted citizen, not a registry key.** The tree: "Containment lives in the provider; the engine knows runtime kinds only as registry keys." and `runtime: ref(engine/runtime) — vm | native as value chunks`. The ontology: "What an engine takes and gives is a chunk in the field, so the field can call it and stand on what it returns, whatever happens inside." Adopt: an `engine/runtime` instance carries a contract (what it takes, what it gives, its capabilities floor), not a bare name; the DSP-engine case the ontology names cannot be a Rust trait object loaded at boot.

3. **Substrate at rest, field open.** The tree uses "substrate" for both the law and the running whole ("substrate-resident", "every substrate operation", "The substrate is branch-aware"). The ontology's cut — "what the kernel holds is the field open, not the field kept" — is cleaner and the tree already half-uses it: "programs see one logical field, evaluated as one." Adopt: substrate = law + store on disk; field = what a kernel opens.

4. **Name the space.** substrate.md's *Every Chunk Is a Dimension* is the ontology's Space — "every holon is placed in some, and is itself a dimension others are placed in" — but the tree's only use of the word is "The space is unstructured". Adopt "space" as the name for addresses-across-dimensions and "address" as the genus over path and place.

5. **Interface built of citizens.** pilot.md: "**Drawing and running are decoupled, and both are field citizens.**" The ontology defines citizen ("reads the medium and writes back into it on the same terms as everything else") and gives its output a role ("A citizen's output is ground the next citizen stands on"). The tree has the word informally; adopt the definition, and the terms — "The medium sets the terms; no tool grants them" — as the reason components and programs share the match.

6. **The trail is derived, never written.** The tree has the mechanism under three names — `linked` ("who points here, labeled `field` (with its key) or `mention`"), `backrefs(key?)`, and `read([db/commits, chunkId])` — and the law "The table is never part of commits and is rebuildable from current bodies." The ontology names the whole: "Never written, always derived" and "Carries history as well as reach". Adopt "trail" as the reader-facing word for the union of `linked` and commit history at one chunk.

7. **Contract as a brief.** pilot.md's "A component's contract is its chunks; a program's contract is its body" is the ontology's "Carries its [contract](#contract) in its body". engine.md's heading "a program's interface is its body" uses the word the ontology reserves for the surface; adopt "contract" there.

8. **Commit as ratification.** The ontology's rule — "Whoever makes a change commits it, standing on what it changes, and the other side ratifies by committing on it in turn" — is a governance reading of what the tree already records ("Every commit carries a `process_id`"; `message?`). Nothing to build; the tree could state it under *Traceability* as what the record is for.

## 4. Where the tree is right and the ontology is loose

Loose sentence first, the tree's precise one after.

1. **The run wall.**
   Ontology: "A rule on acts over a region of the space: who may read there, who may write."
   Tree: "**Three kinds of act, three walls**: reads are governed by `read`, writes by `write`, program starts by `run` — a selection over program chunks, so **the toolset is the run boundary**" (engine.md). The ontology's own Kernel entry says "it judges every act", and starting is an act.

2. **Publishing is placement, not a commit crossing a boundary.**
   Ontology: "Publishing is not a second act. It is a commit that a wider boundary admits readers to."
   Tree: "**To place into a dimension is to publish into it.** A note related onto the inbox is visible to inbox-holders — not a leak, the meaning of the act." (substrate.md) and "**Boundaries decide what you may see.** Permission, viewer-side" (substrate.md). A commit lands in a store on a branch; whether a reader sees it is that reader's boundary. Publishing is the writer's placement act, and it *is* a second act relative to writing the body ("Placing a result onto other places is a second, visible act", engine.md).

3. **A private region is a branch or a frame, not a boundary the knower holds.**
   Ontology: "A knower may keep a region nobody else reads and work there before anyone sees. What is written there is in the field and not yet in anyone else's prism."
   Tree: boundaries are "viewer-side, uniform over everything"; the writer's private places are "`branch` with `write: true` is work on a branch of a shared store — the middle ground" (engine.md) and the frame: "**The frame is `[self]`.** A process's frame is its own dimension." What keeps others out is *their* boundaries not naming the region, or the branch not being theirs — not a boundary the knower "holds".

4. **A boundary is a standing licence, and its walls are selections.**
   Ontology: "Is set before a run and kept by the [kernel](#kernel)".
   Tree: "**A boundary is a standing licence, not a snapshot.** A run's boundary *expression* freezes at start; membership through it stays live" (substrate.md); "**Frozen expression, live membership.**" (engine.md). "Set before a run" is right; that membership keeps moving after is the part the ontology does not say and would mislead a reader about.

5. **Kernels do not join; stores attach.**
   Ontology: "Kernels join by contract, one attaching another's store on declared terms" and, under Program, "Attach a peer's kernel and its programs compound with yours at once."
   Tree: "**Attach brings a store into the running field**" (engine.md); "Peers are separate stores, not partitions of one" (substrate.md); "two engine processes on one db file are not connected. Cross-engine reactivity is horizon." (engine.md). A kernel opens stores; it never attaches another kernel, and two kernels on one store are not connected. The Program sentence is wrong as written.

6. **The kernel's contract to its clients is the wire.**
   Ontology: "Its own contract is held in the substrate it rests in, so a kernel can be replaced and the field stays."
   Tree: "the wire is its only contract: every client — the chassis, a VM program, a browser page — speaks one JSON-lines protocol" (engine.md). The kernel's *archetypes* (`engine/program`, `engine/process`, `engine/status`) are field data, and `attach`/`detach` are "engine programs — `runtime: native`, owned by `engine`" — but the protocol (`read`, `commit`, `run`, `await`, `cancel`, `subscribe`) is not in the substrate. Both sentences are true of different things; the ontology's collapses them.

7. **Commits are rows, deliberately, and a commit is a declaration.**
   Ontology: "Is a [holon](#holon) … Its surface is what changed and why, its depth is the changes themselves, its address is its place in time" and "One write, kept".
   Tree: "A commit is not a chunk with placements: it carries message and timestamp, and its deltas live in its touched-chunks and touched-placements columns … **The edits are not in a body.**" (substrate.md) and "A declaration — one or many chunks, with their placements — succeeds entirely or fails entirely; a commit appears only when every write passes." (substrate.md). A commit's "depth" is not stored in it; a diff "is two temporal reads compared". And a commit is one *declaration*, which may be many chunks — "one write" undersells atomicity, which is the property the tree cares about.

8. **The store keeps commits; the kernel projects them.**
   Ontology: "The [commits](#commit) hold it; the [kernel](#kernel) keeps them, and in the markdown practice git does."
   Tree: "Commits stay in their own table, **per store**; the read layer projects them under the virtual place `db/commits`" (engine.md). Under the ontology's own cut ("stopping a kernel loses nothing") the substrate keeps commits; the kernel only serves them.

9. **A link needs a resolvable target.**
   Ontology: "Needs no target yet … The text reads whole, and the door stands for the day someone opens it."
   Tree: "**Both ends are gated.** Creating a ref requires read over the target" and "every link for read over its target" (substrate.md); staleness is only ever after the fact: "A target that later loses its archetype or is removed leaves refs to it stale — a *legal, permanent state*". The ontology's dangling link is legal in markdown; in the field it is either refused at write or an unresolved root, and the ontology should say it is speaking of the markdown practice.

10. **A comment is not free; it is a `relates` or a `mention`, and they differ.**
    Ontology: "Is free to whoever may read within the [boundary](#boundary) … It lives where everyone who reads there can see it."
    Tree: "**Create** requires **write over the dimension and read over the placed chunk.**" (substrate.md, placements) versus "**Links** (`field`, `mention`) land in the author's own body. The act is speaking from yourself: self-governed. The target is passive — read over it suffices." A comment that "lives where everyone who reads there can see it" is a `relates` onto the brief and needs write over the brief; a comment free to any reader is a `mention` from the reader's own room, "visible only to those who reach you", and reaches the brief's readers only through the trail (`linked`). The ontology's Comment picks the first's visibility with the second's governance. The tree also already has this: "A caller that knows it will exceed may relate explanation prose onto the draft before awaiting — ordinary aboutness" (engine.md) — so "Is held as a sketch, not yet built" understates the tree.

11. **Contracts are one reified value, read three ways — not prose for some and types for others.**
    Ontology: "For the classical program, the contract is types. For people and models, it is prose."
    Tree: "Type terms are themselves data: the reified **`type` value kind** (`{$type: {of, opt?, card?}}`) lets one contract be read identically by validation, by a form, and by a tool-schema adapter." (substrate.md) and "the agent compiles provider tool schemas from the same reified entries" (engine.md). The model reads the typed contract, not a prose one; the tree's precision is that there is one contract with three readers.

12. **Calls are parsed and then matched.**
    Ontology: "A call is matched, never parsed."
    Tree: "Parsing is context-free recursive descent — trivial by construction." (engine.md) and "Starting validates the chunks that already exist, runs the match, and freezes the record". The *text* is parsed; the *offer* is matched. The ontology's sentence is about the match replacing keyword binding, and should say so.

13. **What sits beside the kernel is not only circuitry.**
    Ontology: "Above it, where comprehension happens. Below it, only circuitry: what keeps and what computes."
    Tree: "chassis-desktop/    rust binary: platform machinery; hosts web-dom; a client of engine; declares the entry contract" (pilot.md). The chassis is a client above the kernel and a host below the interface; it is neither circuitry nor comprehension.

14. **The interface does rest on the kernel.**
    Ontology: "Rests on the [medium](#medium)'s law alone, on no tenet beneath. Not on the engine, the substrate or any specific of OpenLight."
    Tree: "Every act — a program's, a mount's, the interface's — is judged by the engine under one call context; reads reach what the boundary admits, writes land where it allows, uniformly." (pilot.md) and "view/sdk = the web-dom glue + adapters  < engine/sdk" (pilot.md). True of the markdown interface of the knowledge arc; false of view.md's interface, whose reach is computed by the kernel. The ontology should scope the sentence to the interface it means.

15. **Time is in the substrate apart from acts.**
    Ontology: "If the code ever needs time apart from any act, this was wrong."
    Tree: `time` is one of the eleven key types ("`joined: time`"), and commits carry "timestamp  when committed" (substrate.md). A body value of type `time` (a birthday, a deadline) is time apart from any act. Either the ontology's claim is about the code only, or it is already contradicted by the vocabulary the field ships with.
