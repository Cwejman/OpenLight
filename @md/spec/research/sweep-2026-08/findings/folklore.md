# Folklore: do not cite

Numbers that circulate as established fact and are not. Each entry gives the claim, what the trace found, and — where one exists — the safe formulation. Where a correction reshapes a live question, the topic fold owns the full account and is linked; this list is the index a writer checks before citing. Two sweep-level rules first: several agents caught summarisers **fabricating quotes and sample sizes**, so any figure is worth its verification note; and most of these traces ran without web search, so a "no primary source found" below means *not found in the indexed literature* — strong for academic claims, weaker for trade press.

## Cognitive limits

- **"7±2."** Miller himself called the number "only a pernicious, Pythagorean coincidence" and distinguished two different spans; recoding into chunks raises capacity. Never a design ceiling. Applied to menus it also confuses recognition with recall.

- **Cowan's "4 chunks" as a design limit.** The 4 is visible only under experimental conditions that *block* chunking; a navigable, re-readable structure meets none of them. Citing it as a ceiling on categories inverts the paper.

- **"Two levels, three at most."** Nothing supports it. The menu studies' winning breadths were 8, 16, and 32 wide at fixed item counts; every result is an optimum for its item count, not a law ([`reading.md`](reading.md)).

- **Chase & Simon as evidence of a chunk limit.** N=3, and Miller's span was their *premise*, not their result.

- **Dunbar's 150.** The deconstruction's 95% CIs run 2–520; the paper's conclusion is that no number can be derived, not that the number is different.

- **Luhmann's Zettelkasten as proof of shallow structure.** The archive documents up to four sub-levels, 108 divisions, one division with 120 subdivisions, and says the ordering is not systematically hierarchical at all. ~90,000 slips over ~45 years (not 40).

- **"3 clicks and users quit."** Tested and falsified (44 users, 8,000+ clicks): no more quitting after three clicks than after twelve.

## Cost curves and process

- **1:10:100 / "IBM Systems Sciences Institute."** The definitive trace: the SSI was an IBM *training unit* in Los Angeles, not a research body; no study, no data, no N. Cite nothing to it, ever.

- **Boehm's 100×.** Boehm & Basili's own 2001 words: "more like 5:1 than 100:1" for small non-critical systems, with architecture compressing it further. Direction established, every specific multiplier soft ([`travel.md`](travel.md)).

- **The delayed-issue effect itself** was tested on 171 projects and not found — "a long-held truth in software engineering should not be considered a global truism."

- **"70–85% of rework from requirements" and kin.** The requirements-quality literature was formally audited: ~48% of claimed impacts merely hypothesized, justifications "anecdotal or folkloric." No percentage in this family has a traceable N.

- **Standish CHAOS rates.** Methodologically invalid (one-sided definitions, solicited failure stories); dismantled in peer review. Not usable.

- **"Design docs reduce defects," "6-pagers produce better decisions."** The former has zero outcome evidence and one controlled study finding format irrelevant; the latter traces entirely to a shareholder letter ([`rationale.md`](rationale.md)).

- **"Team Topologies delivers 25%/20% gains."** No study exists; marketing pages only.

## Documentation and reading

- **DORA's documentation multipliers (2.4×, 656%, 1525%…).** The construct's own arc: 2021 positive → 2022 *negative* → 2023 "no effect" → 2025 removed, with DORA explicitly retreating from causal language. The big lift table appears only in a blog, not the report. Never cite an Nx from this line.

- **GitLab's "605,000-word handbook."** Absent from every GitLab primary source reachable, live and archived. "Over 2,000 pages" is the sourced figure.

- **Nielsen's card-sorting correlations (r=0.90 at 15 users…).** Read off another paper's graph; the numbers appear in no published text. The whole card-sort/tree-test evidence chain is one non-peer-reviewed dataset, blog posts, and a validity test that returned a null ([`reading.md`](reading.md)).

- **"80% tree-test success is good."** No published threshold exists anywhere; the only heuristic on record is a 2009 joke.

- **"Developers spend 10× more time reading than writing."** Originates with Robert Martin recalling Emacs session playbacks — no instrumentation, and the observation was about *scrolling*. The instrumented reality: ~58% of professional working time is comprehension (editing ~5%), much of it reading docs and the web, not code ([`reading.md`](reading.md)).

- **"50% of maintenance is comprehension."** The load-bearing citation (Fjeldstad & Hamlen) is cited-but-unread — its two main citers give incompatible bibliographic records for it. The related "35%" figure is a mis-citation: the primary paper measured *navigation mechanics*.

- **DITA reuse saves 30–40% / structured content beats unstructured for AI by 29–46pp.** No primary study exists for either; the second traces to a page that does not contain the figure ([`practice.md`](practice.md)).

- **Graphic organizers ES 1.24 vs 0.80.** In no primary source; assume blog-laundered.

- **Advance organizers as a strong effect.** d ≈ 0.2, and the high-effect studies contradicted Ausubel's own predicted mechanism.

## AI-era

- **Anthropic's "150,000 → 2,000 tokens (98.7%)."** An illustrative hypothetical in a blog post, not a measurement. The mechanism (navigable beats present) is independently supported at one to two orders smaller magnitude.

- **LazyGraphRAG's "0.1% of cost."** A blog number about code that never shipped, and it referred to *indexing* only.

- **"KGs make LLMs 3× more accurate."** One vendor's 43 questions, hand-authored ontology, deliberately weak zero-shot baseline. Evidence for curated semantic layers only; no replication.

- **"Tool descriptions make agents pick you 260% more often."** Real experiment (20% → 72% *selection among competitors*), misleading relative-lift framing, and it does not measure task success.

- **"Agent Skills won."** Adoption is real (~tens of thousands of specs); the one controlled benchmark found skills *lowering* pass rates at large token cost, and skills go stale silently ([`structure.md`](structure.md)).

- **The "grep tax" (9,649 experiments).** Unverified at source by the agent that chased it; the adjacent verifiable benchmark (TOON's own) shows a null accuracy difference. Both structured-format claims are data-shape-dependent; neither generalizes.

- **"Oxide's RFDs demonstrably work."** Zero published metrics; the public index is behind a login. Employee assertion only.

- **"Nobody runs OWL reasoners in production."** Plausible, consistent with tooling decay, and unmeasured — a folklore-shaped claim on our own side; flag it when tempted.

- **"FIBO uses SHACL."** Zero code matches across the owning org's repositories.

- **"Copilot leaked X at company Y."** No named public incident exists; the only documented case is Microsoft's own hypothetical ([`permissions.md`](permissions.md) has what *is* on the record).
