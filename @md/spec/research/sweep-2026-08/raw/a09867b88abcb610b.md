# WHO ELSE IS SAYING THIS

**Method note (matters for how you read the absences):** the session's web-search budget was exhausted before I ran a single query, and every general search engine I could reach by curl (DuckDuckGo, Bing, Mojeek, Brave, Startpage, Qwant, a dozen SearxNG instances, Google via jina) returned captchas, rate-limits or ad-junk. I rebuilt a research path from three unblocked sources: the **HN Algolia API** (full corpus, exact dates and point counts, current to 2026-08-26), the **yc-oss YC company dataset** (6,191 companies, last updated 2026-08-26), and **direct page fetches**. That is strong on essays-with-traction, YC startups and named sites; it is weak on VC blog posts, academic venues (PLDI/UIST/Onward!), and non-English material. Treat my absences in those three areas as unproven.

---

## 1. HEADLINE

- **Nobody I found is arguing the whole thesis.** Every neighbour holds two or three of the six claims and explicitly rejects or ignores the rest. The combination — typed medium + context as address + harness dissolution + historical enclosure argument — appears nowhere as a single position.
- **Claim 1 ("text is not data") is the loneliest, and it is actively losing an argument in public.** The most-read 2025–26 writing on how to extend LLMs argues the *opposite*: markdown, bash and natural language beat typed tool schemas. Sawyer Hood's "The Bitter Lesson of LLM Extensions" (144 pts, Nov 2025) and Cloudflare's "Code Mode" (Sep 2025) are the two clearest statements, and Anthropic shipped Agent Skills — markdown folders — as the successor to MCP tool definitions.
- **Claim 6 (provenance) has a real, unfunded, technically serious champion: Kendall Clark's Pentad Labs.** PLRN/020 (2026-07-20) defines a fact as a five-slot "Pentad" — subject, predicate, object, **context**, **lineage** — explicitly "rather than burying context within narrative transcripts," with "provable provenance" and "nothing an agent has seen, said, or done is lost." That is claims 2 and 6 stated almost word-for-word. It is a design essay plus a pre-release product (WunderOS), not a shipped substrate.
- **Claim 2 has a crowded commercial lane and a shallow one.** Nessie (YC F25), Hyper (YC P26), OzBrain, Letta, ktx/Kaelio all say "sessions don't compound." Almost all of them solve it by *summarising transcripts into a store you retrieve from* — i.e. they keep the transcript and bolt on a memory layer. None of them make context an *addressable, reproducible location*.
- **Claim 4's specific historical argument — Unix, then the web, then AI, each enclosed by applications that compose only inside their own frame — I could not find anyone making.** Ink & Switch makes the app-enclosure half without the medium-lineage half; Rakhim Davletkaliyev makes the semantic-web half ("AI is impressive because we've failed at semantic web and personal computing", 206 pts, 2025-08-08, 403 to my fetcher). The three-term historical arc is, as far as this search reaches, unclaimed.
- **The strongest opposition is not "structure is hard" — it's Dynamicland's.** Bret Victor's group states the inverse principle outright: *"to maximize agency, minimize what the computer knows."* That is a first-principles attack on the whole idea that a richer typed model of the world is emancipatory.
- **Timing: heating hard on the commercial edge, flat at the foundations.** Six-plus YC companies in the last four batches sell a "context layer"; Microsoft shipped Fabric IQ (Ignite, 2025-11-18); Palantir's ontology got a 85-point HN deep-dive (2026-02-22). Meanwhile Ink & Switch's 2026 notebook, Dynamicland and the Future of Coding community (now renamed feelingof.com) are working on the same problems they were working on in 2023, largely without AI.

## 2. CLAIM BY CLAIM

### Claim 1 — "Text is not data"

**Who says it:** In the shell lineage, **Nushell** ("a shell where all data is structured", shipped since 2019, HN 2025-09-02) and **PowerShell** hold the position without the AI argument. In the code lineage, **Unison** states it precisely — *"Other tools try to recover structure from text; Unison stores code in a database"* — but its site carries **zero** AI-agent positioning as of this fetch. **Glamorous Toolkit** (feenk, shipped) is the closest live statement of the aesthetic: *"Objects, not text"*, *"You cannot perceive anything in a software system except through a tool"* — and in 2026 it added a built-in agent harness while arguing that deterministic moldable views beat AI summaries.

**Who says the opposite, loudly:** Sawyer Hood (2025-11-24) traces plugins → custom instructions → GPTs → MCP → Agent Skills and concludes *"giving an agent general purpose tools and trusting it… might very well be the winning strategy"* and *"we will go back to extending our agents with the most accessible programming language: natural language."* Damon McMillan's study (9,649 experiments, 11 models, 4 formats, up to 10,000 tables; via Simon Willison 2026-02-09) found a **"grep tax"**: TOON, a *more* compact structured format, cost more tokens because models were unfamiliar with it.

**Grade of the closest neighbour:** shipped-system (Nushell, Unison, GT) — but none connects the claim to LLMs. **Nobody found argues that the LLM era is what makes text-as-substrate finally untenable.**

### Claim 2 — "Completion from a point in a field, not a pasted transcript"

**Closest:** **Pentad Labs / PlatypusDB** (above) — context as a first-class addressable slot on every fact. **Letta** is the most-shipped version of half of it: *"Memory cannot be bolted onto an agent as a plugin. Context and state management are core responsibilities of the agent harness itself"* (Apr 2026), with memory blocks as structured context units and an `.af` agent-file format (Apr 2025). Note the tension: Letta locates this in **the harness**, precisely where claim 3 says it must not live.

**Supporting evidence others have generated for you:** Chroma's *Context Rot* (2025-07-14, 260 pts, 18 models) — *"models do not use their context uniformly; performance grows increasingly unreliable as input length grows"*, with focused prompts beating full-transcript prompts on LongMemEval. Anthropic's own context-engineering guidance (2025-09-29) argues for lightweight identifiers and just-in-time retrieval over pre-loading.

**Not found:** anyone proposing *reproducibility* or *branching* of an LLM context as a location. Branching UIs exist (LLM-DAG-UI, TrAIn of Thought, LM Canvas — all 1–2 point Show HNs), but as chat-tree GUIs, not as addressing.

### Claim 3 — "The harness dissolves"

**Nobody argues this.** The 2025–26 movement is the *opposite direction with the same evidence*: Cloudflare Code Mode (2025-09-26) and Anthropic's code-execution-with-MCP (2025-11-04, "150,000 tokens to 2,000 tokens") both say tool registries are the wrong abstraction — and then both build a *better harness* (V8 sandboxes, filesystem tool discovery). "Agent harness" became a common noun in 2026: 41 HN stories over 80 points, including *"The agent harness belongs outside the sandbox"* (182 pts, 2026-05-02) and YC S26's OneCLI. The closest to dissolution-by-simplification are minimalist rebellions — Axe, *"a 12MB binary that replaces your AI framework"* (227 pts, 2026-03-12) and Pu.sh, *"a full coding-agent harness in 400 lines of shell"* — but these shrink the harness, they don't argue the medium removes the need for one.

### Claim 4 — "Knowledge compounds by interconnection; monoliths break it"

**Half of it is well-argued and widely read.** Ink & Switch's *Malleable Software* (Litt, Horowitz, van Hardenberg, Matthews, 2025-06-10, 294 pts) makes the app-as-enclosure case explicitly — apps as "avocado slicers", *"When data is instead shared among applications, it empowers end-users to compose tools in more flexible ways"* — and cites OpenDoc, Smalltalk and the filesystem as the pre-enclosure alternatives. Nessie's YC page makes the compounding case for AI specifically: *"none of that thinking compounds. Every session starts from zero… Science has journals, law has case law, software has git; AI-native knowledge work has had nothing."*

**The full historical arc — Unix (text; pipe and file) → web (page; link) → AI, each arriving with a whole and being enclosed — I could not find.** I searched HN's full corpus for enclosure/walled-garden/Ted Nelson/hypertext/the-web-we-lost framings above 60 points and found nothing making this argument. This is my most confident absence, with the caveat that HN is a poor index of long-form media theory.

### Claim 5 — "The interface is data over the same contracts as the data"

**Closest shipped:** **Jazz** (jazz.tools) — local-first relational DB where schema, permissions and reactive UI state are the same object, with git-like branching histories explicitly motivated by *"users collaborate with agents to modify data at a much higher rate."* **Ink & Switch's Patchwork** stores *"both user data and software code in Automerge documents"* — the strongest research statement of the claim. Its 2026-05-12 note *Progressions* (John Mumm) reports the concrete result: *"Patchwork made it relatively easy to compose tools in ways their authors didn't expect."* The 2026-04-26 note *chitter chatter* (chee) gets nearest to your framing: *"the closer we can make the loop, the less the distinction between what is written and what runs, the better it gets."*

### Claim 6 — Provenance as first-class

**Pentad** (above) is the sharpest. **Palantir** is the only one at scale: Foundry's Ontology is "nouns" (objects, properties, links) plus "verbs" (actions, functions), and the community deep-dive (2026-02-22, 85 pts) reads the strategy as *"branching and review"* being what lets AI act at *"overwhelming speed and governance simultaneously."* Notably, Palantir's own public docs do **not** foreground provenance/lineage — that's the community reading, not the vendor's pitch. **Graphify Labs** (YC S26) is the closest commercial version for code: a self-updating graph plus formal verification of every PR, *"proof that nothing broke, not a guess."* **Zero** of 1,271 YC companies from 2025–26 batches use the word "provenance" in their descriptions.

## 4. THE STRONGEST OPPOSITION

Argued at full strength, the case against runs in three layers, and the layers reinforce each other.

**(a) The bitter lesson, applied specifically to representation.** Sutton's argument, as Lukas Petersson put it (2025-01-08), is that *"betting against flexibility has historically been a losing strategy"* and that scaffolding built to compensate for model limits *"seems like fighting a losing battle."* Sawyer Hood supplies the domain-specific evidence: three years of increasingly structured extension mechanisms (plugins → MCP), each superseded by something *less* structured, ending at Agent Skills — markdown folders. If a model can read your codebase with `grep` and write its own adapter, a typed medium is a cost you pay to solve a problem the model no longer has. Karpathy's Software 3.0 framing ("the hottest new programming language is English"; the 2025-06-19 talk drew 1,481 HN points, 783 comments) is the popular form of this.

**(b) The empirical cost of structure.** McMillan's grep-tax result is the sharpest single data point: a format designed to be *better* structured for machines performed *worse*, because familiarity beats formal fitness. Jai Juneja's "Why AI company brains fail" (2026-07-20) gives the economics: *"A plain vector index costs about 0.1% of a full GraphRAG index. Three orders of magnitude, paid up front, on content nobody may ever query."* He also names the failure the thesis must answer: **entity resolution** — Acme vs ACME Holdings — remains unsolved, which means a typed medium built by extraction degrades into a typed medium of confidently wrong types. And commenters on OzBrain (2026-08-21) named the mechanism: *"LLMs are, even SOTA models, at summarizing non-trivial input batches of text… bad. They get things wrong, distort meaning."* If the thing that populates your typed field is a model, the field inherits the model's distortions and then launders them as structure.

**(c) The philosophical attack, which is the one I'd take most seriously.** Dynamicland/Realtalk has been running the counter-experiment for a decade and reports: *"to maximize agency, minimize what the computer knows."* Their objection to AI is that *"the 'smarter' the product, the less the user needs to understand"* — "outsourcing understanding in its most virulent form" — and their design goal is a system *"fully visible and understandable top-to-bottom."* A rich typed substrate that programs consume and humans mostly don't read is, on this view, another opacity, not a cure for one. Geoffrey Litt's 2026 post title says the same thing from the other side: *"Understanding is the new bottleneck."* The strongest form of the objection: **your thesis optimises for what programs receive; the binding constraint is what people can hold in their heads, and every layer of contract you add to make composition automatic makes comprehension harder.**

The trend line also runs against you: 1M-token context went GA on frontier models on 2026-03-13 (1,220 HN points). Every year the "just paste it in" option gets cheaper.

## 5. TIMING

**Heating up commercially, flat foundationally — and the two are not connected.**

Evidence for heating: "context layer" as a company category did not exist before Spring 2025 and now names at least four YC companies (Kaelio S25, Nessie F25, Corvera W26, plus Hyper P26 and Graphify S26 adjacent); Corvera reports $0→$33k MRR in four weeks (March 2026). "Agent harness" produced 41 HN stories over 80 points, all after 2025-12. Microsoft entered with Fabric IQ on 2025-11-18. Palantir's ontology went from a defence-procurement term to a 85-point HN deep-dive and a category label competitors position against.

Evidence for perennial: the underlying claims are 30–60 years old (Smalltalk, OpenDoc, Xanadu, the Semantic Web) and their current custodians are moving at research pace, not market pace — Ink & Switch published six Patchwork notes in 2026 and none of them is about AI substrates; Dynamicland is publishing FAQ pages that predate the agent era; futureofcoding.org redirected to feelingof.com and its most recent listed episode is about live programming feedback.

Evidence for a specific window closing: the extension-mechanism pendulum has swung *away* from structure twice (plugins→instructions, MCP→Skills). If Skills-style markdown wins a third time, the argument for a typed medium gets harder to make each year, not easier.
