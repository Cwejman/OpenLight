# What the evidence actually says

## ADRs: one real measurement, and it is not flattering

**Origin.** Michael Nygard, "Documenting Architecture Decisions," 2011-11-15 (https://cognitect.com/blog/2011/11/15/documenting-architecture-decisions). Verified: it is an experience report. Its entire evidence base is one sentence — the format had been used "on a few of our projects since early August" with "quite positive" early feedback. **Grade: opinion.** Everything downstream inherits this.

**Ecosystem status (all verified 2026-08-26).** `joelparkerhenderson/architecture-decision-record`: 16,734★, still pushed 2026-08-20 — a link farm, not evidence. **`npryce/adr-tools`: 5,628★, last push 2024-04-25, 69 open issues — effectively dormant.** MADR's latest release is **4.0.0, 2024-09-17** (https://github.com/adr/madr/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md); 3.0.0 was 2022-10-09. adr.github.io is a curated link list making no empirical claims. **Grade: shipped-niche.**

**ThoughtWorks Radar.** Lightweight ADRs: Trial (Nov 2016, Vol 15) → Trial (Mar 2017) → **Adopt (Nov 2017, Vol 17)** → Adopt (May 2018, Vol 18), then never revisited; the blip page reads "Last updated: May 15, 2018" and "NOT ON THE CURRENT EDITION." It reached Adopt in 14 months, on zero data, and **five years before anyone counted whether ADRs are maintained.**

**The one MSR study.** Buchgeher, Schöberl, Geist, Dorninger, Haindl, Weinreich, *IEEE Access* 11:63725–63740, **2023-06-19**, DOI 10.1109/ACCESS.2023.3287654. From 26.4M GitHub users → 282,789 candidates → manual verification → **921 repos, 6,362 ADR files**:
- **>50% of adopting repos (554) have 1–5 ADRs.** Max ever observed: 73.
- **~51% of ADR files (3,255/6,362) were committed once and never modified again.** 278 repos had every ADR edited on a single day, then abandoned.
- 453 repos (~49%) had a single ADR author.
- Nygard template: 723 repos (~75%); MADR: 129 (14%).
- **Only 42 repos (4.6%) meet all three sustained-use criteria** (≥20 ADRs, ≥2 authors, ≥6 months of editing).
- Adoption lag: first ADRs appear 2013 — **~5 years after Nygard's post.**

**Grade: paper-only study of a shipped-niche practice.** This is the closest thing to a superseded-vs-stale count that exists; the authors explicitly decline to analyze what the edits were.

**The impact study (new, and the money finding).** Santana, Figueiredo, Peixoto, Durão, Prazeres, Machado, Silveira Neto, de Almeida, "Architecture Decision Records: Adoption, Impact, and Developer Engagement in Open-Source Software," **ICSA 2026**, DOI 10.1109/ICSA66085.2026.00040. Reuses Buchgeher's 921 repos / ~5,800 ADRs, adds PyDriller + SonarQube. Verbatim: *"ADR-related variables exhibit predominantly small correlations with code quality and productivity metrics, suggesting that ADR adoption is associated with, at most, modest observable effects at scale."* And: **"about 63% of ADRs are opened directly with a status of 'accepted' or equivalent, bypassing the deliberative context that ADRs are intended to capture."** **Grade: paper-only.** This is the first study to look for an effect and it found essentially none.

**Bigger corpus, no outcome data.** de Visser & Babila Niba (VU Amsterdam **bachelor thesis**, 2026), Zenodo DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21464514, 2026-07-21: **164,372 ADR files from 25,051 repos**; median 427 words, **median 3 ADRs/repo (mean 6.6)**. Descriptive only, and it is student work — grade accordingly.

**Only in-situ trial.** Ahmeti, Linder, Groner, Wohlrab, ECSA 2024 (https://rebekkaa.github.io/files/2024_ECSA.pdf): one Swedish consultancy, two teams (8 + 6 people), **7 interviews, 3 months**. Positive on culture and knowledge transfer; unresolved for distributed systems. **The paper never reports how many ADRs were written.** Grade: paper-only, n=1.

**Template comparison.** Nogueira, Silva, Conte, arXiv:2604.27333, **2026-04-30**: DESMET expert analysis + controlled experiment, **n=33 undergraduates**. Nygard beat MADR on comprehension (p=0.002, Cliff's δ=0.64). Crucially, the paper's own related-work states the evidence gap outright — no prior work shows ADRs improve outcomes. Grade: paper-only.

**Foundations.** Jansen & Bosch, WICSA 2005, DOI 10.1109/WICSA.2005.61 — origin of "architectural knowledge vaporization"; a **conceptual paper with no measurement**. Van Heesch/Avgeriou's controlled experiments measure decision *recovery* aided by patterns, not ADR adoption effects. **The vaporization premise is asserted, never measured.**

## RFC processes: no exit path

- **IETF.** RFC 9280 (Jun 2022) → already replaced by **RFC 9920 (Feb 2026)**, which is the genuine retrospective: it admits no appeal pathway existed and that "RFCs are not changed" was unworkable; §9 names why v1/v2 failed ("lack of transparency… unclear lines of authority"). Editorial-stream output: **3 RFCs in 4 years.** Only throughput measurement anywhere: RFC 8963 (Jan 2021), **n=20**, mean 1,200 days draft→RFC, **83% of it inside working groups.** FOLKLORE flag: "IETF is 3–4× slower than 1998" traces to this single n=20 sample.
- **Rust.** Never reformed — routed around. GitHub API, 2026-08-26: **215 open RFCs, median age 905 days**, 35 open >5 years, oldest since 2017. Accepted RFCs merge in a median 70 days; the backlog just never closes. **MCPs (RFC 2904, merged 2020-05-07): 585 filed, 425 accepted, 17 open.** The lightweight process clears; the heavyweight one silts. Best critique: Nick Cameron, 2022-02-19, https://www.ncameron.org/blog/the-problem-with-rfcs/ — **opinion**, but the only one with numbers. No 2024–26 retrospective from Matsakis or withoutboats.
- **Kubernetes.** All 662 `kep.yaml` files, 2026-08-26: **343 KEPs non-terminal, only 14 ever explicitly killed, 184 with no milestone since ~late 2024.** The KEP-fatigue issue (enhancements#3399, filed 2022-06-16 by SIG-CLI) was **closed `not_planned` on 2024-12-30** — it rotted out under the stale bot. **FOLKLORE: "KEP fatigue" has no primary measurement**; those staleness numbers were computed today, not published by the project.
- **Python** is the outlier: 738 PEPs, median resolution ~164 days (2024+). It did not fix the PEP process — it **shrank what a PEP must decide** via four domain councils (PEP 729 Typing, 731 C API, 732 Docs, 772 Packaging). Same move as Rust's MCPs. **This "route around with a lighter process" is the only intervention with a visible before/after in any of the data.**
- **Oxide RFDs.** Source of record: Frazelle, 2020-07-24. Tooling open-sourced (`rfd-site` 211★, `rfd-api` 94★, both active). **Zero published metrics; rfd.shared.oxide.computer redirects to /login.** FOLKLORE: "RFDs demonstrably work at Oxide" rests entirely on employee assertion.

## Design docs: canonical text is opinion; the one controlled study says format doesn't matter

Ubl, "Design Docs at Google," https://www.industrialempathy.com/posts/design-docs-at-google/, published **2020-07-06**, modified 2022-05-27. Zero citations, zero data; no sequel. *Software Engineering at Google* Ch.10's only number is a footnote: **"around 90% of [GooWiki] documents had no views or updates in the previous few months."** Google's engineering-productivity research measures code review, not design docs.

**Best-controlled result:** Ernst & Robillard, *EMSE* 28(5), Sept 2023, DOI 10.1007/s10664-023-10347-2 — **N=65 randomized**, narrative vs structured architecture docs: **no significant effect of format on architecture understanding**; prior code exposure dominated. **Grade: paper-only.**

**DORA** is the strongest evidence, and it is about documentation *quality* generally, not design docs: 2021 report — teams with good internal docs are **2.4× more likely** to have better delivery/operational performance. Self-reported survey constructs.

**FOLKLORE, flagged:** the 1:10:100 cost-of-change curve underwriting up-front design was tested and failed — Menzies, Nichols, Shull, Layman, *EMSE* 22:1903–1935, arXiv:1609.04886, **171 projects**: *"no evidence for the delayed issue effect."* Also folklore: "6-pagers produce better decisions" (traces solely to Bezos's 2017 shareholder letter) and any specific multiplier for design docs. Orosz's 100+-company RFC catalogue (2022-06-21) is entirely descriptive — **I found no company writeup reporting measured results.**

## 2025–26: the AI turn is real and it is where the only positive effect sizes are

- **Kitayama, TechRxiv DOI 10.36227/techrxiv.177205025.54351571/v2, 2026-03-05** — controlled 12.5-turn TypeScript build, ADRs served to an LLM over MCP: **−10.1% development time (496 vs 552 min), −10.8% tool calls**; with a hidden compensating turn, 14.6%. Headline: identical TDD instruction in CLAUDE.md produced **zero E2E tests**, while conditions carrying the *rationale* in ADRs spontaneously produced 16–25. n=1 workflow, single author, preprint — **grade: paper-only, unreplicated** — but it is the first result showing ADRs causing a measured improvement in anything.
- Gupta, Dhar, Feitosa, Vaidhyanathan, **EASE 2026**, arXiv:2604.03826, 2026-04-04: 4,500+ ADRs / 750 repos; **a 3–5 record recency window matches full history** — complete architectural history is unnecessary for generation.
- Dhar et al., arXiv:2504.08207 (2025-04-11), DRAFT: 4,911 ADRs; **median ADR is 56 tokens (~42 words)**. Zhou et al., **TOSEM 2025**, arXiv:2504.20781: LLM design-rationale F1 **0.351–0.389** — recall 0.63–0.72, precision 0.27. Su et al., arXiv:2602.07609 (2026-02-07): 980 ADRs / 109 repos, 91.1% accuracy detecting decision violations.
- Competing artifact: **AGENTS.md**, now under the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation, self-reporting "**60k+ open-source projects**" (https://agents.md/, retrieved 2026-08-26) — self-reported, unaudited, but that is ~65× Buchgeher's ADR-adopting repo count in a fraction of the time.

## Bottom line

Fifteen years after Nygard, **there is exactly one study that looked for an effect of ADRs on quality or productivity (ICSA 2026) and it found "at most, modest observable effects."** Half of all ADRs in the wild are written once and abandoned; 63% skip the deliberation they exist to capture; 4.6% of adopting repos show sustained team use. RFC processes have no published outcome evidence at all — only process documents admitting the previous process failed, and a consistent structural finding that heavyweight processes accumulate an unclosable backlog while teams route around them with lighter ones. Design docs rest on one 2020 blog post and a controlled study saying format is irrelevant. The strongest justification in circulation (late defects cost 10–100×) is refuted by a 171-project study. The one place a positive, measured effect appears is 2026 work on decision records as **LLM context** — and that is a single unreplicated preprint.
