## DORA on documentation quality, 2021–2025 — primary-source findings

**2021 (Accelerate State of DevOps 2021, PDF at `dora.dev/research/2021/dora-report/2021-dora-accelerate-state-of-devops-report.pdf`, published Sept 2021).** N = **1,200** working professionals this cycle ("more than 32,000" cumulative since 2014). Definition (p.21): documentation quality measured "by the degree to which the documentation: • helps readers accomplish their goals • is accurate, up-to-date, and comprehensive • is findable, well organized, and clear," footnoted to Aghajani et al. 2019, Plösch et al. 2014, Zhi et al. 2015. The actual instrument (dora.dev/research/2021/questions/) is **seven agree/disagree Likert items**, all perception-based, e.g. "I can rely on our technical documentation when I need to use or work with the services or applications I work on," "It is easy to find the right technical document…," "Technical documentation is updated as changes are made," plus multi-select practice checklists (ownership, archiving, recognition in performance reviews). Verbatim findings: "about 25% of respondents have good quality documentation"; "teams with higher quality documentation are **2.4 times more likely** to see better software delivery and operational (SDO) performance"; teams with quality documentation are "**3.8 times** more likely to implement security practices," "**2.4 times** more likely to meet or exceed their reliability targets," "**3.5 times** more likely to implement Site Reliability Engineering (SRE) practices," "**2.5 times** more likely to fully leverage the cloud." No confidence intervals, effect sizes, or p-values are published for any of these. Methodology (ch. 8, p.44): "cross-sectional, theory-based design… known as inferential predictive"; recruitment "via email lists, online promotions, an online panel, social media, and asked people to share the survey with their networks (**that is, snowball sampling**)"; constructs via exploratory factor analysis / PCA with varimax, AVE, Cronbach's alpha; "structural equation models (SEM) using **Partial Least Squares (PLS)** analysis, which is a **correlation-based SEM**"; latent class cluster analysis with BIC for the performance clusters.

**2022 (N > 1,350; PDF `dora.dev/research/2022/…`).** Under "Surprises" (p.57), verbatim: "**Documentation practices negatively impacted software delivery performance. This is at odds with previous reports.** One hypothesis is that documentation is becoming an increasingly automated practice… Until we collect additional data, we have little evidence to either support or refute this belief." The widely-cited percentage-lift table (continuous delivery 63%→656%, trunk-based development 36%→1525%, etc.) appears only on `dora.dev/capabilities/documentation-quality/` and the Irvine & DeBellis blog "Documentation is like sunshine" (cloud.google.com/blog/products/devops-sre/deep-dive-into-2022-state-of-devops-report-on-documentation, **dated June 16, 2023**), not in the 2022 PDF itself; there it's an eight-item measure scored 0–1. Treat those percentages as **secondary-source-only**.

**2023 (N "nearly 3,000"; 36,000 cumulative; "3.6x increase in the number of organic respondents compared to 2022").** Measure: "the degree to which documentation is reliable, findable, updated, and relevant… one score for the entire documentation experience." Amplification table (p.28), "*Calculated by: Impact of technical capability with high documentation quality / Impact of technical capability with low documentation quality": continuous integration **2.4x**, continuous delivery **2.7x**, trunk-based development **12.8x**, loosely coupled architecture **1.2x**, reliability practices **1.4x**, AI contribution **1.5x**. Outcomes table: burnout "Substantial decrease," job satisfaction/productivity/team/organizational/operational performance "Substantial increase" — and software delivery performance "**No effect**," footnoted: "**We continue to be surprised to find no effect of quality documentation on software delivery performance. This is the second year we see this behavior.**" That directly contradicts the 2021 headline 2.4x SDO number. Also: as documentation quality rises, some respondents report *higher* burnout, and underrepresented respondents saw no burnout reduction.

**2024 (N "nearly 3,000"; 39,000 cumulative).** Documentation quality = "The perception of internal documentation (manuals, readmes, code comments) in terms of its reliability, findability, updatedness, and ability to provide support." Headline: "A 25% increase in AI adoption is associated with a… **7.5% increase in documentation quality**" (vs 3.4% code quality, 3.1% code review speed, 1.3% approval speed, 1.8% decrease in code complexity). Figure 16: quality internal documentation amplifies user-centricity's effect on product performance — "internal documentation doesn't meaningfully affect predicted product performance without user signals" — no multiplier given. Methodology p.110: "we didn't do longitudinal studies or a proper experiment… **Correlation does not imply causation, but it does imply how you think about causation.**"

**2025 ("State of AI-assisted Software Development," v. 2025.2, PDF at `services.google.com/fh/files/misc/2025_state_of_ai_assisted_software_development.pdf`; landing page `dora.dev/research/2025/dora-report/`; announced ~23 Sept 2025).** N = **4,867** survey respondents ("nearly 5,000"), plus 78 semi-structured interviews; "a global survey conducted between **June 13 and July 21, 2025**." "Documentation quality" is **no longer a named capability**. Its successors are two of the seven amplifier capabilities: **"healthy data ecosystems"** (three indicators: overall quality of internal data sources; accessibility; degree to which sources are siloed) and **"AI-accessible internal data"** (four indicators: perception that AI tools have access to internal company information; that responses used internal company information as context; frequency of inputting internal company info in prompts; frequency of using AI tools to retrieve it). Claims: "With a high degree of certainty, we found that AI adoption's positive benefits depend on organizations having healthy data ecosystems, such that, when they do, AI's positive influence on organizational performance is amplified"; for AI-accessible internal data, "AI's positive influence on individual effectiveness is amplified" and "on code quality is amplified." **Critically, Figures 35–37 publish no numbers at all** — the moderation is shown as ordinal bands ("Unsubstantiated / Small increase / Medium increase / Large increase") across "extremely low → extremely high." Any circulating "Nx" figure for 2025 internal-data quality is **FOLKLORE**.

**Caveats, in DORA's own words.** Self-report throughout; 2025 methodology chapter concedes self-reported data's "inherent subjectivity means that interpretations can vary, and it may be more susceptible to biases (including recall bias and social desirability bias)." Sampling is organic snowball plus a purchased panel — 2023/2024/2025 all repeat: "we ask people in the community to do the same (that is, snowball sampling)… We use the panel approach to supplement the organic channel." 2024 benchmarks its sample against the Stack Overflow Developer Survey to "locate response bias." The strongest explicit retreat from causal language is 2025 endnote 20: "**Last year, we spoke in terms of 'effects'. This year, however, we will speak in terms of comparisons. Although we try to do the work to create the conditions to speak causally, we don't want to give false assurances that we understand the underlying causal structure**," quoting Gelman/Hill/Vehtari's *Regression and Other Stories*: "The safest interpretation of a regression is as a comparison." Also endnote 19: "Adoption is dynamic, but given we have a snapshot… If we had panel data, we could be more explicit about the cycle."

**Independent critique.** Sallin, Kropp, Anslow, Quilty & Meier, "Measuring Software Delivery Performance Using the Four Key Metrics of DevOps," *XP 2021*, LNBIP 419 (DOI 10.1007/978-3-030-78098-2_7; open-access copy at digitalcollection.zhaw.ch/handle/11475/22989): "The FKM in the State of DevOps report is based on surveys. While a survey approach has the advantage that you can raise highly focused questions… it also comes with several disadvantages: the **absence of a clear definition of the measurement, no continuous data, subjective answers, offline data capture and analysis**, and extra effort and cost to generate the data." Their multivocal literature review found that of 93 papers citing Forsgren et al., "**none of them contained more information about the FKM than already presented by Forsgren et al.**" — i.e. no independent replication existed as of 2021. Related automation-motivated work: "A Framework for Automating the Measurement of DORA Metrics," ICSME 2023 (10.1109/ICSME58846.2023.00018); "Fully Automated DORA Metrics Measurement for Continuous Improvement," ICSSP 2024 (10.1145/3666015.3666020). I found **no peer-reviewed paper that specifically critiques or attempts to replicate the documentation-quality construct or its multipliers** — arXiv, Crossref and Semantic Scholar queries returned nothing. (My WebSearch budget was exhausted mid-task, so I worked from direct PDF downloads and the Crossref/Unpaywall/arXiv APIs; the search for critiques is therefore less exhaustive than the primary-source work.)

**Bottom line for citation hygiene.** The "2.4x" is real and primary (2021, p.21) but DORA's own 2022 and 2023 reports report *no effect* — and in 2022 a *negative* association — of documentation on software delivery performance. The 12.8x trunk-based-development amplifier is real (2023, p.28) but is a ratio of two unreported coefficients with no interval. Every figure across all five years is a cross-sectional, self-reported, snowball-plus-panel correlational estimate, and by 2025 DORA explicitly declines to call any of it an "effect."
